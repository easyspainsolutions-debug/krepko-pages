    Frequently asked questions

    Power of attorney or consent: which one do I need?
    A power of attorney gives someone the right to act on your behalf: to sell, sign or collect. A consent confirms that you do not object to someone else's action: a child's trip, or the child living in another country. Not sure? Tell us where you are submitting the document and we will say which one you need.
    Do I need an apostille?
    A document signed before a Spanish notary for a Spanish authority does not need an apostille. A document from Ukraine for Spain and a Spanish document for Ukraine are apostilled: both countries are parties to the Hague Convention. The exception is the border crossing certificate, which is not apostilled.
    Can I get a document from Ukraine without leaving Spain?
    Yes. We request certificates and duplicates in Ukraine without you travelling, have them apostilled, arrange the sworn translation and deliver them to Spain.
    I don't speak Spanish. How does the signing work?
    A Krepko representative is present at the notary's office and translates and explains every clause in your language. You sign knowing what you are signing.
    How much does your support cost?
    It depends on the specific task. Our price is always fixed, agreed and known before we start, with no extra charges or surprises along the way.
    When and how do I pay?
    First we look at your case, free of charge. We agree the price and how you pay before work begins, so that no unexpected amounts come up along the way.
    Do I have to be there in person?
    Not always. Some procedures require you to take part, others can be done with a power of attorney or remotely. We will tell you straight away which category your case falls into, so as not to cause you any unnecessary inconvenience.
    How will I know what is happening with my case?
    We will keep you informed at every step: accepted, filed, ready. You will not need to chase us or ask “so, how is it going?”. You will hear about everything from us first, not after the fact.
    What happens if a document is rejected because of a mistake?
    If the mistake is ours, we redo it at our own expense. If the issue is a change in the authority's requirements, we will tell you plainly and suggest a way out.
    And if, after we talk, I decide not to work with you?
    Then we won't. The conversation commits you to nothing: we tell you what to do, and you decide.
