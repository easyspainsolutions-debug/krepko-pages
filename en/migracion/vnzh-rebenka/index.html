    Residence permit for a child

    • a specialist will reply within 15 minutes
    • terms agreed before we start
    • in Russian, Ukrainian and Spanish
    Message us on WhatsApp The first consultation is free

    In short. A child born in Spain is granted a residence permit under art. 159 of RD 1155/2024, and a child brought to Spain under art. 160; in both cases straight away for 5 years, without a visa. For families under temporary protection, SEM Instruction 2/2026 allows them to apply at any time, even if the parents remain under protection, and under art. 160 it removes the requirements of two years' residence, means and housing. The decision comes within a month; silence from the office means refusal.

    In Spanish immigration law, children have their own routes — and noticeably more generous ones than adults. Both articles on children grant a residence permit straight away for five years, without a visa, and for families with temporary protection, SEM Instruction 2/2026 has removed the main barriers: you can apply at any time, even if both parents remain under protection for now.

    Parents are often busy with their own status and overlook the child's — yet it is simpler to arrange and gives more. We review the situation of the whole family at once: the routes of parents and children are linked, and the right sequence of applications saves months.

    Two routes — determined by one question

    The whole fork comes down to one thing: where the child was born.

    The child was born in Spain — Article 159

    A permit for five years on the basis of the birth certificate from the Spanish Registro Civil (civil registry). For families with temporary protection, the application deadline is not pressing: the six-month window starts running only from the moment a parent obtains a status other than temporary protection. While the whole family is under protection, you can apply at any time.

    You will need the Spanish birth certificate and the documents of the parent with the status. If the parents' documents are Ukrainian, we will put them in order: apostille, sworn translations, checking that names match.

    The child was brought to Spain — Article 160

    For children born outside the country and children under guardianship. Under the ordinary procedure, the article requires two years of the child's residence and proof of the parents' means and housing — for families with temporary protection, all three requirements have been removed by SEM Instruction 2/2026. Conditions: the child is under 18 at the time of application and lives with the parent or guardian.

    The main work here is the Ukrainian documents: the birth certificate with an apostille and a sworn translation and, if necessary, the other parent's consent. The apostille is affixed in Ukraine before the translation — getting the sequence wrong results in a request for additional documents.

    A child's five-year card is the longest permit the family can obtain at this stage, and it does not need to be "renewed": after five years the child moves straight to long-term residence. It is worth dealing with the card not "after your own documents", but together with them.

    What we need from you

    What is needed depends on the route — the list is indicative:

    • birth certificate — Spanish, or Ukrainian with an apostille and a translation;
    • the full valid passport of the child and of the parent;
    • documents on the parent's status;
    • the family's municipal registration;
    • school enrolment (matrícula) — for school-age children;
    • the other parent's consent — if the office requests it (requerimiento).

    Timeframes and payment

    The legal deadline for a decision is one month. Preparing Ukrainian documents with an apostille takes several weeks — that is where to start. Silence from the office after the deadline is deemed a refusal, so the case needs to be monitored, and requests must be answered within ten days.

    The cost is calculated individually after the review. The review is free.

    Teenagers aged 16–17 — apply before eighteen

    Article 160 looks at age at the time of application. It gives a teenager five years with no work or income requirements — whereas after coming of age only the adult routes will remain: one year under arraigo or four under a change of status (modificación), and with conditions. If there is a teenager in the family, their application should go first in the queue.

    Why it is better not to put off the child's status

    • the child's card does not depend on extensions of temporary protection;
    • school, healthcare and travel are easier with their own residence permit;
    • the child's five years count in full towards long-term residence;
    • the child's status can strengthen the parents' future routes — and the child's documents can be submitted at the same time as a parent's arraigo application, with the decisions issued together.

    The order after approval — this is where mistakes are most common

    Renouncing temporary protection is done only after a positive decision on the new permit — the reverse order leaves the child with no status at all. Next come the fingerprint appointment and the card; the child is brought to the appointment, and one parent is enough. We guide you through this sequence step by step, including properly completing the renunciation and handing in the old card.

    Frequently asked questions

    Our child was born in Spain, and we are under temporary protection. What is the child entitled to?
    A permit under art. 159 — straight away for five years. While you are under protection, there is no strict application deadline: the six-month window starts only after a parent changes status. Write to us — we will work out the dates for your family.
    My child is 16 and arrived two years ago. Is there a route?
    Yes, art. 160: for families with temporary protection, the residence, means and housing requirements have been removed. It is important to apply before the child turns eighteen — after that the conditions are noticeably worse. It is worth hurrying.
    Do we first have to leave temporary protection ourselves?
    No. SEM Instruction 2/2026 explicitly allows applying for a child even if neither parent has changed status yet.
    The birth certificate has been lost. What should I do?
    We will obtain a duplicate in Ukraine through our team, have it apostilled and arrange a sworn translation — without you having to travel.
    The child's father stayed in Ukraine. Does this block the procedure?
    It does not block it, but Extranjería (the immigration office) may request the other parent's notarised consent to the child living in Spain. We arrange a notarial deed before a Spanish notary; if the father cannot come, we explain how to sign the consent at a Spanish consulate or before a notary in Ukraine with an apostille and a sworn translation.
    Will the child's card need to be renewed?
    No — there is no "renewal" for children's permits: five years straight away, then long-term residence. Children with old short permits are issued a new one for the remainder up to five years, without proof of means and housing.
    Does the child's status affect my husband's and my documents?
    It may — in family routes the statuses are linked, and the child's and a parent's applications can be combined. That is why we review the family as a whole, not each person separately.
    My daughter has just joined me, and I have a residence permit — where do I start with her application?
    The child's permit is arranged under art. 160 on the basis of your status: you will need the birth certificate with an apostille and a translation and proof that you live together. We will check the set and prepare the application.
    My child has no Spanish documents — will a school accept them?
    Yes, children are enrolled in school regardless of immigration status — municipal registration is usually enough. The residence permit can be arranged in parallel with schooling; the school enrolment will later be useful in the case itself.
