    Switching from temporary protection to a residence permit

    Temporary protection cards have been extended until 4 March 2027

    The Council of the EU extended the status itself until 4 March 2028, but Spain has not yet published how the cards will be extended for that period. Protection has already been extended several times, and each time it was a decision by the authorities, not your right. Your own residence permit does not depend on such decisions.

    Which route is yours

    Three questions and you will see which routes are open to you. The answer appears on screen straight away, no sign-up needed.

    In short. Temporary protection cards in Spain are valid until 4 March 2027, and the Council of the EU extended the status itself until 4 March 2028; Spain has not yet issued an order on the cards for that period. RD 316/2026 and SEM Instruction 2/2026 allow you to switch to your own residence permit right now, without losing the time you have spent under protection. About 263,000 people are under protection (262,830 at the end of March 2026, according to SEM), and most of them have more than two years of residence — the period from which the main routes open up.

    The difference between the two statuses is simple. Temporary protection is extended by a decision from above — it has already been extended several times, and each time it was someone else's decision. Your own residence permit is renewed under clear rules, gives you the right to work and leads to permanent residence.

    What routes exist

    There are more than ten routes. Which one suits you depends on your time in Spain, your work and your family. Here are the main ones.

    If you work officially

    • Change to residence and work (art. 191) — a direct switch from temporary protection to a residence permit. If you have been in Spain for more than a year on a permit with the right to work — protection gives you one — and you work officially, the new permit is issued straight away for four years, whereas under arraigo sociolaboral, social and socioformativo it is issued for one year.
    • Arraigo sociolaboral — through a signed employment contract — or several — totalling at least 20 hours a week, with at least two years of residence in Spain.

    If you do not work officially

    • Arraigo social — through close relatives with a residence permit or through an integration report from the autonomous community.
    • Arraigo socioformativo — through enrolment in vocational training.

    Family situations

    • Residence for minor children — children have their own routes, and separate concessions apply to families with temporary protection. A child born in Spain can receive a permit straight away for five years.
    • Other parent's consent for a child's residence — if Extranjería has sent a requerimiento (request for documents) in your child's case: a deed before a notary within 10 working days.
    • Residence for relatives of Spanish citizens — a separate permit for five years; RD 316/2026 and SEM Instruction 2/2026 opened it to holders of protection. For spouses of citizens of other EU countries — the regime for family members of EU citizens.

    If you have been here a long time

    • Larga duración — with five years of residence you can apply straight away for long-term residence. Time under temporary protection counts.

    What we need from you

    The set depends on the route, so the list below is indicative. We will draw up the exact list after reviewing your situation.

    • your passport and your temporary protection card;
    • municipal registration (empadronamiento) with its history;

    Why it is worth dealing with this now

    Three reasons not to put it off.

    You have already built up the time. Time lived under temporary protection counts towards your period of residence. For most people this is already more than two years — and two years is exactly when the main arraigo (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain) routes open up. Many people have already reached this period and do not know it.

    The rules are favourable today. RD 316/2026 and SEM Instruction 2/2026 removed the restrictions that previously closed the path to arraigo and change of status (modificación) for holders of temporary protection. These rules apply now, but they are not a law: after the elections on 29 November 2026, the new government is entitled to revise them. It makes sense to apply while the current rules are in force.

    Your own residence permit is more reliable. Temporary protection is extended by decisions of the authorities, and its future depends on external circumstances. Your own residence permit — with the right to work, renewal under clear rules and the prospect of permanent residence — does not depend on those decisions.

    How long preparation really takes

    People put it off because it seems you can apply at any moment. In practice, between the decision and the application there is a queue of timeframes set by others.

    1. Criminal record certificate from UkraineThe certificate with an apostille takes about 20 working days (about 10 if urgent), delivery 5–7 working days, then a sworn translation. This is the longest link in the chain. This is exactly where you need to start.
    2. Spanish documentsMunicipal registration with its history, certificates of employment or studies, bank statements: a week or two, if your registration is in order.
    3. Cita previa at ExtranjeríaDepending on the province, the wait for an appointment (cita previa) to apply at Extranjería (the immigration office) ranges from a few days to several weeks. You do not choose the date.
    4. ProcessingBy law, up to three months; in practice it depends on the region and the workload.

    Add it up: from the first message to having the card in your hands usually takes three to six months. Count back from March 2027, when the cards expire, and it becomes clear why “later” has already arrived.

    What you should know, honestly

    The switches for holders of protection were opened by RD 316/2026 and SEM Instruction 2/2026 — a government decree and an instruction from the Secretariat of State for Migration, not a change in the law. The administration is obliged to follow them, and applications under these routes work. But no one can promise the outcome of an immigration procedure: the decision is always made by Extranjería, and in disputed cases a lawyer defends your position.

    Our part is the preparation: a correctly chosen route, a complete set of documents, accurate translations and legalisation, and follow-up at every step.

    Frequently asked questions

    Will I lose temporary protection if I apply for a residence permit?
    No. Filing an application does not cancel your current status — you remain under protection while the application is being processed.
    I do not have official employment. Is the way out closed?
    No. The arraigo social and socioformativo routes do not require a current employment contract. We will review your situation and tell you which path is realistic.
    Do I need to wait for temporary protection to end?
    No, and waiting does not pay off: RD 316/2026 and SEM Instruction 2/2026 allow you to apply right now, and the conditions may change in the future.
    What will change after the elections on 29 November?
    As of today, nothing: applications are processed under the current rules. But these rules were set by the government, not by law, and a new government is entitled to change them without a vote in parliament. When and how, no one can say in advance, so it makes sense to apply while they are in force.
    Which documents from Ukraine will I need?
    Most often, a criminal record certificate with an apostille and a sworn translation. For family routes, birth and marriage certificates. We obtain them through our team in Ukraine, without you having to travel.
    What happens if the residence permit is refused?
    A refusal can be appealed — a lawyer handles this. Your temporary protection is kept in the meantime, so you lose nothing. Most refusals are linked to an incomplete set of documents — which is precisely why we prepare the application so carefully.
    Protection will end sooner or later — what will happen to us then?
    Once the protection regime ends, you will be left with whatever status you have managed to obtain, so it is better to prepare the switch to your own residence permit in advance. We will review your situation and suggest a realistic route.
    There are four of us in the family under protection — should we all switch to a residence permit at once or one by one?
    Not necessarily at the same time: each person may have their own grounds, while the others keep their protection in the meantime. We will lay out the options for each family member and suggest an order for the applications.
