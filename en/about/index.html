    At first it was my problem

    Message us on WhatsApp

    Timeline

    Arrived and understood nothing

    Ukraine was behind me; ahead lay a language I didn’t know and service windows that close at 14:00. My first municipal registration took three months instead of two weeks.

    No company yet. And nowhere to ask.

    Empadronamiento (municipal registration) required a rental contract, the rental required a bank account, the bank required registration, and registration required a set of documents I didn’t have. It was a closed circle, and I only managed to break it through the right contacts: they pointed me to a non-obvious order of steps.

    Figured it out and helped people

    First I helped one acquaintance, then his brother, then strangers from a group chat. I realised that everyone stumbles over the same things.

    A route took shape: a sequence of steps that works. For now, only in my head and in messages.

    The first was a neighbour in my building: he couldn’t get a power of attorney for Ukraine done. Then his friend, then people from online groups. By the end of the year I was going to public offices more often than running my own errands.

    After February 2022 it reached a different scale: people arrived who had not planned to move and knew neither the language nor the procedures. I helped every day and gained experience fast — and realised I could not handle this influx alone. That was when the team began to come together.

    Stopped doing it for free

    When there were more requests than free days, the choice was simple: say no, or make it my work. I chose the second.

    Krepko founded. The first paying clients, the first fixed price, the first promise: the amount is known before work begins.

    A price named before work begins, and it does not go up. This rule came from personal experience: twice I was billed more than I had been promised, and both times it was put down to “the complexity of the case”.

    Realised one city is not enough

    People wrote from Valencia, Murcia, Madrid. Travelling halfway across the country myself is not a service, it is heroics at your expense.

    Local representatives came on board — people who had been through the system themselves in their own city. Plus a team in Ukraine for documents from there.

    Only people who have been through the system themselves and live in the city permanently. A specialist who comes in for a day does not know which window is open in the afternoon or which office sees people without an appointment.

    Doing the same, but not alone

    I still sometimes go to the notary with clients. But now it is a company.

    Six cities in Spain, a team in Ukraine, four languages.

    Krepko’s aim is to erase the border between a person and the state. Not the one on the map, but the one that appears at the service window when you understand neither the language nor the procedure.

    Hence the way we grow: not spreading across the country, but going where there is almost nobody to turn to. Today that is six cities in Spain and a team in Ukraine; tomorrow, the next city where a person is left alone with the paperwork.

    «

    I still remember the feeling of standing at the service window, not understanding a word, while the queue behind me grew. That was when I decided to found Krepko, and now I look back with nostalgia on the days when I didn’t know where to start.

    Four ruleswe haven’tchanged

    Everything else in the company has changed more than once: cities, team, services. These four points have not.

    1. 01

      The price is known before we start

      We look at your task and name the amount — and it does not go up along the way.

    2. 02

      A person by your side, not a chat

      A real representative comes to the notary, the bank or the public office.

    3. 03

      We message you first

      Accepted, filed, done. You don’t need to chase us or ask “so, how is it going?”

    4. 04

      Our mistake — our lesson

      If a document is rejected through our fault, we redo it free of charge.
