    Renouncing an inheritance in Spain

    • a specialist will reply within 15 minutes
    • terms agreed before we start
    • in Russian, Ukrainian and Spanish
    Message us on WhatsApp The first consultation is free

    We prepare the notarial deed renouncing an inheritance (renuncia), check what the estate consists of before you make a decision and arrange the signing before a notary — in person or under a power of attorney from another country.

    A renunciation is made when an inheritance brings more problems than benefits: the deceased's debts, the tax burden, a dispute between co-heirs. The decision cannot be reversed, so we first look into the situation — and only then prepare the deed.

    What we need from you

    As a rule, you will need:

    • the heir's passport, DNI or NIE;
    • the deceased's death certificate;
    • the will or documents proving the family relationship;
    • information on what the estate consists of — property, accounts, debts;
    • the details of the other heirs, if known.

    How the process works

    1. Reviewing your situationWe study what the estate consists of: assets, debts and who the heirs are. If the picture is incomplete, we arrange tracing of inherited assets — the decision must be based on facts.
    2. Preparing the documentsWe prepare the text of the renuncia deed and agree it with the notary.
    3. Signing the documentsWe prepare and organise the signing before the notary. Your presence will be needed for just 30 minutes.
    4. Receiving the finished documentsWe obtain a copy of the deed, arrange the translation and apostille if needed and send the documents to you.

    Timeframes and payment

    Preparing the deed usually takes a few working days — depending on the notary's schedule and how complete the information about the estate is. The preliminary check may take longer: it is safer to establish the assets and debts before signing, not after.

    One all-inclusive payment: we quote the price after reviewing your case, you pay once the document is signed, no advance payment. The consultation and review are free.

    When an inheritance is renounced

    People most often come to renounce an inheritance in three situations:

    • the deceased's debts are comparable to or exceed the assets;
    • the tax burden makes accepting the inheritance unprofitable;
    • the heir does not want to take part in a dispute between co-heirs.

    Where there is a risk of debts, renunciation is not the only way out. The alternative is acceptance with an inventory, a beneficio de inventario: the heir is liable for debts only up to the value of the estate's assets. We discuss this option before you sign a renunciation.

    What you need to know before signing

    A renunciation is irrevocable: once the notarial deed has been signed, the share cannot be reclaimed.

    A renunciation cannot be partial. The approach "I'll take the flat but not the debts" does not work — an inheritance is accepted or declined as a whole. Nor can it be renounced subject to a condition.

    The share of the person who renounces does not disappear — under the rules of succession it passes on to other heirs. We explain in advance to whom your share will pass.

    The signing takes minutes. The preparation takes experience. We take care of both.

    Not sure whether to renounce the inheritance?

    Describe your situation, and we will look into it together: what the estate includes, what debts there are and which is more advantageous — renunciation or acceptance with an inventory.

    For a preliminary assessment, it is enough to tell us how you were related to the deceased, what is known about the estate and the debts, and whether you are in Spain.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I change my mind after renouncing?
    As a general rule — no. A renunciation is irrevocable: once the notarial deed has been signed, the share in the inheritance cannot be reclaimed. That is why we check what the estate consists of and review the alternatives before the visit to the notary.
    Can I renounce in favour of a specific person?
    A simple renunciation follows the law: the share passes to other heirs, and you cannot choose the recipient. A "renunciation in favour" of a specific person is legally treated as accepting the inheritance and then transferring it — such a step has its own tax consequences, and we warn you about them in advance.
    What happens to the deceased's debts?
    The person who renounces receives nothing — neither assets nor debts. If you want to keep the property but limit liability, acceptance with an inventory is usually considered: the heir is liable for debts only up to the value of the estate's assets.
    Can I renounce an inheritance remotely?
    Yes — under a power of attorney with a special power. You sign the power of attorney for an inheritance wherever you are, and a representative in Spain signs the renuncia deed on your behalf.
    What if the heir is a minor?
    A renunciation on behalf of a minor is made with additional protection of their interests — as a rule, separate authorisations are required. We clarify the requirements for the specific case in advance.
    The inheritance was opened in Ukraine, but I am in Spain — can I renounce it from here?
    Yes, the statement of renunciation is signed before a notary in Spain and sent to Ukraine. We will draw up the text to the requirements of the Ukrainian notary and prepare your signing and the delivery of the document.
    Many years have passed since the death and I have not done anything — does that count as a renunciation?
    No, silence does not count as a renunciation: the right to the inheritance is retained, but late-payment surcharges on the taxes may have built up. We will check the state of the case and arrange a renunciation or an acceptance — whichever you decide.
