    Other parent's consent for a child's residence permit

    • about 30 minutes at the notary
    • we translate every clause at the signing
    • a specialist will reply within 15 minutes
    Message us on WhatsApp The first consultation is free

    In short. You applied for a residence permit for your child under art. 160 of RD 1155/2024 and received a request from Extranjería (the immigration office) — a requerimiento de subsanación — asking for the other parent's consent. You have 10 working days to reply, otherwise the case is closed. We review the request, prepare the text of the notarial record (acta de manifestaciones) in Spanish, accompany the signing before the notary with every clause translated, and explain how to submit the reply through Mercurio. One record covers all the children in the family and remains valid until they come of age. No apostille is needed.

    The other parent's acta de manifestaciones: a notarial record in which the father or mother declares their consent for the child to live in Spain with the other parent. It is not a travel consent and not a permission for the child to live in another country: the document is addressed to the Oficina de Extranjería and is drawn up to meet its requirements.

    How the process works

    1. We review the requestWe check the case number, the date of notification and exactly what the office is asking for: only the record or also documents on custody and municipal registration.
    2. We check the family's documentsBirth certificates, marriage or divorce, court decisions, municipal registration, the applicant parent's residence permit.
    3. We draft the text of the record in SpanishThis is what Extranjería needs. We set out the family relationship, parental rights, whom the children live with, the parent's status in Spain, the other parent's consent, the period of validity and the purpose: the Oficina de Extranjería, the case numbers and subsequent procedures.
    4. We prepare and accompany the signingA Krepko representative is present at the notary's office and translates and explains every clause in your language.
    5. We hand over the record and explain the submissionYou submit the reply to Extranjería yourself through Mercurio with Cl@ve or a digital certificate. We tell you what to attach, in what form and by when.

    The signing takes minutes. The right text and the right order of submission take experience. That is what we take on.

    Timeframes and payment

    You have 10 days from notification to reply (art. 160.5 of RD 1155/2024), and these are working days (art. 30.2 LPACAP). An electronic notification not opened within 10 calendar days is deemed to have been delivered (art. 43.2 LPACAP). Without a reply, the case is closed: desistimiento (withdrawal) and archiving.

    You can ask for the deadline to be extended under art. 68.2 and 32 LPACAP: by no more than 5 days and only in case of special difficulties (dificultades especiales); if the other parent is abroad, you can ask for the maximum extension, that is, the full 5 days. The request must be filed before the deadline expires; the decision is up to the office, an extension is not assured, and the decision cannot be appealed.

    We give you the timeframe for preparing the record after reviewing the request. The clock in your case is already running, so write to us straight away: if only a few days are left before the deadline, say so in your first message.

    One all-inclusive payment: we quote the price after reviewing your case, you pay once the document is signed, no advance payment. The consultation and review are free.

    When the other parent's consent is needed

    A parent with a residence permit applies for a child who was not born in Spain under art. 160 of RD 1155/2024 and receives a requerimiento de subsanación. The office asks for one of two things: either a collective municipal registration (padrón colectivo), if both parents live with the child, or documents on custody and actual care (guarda y custodia) together with the other parent's acta de manifestaciones authorising the child to live in Spain.

    The rule is worded as follows: under joint custody it is required that "el otro titular del derecho de custodia haya dado su consentimiento para que resida en territorio nacional" — that the other holder of custody rights has consented to the child residing in Spain (art. 160.1 of RD 1155/2024). The wording of the request is almost identical from case to case: it is the office's template, not a complaint about your family.

    This is most often faced by families switching from temporary protection to a residence permit: one parent is in Spain with the children, the other is in Ukraine or another country.

    Why Extranjería asks for it

    The requirement is written into the rule itself: art. 160.1 of RD 1155/2024 allows a residence permit for the child of one parent either under sole custody with a court's authorisation or with the other parent's consent. Behind this is Article 154 of the Spanish Civil Code: a child's place of residence may be changed only with the consent of both parents or by a court decision.

    Instruction SEM 2/2026 removed, for families with temporary protection, the requirement of two years' stay, means and housing, but it did not remove the other parent's consent: it remains compulsory.

    There is no need to go to court for this: in 2022 the Defensor del Pueblo (the Spanish Ombudsman) examined such a case, and the administration agreed that notarial consent is sufficient.

    If the other parent is abroad

    The record can be signed not only before a notary in Spain:

    • at the Spanish consulate in the country where they are: Extranjería accepts this consent;
    • before a local notary: the document needs an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish. Refusals happen because of the wording or the translation, so we provide the text;
    • with a court decision on sole custody: art. 160.1 accepts it in place of the other parent's consent.

    There is no confirmed practice with the Ukrainian consulate in Spain, so we do not advise relying on this route without checking.

    What else the record covers

    We write one record for all the children in the family and for all open cases. It remains valid until the children come of age, unless it is revoked before a notary or by a court decision. A residence permit under art. 160 has no renewals: it is granted for 5 years, followed by long-term residence (larga duración); the TIE card (foreigner identity card) is issued within the same case. In the record we state the Oficina de Extranjería, the case numbers and subsequent procedures: you will not have to go back to the notary.

    Frequently asked questions

    How many days do I have to reply to a requerimiento?
    10 working days from notification (art. 160.5 of RD 1155/2024, art. 30.2 LPACAP). If you do not reply, the case is closed.
    Can the deadline for the reply be extended?
    You can ask: art. 68.2 LPACAP allows an extension of no more than 5 days in case of special difficulties. The request must be filed before the deadline expires; the decision is up to the office and an extension is not assured. Count on 10 days and keep the extension as a fallback.
    The child's father is in Ukraine or Poland and cannot come. What should we do?
    He signs the consent where he is: at the Spanish consulate or before a local notary. In the second case, an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish are needed, and refusals do happen because of the wording. We provide a text that meets Extranjería's requirements and explain the procedure.
    Does the record need an apostille?
    No. The record is signed before a Spanish notary for a Spanish authority, so no apostille is needed. It will be required only for a document from a foreign notary abroad.
    Is the record valid for the TIE card and long-term residence?
    Yes. We write the record to be valid until the child comes of age, stating the Oficina de Extranjería, the case numbers and any subsequent procedures. The TIE card and the move to long-term residence (larga duración) after 5 years are covered: no second visit to the notary is needed.
    Can we do without a notary?
    Yes, if there is a court decision on sole custody: art. 160.1 accepts it in place of the other parent's consent. If there is no such decision, a notarial record remains the simplest route, and there is no need to go to court for it.
    We have two or three children. Is a record needed for each?
    No. One record covers all the children in the family and all open cases: we state the case numbers in the text.
    Has SEM 2/2026 abolished this requirement for families with temporary protection?
    No. The instruction removed the requirement of two years' stay, means and housing, but the other parent's consent remains: it is written into art. 160.1 of RD 1155/2024.
