So you take the card and a printout of the order from the BOE to the notary, and agree on the matter itself in advance.     Which identity document a Spanish notary will accept

    For foreign nationals, the Spanish identification rules list the main documents: the residence card, the foreigner identity card (TIE) and the passport. The notary checks your identity using one of them, and the document must be valid on the day of signing. An expired passport on its own does not pass this check.

    The rules allow an exception: another identity document with a photo, issued by a public authority and reliably protected against forgery. A valid Ukrainian ID card may qualify, but whether to accept it is up to the notary. So when your passport has expired, the document to rely on is a valid TIE. If you live in Spain not under temporary protection but on another permit, the date printed on your card is what counts.

    What you have Will the notary accept it
    Expired passport and a valid TIE Yes, your identity is established with the TIE
    Temporary protection card with an old date on the plastic Yes, if you have not renounced temporary protection: under the order the card is valid until 4 March 2027. Take a printout of the order with you
    Valid Ukrainian ID card By way of exception, at the notary’s discretion
    Only an expired passport No: the document must be valid on the day of signing

    Temporary protection card: why it is accepted and until when

    Order INT/96/2026 of the Ministry of the Interior was published in the BOE on 17 February 2026. It extended until 4 March 2027 the validity of the TIE held by beneficiaries of temporary protection, directly and automatically. No separate application is needed, and neither is a new card. That is why the plastic still shows the date printed when it was issued, while legally the card is valid.

    The notary will see a past date on the card and may not know about the order. So that the signing does not fall through over a formality, take with you a printout of the order from the BOE website and tell the notary in advance that you will prove your identity with a temporary protection card. When we prepare the signing, we settle this with the notary in advance, not on the day of signing.

    After 4 March 2027. The Council of the EU has extended temporary protection itself until 4 March 2028 (Decision 2026/1912), but the validity of the cards in Spain is extended by a separate order. As of 1 November 2026 there is no new order in the BOE. The 2026 order was published on 17 February, the 2025 order on 1 March. If the next one is delayed, after 4 March 2027 the card will formally be expired, and the notary may not accept it. This is a risk, not a forecast, but we would not put off signing until March 2027.

    What to sign while your card is valid

    Think ahead about which documents for Ukraine or for travel you will need over the next year, and have them done before spring. Most often these are:

    • a power of attorney to sell a flat, house or land, for a car, to collect certificates and documents, for matters at a bank or court;
    • spouse’s consent to the sale of jointly owned property: we explained how to arrange it from Spain in the article on spouse’s consent to the sale of a flat in Ukraine;
    • travel consent for a minor, if your child will travel with one of the parents or with other adults; more details on the page about travel consent for a minor;
    • an application to accept an inheritance: it is signed in person and cannot be filed under a power of attorney; the procedure is in the article on inheritance in Ukraine.

    We write the period of validity into the power of attorney itself, in words: that way there is no question about which country’s law it should be calculated under. Choose a period with a margin for the whole matter, so you do not have to issue a new power of attorney at a time when you will no longer have a document to sign it with. A bank or notary in Ukraine may ask for a more recent power of attorney; we check this before signing.

    If you cannot renew your passport

    First, it is worth checking whether you can still replace your passport in Spain: where to apply is covered in the article on replacing a Ukrainian passport in Spain, and the consulates’ rules for men aged 18–60 are covered in the guide on consular services for men.

    For the notary, a valid TIE is enough. Residence permits are more difficult: to switch from temporary protection to another permit you usually need a copy of a valid passport. Keep this in mind if you are planning a modification of temporary protection or another route.

    For people whose own country cannot provide them with a document, the Spanish Immigration Regulation provides for the cédula de inscripción, a document issued by the Spanish police. Its conditions are strict:

    • the application is made in person and in writing at the police, as soon as you find yourself without documents;
    • the fact that the consulate will not document you is proven with a notarial record stating that a request was made to the consulate and went unanswered (there are narrow exceptions to this rule);
    • you must prove exceptional humanitarian reasons, public interest or Spain’s obligations that require Spain specifically to issue the document;
    • first a temporary document is issued for three months, and the cédula itself is then renewed every year.

    The main risk is refusal: under the regulation, after a refusal the person is returned to the country they came from or expelled from Spain. In addition, the cédula ceases to be valid as soon as any country issues you a document. This is a last resort, and it is not worth taking on without a review of the risks. We will review your case; the application is handled by an immigration lawyer.

    The regulation also allows another exception: a ministerial order can permit the procedures it provides for to be carried out with expired passports of citizens of particular countries, if large numbers of them are unable to renew them. For Ukrainian passports we found no such order as of 1 November 2026.

    Frequently asked questions

    My passport has expired. Will a Spanish notary accept a temporary protection card?
    Yes, if the card is valid: the TIE is one of the documents a notary uses to establish a foreign national’s identity. Temporary protection cards have been extended until 4 March 2027 by Order INT/96/2026, even though the card itself still shows the old date. Take a printout of the order from the BOE and tell the notary in advance.
    My temporary protection card shows an old expiry date. Will that be a problem?
    If you have not renounced temporary protection, the extension applies automatically: you do not need a new card, which is why the date on the plastic does not change. The notary may not know about the order, so it is better to have a printout of the BOE text with you and to agree on the matter in advance. When we prepare the signing, we do this ourselves.
    Can I sign a power of attorney with a Ukrainian ID card?
    Possibly, but only by way of exception: the Spanish rules allow a notary to accept another identity document with a photo issued by a public authority. The notary decides this, and the card must be valid. It is safer to rely on a valid TIE.
    What happens after 4 March 2027 if no new order on the cards is issued?
    Temporary protection itself has been extended by the Council of the EU until 4 March 2028, but the validity of the cards in Spain is extended by a separate order, and as of 1 November 2026 there is no new one. If it is delayed, the card will formally be expired, and the notary may not accept it. So it makes sense to sign before March whatever you will need in spring 2027.
    For how long should I issue a power of attorney if my documents will soon expire?
    We write the period of validity into the power of attorney itself, in words: that way there is no question about which country’s law it should be calculated under. Choose a period with a margin for the whole matter, so you do not have to issue a new power of attorney at a time when you will no longer have a document to sign it with. A bank or notary in Ukraine may ask for a more recent power of attorney; we check this before signing.
