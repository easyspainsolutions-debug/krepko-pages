    Social benefits in Spain for Ukrainians 2026: IMV, RMI, other support

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    Benefits in Spain are not only for citizens: with temporary protection, a residence permit or asylum you can apply for the minimum income (IMV), regional payments, a rent subsidy and money for your children. The difficulty lies not in the law but in the fact that national and regional rules do not match, and refusals often come because of time limits and paperwork: the year of legal residence has not yet passed, or the household composition has not been proven. We go through each benefit: who meets the conditions, which documents are needed, where to apply and where people most often stumble.

    The Spanish social system: who can receive benefits

    The Spanish social protection system is built on universal access: most payments go not only to Spanish citizens but also to residents with a residence permit, refugees and people with a temporary residence status. Nobody asks about your citizenship here; what they ask about is your legal status and where you live.

    There are two basic conditions: you are registered in the padrón (the municipal register of residents) and you live in Spain legally, with a residence permit, refugee status or temporary protection (by then you already have a foreigner identity number, NIE). Without these two points an IMV application will not be accepted, and regional payments will not be granted without the padrón either, so it makes sense to start with them rather than with the application itself.

    IMV (Ingreso Mínimo Vital): the state minimum income benefit

    IMV is the basic state benefit: the state tops up the income of people in difficulty to a minimum level. In 2026 the guaranteed level is €733.60 a month for one adult, and it rises with each member of the household; what is paid is the difference between that level and the household's income for the previous year.

    Who can receive IMV

    All the conditions are checked at once: if one is not met, the whole application is refused:

    • Be at least 23 years old (younger only for an adult with children and a few special cases). If you apply alone: under 30, you must have lived in Spain apart from your parents for at least two years and worked officially for at least 12 months of that time (alta in the Seguridad Social); from 30, you must have lived apart from your parents for the last year (there are exceptions)
    • If you apply as a household, you must have lived together as one household for at least 6 months before applying (not required on the birth or adoption of a child, reunification with children, or for victims of violence or human trafficking)
    • Hold a residence permit, refugee status or temporary protection and have lived in Spain legally and effectively for at least a year before applying; for temporary protection there is no exception to this period
    • Be registered in the padrón: the registration certificate proves your address but does not replace the year of legal residence
    • Income for the previous year below the guaranteed level: €733.60 a month for one person, with a higher threshold for a household; the difference must be at least €10 a month. If your income fell this year, from 1 April to 31 December you can apply on the basis of the current year's income, provided that last year your income and assets did not exceed the limits set by law
    • Assets, excluding the home you live in, of less than €26,409.60 for one person (a higher scale applies to a household)

    Documents for an IMV application

    • Passport and NIE (foreigner identity number), TIE card or the temporary protection decision
    • Padrón registration certificate (certificado de empadronamiento) listing everyone who lives at the address
    • Number of an account (IBAN) opened in your name: the payment will be made into it
    • Certificate of your current income (if you have any)
    • Document on your marital status and household composition
    • Children's birth certificates (if any)

    How to apply

    The decision is made by the Seguridad Social (INSS). You apply at imv.seg-social.es or on the electronic office sede.seg-social.gob.es, which also has a route without a digital certificate or Cl@ve; regional or municipal social services accept applications only if they have an agreement with the INSS. Three regions have their own procedure: in Catalonia, from 1 October 2026 new applications are accepted and processed by the Generalitat through tràmits.gencat.cat (the Seguridad Social still pays), in the Basque Country you apply to Lanbide, and in Navarre to the regional social services. The decision takes up to 6 months, silence means refusal, and if the application is approved the right to the payment starts on the 1st of the month following the application, so delaying the collection of documents does not pay.

    Regional benefits: RMI, RGC, RVI and others

    In addition to the national IMV, each autonomous community has its own regional payments; they usually top up IMV to their own level. The names and conditions differ from region to region:

    • RMI (Renta Mínima de Inserción): in Madrid (Andalusia has a similar RMISA)
    • RGC (Renda Garantida de Ciutadania): in Catalonia (up to €801.85 a month for one person; PIRMI was abolished in 2017)
    • RVI (Renta Valenciana de Inclusión): in Valencia
    • Renta Básica: in some regions

    A regional payment usually tops up to its own level on top of IMV, and it has its own conditions: as a rule, you need at least a year of living in the region according to the padrón (in Catalonia, 2 years), but in Valencia, from 20 October 2026, people with temporary protection are exempt from the residence period. In Catalonia a single person with no other income receives at most €733.60 of IMV plus €68.25 of RGC, €801.85 a month in total, whereas the Madrid RMI (€469.93) is lower than IMV and adds nothing for a single person on full IMV.

    IMV first: almost everywhere the region requires you to apply for IMV first and then pays the difference up to its own level. The exception is Andalusia: there RMISA is granted only after IMV has been refused in the same year. Amounts and time limits by region are in our article on benefits in 2026.

    Unemployment benefit (prestación por desempleo)

    If you worked officially in Spain and lost your job, you are entitled to unemployment benefit: for the first 180 days, 70% of your average contribution base over the last 180 days of work, and 60% after that. It is calculated from your contributions, not from need, so it should not be confused with IMV.

    Conditions:

    • Have worked in Spain for at least 360 days in the last 6 years
    • Register as a jobseeker and submit an application to SEPE within 15 working days of losing your job
    • Actively look for work

    Ukrainians with asylum or temporary protection receive this benefit on the same terms as everyone else, but only if the work was officially registered and contributions to the Seguridad Social were paid for you.

    Housing subsidies (ayudas al alquiler)

    If you rent your home and the household income is low, part of the rent may be covered by the state housing plan for 2026–2030 (Real Decreto 326/2026): usually up to €250 a month per flat, up to €300 for those aged 35 or under, but no more than 60% of the rent. The payments are granted by your autonomous community under its own calls for applications (convocatoria). Everything rests on the contract: without a rental contract in your name, you will not get the subsidy.

    Requirements for housing support

    • A rental contract in Spanish, or a sworn translation if it is in another language
    • Income below the set limit (varies by region)
    • Rent within the plan's ceiling: as a rule, no more than €1,000 a month per flat
    • Be registered in the padrón at the rental address

    How to apply

    Applications under the state plan are accepted and decided by your autonomous community, under a call for applications (convocatoria) with its own filing deadlines; some ayuntamientos (town councils) also have their own programmes. The list of documents and the processing time are given in the convocatoria itself: check them before you apply. If the package needs certificates from Ukraine, we arrange them.

    Child benefit (prestación por hijo a cargo)

    The benefit for a child without a disability or with a disability below 33% (asignación por hijo a cargo) has been closed to new applications since 1 June 2020; those who received it before keep receiving it until they lose the entitlement. Instead, low-income families receive the child supplement CAPI (complemento de ayuda para la infancia under Ley 19/2021). The 2026 amounts according to the Seguridad Social, per month for each child according to the child's age on 1 January:

    • under 3: €115
    • from 3 to 6: €80.50
    • from 6 to 18: €57.50

    CAPI can be received even without IMV itself, but it requires the same year of legal residence in Spain, and the supplement is applied for in the same application as IMV. The branch of the benefit for children with a disability still operates: for a child under 18 with a disability of 33% or more, €1,000 a year. For details, see our article on IMV and CAPI benefits in 2026. To prove the household composition, you are asked for the children's birth certificates; foreign ones need an apostille and a translation into Spanish. If the certificates are Ukrainian, we arrange the apostille on them.

    Support for people with disabilities

    A recognised disability adds several more forms of support to the basic payments:

    • An IMV supplement of 22% of the amount for one adult (€161.39 a month in 2026) if someone in the household has a disability of 65% or more (regional benefits have their own rules)
    • Help with insurance and medicines
    • Tax relief
    • Help with housing and care

    The degree of disability (33%, 65%, 75%) is determined by your autonomous community's assessment board (equipo multiprofesional de calificación y reconocimiento del grado de discapacidad); until it issues its opinion, none of this support applies.

    Requirements for receiving benefits: padrón, residence permit and bank account

    For most benefits three things will be checked (municipal registration is not among the conditions for unemployment benefit):

    Registration in the padrón

    The padrón is the municipal register of residents. You register at the ayuntamiento (town council) of the place where you actually live; a passport and a rental contract are enough. Then comes a waiting period: IMV requires a year of legal residence in Spain, and regional payments usually require at least a year of living in the region according to the padrón, so it is worth registering straight after you move, not when you already need the money.

    Residence permit or refugee status

    Benefits are open to Ukrainians who hold one of these statuses:

    • A residence permit of any kind: based on a visa, on ties to Spain (arraigo) and others
    • Refugee status (asylum)
    • Temporary protection status (protección temporal): this is common among Ukrainians

    A Spanish bank account

    The money arrives by transfer to a bank account opened in your name; the simplest option is to receive it into a Spanish account. You can open an account at any bank (CaixaBank, Santander, BBVA) with your passport and padrón; if the branch refuses because of your status or the language, we accompany you to the bank: you open the account yourself, and we interpret and explain.

    Common mistakes when applying for IMV and regional benefits

    • Applying without the padrón: without a certificado de empadronamiento you cannot prove your address, and if you have had legal residence in Spain for less than a year, IMV will be refused however long you have been registered
    • Incomplete documents: without the registration certificate and documents on the household composition (marriage, children's birth), the application will be returned for correction or refused. The INSS checks income against tax data, but you must declare income from abroad, for example a Ukrainian pension, in the application yourself
    • Savings too high: if assets excluding your home reach €26,409.60 for one person (a higher threshold applies to a household), IMV will be refused
    • Discrepancies in household composition: the INSS treats a spouse, partner and relatives up to the second degree who live with you at the same address as one household and takes everyone's income into account; unrelated flatmates are not included in the household, but if they are registered at your address, you may be asked for a certificate from social services stating that you are not related
    • Undeclared work: receiving the benefit together with cash-in-hand wages is treated as fraud
    Combining benefits with work, and contributions to the Seguridad Social

    You can work and receive IMV: you must inform the INSS about the work, the payment will be recalculated, but part of the increase in earnings is not counted in the annual recalculation. Contributions to the Seguridad Social are compulsory all the same: without them the wages count as undeclared, and the benefit as received unlawfully. Report changes in income and household composition to the INSS within 30 days: reporting them late leads to a demand to repay what was paid. And file an IRPF tax return every year: this is also an obligation of an IMV recipient.

    How and where to apply for benefits in your region

    Where to go depends on exactly which benefit you are applying for:

    • National IMV: through imv.seg-social.es, sede.seg-social.gob.es or at a Seguridad Social office; in Catalonia through tràmits.gencat.cat, in the Basque Country through Lanbide, in Navarre through the regional social services
    • Regional benefits: usually through the municipal social services (servicios sociales); in Catalonia, the RGC is applied for online (with a digital certificate or idCAT Mòbil) or in person at an Oficina de Treball
    • Housing support: from your autonomous community under a call for applications (convocatoria); some ayuntamientos also have their own programmes
    • Child benefit: the CAPI child supplement is applied for in the same application as IMV, through imv.seg-social.es or sede.seg-social.gob.es, and in Catalonia through tràmits.gencat.cat, in the Basque Country through Lanbide, in Navarre through the regional social services

    If you are not sure where to start, go to your local town council (ayuntamiento): they will tell you which payments are available in your municipality and at which address applications are accepted.

    If your package is missing documents from Ukraine (certificates, extracts, an apostille), we take care of that: see documents from Ukraine. The benefit application itself is submitted by the recipient, online (for IMV, even without a digital certificate) or in person.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is IMV and how much is it in 2026?
    IMV (Ingreso Mínimo Vital) is the state minimum income benefit. In 2026 the guaranteed level is €733.60 a month for one adult, and it increases if there are children and other household members. What is paid is the difference between this level and the household's income for the previous year.
    Can Ukrainians with temporary protection receive IMV?
    Yes, but not straight away: the law requires at least a year of legal and effective residence in Spain before applying, and there is no exception for temporary protection; the padrón does not replace this year. We count the year from the date of the first permit: there is no direct clarification from the INSS specifically on temporary protection, so there are no guarantees in any particular case. If you apply alone and are under 30, you also need two years of living independently in Spain, including at least 12 months of official work; a household must have lived together for at least 6 months before applying. The other conditions are general: an NIE, income below the guaranteed level, assets below the limit and age 23 or over (younger only for an adult with children and a few special cases).
    Which regional benefits can you receive in Spain?
    In addition to IMV, each autonomous community has its own payments: RMI in Madrid, RGC in Catalonia (PIRMI was abolished in 2017), RVI in Valencia, RMISA in Andalusia. As a rule, they top up to their own level on top of IMV, so you apply for IMV first; the Madrid RMI for one person is even lower than IMV. The exception is Andalusia: there RMISA is granted only after IMV has been refused.
    How do you apply for IMV?
    You apply at imv.seg-social.es or on the electronic office sede.seg-social.gob.es, which also has a route without a certificado digital (digital certificate) or Cl@ve. The INSS decides; regional social services accept applications only if they have an agreement with the INSS. Three regions have their own procedure: in Catalonia, from 1 October 2026 applications are accepted and processed by the Generalitat through tràmits.gencat.cat, in the Basque Country by Lanbide, in Navarre by the regional social services. The decision takes up to 6 months, silence means refusal; if approved, the right to the payment starts on the 1st of the month following the application.
    Not sure where to go and what to bring
    Benefits are applied for at the Seguridad Social or at the social services where you live, and the application is submitted by the recipient. If you cannot work out which benefit is yours and which papers it requires, message us: we will review your situation and tell you where to go. We prepare the documents that are missing, such as certificates from Ukraine and an apostille.
