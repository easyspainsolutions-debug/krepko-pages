    Digital nomad visa with your family: who can come and what each person must prove

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. Under Article 62.4 of Ley 14/2013, a digital nomad may be accompanied by a spouse or partner, minor children, dependent adult children and dependent parents. Each person submits their own application, together with you or later, and there is no need to wait for a year of residence in Spain, as you would for ordinary family reunification. The UGE fee is €73.26 per person, and applications submitted at the same time are decided at the same time. According to the UGE, relatives may work in Spain without restrictions, as employees and on a self-employed basis; rules checked on 1 November 2026.

    The digital nomad visa extends to your family. A spouse or partner, children and dependent parents receive their own permit and, according to the immigration authority's guidance, may work in Spain. They can apply together with you or later, once your permit has already been granted, and there is no need to wait a year, as you would for ordinary family reunification.

    Below we go through whom the law allows you to bring with you, what each relative must prove, which documents from Ukraine or Russia to order in advance and what changes if your family applies after you. We checked the rules on 1 November 2026 against the BOE and official documents of the UGE, the unit of the immigration authority that examines these applications.

    Who you can bring with you

    The family is listed in Article 62.4 of Ley 14/2013 as amended by Ley 11/2023:

    • a spouse or partner: the law speaks of a person in a relationship similar to marriage, so an unregistered union also qualifies if it can be proven;
    • children under 18;
    • adult children who depend on you financially and have not started a family of their own;
    • dependent parents: according to the UGE guidance note, yours or your spouse's (partner's).

    Other relatives, such as brothers and sisters, are not on the list. The law does not limit the age of adult children; the limits are set by the UGE guidance note for relatives: from 18 to 26, dependency is required; over 26, incapacity for work.

    Legally, this is not family reunification (reagrupación familiar). Under the general rules, you can apply for reunification only after living in Spain for a year and applying for a permit for at least another year, and you have to prove housing (Articles 67.2 and 68.1 of RD 1155/2024). For a nomad's family there are no such requirements: the law allows applications “conjunta y simultánea o sucesivamente”, that is, together with you or after you.

    We handle the digital nomad visa together with your family's applications: we check which relatives meet the requirements and when each of them should apply, gather the documents in Ukraine and Spain, and the application is handled by an immigration lawyer.

    Together with you or later: how to apply

    Your family is not added to your application: each relative has their own application on the MIT form. The fee for an initial application to the UGE, €73.26 under Modelo 790 with code 038, is paid both for the applicant and for each family member (UGE questions and answers, question 20). Applications submitted at the same time are, by law, decided at the same time (Article 62.4).

    Where to apply depends on where the relative is now:

    • is legally in Spain: to the UGE, online. By law, the decision must be made within 20 working days of the application, and if there is no reply, the permit is deemed granted (Article 76.1 of Ley 14/2013, Article 30.2 of Ley 39/2015). If the UGE sends a request for documents, the time limit may be suspended until you respond to it (Article 22.1.a of Ley 39/2015);
    • lives abroad: for a visa at a Spanish consulate: Article 62.4 refers to the permit “and, where appropriate, the visa”;
    • has already arrived on such a visa and wants to stay: for a permit from the UGE within 60 calendar days before the visa expires (UGE guidance note).

    If you apply at the same time, it is enough for you to prove income for everyone. If a relative applies later, income is proven again: your contract, nóminas (payslips) or facturas (invoices) for the three months before their application and a bank certificate showing these payments. We calculated the supplements to the threshold for each family member in the article on income for the nomad visa.

    Your Seguridad Social (social security) registration also affects your family. If, as an autónomo, you do not register immediately after receiving the permit, and an inspection reveals this, the permit will be terminated, and neither you nor your relatives will be able to live and work in Spain (UGE questions and answers, question 7). The requirements are also checked on renewal; see the article on renewing a nomad's residence permit.

    What each relative must prove

    What needs to be proven, according to the UGE guidance note for relatives:

    Who What to prove How
    Spouse marriage marriage certificate
    Registered partner that the union is registered a certificate from the register where it is recorded
    Unregistered partner at least one year of living together immediately before the application; if you have children together, living together at the time of the application for example, two pieces of evidence from the list: a joint account, the same municipal registration, a joint lease, a mortgage or property, a joint business; if you have children together, the record of their birth
    Child under 18 kinship birth certificate; if the child is not a child of both of you and the other spouse has not adopted them, notarised permission from the other parent
    Child aged 18 to 26 that they depend on you financially and have not started their own family certificates from the tax and social security authorities of their country showing no income from work and no state benefits; enrolment in an official educational institution or registration with the employment service; a marital status certificate (certificado de soltería) or an equivalent document
    Child over 26 that they cannot support themselves due to incapacity for work documents on incapacity for work
    Parent, yours or your spouse's economic dependency for at least a year before the application, or dependency on health grounds; for parents over 80, dependency is presumed the birth certificate of whichever of you is the parent's child; joint municipal registration or payment of their housing for a year; bank documents on financial support for a year; certificates showing they have no income from work and no state benefits

    A parent's dependency must be real, ongoing and established before the application, in their country. The guidance note expressly requires a document showing that the parent does not receive state benefits. A state pension is such a benefit, and the guidance note makes no exception for a small pension. The UGE assesses dependency case by case, but if a parent is under 80 and receives a pension, this counts against them, so it is worth going through such a parent's chances with an immigration lawyer before applying. Dependency on health grounds (a serious illness that requires your care, with no one to care for them back home) is proven with medical reports or social services documents.

    Criminal record certificates and insurance

    Article 62.4 requires the family to meet the general conditions of Article 62.3. Adult relatives need a criminal record certificate from the countries where they have lived for the last two years, and a declaration under their own responsibility (declaración responsable) that they have no criminal record in the countries where they have lived for the last five years. Children under 18 do not need a certificate. Nor does anyone who has already held a permit in Spain for more than six months, if a certificate was already submitted for it. We order the criminal record certificate from Ukraine remotely, with an apostille; documents from Russia are covered in a separate section.

    A relative does not need an insurance policy if you register with Seguridad Social and they become entitled to healthcare as a member of your family. An unregistered partner is not entitled to this and needs a private policy. The UGE does not accept travel policies, reimbursement-only policies or policies with co-payments (copagos) or a waiting period (carencia).

    Which documents to order in advance

    Foreign official documents are submitted with an apostille or legalisation, and the translation must be a sworn one (by a traductor jurado authorised by the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs) or a consular one, which is then legalised at the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. An ordinary translation will not do; order the documents before you apply.

    • Marriage and birth certificates. If there is no original or it is damaged, we order a duplicate with an apostille in Ukraine without you having to travel: marriage certificate, birth certificate.
    • An adult child's marital status. The civil registry offices (DRACS) in Ukraine do not issue such a certificate (clarification from the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine), and the UGE guidance note accepts an equivalent document without specifying which. For marriage abroad, the Ministry of Justice refers to a statement with a notarised signature, including at a consulate; there are also extracts from the DRACS register. Whether the UGE will accept such a substitute has not been officially clarified; the options are covered in the article on the marital status document from Ukraine.
    • A child from a first marriage. If the child is not shared by you and your spouse, the UGE asks for notarised permission from the other parent, stating expressly that the child will live in Spain with the parent who is applying. If the other parent can sign it in Spain, the notary authenticates it, and we prepare the text and accompany you at the signing: other parent's consent for a child's residence. A document from a notary in another country will need an apostille and a sworn translation.

    If documents are missing, the UGE will send a request (requerimiento). By law you have 10 days to respond, and these are working days; if you do not respond, the application will be deemed withdrawn (Articles 68.1 and 30.2 of Ley 39/2015). How to meet the deadline is covered in our article on a UGE request for the nomad visa.

    Work and taxes for the family

    In its official answers, the UGE states that relatives' permits give the right to live and work without restrictions, as employees and on a self-employed basis (question 17, with reference to DA 4ª of Ley 14/2013). The nomad's restrictions (as an employee, only your own foreign company; under a services contract, no more than 20% of work with Spanish companies, question 14) apply to you, not to your family.

    The Beckham Law regime is suited primarily to employees: a worker with a teleworker visa is expressly named in Article 93.1.b.1º LIRPF, while an autónomo is covered only as an entrepreneur or a start-up or R&D specialist (93.1.b.3º–4º). The regime is not available if in any of the five previous years you were a Spanish tax resident, for example if you spent more than 183 days a year here under temporary protection (Articles 9.1.a and 93.1.a LIRPF). Your spouse, children under 25 (with a disability, of any age) and, if you are not married, the other parent of your children may also opt for the regime. To do so, they must move with you or later, but before the end of the first year of your regime, become Spanish tax residents and themselves not have been tax residents in any of the five previous years. In addition, they must not receive income through a permanent establishment in Spain, and the sum of their tax bases must be lower than yours each year (Article 93.3 LIRPF). If any of them has already lived in Spain for more than 183 days in one of those five years, for example under temporary protection, the regime is not available to them, even if it is available to you. Each person files the notification themselves within six months of their own entry or within your time limit, if that is later (Article 116.1.b RIRPF). The regime does not extend to parents.

    If your family does not qualify under this visa

    If your income from work is not enough for everyone, the UGE guidance note allows you to cover the difference with savings: a bank certificate showing they will be enough for the entire term of the permit. Or apply alone first, and your family later, when your income has grown. With a different residence permit, not issued under Ley 14/2013, the family is usually brought over through ordinary family reunification: the requirements are in the article on family reunification. If you are under temporary protection, the conditions for switching are collected in the article from temporary protection to the nomad visa.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I bring my family on the digital nomad visa?
    Yes. Under Article 62.4 of Ley 14/2013, you may be accompanied by a spouse or partner, minor children, dependent adult children and dependent parents, including your spouse's parents. Each person has their own application; the UGE fee is €73.26 per person (according to the UGE, checked on 1 November 2026). They can apply together with you or later.
    Family reunification on the nomad visa: do you have to wait a year?
    No. This is not family reunification (reagrupación familiar) but a separate procedure for the family of an international teleworker, and there is no need to wait a year. Relatives apply together with you or later, once you already have your permit. If the applications are submitted at the same time, the decision on them is made at the same time.
    Can a nomad's wife work in Spain?
    Yes. In its official questions and answers, the UGE states that relatives' permits give the right to live and work without restrictions, as employees and on a self-employed basis. The restrictions on working with Spanish companies apply only to the nomad applicant.
    Can I bring my parents on the nomad visa?
    Yes, if they are dependent on you or on your spouse. For economic dependency, you need to show that for at least a year before the application you have lived together or paid for their housing and supported them financially, and that they have no income from work and no state benefits. For parents over 80, dependency is presumed.
    Can I move alone first and sort out my family's papers later?
    Yes, the law expressly allows this. But if they apply later, income is proven again: your contract, nóminas or facturas for the three months before the relative's application and a bank certificate. Each relative pays the UGE fee.
    My wife has a child from her first marriage. What is needed for the child's residence permit?
    A birth certificate and notarised permission from the other parent, stating expressly that the child will live in Spain with the mother; if you have adopted the child, it is not needed. If the other parent can sign it in Spain, the notary authenticates it, and we prepare the text and accompany you at the signing. A document from a notary in another country will need an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish.
