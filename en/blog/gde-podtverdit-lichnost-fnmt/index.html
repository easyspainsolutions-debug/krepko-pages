    Where to verify your identity for a certificado digital if you have an NIE

    Krepko · 11 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. A foreigner with an NIE cannot yet verify their identity for an FNMT certificate by video: video identification works only with a valid Spanish DNIe, so a visit to an accreditation office is needed, made by you or by a trusted person if your signature on the FNMT contract has been authenticated by a notary. Such offices exist at Hacienda (AEAT), Seguridad Social, some city councils and other public bodies, and abroad their role is performed by Spanish consulates; the nearest office is shown by the Localizador, FNMT's office finder. Hacienda, Seguridad Social and other offices require an appointment. Bring the request code from the FNMT email, the document with your NIE and a valid passport (EU citizens can also use the identity card of their country); FNMT accepts an expired TIE, but it is better to check this with the office. Checked on 11 November 2026 against sede.fnmt.gob.es and the AEAT and Seguridad Social websites.

    The request for an FNMT (Spanish Royal Mint) certificate is sent online, but a foreigner with an NIE cannot complete the next step that way: identity is verified at an office. Where exactly depends on the city: in some cities the list of offices includes the city council, in others only state bodies such as Hacienda (the tax office) and Seguridad Social (Social Security), and almost everywhere you need an appointment.

    The whole route to the certificate is described in the article on the certificado digital, and here we look only at this step: which offices are suitable, how to find the nearest one, where to get an appointment and which documents to bring. We checked the information on 11 November 2026 against the websites of FNMT, the tax office (AEAT), Seguridad Social and the BOE.

    Which offices verify identity

    The certificate is issued by FNMT, while the applicant's documents are checked by accreditation offices (oficinas de acreditación de identidad) that FNMT has set up in various public bodies. According to FNMT, there are more than 2,400 such offices throughout Spain. These are tax office (Agencia Tributaria, AEAT) and Seguridad Social offices, some city councils, in a number of regions offices of the regional governments, and other public bodies.

    If you are outside Spain, identity is verified at a Spanish consulate. FNMT names career consular offices (oficinas consulares de carrera), and AEAT states that non-residents obtain the certificate through consulates without travelling to Spain. The list of consulates on the AEAT website was compiled as of 7 June 2021, and an asterisk marks the consulates that do not provide this service, so check the appointment procedure with the consulate itself.

    Correos (the Spanish post office) does not issue certificates for individuals; FNMT says so explicitly. Nor are the police stations where TIEs are issued, or the Registro Civil (civil registry), on FNMT's list. You can choose any office on the list, not necessarily in your own city: according to AEAT guidance, any registration office is suitable for an individual's certificate.

    If you have no time to deal with offices and appointments, we handle getting your digital certificate: we submit the request, prepare your submission and accompany you to the visit, where a representative is beside you and interprets.

    How to find an office near you: the FNMT Localizador

    The up-to-date list of offices is in the Localizador de Oficinas: the link to it is on the FNMT page “Acreditar identidad”. In it you select the “Persona Física” certificate and enter an address and a search radius in kilometres. The office card shows the address and, if the office has provided them, its opening hours and how to book: by phone or via a link. Seguridad Social has its own office directory with the filter “Oficinas de Registro de Certificados Digitales”.

    Here is what the Localizador shows in the cities where we work (data as of 11 November 2026, only offices in the city itself):

    The list changes, so check the Localizador before booking. If your city council is not on it, book at Hacienda or Seguridad Social: in each of these six cities they are on the list.

    Where to get an appointment

    Hacienda, Seguridad Social and other offices require an appointment, and FNMT advises checking the booking procedure with the office itself.

    • Hacienda (AEAT). Appointments are made in the “Asistencia y Cita” section on the home page of sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es. You do not need a digital certificate to book: your NIE number, first name and surname are enough. The service is selected under “Otras gestiones”, the office by province or postcode. You can give a foreign phone number; the confirmation arrives by email, and you use that email to change or cancel the appointment later.
    • Seguridad Social. Appointments at INSS centres (CAISS) are made at sede.seg-social.gob.es: “Ciudadanos” → “Cita previa para prestaciones y otras gestiones” → “Obtener cita previa para pensiones y otras prestaciones (INSS). Sin certificado”. Seguridad Social reminds you that the FNMT request code must be obtained before the visit and brought with you.
    • City councils and other offices. Each has its own booking system; the phone number or link is shown on the office card in the Localizador, if the office has provided them.

    AEAT advises getting the appointment first and making the request on the FNMT website afterwards. Make it on the computer where you will use the certificate: the certificate can only be downloaded on the computer where the request was made, and under the same user account. Until the download, AEAT advises against reinstalling the operating system or updating the browser.

    What to bring

    The future holder of the certificate must come in person or, if they cannot, a trusted person must come instead (procedure below). A certificate can be obtained by anyone aged 18 or over, or by an emancipated minor. The office checks documents: FNMT lists only documents, with no photos or fingerprints. What you need depends on your status:

    Who you are Document with your NIE Identity document
    Citizen of a non-EU country Foreigner's card with the NIE (FNMT names the red, green and white cards) or an official document assigning the NIE Passport
    Citizen of an EU country EU citizen certificate with the NIE, a foreigner's document with the NIE or an official document assigning the NIE Passport or the identity card of your country

    And everyone needs the request code (Código de Solicitud) from the FNMT email: FNMT uses it to link the visit to your request. For citizens of non-EU countries, according to the FNMT page “Acreditar identidad”, the foreigner's card with the NIE is accepted regardless of its expiry date, as is the document assigning the NIE: they are needed to confirm that you have the number. So under FNMT's rules an expired TIE will also do. But the FNMT FAQ (question 1343) and the AEAT help page contain no such proviso for the card, so check this with the office before your visit and, if you have it, also bring the document assigning the NIE. With the passport it is the other way round: it must be valid and the original, and instead of the original FNMT accepts only an officially certified copy (fotocopia compulsada). If your passport has expired, you will first need a new one; for Ukrainian citizens we have covered the replacement procedure in the article on replacing a Ukrainian passport in Spain. In its help page AEAT generally refers to the original and a photocopy of the document, so it is better to bring copies of your passport and card with you.

    According to FNMT's description of the steps, the certificate can be downloaded about an hour after the visit, and on the identity verification page FNMT advises doing so as soon as possible. Make a backup immediately after installation: with it, the certificate can be transferred to another computer or phone. If the download does not work, the reasons are covered in the article on why the FNMT certificate will not download.

    If you cannot come in person

    Personal attendance is required by law: the identity of the person obtaining a qualified certificate is checked in their presence, using a DNI, passport or other acceptable document. It can be done without attendance if the signature on the application for the certificate has been authenticated by a notary (Article 7.1 Ley 6/2020). The law also allows remote identity verification, for example by video, under conditions set by a ministerial order (Article 7.2). At FNMT this is video identification, but both on the website and in the app it currently works only with a valid Spanish DNIe. So with an NIE, the options that remain are an office visit or a trusted person.

    FNMT describes the procedure for a trusted person in question 1633. You print the FNMT contract from the “Descarga de contratos” section: the front contains your details and those of the person you are entrusting with the visit, the back contains the terms of use of the certificate. The front is filled in completely, and you sign both sides in the presence of a notary, on every page: the notary authenticates the signature (legitimación de firma), stamps each page and states in the note that the signature was made in their presence. The trusted person then brings to any accreditation office the original contract, a copy of it and the original of their own identity document.

    A notarial power of attorney will not do. The contract contains the terms of use of the certificate, and FNMT requires you to accept them with your authenticated signature, so a poder notarial (notarial power of attorney) will not be accepted instead of signature authentication. The new form of contract with a trusted person has been available since 6 October 2025, and offices have not accepted the previous forms since 1 December 2025.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I verify my identity for FNMT at the police station where I got my TIE?
    No. Police stations and the Registro Civil are not on FNMT's list of offices, and Correos does not issue certificates for individuals. Identity is verified at accreditation offices: at Hacienda (AEAT), Seguridad Social, some city councils and other public bodies. The nearest office is shown by the Localizador; the link to it is on the FNMT page “Acreditar identidad”.
    My TIE has expired and the new one has not been issued yet. Will the office accept it?
    Under FNMT's rules, yes: the “Acreditar identidad” page says that the foreigner's card with the NIE is accepted regardless of its expiry date, since it is needed to confirm that you have the number. But the FNMT FAQ (question 1343) and the AEAT help page contain no such proviso for the card, so check this with the office before your visit and, if you have it, bring the official document assigning the NIE: FNMT accepts it regardless of its expiry date. Your passport, however, must be valid, either the original or an officially certified copy.
    I only have an NIE. Can I do the check by video?
    Not yet. FNMT video identification, both on the website and in the app, works only with a valid Spanish DNIe. With an NIE, identity is verified at an accreditation office or through a trusted person, if your signature on the FNMT contract has been authenticated by a notary.
    How do I book an appointment at Hacienda for a certificado digital?
    Through the “Asistencia y Cita” section on the home page of the tax office website, sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es. Your NIE number, first name and surname are enough to book; the office is chosen by province or postcode, and you can give a foreign phone number. AEAT advises getting the appointment first and making the request on the FNMT website afterwards.
    Can my husband or daughter go instead of me?
    Yes, if you enter the relative's details in the FNMT contract in advance, sign the contract in the presence of a notary and the notary authenticates the signature on every page. The relative takes the original contract, a copy of it and their own identity document to any accreditation office. A notarial power of attorney will not work instead: FNMT needs your signature on the contract itself, authenticated by a notary.
    I do not live in Spain. Where do I verify my identity?
    At a Spanish consulate: AEAT states that non-residents obtain the FNMT certificate through consulates without travelling to Spain, and FNMT names career consular offices (oficinas consulares de carrera). The list on the AEAT website was compiled as of 7 June 2021, and an asterisk marks the consulates that do not provide this service, so check the appointment procedure with the consulate itself.
