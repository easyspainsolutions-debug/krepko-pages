    General power of attorney in Spain: what it can and cannot be used for

    Krepko · 5 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    In short. Under a general power of attorney drawn up in general terms, the representative can only manage your affairs and property. They can sell, give away or pledge property, reach a settlement or carry out any other act of disposal only when that power is expressly named in the text (art. 1713 of the Spanish Civil Code). That is why the notary at a transaction and the bank check not the title of the document but the specific powers in it. A general power of attorney is executed before a notary as a public document (escritura pública de poder) and signed in person: the law does not allow general powers of attorney to be authorised by video link (art. 17 ter of the Notarial Law).     A general power of attorney is usually drawn up before a long absence from Spain or when someone in the family starts handling the affairs of an elderly relative: one document instead of several. But a broad power of attorney has a limit in Spanish law that is easy to discover too late — at a transaction or at the bank. General wording gives the representative the right to manage your affairs, but they can sell, pledge or give away property only when that act is expressly named in the text.

    Below we look at where this line runs, where the power of attorney is checked and what to include in it.

    What is meant by a general power of attorney

    The Spanish Civil Code distinguishes between a general and a special mandate (mandato): a general one covers all the principal's affairs, a special one covers one or several specific matters (art. 1712). Notaries call a power of attorney general (poder general) when it gives the representative as many powers as possible at once. If there is a single task, for example selling a particular flat, a special power of attorney is usually more convenient: the difference between them is explained in the questions on the general power of attorney page.

    The form is set by law: a power of attorney to manage property, as well as one for acts that require a notarial deed or that must have effect against third parties, must be a public document (art. 1280 of the Civil Code). That is why a general power of attorney is signed before a notary. The notary authorises it, while we prepare the text tailored to your needs, in the language you need from the start, agree the wording with the notary and accompany you at the signing; at the appointment a Krepko representative translates every clause. Why it is more convenient to sign a power of attorney in your language is explained in the article on a power of attorney in Russian.

    The main rule: general wording only gives the right to manage

    The key provision is art. 1713 of the Spanish Civil Code. A mandate drawn up in general terms covers only acts of administration. A settlement, the alienation of property, a mortgage and any other act of disposal (in the law, “riguroso dominio”) require an express mandate. And the right to reach settlements does not in itself give the right to refer a dispute to arbitration.

    For a power of attorney, this means that wording such as “to represent my interests in all matters” or “to manage my property” does not give the right to sell a flat. The required act is named expressly, for example “to sell” or “to pledge”, and the asset is specified if you want to narrow the power.

    If the representative goes beyond these limits, obligations beyond their powers do not bind you unless you ratify them expressly or through your conduct (arts. 1714 and 1727 of the Civil Code). A contract concluded in your name without authority is invalid unless you ratify it before the other party withdraws from it (art. 1259). That is why those who accept a power of attorney check the powers in advance.

    Where a general power of attorney is checked

    The notary at a transaction. When the representative signs a contract before a notary on your behalf, for example the sale of a flat, the notary enters the details of the power of attorney in the deed and states that, in their judgement, the powers are sufficient for this transaction (art. 98 of Law 24/2001). The land registrar checks only this note, the notary's judgement and whether they match the transaction, and has no right to require the power of attorney itself. If the right to sell is not in the text, the notary will not consider the powers sufficient, and it will not be possible to sign the sale under this power of attorney.

    The bank. Under the anti-money-laundering regulations, a bank before which a representative acts must obtain copies of identity documents (yours and the representative's), as well as the public document granting the powers (art. 6 of Real Decreto 304/2014). It then checks whether these powers are sufficient for the transaction: the Bank of Spain calls this check bastanteo and warns that the bank may charge a fee for it. According to the same guidance, the power of attorney must contain specific powers, as well as limits on amounts and transactions if you decide to set them. If dealing with the account is the representative's main task, a separate power of attorney for a bank account is more convenient: we check its text in advance against your bank's requirements.

    Another country. How a Spanish power of attorney works abroad is decided by the receiving party under the law of its country; the Spanish code also refers the question of powers to the law of the country where the representative acts, unless another law is chosen in the power of attorney (art. 10(11) of the Civil Code). That is why we will not promise in advance that a Spanish general power of attorney will be accepted by a Ukrainian notary or bank. For matters in Ukraine, a separate power of attorney for Ukraine tailored to a specific task is more convenient, and what to include in it is explained in the article on a power of attorney from Spain for Ukraine.

    What to include in a general power of attorney

    A general power of attorney does not have to be short: the more precisely the acts are listed, the fewer questions the notary at the transaction and the bank will have. We build the text from your tasks, not from a template:

    • details of the parties — yours and the representative's, as in your documents; if there are two representatives, whether each can act separately or only jointly;
    • management by area — accounts and banking transactions, rent and housing payments, the tax office and other public authorities, a car;
    • disposal of property — by expressly named acts: sell, buy, give away, pledge, take out a loan, accept an inheritance or renounce it; if you wish, a specific asset and a minimum sale price;
    • substitution — allow or prohibit it: if you have not prohibited it, the representative may appoint a substitute, and the acts of a substitute appointed despite a prohibition are void (art. 1721 of the Civil Code);
    • purchase by the representative — the representative cannot buy property they manage or have been instructed to sell, either personally or through another person (art. 1459 of the Civil Code); if you want to sell the home to them specifically, tell us before the text is prepared: we will check with the notary in advance whether such a sale is possible and how to formalise it;
    • continued effect — if the power of attorney is granted for years ahead, for example by an elderly parent, it can include a clause that it will continue to apply if the principal comes to need support in exercising their legal capacity (art. 256 of the Civil Code). The notary reports such a power of attorney to the civil registry (Registro Civil, art. 260 of the Civil Code) on their own initiative. If it covers all the principal's affairs, then, once support is needed, in everything not provided for in the power of attorney the rules on curatorship (curatela) apply to the representative, unless the principal has specified otherwise (art. 259 of the Civil Code). That is why it is better to think this case through in the text from the start;
    • term — optional; if there is none, the power of attorney remains in force until it is revoked or another ground for termination arises.

    When a power of attorney ceases to have effect

    The grounds are listed in art. 1732 of the Civil Code: revocation; the representative's renunciation; death or insolvency proceedings (concurso) of the principal or the representative; support measures put in place for the representative themselves, if they concern acts under the power of attorney; representative curatorship (curatela representativa) established for the principal by a court (art. 269) — except for a preventive power of attorney. This means that after the principal's death acting under their power of attorney is, as a general rule, not possible: the law requires the representative only to complete a matter already begun if delay would be dangerous (art. 1718). Transactions the representative concluded without knowing of the death remain valid for third parties acting in good faith (art. 1738).

    The principal is entitled to revoke the power of attorney at will (art. 1733 of the Civil Code). To ensure that no one acts under the old document any longer, the representative and those with whom they may conclude a transaction are notified of the revocation (arts. 1734 and 1738). How this works is described on the page on revoking a power of attorney.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can a flat be sold under a general power of attorney?
    Yes, if the power of attorney expressly names the right to sell real estate. A power of attorney in general terms covers only acts of administration, while a sale, gift or pledge requires an expressly stated power (art. 1713 of the Spanish Civil Code). The notary handling the transaction must state in the deed that the powers are sufficient for this sale (art. 98 of Law 24/2001), so it is better to check the text of the power of attorney before scheduling the transaction.
    What can a representative do if the power of attorney contains only general wording?
    Only manage: handle day-to-day matters concerning your property, for example letting a home, but without a special power for no more than six years (art. 1548 of the Spanish Civil Code). Selling, giving away or pledging property and reaching a settlement is not possible without an expressly named power (art. 1713).
    Will a bank accept a general power of attorney?
    The bank itself checks whether the powers are sufficient for the transaction. The Bank of Spain calls this check bastanteo, warns that the bank may charge a fee for it and states that the power of attorney must contain specific powers, as well as limits on amounts and transactions if you decide to set them. That is why we set out banking transactions separately, and if the account is the main task, a power of attorney for a bank account is more convenient.
    Will a Spanish general power of attorney work in Ukraine?
    That is decided by the receiving party in Ukraine under its own law, so we will not promise acceptance in advance. For matters in Ukraine, a separate power of attorney for Ukraine tailored to a specific task, with an apostille, is more convenient. What to include in it is explained in the article on a power of attorney from Spain for Ukraine.
    Can the representative buy my flat themselves?
    As a general rule, no: the representative cannot buy, either personally or through another person, property they manage or have been instructed to sell (art. 1459 of the Spanish Civil Code). If you want to sell the home to them specifically, tell us before the text is prepared: we will check with the notary in advance whether such a sale is possible and how to formalise it.
    Does a power of attorney remain in force after the death of the person who granted it?
    As a general rule, no: the death of the principal or the representative terminates the power of attorney (art. 1732 of the Spanish Civil Code). The representative is only obliged to complete a matter already begun if delay would be dangerous (art. 1718). Transactions they concluded without knowing of the death remain valid for third parties acting in good faith (art. 1738), but no new matters may be handled under such a power of attorney.
