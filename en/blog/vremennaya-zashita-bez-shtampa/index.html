    Temporary protection in Spain without a stamp: how to prove your departure

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. Since 5 August 2026 new applicants for temporary protection must prove that they have fulfilled their military obligations, and an exit stamp or a lawful border crossing count as proof (Council of the EU Implementing Decision 2026/1912). Ukrainian citizens' passports are stamped only at their request (Law No. 1710-VI, Art. 12), so most people do not have a stamp. Without a stamp, the fact of departure is confirmed by a DPSU certificate (on your own, free of charge: up to 30 calendar days; through a lawyer: by law up to 5 working days with a possible extension to 20 working days), and exemption from military obligations by documents from Reserve+. Spain has not published a list of documents; there is no guarantee of acceptance. The condition does not apply to those who were under protection in Spain on 4 August 2026 and have kept it without interruption.

    Since 5 August 2026 new applicants for temporary protection in the EU have had to prove that they have fulfilled their military obligations in Ukraine. The simplest proof is an exit stamp, but most often there is none in the passport: for many years Ukrainian citizens have not been given the border crossing stamp (штамп про перетин кордону) unless they ask for it, and since 10 April 2026 Schengen has not been stamping passports either. Without a stamp, what remains is a certificate from the State Border Guard Service of Ukraine (DPSU), in Ukrainian довідка про перетин кордону (dovidka pro peretyn kordonu), which confirms the fact of departure, and documents from the Reserve+ app on your military registration status. Which of these will be accepted in Spain has not been officially determined.

    Below we look at what the EU decision says, how the European Commission divided the checks into levels, who the new condition does not affect and what is known about Spain. We checked the rules and official pages on 1 October 2026.

    What does EU Decision 2026/1912 require, and from what date?

    Council of the EU Implementing Decision 2026/1912 was published in the Official Journal of the EU on 4 August 2026. It extended temporary protection and added a condition for new applications. Taking into account the corrigendum published on 5 August (Art. 2 and 3), from 5 August 2026 protection is granted to those who have fulfilled their military obligations in Ukraine, and where applicable, this must be proven. We covered the extension and the condition itself in the article on temporary protection; here we deal only with the evidence.

    The decision explains how to prove it in its preamble, recital 18. A lawful border crossing on departure from Ukraine or an exit stamp in the passport count as proof. If departure cannot be proven that way, what is needed is an easily verifiable official document, on paper or electronic, confirming exemption from military obligations or their fulfilment, for example from Reserve+. The burden of proof is on the applicant. Recital 20 adds that Ukrainian citizens may have military obligations regardless of age and sex. The European Parliament briefing of 4 September 2026 names the same three types of proof: a stamp, a record of a lawful border crossing and an official document.

    Why is there no exit stamp (штамп про перетин кордону) in the passport?

    The rule is not new. Under the Law of Ukraine “On Border Control” («Про прикордонний контроль») No. 1710-VI (Art. 12, part 3, item 1), no border crossing mark is put in the passport of a Ukrainian citizen unless they ask for it. The rule was already in the original 2009 version of the law. The procedure for marks is set by Order No. 714 of the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Ukraine (MVS), in force since 1 January 2020. In its FAQ the European Commission also acknowledges that the passports of Ukrainian citizens are systematically not stamped. Foreign nationals and stateless persons are given a mark by law: in the passport or, at their request, on an immigration card (Art. 12, parts 1 and 4).

    What is new in 2026 is something else. Since 10 April 2026 the European Entry/Exit System (EES) has been fully operational: at the external borders of Schengen passports are no longer stamped on short-stay entry, and the data are recorded in an electronic database. As a result, a person who entered the EU after 10 April 2026, as a rule, has no mark from either the Ukrainian or the European side. A Ukrainian stamp is not added retroactively: in August 2026 a DPSU spokesperson confirmed this (according to hromadske).

    How is departure checked, and what is the 90-day rule?

    In August 2026 the European Commission published an FAQ on the checks under Decision 2026/1912 (PDF of 18 August). In it the checks are divided into two levels. The first level is the basic one. In countries that border Ukraine, the lawful border crossing at a border checkpoint is enough in itself: departure under martial law is checked by Ukrainian border guards. In the other countries, including Spain, a Ukrainian exit stamp is enough. If there is a stamp and there are no doubts, the check ends there.

    The second level applies when there is no stamp or doubts have arisen. The Commission names as the basic document a certificate from Reserve+ with your full name, date of birth, the fact of exemption and its period of validity. For the certificate from Reserve+ the Commission recommends a sworn translation into English or the language of the country, for example through a Ukrainian consulate. The Commission does not explicitly name the DPSU certificate. “Other documents” must clearly show that the person left lawfully and in compliance with military obligations, and each country decides whether to accept them. The FAQ says nothing about translating the DPSU certificate or about an apostille.

    The 90-day benchmark. According to the Commission's FAQ, the date of the exit stamp should, as a rule, be no earlier than 90 days before the application for protection; exceptional circumstances are taken into account. This rule is not in the text of the decision itself; it is the Commission's interpretation. Whether Spain applies it has not been officially confirmed. If you left Ukraine long ago, a certificate with your departure date may not settle the question, and then second-level documents will probably be needed.

    Who needs to prove their departure, and who does the rule not affect?

    The new condition applies only to new applications. Under Art. 2 of the decision and sections I and IV of the Commission's FAQ, it does not apply to three groups:

    • those who enjoyed temporary protection in Spain on 4 August 2026 inclusive and have kept it here without interruption;
    • those who applied before the rules came into force but have not yet received a decision;
    • current holders of protection whose military obligations arose later: the FAQ gives the example of a man who was 22 and then turned 23.

    Departure will have to be proven by new applicants who apply from 5 August 2026 and do not have a stamp. This also includes those who received protection in another EU country, for example in Poland or Germany, and are now applying again in Spain (section III of the FAQ). The Commission does not explain how the 90-day benchmark applies to those who left Ukraine in 2022–2025.

    A separate group is those who returned to Ukraine and lost their status. Returning to Ukraine does not in itself end protection in Spain. It ends if the person has expressly declared their return to the authorities or renounced protection, and also on an official transfer to another EU country (RD 1325/2003, Art. 24). A renunciation may also be treated as tacit: if a person fails to appear when summoned for a mandatory procedure within 45 days without a valid reason. So few people have to go through the check again in Spain after a trip home.

    What documents are accepted in Spain?

    There is no official list. The government portal Ucrania Urgente says that since August 2026 applicants “deben justificar el cumplimiento de las obligaciones militares”, that is, they must prove the fulfilment of their military obligations, but it does not give a list of documents. On 12 August 2026 the Embassy of Ukraine in Spain officially requested the list from the Spanish side and promised to publish the response. As of 1 October 2026 the response has not been published.

    Practice: so far only according to the media. According to a report by focus.ua of 11 August, in Alicante men were refused and asked for an extract from Reserve+ with a translation into Spanish, a DPSU record of departure and documents on removal from the military register or unfitness for service. On 14 August TSN wrote about refusals in Madrid and approvals in Alicante, Barcelona and Valencia. There are reports that women were also asked for Reserve+ documents; in August 2026 the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Ukraine confirmed such isolated cases in some EU countries, mainly among those who could not prove a lawful departure. The other reports are not official data, and they contradict each other, so it is better to check the requirements of the place of application in advance.

    What does a DPSU certificate (довідка про перетин кордону) prove?

    The border crossing certificate is issued by the DPSU Main Centre for Processing Special Information (GTsOSI) in Kyiv; border checkpoints do not issue such certificates. According to descriptions by the DPSU in the media and by Ukrainian lawyers, the response states your full name, date of birth, citizenship, passport details, the date and time of each crossing, the border checkpoint and the direction. We have not found a channel through Diia, a TsNAP (administrative services centre) or a consulate for this certificate.

    The main limitation of a DPSU certificate: it confirms the fact, date, border checkpoint and direction of the crossing, but not the grounds for leaving. A deferment or exemption is not mentioned in descriptions of the response. So the certificate does not replace Reserve+ or documents on a deferment or exemption, if they are asked for at the second level of the check. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Ukraine, according to RBC-Ukraine of 15 September, believes that the certificate may be sufficient for EU authorities, but this is the Ukrainian side's position; there is no Spanish confirmation. On military registration documents at the consulate, see a separate article.

    How do you get a DPSU certificate: on your own for free or through a lawyer?

    You can request information about your crossings yourself, free of charge. A written application and a scan of the first page of your passport are submitted to the GTsOSI in person or by post to Kyiv. The DPSU page of 18 December 2025 also gives the e-mail address gcosi-dpsu@dpsu.gov.ua, although a later page of 24 March 2026 mentions only submission in person and by post. Under the Personal Data Protection Act No. 2297-VI (Art. 16), a response is given within 30 calendar days.

    The second route is a lawyer's request. Under the Law on the Bar No. 5076-VI (Art. 24), a response is given no later than 5 working days. If the request requires processing a large amount of data, the DPSU may extend the period to 20 working days, notifying the lawyer in writing, with reasons, within the first five days. Information on border crossings is confidential personal data, and the Law on the Bar excludes restricted-access information from such requests. That is why your written consent is needed for the request. In practice, timeframes are currently unstable: some people get a response in 5 working days, others in 15 or 20.

    By law a lawyer's request is faster: a response no later than 5 working days. But with an extension to 20 working days (about four weeks), the timeframe is comparable to the 30 calendar days of a request made on your own, and the paper original and delivery to Spain add time. You are entitled to obtain the certificate yourself; a lawyer is not required for this.

    What should you do before applying for temporary protection?

    1. Check your passport. If there is an exit stamp, you most likely do not need a certificate. The next time you leave Ukraine, you can ask for a stamp at the checkpoint.
    2. Check whether you were under protection in Spain on 4 August 2026 without interruption.
    3. Count the days since your departure date, bearing in mind the 90-day benchmark.
    4. Gather everything you have: the DPSU certificate, documents from Reserve+, on a deferment or exemption. The certificate does not replace the rest.
    5. Check the requirements of the place of application, the police or the reception centre (CREADE): original, translation.

    For switching to another residence permit, arraigo (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain) and long-term residence (larga duración), a DPSU certificate is not needed: neither instruction SEM 2/2026 nor the Immigration Regulation requires such a document, and for these purposes we do not obtain the certificate. By law, foreign nationals are given a Ukrainian border crossing mark: in the passport or, at their request, on an immigration card. What Spain asks of foreign nationals with a Ukrainian residence permit is not officially known, so it is better to check the requirements at the place of application.

    If you need the certificate, we first determine the form: a PDF with an electronic signature, a paper original, a translation. We prepare the consent and the details for the request, a lawyer in Ukraine sends the request to the GTsOSI DPSU, we check the response and send you the PDF or bring the original to Spain. According to practising lawyers in Ukraine, the response to such a request comes as a certificate in your name, not as a letter addressed to the lawyer; we agree the form of the response before the request. We do not guarantee that a Spanish authority will accept the certificate, and we say so before we start. We will update the article when Spain publishes the list.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can you get temporary protection in Spain without an exit stamp?
    EU Decision 2026/1912 does not require a stamp specifically: a lawful border crossing also counts as proof, as does an easily verifiable official document, on paper or electronic, confirming exemption from military obligations or their fulfilment, for example from Reserve+. But as of 1 October 2026 Spain had not published which documents it accepts, so there are no guarantees. Gather in advance everything that confirms your departure and your military registration status: a DPSU certificate, documents from Reserve+, on a deferment or exemption.
    Can an exit stamp be added retroactively?
    No. According to a DPSU spokesperson (August 2026, as reported by hromadske), stamps are not added retroactively. If there is no stamp, the fact of departure is confirmed with a border crossing certificate issued by the DPSU Main Centre for Processing Special Information.
    Does the new condition apply to those already under protection in Spain?
    No, if you enjoyed temporary protection in Spain on 4 August 2026 inclusive and have kept it without interruption. Departure must be proven by new applicants from 5 August 2026, by those moving from another EU country and applying again, and by those who have officially declared their return to Ukraine or renounced protection.
    Does the 90-day rule apply in Spain?
    This has not been officially confirmed. The 90-day benchmark between the departure date and the application for protection is in the European Commission's FAQ of August 2026, and the Commission allows exceptions. This rule is not in the text of Decision 2026/1912 itself. How Spain applies it was unknown as of 1 October 2026.
    Does a DPSU certificate replace Reserve+?
    No. The certificate shows the date, time, border checkpoint and direction of each crossing, but, judging by descriptions of the response, not the grounds for leaving. It does not replace documents on a deferment or exemption or an extract from Reserve+, if they are asked for.
    How long does it take to get a DPSU certificate?
    If you request it yourself, a response is given within 30 calendar days (Law of Ukraine No. 2297-VI, Art. 16), and it is free of charge. For a lawyer's request: by law no later than 5 working days, extendable to 20 (Law No. 5076-VI, Art. 24), but in practice timeframes are currently unstable: some people get the certificate in 5 working days, others in 15 or 20.
    Do you need an apostille and a translation of the certificate for Spain?
    An apostille is not needed: it is not placed on a DPSU certificate, which is a response from a public authority with a qualified electronic signature (KEP) or a seal. Whether a translation is needed has not been officially determined: Spain has not published a list of documents, and the European Commission recommends a sworn translation for documents from Reserve+. If your authority asks for a translation of the certificate, we arrange a sworn translation from Ukrainian.
