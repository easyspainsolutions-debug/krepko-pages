    Permanent residence in Spain after temporary protection: when to apply and what to check in advance

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. Time under temporary protection counts in full towards the five years for long-term residence (instruction SEM 2/2026). You can apply directly from your protection card; an intermediate residence permit is not needed. You renounce protection only after the new authorisation is approved, not before. Nobody reaches five years on protection before 10 March 2027. For those who received protection in spring 2022, the five years will mostly be reached in April–June 2027, for others later.

    In spring 2027 the first Ukrainians who received temporary protection in Spain will reach five years. That is the threshold for long-term residence, which people commonly call permanent residence. For a long time it was unclear whether the years under protection count towards this period, and the answer “they do not count” still turns up online. Since 22 June 2026 the question has been settled by an official instruction: they count, in full.

    Below we look at the month from which those who arrived in 2022 can apply, whether you need to leave protection first, how to count trips and what can get in the way of the application: gaps in your municipal registration and certificates. We checked the rules on 1 October 2026 against the BOE and documents of the migration authority.

    What long-term residence is and what it has to do with permanent residence

    The official name of the status is autorización de residencia de larga duración nacional. “Permanent residence” is the everyday name; from here on we write “long-term residence”. Under art. 182 of the Reglamento de Extranjería (RD 1155/2024), it gives the right to live and work in Spain indefinitely on the same terms as Spaniards. This status has nothing to do with the Ukrainian consular registration of departure for permanent residence abroad.

    There are two similar statuses, and people confuse them:

    • Larga duración nacional (arts. 182–185). The main requirement is five years of legal and continuous residence. There is no income threshold and no health insurance requirement.
    • Larga duración-UE (arts. 175–178). The same five years plus a stable income and insurance: for a family of two, 150% of IPREM and a further 50% for each additional person.

    The instruction on temporary protection covers only the first option. You cannot apply for larga duración-UE directly from a protection card: the EU directive on long-term residents (2003/109/CE, art. 3.2.b), on which this status is based, does not apply to holders of temporary protection. Whether these years will count if you later switch to another residence permit has not been officially clarified. So from here on we deal only with the nacional option.

    Does time under protection count: yes, in full

    On 22 June 2026 the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) signed instruction 2/2026 on the transition from temporary protection to other statuses. Point six answers directly: time under protection “computará íntegramente”, that is, it counts towards the five years in full and is added to the time on the next authorisation.

    Three practical conclusions follow from this.

    • An intermediate residence permit is not needed. You can apply for long-term residence directly from your temporary protection card.
    • As a rule, there are no gaps in the records. Protection was extended for everyone at once, without individual decisions, and in 2024 the central register of foreign nationals automatically extended the authorisations following the cards. So for most people the five years in the official records run without breaks. If your protection ever ended, your residence time needs to be checked separately.
    • The years are not lost when you switch. If you have already replaced protection with another residence permit, the time on protection is added to the new authorisation.
    From which date to count. The instruction counts the time during which you held the residence authorisation under protection. This authorisation is part of the protection decision itself (art. 5.1 Orden PCM/169/2022), and before the decision there was only a temporary right to remain in the country while the application was being examined (art. 3). So it is logical to count from the date of the decision, not from entry or from the application: the weeks before the decision are not covered. There is no official clarification of which date the office uses, so if your decision was delayed in 2022, do not apply on the very first possible day.

    When to apply if you arrived in spring 2022

    The Spanish order on temporary protection (Orden PCM/169/2022) entered into force on 10 March 2022. Under the order, the decision was to be issued no later than 24 hours after the application, and it immediately gave the right to live and work. Before that date protection was not granted in Spain, so five years cannot be reached before 10 March 2027.

    For most people the date is later. According to statistics from the migration authority, by the end of March 2022 protection was in force for 27,109 Ukrainians, and in the second quarter their number grew by 93,873. If your decision is from April–June 2022, the five years will be reached in April–June 2027.

    The procedure is simple: find your protection decision, add five years to its date and leave a margin of a few days. You cannot apply earlier: the requirement of art. 183 is five years immediately before the application.

    You do not need to wait for your card to expire. The rule on applying two months before the authorisation expires does not apply to those whose authorisation is valid and who have already reached five years (art. 184.2): you can apply on any day after that date.

    A mismatch in dates worth knowing about. Protection itself has been extended by a decision of the Council of the EU until 4 March 2028 (decision 2026/1912) and applies directly: individual extension decisions are not needed, as the Spanish order INT/96/2026 expressly states. But this order extended only the cards, until 4 March 2027. As of 30 September 2026 there is no new order in the BOE; previous ones were published between mid-February and early March. Everyone reaches five years only after 4 March 2027, and how the office will see your authorisation in the register if the new order is delayed has not been officially clarified.

    Do you need to leave protection first

    No. Point seven of instruction SEM 2/2026 sets the opposite order:

    1. you apply for long-term residence while remaining under protection;
    2. you receive approval;
    3. you submit an express renunciation of temporary protection;
    4. you request the new card (TIE) and show the receipt for the renunciation you submitted.

    Renouncing in advance is risky: under RD 1325/2003 (art. 24.1) protection ends automatically at the moment of express renunciation, and until the new authorisation is approved you will be left without status. It also ends just as automatically if you have told the authorities that you are voluntarily returning to your home country. And if, without a valid reason, you fail to attend a mandatory procedure within 45 days of being summoned, the Ministry of the Interior will declare protection terminated (renunciation by default, art. 24.2).

    The instruction itself does not set out where to submit the renunciation. It is submitted not to Extranjería (the immigration office), but to the Ministry of the Interior's asylum office (Oficina de Asilo y Refugio) using the official form “Desistimiento o renuncia PT”: through the electronic registry, with “RENUNCIA PT” and the case number in the subject line, or on paper at any public administration registry office. Keep the submission receipt: you show it when you request the new card. The old protection card must be handed in after the renunciation, as order INT/96/2026 points out. The new card is requested in person at the police station where you live within a month of the notification of approval.

    Continuity: which trips are allowed

    The five years must be legal and continuous. Continuity does not mean “not a single trip abroad”. Under art. 183.2 it is not broken by:

    • absences of up to 6 months in a row, if in total over the five years they come to no more than 10 months;
    • work trips — up to 6 months in a row and up to 18 months in total;
    • trips due to force majeure, which the authorities assess individually.

    There is one exception: leaving in breach of the rules (salida irregular).

    The rule does not distinguish between countries. Trips to Ukraine do not in themselves reset your residence time; what matters is how long they last. It is a different matter if you officially told the authorities that you were returning to your home country: then protection ended, and the question becomes whether the status was in force for all five years. We look at such cases separately.

    How offices check absences has not been officially described. Count them yourself using your passport and tickets. Example: three trips of two months each and one of four come to exactly 10 months. That is the limit, and every extra week abroad is already beyond it.

    Do you need a criminal record certificate from Ukraine

    There is no clear-cut answer. In its requirements, the official information sheet of the migration authority (Hoja 49) refers to the countries where you have lived for the last five years: you must have no criminal record in Spain or in those countries. The office requests the Spanish certificate itself (art. 184.4).

    Compare this with arraigo (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain): there the certificate is required from the countries where you lived for five years before entering Spain (art. 126), so almost everyone needs the Ukrainian one. For long-term residence the window is different: the five years before the application.

    But in the list of documents both the information sheet and the regulation (art. 184.3) mention the certificate with the proviso “en su caso”, that is, “where applicable”, and alongside the countries of residence they mention the country of origin. How offices in Alicante, Madrid or Valencia read this, and whether trips to Ukraine count as living there, has not been officially confirmed.

    Settle this question two or three months before you apply. If the certificate is needed, it requires an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish, and that takes weeks. How to obtain it without leaving Spain we explained in the article on the two criminal record certificates.

    Municipal registration: why its history matters

    The official list of documents for long-term residence does not include the padrón (municipal register): the office checks legal residence itself (art. 184.4). But the padrón is the simplest proof that you have physically lived in Spain all these years, and a gap in it will have to be explained if the office has questions. For arraigo such a gap is a direct problem.

    A gap creeps in unnoticed. Foreign nationals from outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland who do not have an EU family member card or long-term residence must confirm their padrón entry once every two years (art. 16.1 of the Local Government Act). If you do not do this, the town hall will remove you from the padrón, and it is not obliged to hear you beforehand. The removal decision is served on you, and the entry ends from the date it is served, so do not ignore letters from the town hall about the padrón. Under the 2020 rules on keeping the padrón:

    • renewal is a separate step; it does not happen by itself;
    • the town hall may warn you about the deadline, but is not obliged to;
    • a change of address does not in itself renew the entry;
    • a new entry takes effect from the date of the application; it cannot be backdated, and a hole remains in the history.

    What to do now: order a historical padrón certificate in each municipality where you were registered during these years, and check that the dates follow on without gaps. A town hall shows the history only for its own municipality, and if you have moved, for example from a reception centre, it is split across several extracts. How to order the certificate is explained in the article on the certificado de empadronamiento. If you have found a gap, register again as soon as possible and gather other proof for that period: a rental contract, bills, records from your health centre, a statement of your employment record. With long-term residence you no longer need to renew the padrón every two years.

    Documents, fees and timeframes

    Under art. 184.3 and information sheet Hoja 49, the application includes:

    • an application on form EX-11;
    • a copy of all pages of your valid passport;
    • the receipt for payment of the fee (tasa, Modelo 790, code 052) — €21.87, paid within 10 working days after the application is accepted for processing;
    • for school-age children, a certificate that they are attending school;
    • “where applicable”, a criminal record certificate (see above).

    Foreign documents are submitted with a sworn translation and an apostille. How this works we explained in the article on the apostille and sworn translation. You apply in person at the Oficina de Extranjería of your province or online through the Mercurio platform on the portal sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es.

    A decision is made within three months from the day after the application is registered. If there is no answer, the application is deemed approved (silencio positivo). After approval, the card is issued using form EX-17 with fee 790-012 — another €21.87. In total, the fees come to €43.74 (as of 1 October 2026).

    Approval is not guaranteed: in addition to checking residence time and trips, the regulation (art. 184.5) allows refusal if the applicant is considered a threat to public order or security.

    What to do before spring 2027

    1. Find your temporary protection decision and work out the date when you will reach five years.
    2. Write down all trips outside Spain with dates and add up the months.
    3. Order a historical padrón certificate in each municipality where you were registered, and check that the dates follow on without gaps.
    4. Check your passport's expiry date: you need a copy of a valid one. If it is expiring, you can replace your passport in Spain.
    5. Settle the question of the Ukrainian criminal record certificate in advance.
    6. Do not renounce protection before approval. If you do not yet have five years, other routes are covered on the page on switching from temporary protection.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does time on temporary protection count towards the five years for permanent residence?
    Yes, in full. This is set out in instruction SEM 2/2026 of 22 June 2026: time under protection counts towards the five years for long-term residence (larga duración nacional) and is added to the time on the next authorisation if you switch to one.
    I arrived in Spain in March 2022. When can I apply?
    No earlier than five years after the temporary protection decision. Spain began granting protection on 10 March 2022, so the earliest date is around 10 March 2027. If your decision is from April–June 2022, you can apply in April–June 2027. The instruction counts the time during which you held the authorisation under protection, and the authorisation is part of the protection decision, so it is logical to count from the date of the decision. There is no official clarification, so leave a margin of a few days.
    Do I need to wait until my temporary protection card expires?
    No. If you have already reached five years and your authorisation is valid, you can apply at any time: the rule on applying two months before expiry does not apply in this case (art. 184.2 RD 1155/2024).
    Do I need to renounce temporary protection before applying?
    No, the order is the opposite. Under instruction SEM 2/2026, you first obtain approval of the new authorisation, then submit an express renunciation of protection, and when the new card is issued you show the receipt for that renunciation. A renunciation submitted in advance ends protection immediately.
    I have travelled to Ukraine several times. Is continuity broken?
    Not necessarily. Under art. 183.2 it is not broken by absences of up to 6 months in a row, if in total over the five years they come to no more than 10 months. For work trips the limit is 6 months in a row and 18 in total. The rule does not distinguish between countries; what counts is the duration.
    Do I need a criminal record certificate from Ukraine?
    There is no clear-cut answer. In its requirements, the migration authority's information sheet refers to the countries where you have lived for the last five years before applying, and the office requests the Spanish certificate itself. But in the list of documents both the information sheet and the regulation mention the certificate with the proviso “where applicable” and mention the country of origin, and the practice of specific offices has not been confirmed. Check this two or three months before you apply: if the certificate is needed, the apostille and translation take weeks.
    Do I need an income and health insurance?
    For long-term residence nacional, no: the main requirement is five years of legal and continuous residence. Income and insurance are required for a different status, larga duración-UE. But you cannot apply for it directly from a protection card: the EU directive on long-term residents does not apply to holders of temporary protection. Whether these years will count if you later switch to another residence permit has not been officially clarified.
