    How to enrol a child in school in Spain: a guide for Ukrainian parents

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~6 min read
    School in Spain is free for all children aged 6 to 16: the parents' citizenship and the child's status do not affect this. The difficulty lies elsewhere: admissions have their own calendar, and some documents will most likely have to be brought from Ukraine with an apostille and a sworn translation, which takes weeks, not days. Below is the whole route: what types of school there are, when to apply, what to gather in advance and what to do if your child does not yet speak Spanish. We handle the paperwork side.

    The education system in Spain

    First, get to know the levels, since they determine which year your child will be placed in:

    • Educación Infantil (0–6 years, at a colegio usually from age 3): not compulsory, but popular
    • Educación Primaria (6–12 years): 6 school years, compulsory
    • ESO — Educación Secundaria Obligatoria (12–16 years): 4 school years, compulsory
    • Bachillerato (16–18 years): two years of secondary education after ESO

    In Spain, the year group is determined not by the Ukrainian school year but by age, that is, by year of birth: children start the 1st year of Primaria in the year they turn 6, and they can be placed one year below their age only if they are two or more years behind in the curriculum. For example, if a child turns 7 in the same year the school year begins, by age they go into the 2nd year of Primaria. If your children are still in Ukraine and you are only preparing the move, you need to start not with the school but with their status: the route is covered in the guide on family reunification (reagrupación familiar).

    State, municipal and private schools

    There are three types of school, and the difference between them lies in money and in admission conditions:

    • Colegios públicos (state schools): completely free, funded by taxes; most families go here
    • Colegios concertados (subsidised schools): private schools with a state subsidy: tuition is almost free, but voluntary contributions are requested; admission follows the same rules of the autonomous community as for state schools
    • Colegios privados (private schools): fully fee-paying; the price depends on the programme and the school's reputation

    For the first school after the move, we advise looking at colegios públicos: they are free and almost always have a language support programme for foreign children. A concertado is chosen when you need a specific profile or a school close to home, a privado if an international programme matters.

    How to choose a school and when to submit documents

    Every year the autonomous communities announce the proceso de admisión (application period): usually March–April for the school year starting in September; the matrícula (enrolment) is completed later, after places are allocated. During this period, you apply to several schools at once, ranking them in order of priority. If you miss the deadline, the child must still be given a place, but through the allocation committee and at a school where places remain.

    What to look at when choosing:

    • Proximity to home (a school in your area of residence often gives you priority)
    • Classes in Spanish as a foreign language or support programmes for foreigners
    • Reviews from families whose children already attend the school
    • A canteen (comedor), if your child has nowhere to have lunch between classes

    The application is submitted in person at the school or through the Educación portal of your autonomous community; the deadlines and the list of attachments differ in each region. If you do not want to deal with this yourself, message us on WhatsApp: we will check your community's requirements and tell you what to order from Ukraine and what is arranged here.

    Documents for school enrolment

    For the application you need to gather:

    • The child's birth certificate: original, apostille and sworn translation; without them the document may not be accepted
    • The child's passport: a copy
    • A parent's passport: a copy
    • Empadronamiento (padrón): municipal registration at your place of residence. It is done at the town hall; you need a housing document (a lease, a property document or written consent from the owner or from the person renting the home) and the parent's documents. It is the padrón that links the child to a school catchment area
    • Temporary protection card or TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero): whichever of these documents the child already has
    • A record of vaccinations received (cartilla de vacunación)
    • School report or certificate from the previous school (if available): an apostille and translation may also be requested for these
    Important: Foreign children, including Ukrainians, have the right to free compulsory education in Spain on an equal footing with citizens until the age of 16. No status (temporary protection, a residence permit, even having no documents) deprives them of this right. But the school usually asks for a birth certificate with an apostille and a sworn translation: we order it in Ukraine and get it ready for submission while you deal with the rest of the move.

    Language support for children without Spanish

    Parents' main fear is that their child will sit down at a desk and not understand a word. Spanish schools have specific arrangements for this:

    • Programmes for children who do not know the language of instruction: each autonomous community has its own; the child studies in a regular class and at the same time receives separate help with the language
    • ATAL (Aulas Temporales de Adaptación Lingüística): in Andalusia, special language adaptation lessons
    • LIC (llengua, interculturalitat i cohesió social): in Catalonia; newly arrived children may be taken to an aula d'acollida for some lessons, where the focus is on Catalan
    • Foreign language assistant teachers: some schools have staff who speak Ukrainian, Russian or Arabic

    Children usually pick up conversational Spanish within 3–6 months in a class with their peers, noticeably faster than adults. For the first months it makes sense to add a tutor so that your child arrives at school with at least some everyday phrases.

    Psychological support in schools

    Spanish schools have an orientador escolar, a specialist in psycho-educational guidance, free of charge and for all pupils: at secondary school they are on the staff, at primary school they often come from the district guidance team. They oversee the adaptation of newcomers, deal with language difficulties and keep an eye on the child's emotional state. Ask for a meeting with them as soon as you enrol: this is your child's right, not a favour from the school.

    What to do if your child does not speak Spanish

    • A month before school starts, find a Spanish tutor: 2–3 lessons a week give your child a basic vocabulary
    • Prepare them emotionally: talk about the new school calmly and without frightening them, show them the building in advance
    • Find friends in advance: if possible, go to playgrounds and get to know Spanish and Ukrainian families
    • Keep in touch with the school: let the teachers know that your child speaks Ukrainian at home, and ask for extra support in the first weeks
    • Be patient: the first month will be hard, but most children adapt quickly

    After submitting the documents

    After places are allocated (in the regular admission round, according to your community's calendar; for late applications, after the committee's decision), the school sends a confirmation of admission and a list of what to buy for the first day. Usually this is:

    • School uniform (if required)
    • A backpack, pencil case and stationery
    • Sports kit for PE
    • Lunches, if the school has no canteen

    In the first days, your child may come home feeling low: this is normal and passes. What is needed from you this month is patience and contact with the class teacher. We take on the paperwork side: we order the birth certificate and certificates from Ukraine, obtain the apostille, arrange the sworn translation, prepare notarial consents for the child and check the set of documents against your school's requirements. From you: the child's details and your city; then you receive a package ready for submission. Timeframes depend on which document it is and where it comes from; we discuss the cost once we understand the scope.

    Frequently asked questions

    From what age do children go to school in Spain?

    Compulsory education in Spain runs from 6 to 16: Educación Primaria (6–12 years) and ESO (12–16 years). Before 6 there is the optional but popular Educación Infantil, and after 16, Bachillerato (16–18 years).

    When should you submit documents to enrol in a Spanish school?

    The application period (proceso de admisión) in most autonomous communities is March–April for the start of the school year in September; the matrícula is completed later, after places are allocated. During this period, you can apply to several schools in order of priority through the Educación portal.

    Is education in Spain free for Ukrainian children?

    Yes, state colegios públicos are completely free for all children regardless of their parents' citizenship and status. Subsidised colegios concertados may ask for voluntary contributions, and colegios privados charge fees.

    What documents are needed to enrol a child in school in Spain?

    A birth certificate (translated and legalised), the parents' and the child's passports, a certificado de empadronamiento linking you to the school catchment area, a medical card with vaccinations and a school report or certificate from the previous school.
