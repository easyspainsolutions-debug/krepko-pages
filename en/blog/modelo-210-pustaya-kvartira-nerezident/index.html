    Your flat in Spain stands empty and you are not a resident: Modelo 210 by 31 December

    Krepko · 24 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. A non-resident who has a home in Spain without tenants pays tax on imputed income every year: 1.1% or 2% of the cadastral value, multiplied by a rate of 19% for residents of the EU and EEA or 24% for everyone else (art. 24.5 and 25 TRLIRNR, art. 85 Ley IRPF). With a cadastral value of €100,000, this comes to €209 to €480 a year. The return for 2025 is filed on Modelo 210 by 31 December 2026 (by 23 December if you pay by direct debit — domiciliación), and for 2026 the window moves to the period from 1 April to 31 December 2027 (Orden HAC/623/2026). If you spent more than 183 days in Spain in the year, you are a tax resident and this income goes into the annual IRPF return, not into Modelo 210.

    Your flat in Spain is locked up, you live in another country and receive no income from it. Tax is charged all the same: Spanish law treats the owner as receiving notional income from the very right to use the home, and a non-resident declares it every year on Modelo 210.

    Below we look at who files Modelo 210 and who reports the same income in the ordinary IRPF return, how the tax is calculated, the deadline for filing the return for 2025 and what changes from 2027. We checked the rules on 24 November 2026 against the BOE and the tax office (AEAT) website.

    For an empty flat, Modelo 210 is filed for every year of ownership. We work out for which years you have an obligation, gather the data on the flat, and the calculation and filing are done by our tax specialist — details on the Modelo 210 for non-residents page.

    Who files Modelo 210 and who files IRPF?

    The form is determined not by citizenship or the type of document, but by tax residence for the calendar year. Under art. 9.1 Ley IRPF, a person is considered a tax resident of Spain if any of the following conditions is met:

    • they spent more than 183 days in Spain in the calendar year, and short trips abroad count as time in Spain unless tax residence in another country is proven;
    • the main centre of their business or economic interests is in Spain;
    • the law also presumes residence if their spouse (not legally divorced) and the minor children who depend on them live permanently in Spain; this presumption can be rebutted.

    Non-residents who have income in Spain pay non-resident income tax (IRNR) — art. 5 TRLIRNR. It is for them that Modelo 210 exists.

    The first trap is temporary protection. Art. 9.1 Ley IRPF defines residence by days in Spain and the centre of interests, and immigration status is not mentioned in it. If you have lived in Spain with a temporary protection card for more than 183 days in a year, under art. 9.1 you are a tax resident. In that case Modelo 210 is not filed for a second flat: the imputed income from it is reported in the annual IRPF return (Renta), while the flat you live in yourself is not caught by this rule — art. 85 excludes the main home (vivienda habitual) from the calculation. Who needs to file the annual return is covered in our article on the mandatory IRPF return.

    The reverse situation: you have left Spain, but kept the flat. Residence is assessed for each calendar year, so for the year you leave, the answer depends on how many days you spent in Spain and where your main interests were. From the year in which none of the conditions of art. 9.1 is met, the imputed income from the Spanish flat is declared on Modelo 210.

    How much to pay: the formula and an example

    For non-residents, imputed income from urban property is determined under the rules of the IRPF law (art. 24.5 TRLIRNR refers to them). Under art. 85 Ley IRPF, in the wording in force for 2025, the calculation is as follows:

    • 2% of the cadastral value (valor catastral) — the general rule;
    • 1.1% — if the cadastral values in the municipality have been revised in a general valuation (procedimiento de valoración colectiva de carácter general) and came into force within the last ten years. For 2023–2025 the threshold is relaxed by additional provision 55 of Ley IRPF: 1.1% applies if the revised values came into force on or after 1 January 2012;
    • if on 31 December the flat has no cadastral value or it has not been notified to the owner — 1.1% of half of the higher of two amounts: the purchase price or the value verified by the administration for other taxes.

    The rate under art. 25.1.a TRLIRNR is applied to the resulting base: 24% in general and 19% if you are a resident of another EU or EEA country with an exchange of tax information (according to the AEAT instructions — the EU, Iceland, Norway and Liechtenstein). The rate depends on the country of your tax residence, not on your passport: a Ukrainian citizen who lives and pays tax in Poland or Germany uses the 19% rate, while one living in Ukraine uses 24%.

    An example with a notional cadastral value of €100,000 for a full year:

    Imputation percentage Imputed income Tax at 19% Tax at 24%
    1.1% €1,100 €209 €264
    2% €2,000 €380 €480

    What else matters according to the AEAT instructions for form 210:

    • Expenses are not deducted. No type of expense can be deducted from imputed income — neither IBI property tax nor contributions to the community of owners.
    • Income is calculated in proportion to days. If the flat was bought or sold during the year, or was let for part of the year, the amount is reduced proportionally. The months of letting are declared separately, as rental income, under their own rules and deadlines.
    • Co-owners each file for themselves. The income is divided according to ownership shares: spouses with 50/50 shares file two returns, each with half of the amount.
    • The accrual date is 31 December. Income for the year is considered received on the last day of that year, and the return is filed in the following year.

    The cadastral value is protected Catastro data: the owner can see it in their personal area on sedecatastro.gob.es after logging in with an electronic certificate.

    What is the deadline for 2025 and 2026?

    Orden HAC/623/2026 (BOE of 23 June 2026) moved the start of the filing window for imputed income, but this does not affect the return for 2025 — AEAT confirms this in a separate information note.

    Income for the year File and pay Direct debit of the payment (when filing online)
    2025 from 1 January to 31 December 2026 by 23 December 2026
    2026 from 1 April to 31 December 2027 from 1 April to 23 December 2027

    The form filed from 1 January 2027 will have new fields: the number of days the flat was at your disposal, and your share of ownership. For income for 2026, the relaxed 1.1% threshold (values that came into force from 2012) has been extended by Real Decreto-ley 29/2026; the Congress of Deputies approved it, and the resolution of 12 November was published in the BOE on 13 November 2026. From 1 January 2027 the same decree amends art. 85 Ley IRPF: instead of the 1.1 and 2% rates, a scale from 1.1 to 3% is introduced depending on the total of the cadastral values. This concerns income for 2027, for which the return is filed in 2028.

    The return can be filed online with an electronic certificate or on paper using the pre-declaration form (predeclaración) on the AEAT website. Payment can be made at a Spanish bank that partners with the tax office, by direct debit from an account in the SEPA zone or by transfer from abroad: the transfer is made only in euros, and the payment reference is valid for 30 days. The FNMT digital certificate is a convenient way to file online.

    What happens if the deadline is missed?

    If you file the return late on your own, before any demand from the tax office, art. 27 Ley General Tributaria provides for a surcharge (recargo): 1% plus a further 1% for each full month of delay. After 12 months the surcharge is 15%, and late-payment interest is added to it. If the tax office has already sent a demand or started an inspection that you have been notified of, this is no longer voluntary filing, and the consequences are different.

    Each missed year is covered by a separate return, which states its own accrual year and only that year's data (para. 4 of art. 27). Each year has its own imputation percentage and rate.

    How does the annual tax differ from the tax on a sale?

    Imputed income is an annual tax on ownership. A sale is a separate event with its own rules. If you are a non-resident in the year of the sale, the buyer withholds 3% of the price and pays it to the tax office (art. 25.2 TRLIRNR). You file Modelo 210 for the capital gain: the rate is 19% (art. 25.1.f), and the filing period is three months, starting one month after the date of the transaction. For the year of the sale, imputed income is also declared, but only for the days before the transaction. How to sell a flat without flying in for the transaction, and which taxes the seller pays, is covered in our article on selling a flat under a power of attorney.

    Frequently asked questions

    My flat in Spain is empty and earns no income. Do I need to pay tax?
    Yes, if you are not a resident of Spain. The law imputes to the owner notional income from the flat itself when it is not let: 1.1% or 2% of the cadastral value per year (art. 24.5 TRLIRNR and art. 85 Ley IRPF). Tax is paid on it at 19% if you are tax resident in the EU or EEA and 24% in other cases; the return is Modelo 210.
    What is the deadline for filing Modelo 210 for 2025?
    By 31 December 2026: the window has been open since 1 January. If you pay by direct debit from an account when filing online, you need to file by 23 December 2026. Orden HAC/623/2026 did not change this deadline — AEAT confirms this in its note on the new deadlines.
    When do I file Modelo 210 for 2026?
    From 1 April to 31 December 2027 — this is the new window under Orden HAC/623/2026; previously it opened on 1 January. Direct debit of the payment is possible from 1 April to 23 December 2027. The form will have fields for the number of days of ownership and the ownership share.
    I have temporary protection and a flat in Spain. Do I need to file Modelo 210?
    Most likely not. If you have lived in Spain for more than 183 days in a year, under art. 9.1 Ley IRPF you are a tax resident: the law counts days and the centre of interests, and the type of document is not mentioned in this article. In that case the imputed income from a second flat is reported in the annual IRPF return, and the home you live in is not included in the calculation. Modelo 210 is filed by non-residents.
    How much do I pay if the flat belongs to me and my husband half and half?
    Each of you files your own Modelo 210 return for your share: the imputed income is divided according to ownership shares. For example, with a cadastral value of €100,000 and an imputation percentage of 1.1%, the income from the flat is €1,100, each spouse's share is €550, and the tax at 24% is €132 each. Expenses are not deducted from imputed income.
    What happens if I do not file Modelo 210 on time?
    If you file on your own before any demand from the tax office, a surcharge applies: 1% plus 1% for each full month of delay, and after 12 months, 15% plus interest (art. 27 Ley General Tributaria). A separate return is filed for each missed year. If the tax office has already sent a demand or started an inspection, the consequences are different.
