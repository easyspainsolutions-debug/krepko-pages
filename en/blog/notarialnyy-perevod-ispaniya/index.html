    Notarised translation into Spanish: where it is accepted in Spain and what to submit instead

    Krepko · 20 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. The Spanish immigration office accepts three kinds of translation: by a sworn translator appointed by the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, by a Spanish consulate abroad, or by the consulate of the country that issued the document. A Ukrainian notarised translation with a double apostille is neither on this list nor on the Ministry of Justice list for nationality. The civil registry (Registro Civil) does not accept it in practice either and asks for a sworn translation. A notary in Spain and a bank, as a rule, do accept a Ukrainian notarised translation, and a court decides for itself. Check the format with the authority before you pay for the translation.

    You had a certificate or record from Ukraine translated at a notary's office, had it apostilled, brought it to Spain, and now the immigration office is asking for a different translation. The Spanish immigration service and Ministry of Justice recognise a sworn translation (traducción jurada) or a consular translation as official. A Ukrainian notarised translation is not on their lists, even with two apostilles. Meanwhile, there are exactly four sworn translators of Ukrainian in the register of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs for the whole country.

    Below we look at the three translation formats, what each authority accepts, the legal routes other than queueing for the four translators, and the reverse journey: a Spanish document for Ukraine or Russia. The rules and figures were checked against the BOE and official websites on 20 November 2026.

    Three translations that go by the same name

    A sworn translation (traducción jurada) is done by a translator holding a title from the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs (MAEC). They certify the translation with their signature and seal and attach a copy of the original, stamping and dating each of its pages (RD 724/2020, art. 18). For Spanish authorities, such a translation is valid without legalisation, wherever the translator lives (art. 12.2).

    A “notarised” translation in the Ukrainian and Russian sense works differently. The notary certifies the accuracy of the translation personally only if they know both languages. Usually the translation is done by a translator, and the notary authenticates their signature (art. 79 of the Law of Ukraine “On Notaries”; in Russia, art. 81 of the Fundamentals of Legislation on Notaries). In this case the notary does not check the content of the text. The closed list of translations with official force (art. 17 of RD 724/2020) does not include a translation certified by a notary of a third country.

    An ordinary translation without certification is fine for understanding the document yourself. It has no official force, although it may also work in a civil court (more on this below).

    What each authority accepts

    There is no single rule “for the whole of Spain”: each authority has its own rule.

    Authority Translation required by the rules Ukrainian notarised translation with an apostille
    Immigration office (Extranjería): residence permits, switching from temporary protection Sworn, or one of the two consular routes (information sheet Hoja 61) Not on the list
    Nationality, Ministry of Justice Consulate, with legalisation at the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or sworn Not on the list
    Civil registry (Registro Civil) Translation by a “competent authority or official” (Ley 20/2011, art. 95.1) Not accepted in practice; a sworn translation is requested
    Civil court A private one is allowed; if challenged, an official one (LEC, art. 144) May be accepted as a private translation
    Notary in Spain Translates personally if they know the language; for a certified copy, an official translation (Reglamento Notarial, art. 150 and 252) As a rule, accepted; the notary decides
    Land Registry The translation office of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, an official authorised by law or by agreement, or a notary who vouches for the accuracy of the translation; the registrar may do without a translation if they know the language (Reglamento Hipotecario, art. 37) The registrar decides
    Bank No rule; the bank's internal policies apply As a rule, accepted; check at the branch

    For immigration matters, instruction DGM 2/2019 specifies that a translation into Spanish is required for any document from a country outside the European Union. The exemption from translation for EU multilingual forms applies only to documents from EU member states; it does not apply to Ukrainian ones.

    With Russian, the logic is the same. There are more sworn translators of Russian in the register: 34, three of them in the province of Alicante.

    Double apostille: where it works and where it does not

    The “double apostille” route looks like this: an apostille is placed on the Ukrainian original, a translator in Ukraine translates the document, a notary authenticates the translator's signature, and a second apostille is placed on this notarial document. Legally, the result is a Ukrainian notarial document, not an official Spanish translation.

    For the immigration office and nationality, this option is not on the official lists: residence permits, switching from temporary protection and nationality require a sworn or consular translation. The Registro Civil does not accept a Ukrainian notarised translation in practice either and asks for a sworn one. A notary in Spain and a bank, as a rule, accept a Ukrainian notarised translation. Courts and the Land Registry decide for themselves, so we check the format with the authority before ordering.

    Electronic apostille from Ukraine. For documents apostilled by the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine (civil registry certificates issued by DRACS, notarial, court and archive documents), since 1 February 2026 the apostille has been issued through the Electronic Register of Apostilles, within up to three working days. According to Ministry of Justice reports as relayed by legal publications, since March such an apostille also has an electronic version: a file with a qualified signature in ASiC-E format and a QR code for verification at apostille.minjust.gov.ua. The criminal record certificate is apostilled by the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Ukraine (MVS), and education documents by the Ministry of Education and Science (MON), and this innovation does not yet apply to them. We have not found an official position of Spain on the ASiC-E file, so count on a paper apostille until the authority explicitly confirms otherwise.

    Four translators for the whole country: how to order a sworn translation remotely

    As of 20 November 2026, the register of sworn translators of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists four active people with Ukrainian: two in Madrid, one in Seville and one in Valencia. In the provinces of Alicante, Murcia, Málaga and Barcelona there are none. One of the reasons: only citizens of Spain, the EU, the EEA and Switzerland may sit the sworn translator exam (RD 724/2020, art. 10), so a Ukrainian without European citizenship cannot take it.

    The translator's city does not matter, however. The title is valid regardless of where the translator lives (RD 724/2020, art. 12.2), and since March 2025 a sworn translator can sign a translation with a qualified electronic signature (Orden AUC/213/2025). The whole file is signed, the original together with the translation; the seal is replaced by a frame with the translator's name, language and number, and the signature is verified at valide.redsara.es. The paper version with a seal also remains valid. Before ordering, we check whether the translator works from a scan and issues translations with an electronic signature.

    Two caveats. The electronic signature is visible only in the file, and practice has not yet confirmed whether a printout of such a translation will be accepted at the counter: if you are submitting on paper, check in advance. And allow time: there is a queue for the four translators in the whole country, and the timeframe depends on their workload and the volume of documents. It is a matter of weeks, not days. There is no official rate; the translator sets the price themselves (art. 16). We arrange the sworn translation and give you the cost and the exact timeframe based on scans of the documents.

    Translation at a consulate: a legal route that is rarely remembered

    The law recognises a translation made by a career (not honorary) consulate of the document's country right here in Spain (RD 724/2020, art. 17.1.d). The immigration office information sheet Hoja 61 also recognises a translation checked by a consulate. The Embassy of Ukraine in Spain charges a consular fee for certifying the accuracy of a translation: €37 per page (rates published on 13 May 2026; before booking, check the amount on the embassy's page).

    Before taking this route, check three things. First: whether the consulate currently does such translations into Spanish, since the consul must know the language personally. Second: whether the authority will then require legalisation at the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Information sheet Hoja 61 requires it, although the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs itself states on its legalisation page that consular documents are exempt from it under the 1968 London Convention (in force for Ukraine since 6 April 2023). Third: whether you can get an appointment. For men aged 18–60, the consul currently performs consular acts only if the applicant has a valid military registration document; more on this in the article about consulates refusing services to men aged 18–60.

    The name in the translation must match your passport and TIE

    Ukrainian transliteration has changed. The official table was approved by Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 55 in 2010 and has been used in passports since 2015, but at the holder's request the Latin spelling may follow a previous passport or foreign documents. Passports with different spellings remain valid until they expire. That is why the same person may have Yurii, Iurii and Yuriy on different papers.

    There is no official Spanish rule on how to write a name in a sworn translation. In practice, translators write it in Latin letters as it appears in the passport or resident card, and transliterate other names according to the transliteration rules for passports. If the spelling in the translation differs from your passport, TIE or documents already submitted, this is a frequent reason for questions and for the file being returned for correction. That is why, before the translation, we ask for a photo of your passport page and TIE, ask which document the authority knows you by, and check the translation against it.

    Where a sworn translation is not required

    • Ukrainian driving licences under temporary protection. Under art. 3.2 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1280, EU member states do not require holders of valid Ukrainian licences to have either a sworn translation or an international driving permit while protection is in force. For exchanging the licence for a Spanish one, the DGT does not ask for a translation either. How long you can drive on them and how the exchange works is covered in the article about Ukrainian driving licences in Spain.
    • Civil court. The translation may be a private one (LEC, art. 144.2). If the other party challenges it within five days, the court will order an official translation at the expense of the party that submitted the document. If the official translation substantially matches the private one, the costs are borne by the party that challenged it.
    • A Spanish notary who knows the language. They are entitled to translate a document they include in the deed themselves (Reglamento Notarial, art. 150). For a certified copy of a document in a language they do not know, an official translation is required (art. 252).

    The reverse journey: a Spanish document for Ukraine or Russia

    First, a Spanish apostille is placed on the Spanish document: it is issued by the Ministry of Justice, the offices of the regional High Courts of Justice (TSJ), and, for notarial documents, the notarial associations. Next, you need a translation according to the rules of the destination country.

    Ukraine. The civil registry office (DRACS) accepts a document in a foreign language only with a translation into Ukrainian “certified in the established manner” (засвідченим в установленому порядку; Rules for State Registration of Civil Status Acts, Order of the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine No. 52/5). There is no confirmation that a Spanish sworn translation into Ukrainian will be accepted this way. The reliable route is a translation on which a Ukrainian notary or consul has authenticated the translator's signature. If needed, we arrange such a translation through the Krepko team in Ukraine.

    Russia. Under art. 81 of the Fundamentals of Legislation on Notaries, the accuracy of a translation is certified by a notary who knows the language, or the notary authenticates the translator's signature. Under the amendments of Federal Law No. 251-FZ of 8 August 2024, the notary also checks the translator's qualification documents. There is no official confirmation that Russian authorities will accept a Spanish sworn translation, so it is more reliable to translate according to Russian rules.

    If the document has yet to be signed, for example a power of attorney for Ukraine, it can be drawn up straight away before a Spanish notary in the language you need, and the text will not need translating. If the Ukrainian side asks for a translation, usually only the notary's certification and the apostille are translated, following the same rules as described above.

    Four questions to ask the authority before paying for a translation

    1. Which translation will you accept: sworn, consular or Ukrainian notarised with an apostille?
    2. Is an apostille needed on the original, and should it be translated together with the document?
    3. Will you accept an electronic translation with the translator's signature, or only a paper one?
    4. Which document should the name follow: the passport or the TIE?

    The answers determine how much you will pay and how long you will wait. A mistake in any of them usually means a second translation at your own expense.

    Frequently asked questions

    Will the Spanish immigration office accept a Ukrainian notarised translation with a double apostille?
    It is not in information sheet Hoja 61. That sheet names three kinds of translation: a sworn translator appointed by the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, a Spanish consulate abroad, and the consulate in Spain of the country that issued the document. The Ministry of Justice list for nationality does not name such a translation either. Residence permits, switching from temporary protection and nationality require a sworn or consular translation. The Registro Civil does not accept a Ukrainian notarised translation in practice either and asks for a sworn one. On the other hand, a notary in Spain and a bank, as a rule, do accept it.
    How many sworn translators of Ukrainian are there in Spain?
    Four active ones as of 20 November 2026, according to the register of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs: two in Madrid, one each in Seville and Valencia. In Alicante, Murcia, Málaga and Barcelona there are none. There are 34 sworn translators of Russian, three of them in the province of Alicante.
    Can I order a sworn translation if the translator is in another city?
    Yes. The title is valid regardless of where the translator lives, and since March 2025 a translation can be signed with a qualified electronic signature (Orden AUC/213/2025). If you are submitting a printout of an electronic translation at the counter, check in advance whether it will be accepted.
    How long does a sworn translation from Ukrainian take and how much does it cost?
    The timeframe depends on the translator's workload and the volume of documents and is counted in weeks, not days: there are four sworn translators of Ukrainian in the whole country. There is no official rate; each translator sets the price themselves (RD 724/2020, art. 16). We arrange the sworn translation and give you the cost and the exact timeframe based on scans of the documents.
    Do Ukrainian driving licences need a sworn translation?
    Not under temporary protection: under art. 3.2 of Regulation (EU) 2022/1280, EU member states require neither a sworn translation nor an international driving permit while protection is in force. For exchanging the licence for a Spanish one, the DGT does not ask for a translation either.
    Will an electronic apostille from Ukraine be accepted in Spain?
    We have not found an official position of the Spanish authorities on the ASiC-E file. Moreover, only apostilles issued by the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine (DRACS civil registry certificates, notarial, court and archive documents) get the electronic version, while the criminal record certificate is apostilled by the Ministry of Internal Affairs (MVS). Until the authority explicitly confirms acceptance, count on a paper apostille.
    What translation do I need to submit a Spanish document to a civil registry office in Ukraine?
    A translation into Ukrainian certified in the established manner, plus a Spanish apostille on the document itself. The reliable route is a translation on which a Ukrainian notary or consul has authenticated the translator's signature. There is no confirmation that DRACS will accept a Spanish sworn translation into Ukrainian.
