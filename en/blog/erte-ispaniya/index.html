    ERTE in Spain: what it means for employees and how to claim the benefit

    Krepko · 11 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. An ERTE is a temporary suspension of the employment contract or a reduction of working time by 10–70% for economic, technical, organisational or production reasons, or due to force majeure (Article 47 of the Estatuto de los Trabajadores, the Workers' Statute). The contract remains in place, and during the period of inactivity the employee can receive unemployment benefit from SEPE. As a general rule, the employee applies for it personally within 15 working days; under the RED mechanism the company submits a collective application. The benefit is subject to IRPF, and for the tax office SEPE is one more payer: if the second and subsequent payers by amount paid more than €1,500 in the year, a tax return is compulsory once income from employment exceeds €15,876 rather than €22,000 (Article 96.3 of the IRPF Act).     What an ERTE is and how it differs from dismissal

    An ERTE (expediente de regulación temporal de empleo, a temporary employment regulation procedure) is the procedure by which a company temporarily reduces its employees' working time or suspends their contracts (Article 47 ET). The reduction is from 10 to 70%, calculated on a daily, weekly, monthly or annual basis. The contract remains in force: you are not deregistered from Seguridad Social (Social Security); the company merely notifies it of the change in your employment (SEPE guidance).

    For economic, technical, organisational and production reasons (ETOP for short), the company holds consultations with the workers' representatives — for up to 15 days, or up to 7 if it has fewer than 50 staff — and notifies the employees and the labour authority (autoridad laboral) of its decision. Force majeure is confirmed by the labour authority within five days of the company's application; if there is no response, the ERTE is deemed authorised. Since 30 November 2024, force majeure also covers cases where it is impossible to get to work because of travel restrictions imposed by the authorities or a serious risk, such as bad weather, for longer than four days of paid leave (permiso), and remote work is not possible (Articles 37.3.g and 47.6 ET).

    The decision can be challenged in the social court (jurisdicción social). If the court finds the measure unjustified, the contract is resumed and the company pays the wages you did not receive or the difference between them and the benefit you received (Article 47.3 ET).

    How much SEPE pays: three regimes

    The benefit is calculated from the average unemployment contribution base over the last 180 days of work (Article 270.1 LGSS); the rest depends on the type of ERTE and on whether the RED mechanism has been activated in the sector.

    Regime Who applies Contribution record required Amount Future benefit
    ERTE for ETOP reasons You, within 15 working days Yes: 360 days of contributions in 6 years not counted towards a previous benefit 70% of the base for the first 180 days, then 60% Your entitlement is used up; with reduced hours, by the hour
    ERTE for force majeure You, within 15 working days No 70% of the base for the whole period Contributions accumulated before the ERTE are not used up
    RED mechanism The company, by collective application, within a month No; the contract must have started before the decision to activate the mechanism 70% of the base for the whole period, capped at 225% of IPREM Time on the benefit is not deducted from a future benefit

    The maximum and minimum of the ordinary benefit depend on whether you have dependent children (Article 270.3 LGSS). With reduced hours, SEPE pays only for the hours not worked (Article 270.5 LGSS). If the inactivity falls on individual working days, they are multiplied by 1.25 to account for weekends: with inactivity on Fridays in a month with four Fridays, five days are paid and deducted, and for five or six consecutive working days, seven (SEPE guidance). The benefit lasts as long as the measure applies, and under ETOP no longer than your contribution record allows. If you have fewer than 360 days of contributions, a subsidy (subsidio) is possible under ETOP: since 1 November 2024 it is enough to have 90 days of contributions, whether or not you have a family. Income must not exceed 75% of the minimum wage (SMI): your own or, if you have a spouse or children, the family income per person. The more contributions, the longer the subsidy: with 90 days, 3 months; with 180 days, 6 months; and with a family, up to 21 months (Articles 274, 275 and 277 LGSS). The general rules on the benefit are in the article on social benefits.

    What to do in the first 15 working days

    Do not expect the company to arrange the benefit for you: it submits a collective application under the RED mechanism and in the ERTEs of companies affected by the DANA storm in October 2024, whereas in an ordinary ERTE SEPE expects an application from each employee. The company may ask SEPE for a different procedure — check with your employer.

    1. The company's written decision
    It states the measure — suspension or reduction — and the period during which it applies (Article 47.3 ET); the company also enters the reason and the date in the certificate for SEPE (Article 267.3 LGSS).
    2. Registration with the employment service
    Without registration as a jobseeker (demandante de empleo) with the employment service of your region, your application will not be accepted (Article 268.1 LGSS). In an ERTE, the region may register employees collectively, without a visit to the office (SEPE guidance).
    3. Application to SEPE
    Under ETOP, the 15 working days are counted from the date the company notified the labour authority of its decision, or from the date from which the measure applies to you, if that is later; under force majeure, from the date of notification of the labour authority's decision or of authorisation by silence (SEPE guide). You can apply on the SEPE sede (online portal) with a digital certificate, DNIe or Cl@ve permanente, or at an office by appointment (cita previa); in the “Observaciones” field SEPE advises stating “ERTE ETOP” or “ERTE FM” and the company's name. If you are late, the entitlement arises from the day of the application, and the missed days are lost (Article 268.2 LGSS).
    4. Documents
    The application form, a TIE with a photo (for non-EU citizens; have your passport with you as well), a bank document showing an account in your name and, if children are listed in the application, their birth certificates (a foreign one with an official translation into Spanish). The company certificate (certificado de empresa) is sent by the employer. A municipal registration certificate (padrón) is neither on SEPE's list nor among the statutory conditions for the benefit (Article 266 LGSS), but your address must be up to date: a change of address must be reported immediately (Article 299 LGSS).
    5. Payments
    SEPE pays only after the company's monthly file on your days of work and inactivity. The file for a month is sent between the 1st and the 20th of the following month, so in the example from the SEPE guide, days of inactivity in April are paid around 10 June.

    We explained how to get Cl@ve with an NIE for the online application in a separate article. While your contract is suspended, you can work for another company or for yourself, but new work must be reported to SEPE, and the payment is suspended; part-time work can be combined with the benefit on application, and the benefit is reduced proportionally (SEPE guidance). Under the RED mechanism, the benefit is not reduced when you work part-time for another company (Additional Provision 41 LGSS).

    Contributions and your contribution record during an ERTE

    The company continues to pay its share of Seguridad Social contributions, and your share for the period of inactivity is paid by SEPE, which deducts it from the benefit (Article 273.2 LGSS). However, time on the benefit in an ERTE does not count towards the contribution record for a future unemployment benefit (Article 269.2 LGSS, SEPE guidance).

    The RED mechanism: when the company applies

    RED (Article 47 bis ET) is activated by the Council of Ministers for the whole economy or for a sector; companies then obtain authorisation from the labour authority to reduce hours or suspend contracts. As of 11 November 2026, RED is in force for car manufacturing and its production chain: since 1 January 2025, extended for the first half of 2026, and by Orden PJC/654/2026 until 31 December 2026. The law allows no more than two extensions of six months each, and this is the second. The company submits the application on behalf of the employees; what is needed from you is consent to the transfer of your data, registration with the employment service and up-to-date contact details (SEPE).

    The benefit and tax: when you need a tax return after an ERTE

    Unemployment benefit is income from employment (Article 17.1.b of the IRPF Act), and the tax office explicitly describes the ERTE benefit as taxable. Over the year you have two payers: the company pays for the time worked, SEPE for the inactivity.

    This determines the threshold for a compulsory tax return: with one payer, €22,000 of income from employment; and if the second and subsequent payers, in descending order of amounts, together paid more than €1,500, €15,876 (Article 96.3 of the IRPF Act). The second payer is the one who paid less: after a long ERTE, this may already be the company. All the cases with thresholds are in the article on tax returns with income below €22,000.

    The second point is withholding. SEPE states that in an ERTE IRPF withholding is unlikely if the expected amount of the benefit is below the threshold from which tax is withheld under the tax rules. And withholding is only an advance payment of tax (as AEAT explains), so the amount not withheld over the year may have to be paid in the tax return. To avoid paying it all at once in spring, you can ask SEPE to withhold more: the Modelo 145 form is submitted no later than five days before the end of the month preceding the payment, and the new rate applies at least until the end of the year, and after that until you waive it in writing.

    Certificate from SEPE. The certificate of withholdings (certificado de IRPF) can be obtained on the SEPE sede, section “Obtenga un certificado sobre su prestación”, with a digital certificate, DNIe or Cl@ve. The tax return for 2026 is filed in spring 2027; the campaign usually runs from early April to 30 June.

    Frequently asked questions

    My boss said the company will arrange the ERTE benefit for us. Do I need to do anything?
    Check with your employer whether they have agreed a different procedure with SEPE. As a general rule, in an ERTE for economic, technical, organisational or production reasons and for force majeure, the employee applies personally within 15 working days, and the company sends SEPE the certificate and the monthly data on days of inactivity. The company submits a collective application on behalf of employees under the RED mechanism. If the deadline is missed, the entitlement arises from the day of the application, and the missed days of benefit are lost.
    Do I need municipal registration (empadronamiento) to receive the ERTE benefit?
    Municipal registration is not among the statutory conditions for the benefit (Article 266 LGSS), and a registration certificate is not on SEPE's list of documents for an ERTE. You need registration with the employment service, an application, an identity document (a TIE for non-EU citizens) and a bank account in your name. The address you gave the employment service and SEPE must be up to date: a move must be reported immediately.
    I have less than a year of contributions in Spain. Will I be paid anything in an ERTE?
    In an ERTE for force majeure, no minimum contribution period is required, and the benefit is 70% of the base for the whole period; you still apply yourself within 15 working days. In an ERTE for economic and organisational reasons, you need at least 360 days of contributions in 6 years. If you have fewer, a subsidy is possible: from 90 days of contributions, whether or not you have a family, if your income does not exceed 75% of the minimum wage, or if you have a spouse or children and the family income per person does not exceed that threshold.
    Can I do some extra work while I am in an ERTE?
    If your contract is suspended, you may work for another company or for yourself, but you must report the new job to SEPE, and the payment will be suspended. Part-time work can be combined with the benefit on application: in an ERTE the benefit is reduced in proportion to the time worked, and under the RED mechanism it is not reduced.
    I earn about €18,000 a year and have never filed a tax return before. Will I have to after an ERTE?
    If the second payer by amount (SEPE or the company, whichever paid less) paid you more than €1,500 over the year, the threshold for a compulsory tax return drops from €22,000 to €15,876 (Article 96.3 of the IRPF Act). This means that if your salary and benefit together come to more than €15,876 for the year, you will have to file. If no tax was withheld from the benefit, the return may show an amount to pay. We check your data and prepare it, while the calculation and filing are done by our tax specialist.
    While I am in an ERTE, are Seguridad Social contributions paid for me?
    Yes. The company pays its share of contributions, and SEPE pays your share for the period of inactivity out of the benefit (Article 273.2 LGSS). However, the time during which you received the benefit in an ERTE does not count towards the contribution record for a future unemployment benefit.
