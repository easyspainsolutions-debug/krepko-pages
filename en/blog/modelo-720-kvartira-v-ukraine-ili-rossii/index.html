    Modelo 720 by 31 March 2027: a flat and accounts in Ukraine or Russia

    Krepko · 5 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. Modelo 720 for 2026 is filed from 1 January to 31 March 2027 by tax residents of Spain who have more than €50,000 abroad in at least one of the blocks: accounts, securities and insurance, property (Orden HAP/72/2013, art. 7; RD 1065/2007, arts. 42 bis, 42 ter, 54 bis). A flat in Ukraine or Russia is valued not at today's market value but at its acquisition value: a purchased one at the transaction amount plus the costs and taxes of the purchase, an inherited or gifted one at its real value on the date of acquisition, and in ruling V1507-22 the DGT treated free privatisation as an acquisition free of charge. An amount in hryvnias, roubles or dollars is converted into euros at the exchange rate on 31 December of the year for which the return is filed. Rules checked on 5 November 2026.

    You have moved to Spain, but a flat, a share in one or a bank account has stayed behind in Ukraine or Russia. If you were a tax resident of Spain in 2026, the tax office may expect information about these assets in Modelo 720: it is filed when one of the blocks abroad exceeds €50,000, and for 2026 only from 1 January to 31 March 2027. The general rules of the return are set out on the page Modelo 720 — assets abroad, while this article covers housing in Ukraine or Russia: which value of the flat to use and how to prove it.

    Below we look at purchase, inheritance, gift and privatisation, converting the amount from hryvnias, roubles or dollars, flats with several owners and the documents that are best ordered in advance. We checked the rules on 5 November 2026 against the BOE, the explanations of the tax office (AEAT) and a ruling of the Directorate General for Taxes of the Spanish Ministry of Finance (DGT).

    Who files the return for 2026

    Modelo 720 concerns those who were tax residents of Spain in 2026: they spent more than 183 days in the country in the calendar year or have their main centre of business or economic interests here (art. 9 LIRPF). Residence is assessed for the year to which the return relates (AEAT explanation), so if you became a resident in 2026 and the threshold is exceeded, your first return is for 2026. If you were a tax resident before and the threshold was already exceeded then, the first return was due for that earlier year: we work through such cases based on your dates together with a tax specialist. Immigration status, including temporary protection, and tax residence are different things: for a resident the Modelo 720 rules are the same as for everyone.

    The threshold for property is calculated across all properties abroad together: if their total acquisition value exceeds €50,000, all of them go into the return (art. 54 bis.6 RD 1065/2007). Accounts are a separate block with their own threshold. How the blocks work and what happens if you are late is described on the page Modelo 720 — assets abroad. We check whether it concerns you and gather the data, and the return is filed by our tax specialist.

    An inherited flat is declared from the moment you accepted the inheritance, expressly or tacitly (AEAT explanation). In ruling V1507-22 the DGT takes the date of acquisition to be the day of the deceased's death: an accepted inheritance is considered received from that day. How to accept an inheritance in Ukraine without leaving Spain we explained in the article on inheritance in Ukraine.

    Which value of the flat to use

    Modelo 720 records the date and the acquisition value of the flat (art. 54 bis.2 RD 1065/2007), not its current market value. How to determine the acquisition value depends on how the flat came to you.

    How you got the flat What counts as the acquisition value
    Bought it The amount in the contract plus the costs and taxes related to the purchase
    Inherited it The real value of the flat on the date of acquisition — according to the DGT, on the day of the deceased's death
    Received it as a gift The real value of the flat on the date of the gift
    Privatised it Not mentioned in the AEAT explanations; in ruling V1507-22 the DGT applied the rule for acquisitions free of charge

    The rule for a purchase and the rule for an inheritance or a gift are set out directly in the AEAT explanations on Modelo 720. In ruling V1507-22 of 23 June 2022 the DGT derives the acquisition value from articles 35 and 36 of the IRPF law: when an asset is received free of charge, its value is taken under the rules of the inheritance and gift tax, but not above the market value.

    A privatised flat. The AEAT explanations on Modelo 720 do not mention privatisation. In ruling V1507-22 the DGT looked at a flat in Moscow: the owner received a share in it through free privatisation in 1994, and the rest by inheritance. The DGT called the whole acquisition free of charge, and took as the date of acquisition of the privatised share the date it was transferred by the state. The DGT made both findings in the part on the tax on a future sale (IRPF), and for Modelo 720 it referred to the same rules. This is an answer to a specific question, not a general rule, so for your flat we check it against the privatisation documents together with a tax specialist.

    If shares were received at different times and in different ways, for example part through privatisation and part by inheritance, each part has its own date and its own value: in the same ruling, in the same part on the tax on a sale, one flat ended up with three dates and three acquisition values. For Modelo 720 the DGT referred to the same articles 34–36 of the IRPF law, and did not look separately at filing by parts. We do this calculation with a tax specialist before filing, not after the tax office asks questions.

    Amount not in euros: which exchange rate to use

    If the flat was bought for hryvnias, roubles or dollars, the acquisition value is converted into euros not at the rate on the date of purchase but at the rate on 31 December of the year for which the return is filed: for Modelo 720 for 2026, on 31 December 2026. This is what the AEAT explains, and in ruling V1507-22 the DGT specifies that this is the European Central Bank rate (art. 36 of Law 46/1998 on the introduction of the euro).

    For the dollar such a rate exists. For the hryvnia the ECB does not publish a reference rate, and it has suspended publication of the rouble rate: it was last published on 1 March 2022. Which rate to apply to an amount in hryvnias or roubles is decided by our tax specialist when preparing the return, and we show you the result before filing.

    Property has a particular feature. Once the acquisition value has been determined, exchange rate fluctuations in the following years are not taken into account when checking whether the value of the block has grown by more than €20,000 (AEAT explanation). With accounts it is the other way round: the rate is taken into account every year. Balances in foreign currency, including the average balance for the fourth quarter, are converted at the rate on 31 December, and if the account was closed during the year, at the rate on the date of closure. For balances in hryvnias or roubles the question of the rate is the same as for the flat: which rate to apply is decided by our tax specialist when preparing the return.

    A flat with several owners

    If a flat has several owners, the €50,000 threshold is checked against the value of the whole flat, not your share: the return states the full value without dividing it and your percentage (AEAT explanation). If the co-owners received their shares at different times and for different amounts, each one scales the value of their own share up to the whole flat. So it may turn out that one co-owner has to file and another does not.

    If the flat is the spouses' joint property but only one of them is named in the documents, according to the AEAT explanation both resident spouses file: the named owner for 100%, and the other as the real owner (titular real) for 50%. The explanation was given for the Spanish regime of community property (gananciales), so whether it applies to your flat is checked by our tax specialist against the marriage and purchase documents.

    How to prove the value and the date

    Modelo 720 records data about the flat: where it is located, the date and the acquisition value (art. 54 bis.2 RD 1065/2007). Every figure must be backed by a document in case the tax office has questions. What to prepare:

    • a purchased flat — the sale and purchase contract with the amount and date, documents on the costs and taxes of the purchase;
    • an inherited one — the certificate of the right to the inheritance and a document on the value of the flat, for example a valuation report, if one was made when the inheritance was formalised;
    • a gifted one — the deed of gift and a document on the value of the flat on the date of the gift;
    • a privatised one — the document issued at privatisation, with the date the flat was transferred into ownership;
    • any flat — an extract from the state register of rights to real estate of the country where the flat is located, if the right is registered there.

    If you do not have the originals, you do not have to travel to get them. In Ukraine duplicate documents and register extracts are requested by the Krepko team in Ukraine — more in the section Documents from Ukraine. We also obtain documents from Russia without you travelling — more in the section Documents from Russia. Issue times depend on the authority that issues the document, so it is better to order them in advance, not in March.

    When to file again and who does not need to

    Once you have filed Modelo 720, you file it again only if the value of the block has grown by more than €20,000 since the last return (art. 54 bis.7 RD 1065/2007). If a declared flat has been sold, you must file in any case, with the date and amount of the sale (arts. 54 bis.5 and 54 bis.7). If, however, the property block did not previously exceed the threshold and was not included in the return, the sale is not shown in Modelo 720 (AEAT explanation).

    Those who apply the Beckham Law regime (art. 93 LIRPF) do not need to file Modelo 720, as the AEAT explains. But the exemption applies only to the person who applies the regime themselves: family members who do not apply it file under the general rules if they are tax residents of Spain and have assets abroad above the threshold. More on this on the Beckham Law regime page. Cryptocurrency on foreign exchanges is declared separately, in Modelo 721; more on it on the Modelo 720 and 721 page.

    Frequently asked questions

    We privatised a flat in Ukraine and paid nothing for it. Does it have to be counted in Modelo 720?
    Yes, it is part of the property block, just like a purchased one, and for the threshold the acquisition value is used, not today's value. The AEAT explanations contain no separate rule for privatisation. In ruling V1507-22 the DGT treated free privatisation as an acquisition free of charge: the value is taken on the date the flat was transferred into ownership under the rules of the inheritance and gift tax, but not above the market value. This answer concerns a specific case, so we check the value of your flat against the privatisation documents together with a tax specialist.
    I inherited the flat from my mother. What value should I state?
    The real value of the flat on the date of acquisition, not the amount your parents once paid for it (AEAT explanation). The DGT takes the date of acquisition to be the day of the deceased's death (ruling V1507-22), and the flat must be declared from the moment you accepted the inheritance, expressly or tacitly. The value is proved with a document on it, for example a valuation report, if one was made when the inheritance was formalised.
    The flat was bought in 2008 for dollars. Which rate should I use — the one on the date of purchase or today's?
    Neither: the purchase amount together with its costs and taxes is converted into euros at the rate on 31 December of the year for which the return is filed — for Modelo 720 for 2026 that is 31 December 2026 (AEAT explanation). The DGT specifies that the European Central Bank rate is used. Exchange rate fluctuations in the following years are no longer taken into account for the flat when checking whether you need to file again.
    The flat is shared three ways with my parents. Do I count my share or the whole flat?
    The €50,000 threshold is checked against the acquisition value of the whole flat, and the return states the full value and your percentage (AEAT explanation). If the shares were received at different times and for different amounts, each co-owner scales the value of their own share up to the whole flat, so one may need to file and another may not. Only co-owners who are tax residents of Spain file.
    I am on the Beckham Law regime, but my wife is not. Does she have to file Modelo 720?
    The Beckham Law regime exempts from Modelo 720 only the person who applies it. If your wife does not apply the regime, she files under the general rules when she is a tax resident of Spain and has assets abroad above the threshold (AEAT explanation). If the flat in Ukraine or Russia is registered in your wife's name or she owns a share in it, we check her block separately.
    I do not have the documents for the flat with me. Do I need to go to Ukraine?
    Not necessarily. The return records data, not documents, but the value and the date must be backed by papers. Duplicate documents and register extracts in Ukraine are requested by the Krepko team in Ukraine. Timeframes depend on the authority that issues them, so it is better to order them in advance. We also obtain documents from Russia without you travelling.
