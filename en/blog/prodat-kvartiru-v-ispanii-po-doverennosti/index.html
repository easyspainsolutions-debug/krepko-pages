    How to sell a flat in Spain if you are leaving: power of attorney and the non-resident seller's tax

    Krepko · 5 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. The deed of sale will be signed on your behalf by a representative under a notarial power of attorney that names the sale expressly: a power of attorney in general terms only gives the right to manage property (art. 1713 of the Spanish Civil Code). The owner signs it personally: before leaving, at a notary in Spain; from abroad, at a local notary with an apostille and translation, or at a Spanish consulate; if there are two owners, each one grants the powers. If you are a non-resident in the year of sale, the buyer withholds 3% of the price and pays it to the tax office within a month using form 211, and you file Modelo 210 within three months after that month ends, and pay the difference or, after review, receive a refund; a resident with a certificate from the tax office sells without withholding and declares the gain in the annual IRPF return. The return for the plusvalía municipal is filed with the town hall within 30 working days after the transaction. Rules checked on 5 November 2026.

    Your flat is in Spain, and you are leaving or already live in another country. You do not have to fly in for the transaction: your representative can sign the deed of sale before the notary under a power of attorney. You sign the power of attorney itself in person, and its wording determines whether the sale goes through without another trip.

    Below we explain what to include in the power of attorney, where to sign it, before you leave or from abroad, and which taxes the seller pays: the 3% withholding and Modelo 210 if you are a non-resident in the year of sale, or tax through the annual IRPF return if you are a resident, as well as the plusvalía municipal. We checked the rules against the official texts on 5 November 2026.

    Who signs the transaction if you are not in Spain

    The deed of sale (escritura de compraventa) is signed on your behalf by your representative. The power of attorney for this is drawn up before a notary: if the transaction is executed by notarial deed, the authority for it must also be in a public document (point 5 of art. 1280 of the Spanish Civil Code). The notary handling the sale checks the power of attorney and states in the deed that the powers are sufficient (art. 98 of Law 24/2001). If the required action is not in the text, the representative will not sign the transaction.

    The sale must be named in the power of attorney expressly; what can be done under a general power of attorney is covered in a separate article. For a single flat, a specific power of attorney to sell property is more convenient: it describes that particular flat, the price and the payment arrangements. The notary authenticates the power of attorney, while we prepare the text, check the flat's details against the register, agree the wording with the notary and accompany you at the signing.

    No one can sign the power of attorney for you: it is signed by the owner personally, once. While you are in Spain, this is one visit to the notary, about 30 minutes, and after that the representative handles the transaction. That is why it is easier to arrange the power of attorney before you leave, even if there is no buyer yet.

    What to include in a power of attorney to sell

    The representative may not go beyond the limits of the power of attorney (art. 1714 of the Civil Code), so the text lists everything they will need to do:

    • the flat — the address, cadastral reference and Land Registry details; these are taken from the register extract (nota simple);
    • the price — a fixed amount, a minimum threshold or sale only with your consent; a threshold prevents the flat from being sold for less than you expected;
    • the deposit agreement (arras), if there will be one before the deed of sale, and the deed of sale itself;
    • the money — the right to receive payment and the account it is paid into; it is safer to specify your own;
    • the mortgage, if there is one — paying it off from the price and cancelling the entry in the register;
    • the certificate of debts to the owners' association (comunidad de propietarios) — without it the notary will not authenticate the sale unless the buyer expressly releases the seller from it; it must be issued within seven calendar days of the request (art. 9.1.e of Law 49/1960 on Horizontal Property);
    • taxes — paying them, filing returns and receiving refunds on your behalf: for returns and refunds the tax office checks that the representative is authorised to do so (art. 46 of the Ley General Tributaria).

    The deed of sale states the tax identification numbers of the parties and of those they represent (art. 23 of the Notaries Law), so the representative will need your NIE.

    If the flat belongs to two people, for example spouses, each owner grants the powers. Each disposes of their own share (art. 399 of the Civil Code), and the spouses' joint property under the Spanish gananciales regime is sold only with the consent of both (art. 1377). If the marriage took place abroad, the spouses' property is usually governed by foreign law (art. 9.2 of the Civil Code), and the flat is entered in the register taking the spouses' regime into account (art. 92 of the Reglamento Hipotecario). In that case the notary may ask for the other spouse's consent, even if the flat is registered in the name of only one of you. Usually each spouse has their own power of attorney drawn up, on the same day at the same notary; if you live in different countries, each signs where they are.

    The law does not limit the period of validity of a power of attorney: it remains valid until you revoke it, and ends in the other cases listed in art. 1732 of the Civil Code, for example on the death of the grantor or the representative. We usually include a period covering the transaction, from six months to a year, and if there is no buyer yet, with time to spare for the search and the deposit agreement; if the sale has not taken place within that time, a new power of attorney is drawn up. If your plans change, the power of attorney can be revoked before a notary.

    Where to sign: before you leave or from abroad

    Before you leave, at a notary in Spain. The shortest route: the power of attorney is Spanish from the start, and for a transaction in Spain it needs neither an apostille nor a translation. Bring a valid passport or TIE: the document by which the notary establishes your identity must be in force (art. 6 of Real Decreto 304/2014). If your passport has expired, read our separate guide.

    If you do not speak Spanish. A notary who knows your language translates the text themselves. If not, an interpreter of your choice translates, and they also sign the document (art. 150 of the Reglamento Notarial). We prepare the text in the language you need straight away, and at the signing a Krepko representative translates every clause.

    From abroad, at a local notary. The power of attorney is drawn up under the rules of the country where you live and apostilled: Spain, Ukraine and Russia are parties to the 1961 Hague Convention, so consular legalisation is not needed. The transaction will require a translation into Spanish. The sufficiency of the powers is assessed by the Spanish notary who authenticates the sale (art. 98 of Law 24/2001), and for Spanish registers the foreign document must be issued by an authority with functions equivalent to Spanish ones (art. 60 of Law 29/2015). That is why we check the text before the power of attorney is signed, and the apostille and translation before the transaction.

    At a Spanish consulate. Spanish consuls abroad act as notaries (art. 1 of Annex III to the Reglamento Notarial), so such a power of attorney is a Spanish document and needs no apostille.

    By video link. Since 9 November 2023, a Spanish notary may authenticate by video link a power of attorney for specific acts, but not a general one (art. 17 ter of the Notaries Law). You can connect only through electronic identification recognised by Spanish law, such as a digital certificate; this option is agreed with the notary in advance.

    Seller's taxes: resident or non-resident

    Everything depends on your tax residence in the year of sale. A Spanish resident is someone who has spent more than 183 days here in a calendar year or whose main centre of activities or economic interests is in Spain. If your spouse and dependent children under 18 live in Spain, you are considered resident unless proven otherwise (art. 9 of Law 35/2006 on IRPF). Days are counted per calendar year, so if you leave in the middle of the year, it is better to check your status before the transaction: it determines whether there will be withholding.

    Resident in the year of sale Non-resident in the year of sale
    3% withholding from the price No, if you prove your status with a certificate from the tax office The buyer withholds it and pays it within a month using form 211
    Tax return for the gain Annual IRPF return for the year of sale Modelo 210, even if there was no gain
    Rate on the gain IRPF scale for the savings base 19%
    Plusvalía municipal Paid by the seller The buyer pays it to the town hall and may claim the amount from the seller

    If you are a non-resident. The buyer must withhold 3% of the price on account of your tax (art. 25.2 of the IRNR Law, on non-resident income tax) and, within a month of the transaction, file form 211 and pay the withholding to the tax office (art. 14.3 of the IRNR Regulation). They then give you the seller's copy of form 211 (art. 8 of Orden EHA/3316/2010): keep it, as the withholding is credited against your tax on the basis of it.

    You file Modelo 210 within three months after one month has passed since the date of sale, so the deadline falls about four months after the transaction. It is filed even if there was no gain (art. 14.4 of the IRNR Regulation, art. 5 of Orden EHA/3316/2010). The rate on the gain for a non-resident is 19% (art. 25.1.f of the IRNR Law). The 3% withheld is credited against the tax: if it exceeds the tax, the tax office refunds the difference after review; if it is less, you pay the balance. Form 210 returns filed from 1 January 2027 are completed on the updated form, even if the flat was sold earlier; the deadline for a sale has not changed (Orden HAC/623/2026). If the flat was not let, you file one more 210 — for the imputed income from it for the period of ownership in the year of sale (art. 24.5 of the IRNR Law), from 1 April to 31 December of the following year (art. 5 of Orden EHA/3316/2010).

    If you are a resident. The buyer does not withhold 3% if you prove with a certificate from the tax office that you are an IRPF taxpayer (art. 14.2.a of the IRNR Regulation). The gain from the sale is included in the savings base (base del ahorro) of the annual IRPF return for the year of the transaction (art. 46 of Law 35/2006).

    Plusvalía municipal: who pays and by when

    This is a municipal tax on the increase in value of urban land. On a sale it is paid by the seller (art. 106 of the Local Finance Law, TRLRHL). But if the seller is a non-resident individual, the law makes the buyer liable for the tax to the town hall (art. 106.2), and the buyer may claim this amount from the seller (art. 36.3 of the Ley General Tributaria). It is better to set out in the contract how you will settle this between you.

    The return is filed with the town hall within 30 working days of the transaction (art. 110.2 TRLRHL), and the town hall may introduce self-assessment and payment of the tax within the same period (art. 110.4). If the value of the land has not increased during ownership, there is no tax, but to prove this the sale is declared to the town hall with the purchase and sale documents attached (art. 104.5). The notary informs you of the deadline when the deed is signed (art. 110.7). If you are a resident, the return and payment can be entrusted to your representative; if you are a non-resident, the buyer files the return and pays the tax, and your representative, if necessary, declares to the town hall that the value of the land has not increased (art. 104.5).

    Sale proceeds and questions from the bank

    In the deed of sale the notary states the amount, timing and method of payment (art. 24 of the Notaries Law), so if the bank asks where the money came from, the main document is a copy of the deed. A statement showing the money coming in is attached to it, and a non-resident will also find the copy of form 211 and their own 210 useful. What else the bank may request is covered in our article on the source of funds.

    If you are a Russian citizen, the bank additionally checks such transactions for compliance with EU restrictive measures (Council Regulation (EU) No 833/2014). If you do not have citizenship or a residence permit of an EU country, the EEA or Switzerland, a bank in the EU may not accept deposits from you exceeding €100,000 in total across all accounts at one bank, including current accounts (art. 5b), so discuss the account for the payment with the bank before you include it in the power of attorney.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I sell a flat in Spain without coming for the transaction?
    Yes. The deed of sale before the notary will be signed on your behalf by a representative under a notarial power of attorney that names the sale expressly (art. 1713 of the Spanish Civil Code). You sign the power of attorney itself in person, once: before leaving, at a notary in Spain; from abroad, at a local notary with an apostille and translation, or at a Spanish consulate.
    Can I include a minimum price in the power of attorney so that the flat is not sold for less?
    Yes. The text specifies a fixed amount, a minimum threshold or sale only with your consent, and the representative may not go beyond these limits (art. 1714 of the Spanish Civil Code). It also specifies the account into which the buyer transfers the money.
    The flat is registered in my name and my husband's. Do we both need to grant a power of attorney?
    Yes, each owner grants the powers: each disposes of their own share, and the spouses' joint property under the gananciales regime is sold only with the consent of both (arts. 399 and 1377 of the Spanish Civil Code). Usually each spouse has their own power of attorney drawn up, on the same day at the same notary. If you are in different countries, each signs where they live.
    How long is a power of attorney to sell valid, and can it be cancelled?
    The law does not limit the period: the power of attorney remains valid until you revoke it, and ends in the other cases listed in art. 1732 of the Spanish Civil Code, for example on the death of the grantor or the representative. We usually include a period covering the transaction, from six months to a year, and if there is no buyer yet, with time to spare for the search and the deposit agreement; if the sale has not taken place within that time, a new power of attorney is drawn up. A power of attorney can be revoked before a notary.
    We have already left. Where should we sign the power of attorney, and is an apostille needed?
    At a local notary, with an apostille and translation into Spanish; Ukraine and Russia are parties to the 1961 Hague Convention, so consular legalisation is not needed. At a Spanish consulate, the consul acts as a notary, and such a power of attorney needs no apostille. A power of attorney for specific acts can also be signed by video link with a Spanish notary if you have electronic identification recognised by Spanish law, such as a digital certificate. We check the text before the power of attorney is signed, and the apostille and translation before the transaction.
    I do not speak Spanish. How do I sign a power of attorney at the notary's office?
    A notary who knows your language translates the text themselves; if not, an interpreter of your choice translates, and they also sign the document (art. 150 of the Reglamento Notarial). We prepare the text in the language you need straight away, and at the signing a Krepko representative translates every clause.
    I am no longer a Spanish resident. How much will the buyer withhold, and when do I file the 210?
    The buyer withholds 3% of the price and pays it within a month using form 211 (art. 25.2 of the IRNR Law, art. 14.3 of the Regulation). You file Modelo 210 within three months after one month has passed since the date of sale, so the deadline falls about four months after the transaction, even if there was no gain (art. 14.4 of the Regulation). The rate on the gain for a non-resident is 19% (art. 25.1.f of the IRNR Law). If the withholding exceeds the tax, the difference is refunded after review; if it is less, you pay the balance. If the flat was not let, you file one more 210 — for the imputed income from it for the period of ownership in the year of sale (art. 24.5 of the IRNR Law), from 1 April to 31 December of the following year.
