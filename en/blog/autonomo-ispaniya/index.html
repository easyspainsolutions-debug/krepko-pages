    Registering as autónomo in Spain: taxes, contributions and how to work legally

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    Autónomo is the Spanish status of a self-employed person. If you work for yourself in Spain, the law requires you to register with the tax office and Seguridad Social (social security) before you complete your first job. People usually put it off because contributions and tax returns seem murky: it is unclear how much will go out each month and what has to be filed every quarter. Below is the whole route: which forms are filed, which RETA contribution you will pay at your income level, what is paid every quarter and which expenses you can deduct. We handle these registrations and work through disputed cases involving the status, so we write about what we see in practice.

    Who needs autónomo status

    If you regularly provide services to individuals or companies for money, you are required to be registered as autónomo. This covers freelancers, consultants, designers, programmers, tutors, repair workers, drivers and carriers — almost any independent activity. A one-off sale of an old sofa does not count; regular work does.

    If a Spanish company hires you on an employment contract, you do not need autónomo status: the employer does everything. Autónomo is when you are your own employer. If you plan to scale up or work with partners, it is worth comparing autónomo with setting up an SL (sociedad limitada). And for remote workers with foreign clients there is a separate route: the digital nomad visa.

    A point for Ukrainian citizens: to register as autónomo you need a valid permit to work in Spain. Temporary protection gives you this right automatically. If you have a different status, message us before filing the forms: we look at your residence card and tell you whether it allows you to work for yourself. All we need from you is a photo of the document.

    What you need to register

    Registration runs in two parallel steps: the tax office and social security. You cannot skip either: without the second, the first does not work.

    1. Registration with the AEAT (the tax office — Agencia Tributaria)

    • Form 036 — the application to register an economic activity (the simplified form 037 was abolished as of 3 February 2025)
    • You need to choose an IAE heading (epígrafe) — the code for your activity (similar to the OKVED activity code). The code determines the IVA rate, the IRPF withholding on your invoices and the set of quarterly forms, so it is chosen before filing, not after
    • It is filed online through sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es with a digital certificate or through a representative

    2. Registration with Seguridad Social (social security)

    • Form TA.0521 — the application to join the RETA scheme (Régimen Especial de Trabajadores Autónomos)
    • You need to choose a contribution base — it determines your monthly payment, your future pension and your sick-leave benefits
    • It is filed online, either yourself through Import@ss or through a representative: since October 2018, autónomos have been required to deal with Seguridad Social electronically

    You must register before you start the activity (or on the day you start). Late registration carries fines.

    RETA contributions: how much to pay

    Monthly contributions to Seguridad Social are an autónomo's main expense. Since 2023, Spain has had a contribution system linked to actual income. You choose a band (tramo) for your expected net income yourself, and at the end of the year Seguridad Social compares it with your actual income and recalculates the difference.

    Net monthly income RETA contribution (approximate)
    Up to €670 ~€206 / month
    €670 – 1,300 ~€226–300 / month
    €1,300 – 1,700 ~€303 / month
    €1,700 – 2,330 ~€360–401 / month
    Over €3,620 ~€504–607 / month

    The figures are approximate: the exact amount depends on the base you choose and the annual indexation. You are allowed to change band during the year: if your income has dropped, it pays to lower the base straight away rather than wait for the annual recalculation. We work out your band before filing and show you what it will mean as a monthly payment.

    Tarifa plana — the reduced contribution for new autónomos

    If you are registering for the first time and have not been autónomo in the last 2 years (3 years if you have already used the tarifa plana), you are entitled to the tarifa plana — a fixed reduced rate of €80 a month (plus the compulsory MEI contribution, €88.56 in total in 2026) for the first 12 months of activity. Your entitlement can be seen from your history with Seguridad Social: we check it before filing so that the reduction is not lost on the very first payment.

    At the start this is a noticeable saving. When the tarifa plana ends, contributions move to the standard level for your income. Factor this jump into your calculations in advance: it is the thing that most often catches people off guard.

    Autónomo taxes: what to pay and when

    IRPF — personal income tax

    IRPF (Impuesto sobre la Renta de las Personas Físicas) is an autónomo's main tax. It is paid every quarter as advance payments (pagos fraccionados) using Modelo 130.

    It is calculated like this: income minus expenses for the quarter, multiplied by 20% — that is your advance payment. Once a year, by 30 June of the following year, you file the IRPF return (renta), which brings together all your income and expenses for the year. The result may be either an extra payment or a refund — the more carefully you collect invoices for your expenses, the more often you get a refund.

    IVA — value added tax

    Most autónomos are required to issue invoices with IVA (VAT) at 21% (some activities at 10% or 4%). Every quarter you file Modelo 303: IVA charged minus IVA paid to suppliers = the amount payable, and if you paid more than you charged, the difference is carried forward to the following quarters (you can claim the balance back as money in the return for the fourth quarter). The IVA you charge is not your money: you hold it for the treasury, so do not spend it before the quarterly filing.

    Once a year you file Modelo 390, the annual IVA summary return: it does not add any tax, but the tax office notices immediately if it is missing.

    Exception: some activities are exempt from IVA — medical and educational services, financial transactions. If all your clients are companies from other EU countries, the reverse charge applies (inversión del sujeto pasivo): the invoice is issued without IVA, but this work has to be declared to the tax office in advance. We check exemption and the reverse charge at the stage of choosing the heading: changing things later costs more.

    IRPF withholding on invoices (retención)

    If, under your IAE heading, your activity is professional and your clients are Spanish companies, they are required to withhold 15% IRPF (7% for new autónomos — in the year the activity starts and the two following years) directly from your invoices and pay it to the tax office themselves. This is not an extra cost: the tax is withheld at source, and at the end of the year you offset it in your IRPF return. Check that the withholding is stated on the invoice — otherwise there will be nothing to offset.

    An autónomo's real monthly costs

    Item Amount
    RETA contributions (tarifa plana, 1st year) ~€89 (€80 + MEI)
    RETA contributions (standard, income ~€1,500/month) ~€303
    IRPF advance payment (20% of profit) depends on income
    Gestoría (accountant) — not compulsory €50–150 / month
    Professional insurance (optional) €20–80 / month

    What you can deduct from the tax base

    As an autónomo you deduct expenses related to your activity: office rent, mobile phone (partly), internet, equipment, software, transport, professional training. There is one rule: every deduction needs an invoice (factura) with your details; the tax office does not accept an ordinary shop receipt. The invoices you collect are your tax shield.

    Partial deduction for a home office. If you work from home, you can deduct up to 30% of the rent and utility costs in proportion to the working area. The address of the activity and the working share of the floor area are declared at registration — if this is not done, the tax office removes the deduction entirely in an inspection. We put this into the forms straight away so that nothing has to be redone later.

    Filing deadlines

    • April (1–20 April) — Modelo 130 and Modelo 303 for the first quarter
    • July (1–20 July) — Modelo 130 and 303 for the second quarter
    • October (1–20 October) — Modelo 130 and 303 for the third quarter
    • January (1–30 January) — Modelo 130 and 303 for the fourth quarter
    • April–June — the annual IRPF return (renta)

    Missing deadlines costs money: if you file late without a request from the tax office, a surcharge (recargo) is added to the tax — 1% plus another 1% for each full month, and after a year 15% plus interest; for a late return with nothing to pay, a fine of €100 is possible; and if the tax office acts first, interest is added to the tax and a fine starting from 50% of the unpaid amount may be imposed. Put the quarterly filing dates in your reminders straight after registering: the tax office does not warn you about anything in advance.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who in Spain is required to register as autónomo?
    Anyone who regularly provides services to individuals or companies for money: freelancers, consultants, designers, programmers, tutors, tradespeople and drivers. If a Spanish company hires you on an employment contract, you do not need autónomo status.
    How much are monthly RETA contributions in 2026?
    Since 2023, Spain has had a progressive scale of contributions depending on actual income. The minimum base means contributions of around €206–300 a month; the higher the income, the higher the contribution.
    Which forms do you need to file to register as autónomo?
    Form 036, with the choice of an IAE heading, is filed with the tax office (AEAT), and form TA.0521 is filed with Seguridad Social to join the RETA scheme. They must be filed before you start the activity, otherwise fines apply.
    Can a Ukrainian with temporary protection become autónomo?
    Yes, temporary protection automatically gives you the right to work in Spain, including registering as autónomo. We prepare forms 036/TA.0521 and bring in a gestor.
