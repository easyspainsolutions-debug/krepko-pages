    Birth certificate from Ukraine without leaving Spain

    Krepko · 1 October 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. A reissued certificate has the same legal force as the first one. You can get it without leaving Spain in three ways: through your parents (for a birth certificate), through a representative holding a power of attorney, or at the Embassy of Ukraine in Madrid. A document issued in Ukraine is apostilled for Spain and then translated by a sworn translator, with the names checked against the passport and TIE.

    The Spanish civil registry is accepting documents for a marriage, a family reunification package is being put together, a notary is opening an inheritance case — and every list includes a Ukrainian birth or marriage certificate. But the original stayed with your parents in Zaporizhzhia, got lost during the move or was issued in 1983 on a form that is coming apart at the folds.

    A detail few people know: a reissued birth certificate of an adult can be obtained by their mum or dad, without any power of attorney. Another detail: in Ukraine an apostille is not placed on a Soviet original at all, so for Spain there is only one reliable route — a reissued certificate on a modern form. Below we go through the routes from Spain, the separate rules for marriage and divorce, and the points where the document may stumble when you submit it.

    When a Ukrainian certificate is requested in Spain

    The certificate proves kinship or marriage. It is requested in five typical procedures:

    • family reunification — documents proving kinship or marriage with an apostille and a sworn translation (how family reunification works);
    • registering a marriage at the Registro Civil — the bride and groom submit birth certificates, and divorced applicants also a document on the previous marriage (marriage of foreigners in Spain);
    • a child's documents — NIE, TIE, school enrolment: kinship is proven by the child's birth certificate, and the exact set of papers depends on the region and the school;
    • inheritance — a notary opening an intestate succession asks for documents proving the heirs' kinship with the deceased (Ley del Notariado, art. 56);
    • nationality by residence — you need a birth certificate from your country of origin, legalised and translated.
    “No older than three months” is not a general rule. There is no single period in Spain: the Registro Civil of the Valencian Community asks, for a marriage, for a birth certificate issued less than a year ago, while the Ministry of Inclusion information sheet on family reunification sets no period for documents proving kinship. Check the period with your authority before ordering.

    A reissued certificate is not a copy

    A Ukrainian civil registry office (a DRACS office) issues a reissued certificate as a new document: based on the data of the civil status record, on a modern form, marked «Повторно» (Reissued). The Embassy of Ukraine in Spain puts it plainly: it is a document with the same legal force as the certificate issued the first time.

    If the original is lost, stolen, damaged or destroyed, a reissued certificate is issued by any DRACS office in Ukraine, regardless of where the record was made and where you live. The record is checked against the State Register. If there is no record for the stated year, the books for seven years around that date are checked, and if the record has been transferred to a regional archive, DRACS itself requests an archival copy.

    Two details worth knowing in advance:

    • The place of birth is written as in the civil status record. If the town has since been renamed, the reissued certificate will, as a rule, keep the former name — and it may not match what is stated in your passport or TIE.
    • Blank fields are filled in only when you apply at the office, not by post. If the old record has no patronymic or place of birth, they are added at the DRACS office where the record is kept — in person or through a representative. With an application by post, such fields are struck through.

    Who can get the certificate for you

    The Rules for state registration of civil status acts (Order No. 52/5 of the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine) list to whom a reissued certificate is issued:

    • to you — in Ukraine or through the embassy;
    • to your parents — they are issued their child's birth certificate regardless of the child's age, no power of attorney is needed. The exception is a parent deprived of parental rights;
    • to a representative holding a power of attorney certified «відповідно до Закону України «Про нотаріат»» (“in accordance with the Law of Ukraine ‘On Notaries’”);
    • to a lawyer — on the basis of a lawyer's warrant and a copy of the legal aid agreement.

    The rules explicitly allow parents to obtain only their child's birth certificate. You should not count on getting an adult child's marriage certificate through the parents: it is more reliable for the spouses themselves or their representative to apply.

    The application states whose certificate is needed, when and by which authority the record was made, the purpose of the request and the full names of the parents or of both spouses. If you do not remember something exactly, a request is still possible: the record is searched for in the register, not from your copy.

    Post and Diia are not a shortcut from Spain. An application to DRACS can be sent by post, but the finished certificate is forwarded to the DRACS office at the applicant's place of residence in Ukraine, not abroad. The Diia online service for a reissued birth certificate is temporarily unavailable as of 1 October 2026, and you cannot get the document through it by power of attorney.

    Power of attorney before a notary in Spain

    If your parents cannot deal with the document or you need a marriage certificate, the route is as follows: the power of attorney is executed before a notary in Spain, receives an apostille and a translation into Ukrainian, after which the representative submits the application to DRACS and collects the certificate. This is how our power of attorney for acting in Ukraine works: we prepare the text straight away in the language you need, and the application under it is submitted by your representative or the Krepko team in Ukraine.

    An honest caveat. The DRACS rules refer to a power of attorney certified under the Ukrainian law “On Notaries”. In a clarification of April 2025, the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine explicitly names a notary in the country of residence as one way to execute a power of attorney for Ukraine — with an apostille and a translation into Ukrainian. There is no separate clarification specifically for DRACS, and here the route also relies on the Law of Ukraine on private international law: the form and procedure for issuing a power of attorney are governed by the law of the country where it was executed (arts. 31 and 34). That is why we check the text of the power of attorney against DRACS requirements before signing.

    For men aged 18–60: the requirement for a military registration document (part 5 of art. 20 of the Law of Ukraine on the legal regime of martial law; the verification procedure is set by Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 981) applies to consular services. We have not found such a requirement in the DRACS rules on reissuing certificates.

    Embassy of Ukraine in Madrid

    Since February 2023 some of Ukraine's missions abroad have had direct access to the register, and since 15 July 2024 the Embassy of Ukraine in Spain has been among them: it issues reissued certificates of birth, marriage, divorce, change of name and death itself, as well as extracts (vytiahy). Appointments are made through an electronic queue; the consular fee is stated on the embassy's page.

    Bring: the application, your internal passport and passport with copies, a copy of the certificate if you have one, a copy of your tax number (INN), documents on a change of surname. Men aged 18–60 add the electronic military registration document from the Reserve+ app — more in the guide to consular services for men. An exception has been made, in particular, for consular services concerning children if the other parent is a foreign national.

    This route has an open question. A Ukrainian apostille is not placed on an embassy document for the country where the embassy is located. The Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs confirms that documents of foreign consulates benefit from the exemption from legalisation under the 1968 London Convention, to which Ukraine has been a party since April 2023. But Ukraine is not on the latest list we found from the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs of countries whose consulates may issue civil registry certificates for Spain: it is dated 27 January 2022, that is, it was drawn up before the embassy got access to the register. So before submitting, ask your authority whether it will accept a certificate from the embassy.

    Soviet form: why it will not be apostilled

    The Rules for affixing the apostille in Ukraine (a joint order of five ministries of 17 March 2023) explicitly exclude originals of official documents issued by authorities of the former Soviet republics of the USSR. The only exception is documents on education and academic titles of the Ukrainian SSR. A Soviet birth or marriage certificate is not covered by this exception, whatever its condition.

    Even a modern form will not be apostilled if the text cannot be read because of damage, the document contains unauthorised corrections or additions, it is filled in in pencil or raises doubts about its authenticity. The route via a certified copy of a Soviet certificate is described inconsistently in the rules, and it is unknown how a Spanish authority would view such a copy. The reliable option is a reissued certificate of the modern type, which is apostilled in the usual way.

    Separately, about a myth. The 1984 exchange of notes between the USSR and Spain exempts from legalisation civil registry certificates of Spain and Russia only. It does not apply to Ukrainian documents: an apostille is needed.

    Apostille and translation: in what order

    Since 1 February 2026, the apostille of the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine has been issued through the Electronic Register of Apostilles, and since 2 March applications have been accepted by DRACS offices and state notary offices. At any DRACS office you can ask for a reissued certificate and an apostille for it at the same time. The apostille is sewn on as a separate sheet with a QR code.

    Every apostille now also has an electronic version, but Spanish rules are silent on whether it will be accepted without paper. So for Spain we work with a paper document with the apostille sewn on.

    The translation is done after the apostille: the stamp is translated too. An official translation in Spain is a translation by a sworn translator (traductor jurado) from the register of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. A notarised translation made in Ukraine can be legalised, but the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs warns that some Spanish authorities may not accept it. For family reunification and for marriage at the Registro Civil, the official requirements specifically name a sworn translator. How to choose the translation format for your authority is explained on the page sworn translation.

    Names in Latin script — as in the passport and TIE

    In Ukrainian certificates names are written only in Cyrillic; the Latin spelling comes from the translator. In the passport, the spelling is taken by default from the Cabinet of Ministers' 2010 transliteration table, but on request from earlier documents, so your spelling may differ from the “standard” one. We have not found a Spanish rule on how a translator should render Ukrainian names; in practice authorities check the translation against the passport and TIE.

    Before handing the certificate over for translation, collect the spelling for everyone mentioned in it: for a birth certificate — the child and both parents, for a marriage certificate — both spouses, with the pre-marriage surname. The translator renders the place of birth as it is written in the document.

    Marriage and divorce certificates

    With marriage, the routes are the same as with birth, except for two points.

    After a divorce, the marriage certificate is not reissued. Instead, upon a written application you get a marriage extract (vytiah), which confirms the pre-marriage surname.

    There may be no divorce certificate at all. Since 2010, DRACS has registered a divorce only in certain cases: on a joint application of spouses without children or on the application of one of them in cases expressly named in the law. If the marriage was dissolved by a court, a note is entered in the civil status record of the marriage, and the divorce itself is proven by the court decision.

    This matters for a new marriage in Spain. The Registro Civil of the Valencian Community asks divorced applicants for a certificate of the previous marriage with a note of the divorce and does not accept a court decision instead. Which Ukrainian document will play this role has not been officially determined. The closest in content is a full extract of the marriage record with the whole history of changes, but the format should be agreed with the civil registry before ordering.

    What to check before ordering

    1. Ask your authority: a certificate or an extract, whether an apostille is needed, which translation, whether there is a maximum age for the document.
    2. Choose a route: parents, a representative holding a power of attorney, or the embassy.
    3. Collect the details: full name, date and place of registration, full names of the parents or spouses, a photo of the old document.
    4. Do not send a Soviet original for an apostille — order a reissued certificate. If the original is intact, state in the application why you need the document, for example for an apostille: the “any office” rule is formally tied to loss or damage, so the office may refer you to the place where the record is kept.
    5. Translation — after the apostille, by a sworn translator, with names as in the passport and TIE.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is a reissued certificate equivalent to the original?
    Yes. The Embassy of Ukraine in Spain states explicitly: a reissued certificate has the same legal force as the one issued the first time. It comes on a modern form marked «Повторно» (Reissued), and for Spain it is apostilled and translated in the same way as any Ukrainian document.
    My mum lives in Ukraine. Can she get my birth certificate?
    Yes, and without a power of attorney: parents are issued their child's birth certificate regardless of the child's age. She will need her passport and your details for the application. The exception is a parent who has been deprived of parental rights. Do not count on this route for a marriage certificate: the rules name parents only for the birth certificate, so it is more reliable for the spouses themselves or their representative to apply.
    Will an apostille be placed on a Soviet certificate if it is in good condition?
    No. Ukrainian apostille rules do not accept originals of documents issued by authorities of the former Soviet republics, except documents on education of the Ukrainian SSR. The condition of the form does not matter here. The solution is a reissued certificate of the modern type.
    Can I order a reissued certificate from Spain by post?
    You can send the application by post, but the finished certificate will be forwarded to the DRACS office at your place of residence in Ukraine, not to Spain. Someone has to collect it there, have it apostilled and send it on to you.
    I am a man aged 18–60. Will the embassy issue me a certificate?
    The embassy requires men aged 18–60 to submit, together with their documents, the electronic military registration document from the Reserve+ app. If you do not have it, the routes through Ukraine remain: your parents — for a birth certificate — or a representative holding a power of attorney executed before a notary in Spain.
    My divorce was granted by a court, and there is no divorce certificate. What do I submit for a marriage in Spain?
    For a court divorce after 2010, the divorce is proven by the court decision, and a note is entered in the marriage record. The Registro Civil of the Valencian Community does not accept the court decision and asks for a certificate of the previous marriage with a note of the divorce. The closest Ukrainian equivalent is a full extract of the marriage record, but its suitability has not been officially confirmed. Check the format with the civil registry before ordering.
    Must the certificate be no more than three months old?
    There is no general rule. The Registro Civil of the Valencian Community asks, for a marriage, for a birth certificate issued less than a year ago, while the information sheet on family reunification sets no period for documents proving kinship. Individual authorities set their own periods, so ask yours before ordering.
