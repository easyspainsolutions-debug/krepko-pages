    How to transfer money to Spain in 2026

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    Moving to Spain or living between two cities inevitably raises the question: how do you transfer money from Ukraine, Russia or another country to Spain? We go through all the current options — from Wise to bringing in cash — with real fees and hidden pitfalls.

    If you have a Spanish residence permit, the €100,000 limit on accounts of Russian citizens does not apply to you. The EU sanctions regulations explicitly exempt from the limit those who hold a residence permit in an EU country. This is hardly mentioned anywhere, yet a great deal depends on it.

    From Ukraine

    On 11 August 2026, the National Bank of Ukraine (NBU) eased currency restrictions — NBU Board Resolution No. 90 of 10 August. The limits doubled, and something appeared that did not exist before.

    What you are paying forMonthly limit
    Goods, works, servicesabout €4,000 (twice the previous amount)
    Rent and accommodation abroadabout €10,000
    Medical treatmentno limit
    Educationno limit
    Alimonyno quantitative limit

    The limits are set in the national currency, so the amount in euros fluctuates with the exchange rate. Exact figures are on the National Bank of Ukraine website; here we give the order of magnitude so that you have something to compare with.

    Three things people most often trip over:

    • This is not a transfer to yourself. The payment goes to a specific recipient for a specific service — a landlord, a clinic, a school. You cannot send money to your own Spanish account through this channel.
    • Tuition is paid only to the account of the educational institution or of a company it has authorised to accept payment — not to the student's account.
    • Medical treatment — on the basis of the clinic's documents. You need an invoice or a contract; the amount must not exceed the confirmed expenses.

    The limit on cash withdrawals from a foreign currency account has also doubled, from the same date, 11 August.

    What does not work: Wise in the “from Ukraine” direction — you cannot send hryvnias, the service only works for receiving. Revolut has stopped serving residents of Ukraine. You can top up these services from a Ukrainian card only from a foreign currency account and within the monthly NBU limit — about €2,000, shared between transfers to cards abroad and top-ups of e-wallets.

    Is the bank asking you to prove the source of funds?

    Most often it comes down to documents: a contract, an inheritance, a sale of property. Tell us where the money comes from and what it is for, and we will tell you what to prepare. We provide support with the bank.

    From Russia

    Here EU law applies, and it is more complex than it is usually described.

    The €100,000 ceiling. An EU bank may not accept deposits from a Russian citizen if the total balance of their accounts at that bank exceeds €100,000. The rule is Article 5b of EU Council Regulation 833/2014. What counts is the total of all of a person's accounts at one bank, not each account separately.

    And straight away, the exception that makes it worth reading to the end: the restriction does not apply to citizens of EU and EEA countries and Switzerland — or to those who hold a residence permit in these countries. If you have a Spanish TIE (foreigner identity card), the ceiling does not apply to you.

    If you have no residence permit and need a larger amount, an authorisation through the Spanish Treasury is provided for. It is issued for a specific purpose and amount, not just in case.

    What else is prohibited: transactions with banks on the sanctions list (Annex XIV of the regulation), crypto services for citizens and residents of Russia, issuing payment instruments and acquiring.

    What works: a SWIFT transfer from a bank not under sanctions to your Spanish account. The receiving bank will check the source of funds — this is normal, and you prepare for it in advance.

    A word on what people advise online: transferring money via your own account in a third country — Kazakhstan, Armenia, Georgia. The ceiling is based on citizenship, not on the sending country, so this route does not remove it, but it does give the bank more questions. We do not recommend this.

    Cash across the border

    A legal method, but the most demanding in terms of paperwork.

    The threshold is the same everywhere — €10,000. When leaving Ukraine, amounts below this can be taken out without a written declaration; from this amount, only with a full written customs declaration. At the EU external border, amounts of €10,000 or more must be declared.

    A separate detail people learn about too late: Spain requires its own declaration from €10,000 even when arriving from another EU country. If you flew in from Warsaw with cash, declare it.

    Without a declaration, the money is seized: first for 30 days, and the period can be extended to 90.

    And one more thing: in Spain you cannot pay in cash for an amount of €1,000 or more if one of the parties is acting as a business. You will not be able to buy a car from a dealership for cash.

    What a Spanish bank will ask

    With an incoming transfer, the bank looks at the source of funds. The larger the amount, the more detailed the check. Prepare in advance:

    • the underlying document: a sale and purchase agreement, an invoice, a rental agreement;
    • a statement from the sender's account if the money is your own;
    • proof of income if these are savings.

    A transfer is frozen not as a punishment but for the duration of the check. The one drawback is that you have to deal with it in Spanish, with the compliance department, which does not talk to the customer directly.

    This is our part of the work. We explain the source of funds to the bank in its language, gather what it asks for and see the matter through until the transfer is unfrozen. If your transfer is already stuck, write to us and we will look into your situation.

    Common problems with transfers

    The bank asks for the source of funds

    If you are transferring a large amount (usually from €5,000–10,000), the Spanish bank is required by law to check where the money comes from and, as a rule, asks you to prove it. Prepare: bank account statements for 3–6 months, proof of the sale of property or a car, an employment contract, tax returns. Without these documents, the bank may delay crediting the funds or return the transfer.

    SWIFT delays due to sanctions checks

    Transfers from Russia and a number of other countries undergo enhanced compliance checks at correspondent banks. Timeframes can stretch to 2 weeks. If a transfer is stuck, contact the sending bank and request SWIFT GPI tracking to follow its status.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is Wise's fee for a transfer to Spain?
    For EUR-EUR SEPA transfers within Europe, Wise's fee starts from 0.33%. When converting USD or GBP to EUR, it is 0.5–1.5% plus a small fixed fee, always at the real mid-market exchange rate.
    How much cash can you bring into Spain?
    Without a declaration you can bring in up to €10,000 per person when arriving from non-EU countries. An amount of €10,000 or more must be declared at customs on form E-1 (when arriving from another EU country a declaration is also required — on form S-1), otherwise the money may be seized and a fine imposed.
    How much does a SWIFT transfer to Spain cost?
    In total, SWIFT costs €25–80 per transfer: €15–50 sending bank fee, €10–30 intermediary bank, plus a 1–3% margin on the conversion rate. It takes from 1 to 5 business days. It pays off for amounts from €5,000–10,000.
    Can you transfer money from Ukraine to Spain via Wise?
    Only with restrictions: you cannot send hryvnias — Wise only handles them for receiving — and you can top up Wise from a Ukrainian card only from a foreign currency account and within the monthly NBU limit of about €2,000. Many Ukrainians use intermediary accounts in Poland or other EU countries.
