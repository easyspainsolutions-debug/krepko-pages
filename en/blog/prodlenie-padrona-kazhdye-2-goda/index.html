    Renewing your municipal registration every 2 years: who it applies to and what happens if you miss it

    Krepko · 24 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. Foreigners from outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland who have neither long-term residence (larga duración) nor a card as a family member of an EU citizen must renew their padrón entry every two years (art. 16.1 of the Local Government Act, Ley 7/1985). The two years run from the date of registration or of the last express renewal, and renewal is a separate application: it does not happen by itself. If the deadline is missed, the town hall may remove the entry (baja por caducidad) without hearing you first, and the entry ends on the date the decision is served on you. There is no separate rule for temporary protection: its holders renew their registration in the same way as everyone else without larga duración. Checked on 24 November 2026 against the BOE (Ley 7/1985 and the 2020 INE instructions) and the websites of the town halls of Alicante, Valencia and Málaga.

    Foreigners complete their municipal registration (padrón municipal) once, but many then have to confirm it: every two years the town hall expects a statement that you still live at the same address. It does not renew by itself, a letter from the town hall may never arrive, and the town hall is entitled to remove an unrenewed entry without hearing you first — leaving a gap in your residence history.

    Below we go through who has to renew, how the two years are counted, how the town hall removes an entry and what that means for arraigo, citizenship, school and benefits, how renewal works in Alicante, Valencia and Málaga, and what to do if your registration has already been removed. How to register for the first time is covered in our article on the padrón municipal. The rules were checked on 24 November 2026 against the BOE and the town halls' websites.

    Renewal is a single application, but a mistake in it or a missed deadline is costly: a gap in your registration later has to be explained when you apply for arraigo or benefits. We work out the deadline from your certificate and prepare the application in Spanish and your submission — online, or at the town hall office with us accompanying you.     Who needs to renew their registration?

    Article 16.1 of Ley 7/1985 requires the padrón entry of non-EU foreigners without a long-term residence authorisation to be renewed every two years. In the instructions of the National Statistics Institute (INE) for town halls, in force since 2020, this group is called ENCSARP. Renewal is not required for:

    • citizens of the EU, as well as of Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Switzerland;
    • third-country nationals holding a card as a family member of an EU citizen (tarjeta de residencia de régimen comunitario);
    • those who hold a long-term residence authorisation (larga duración).

    All other foreigners from outside the EU renew their registration, whatever their temporary card: studies, work, arraigo, the digital nomad visa, temporary protection — or no status documents at all. There is no separate rule for temporary protection in either the law or the INE instructions: the only thing that matters is whether you hold larga duración or a card as a family member of an EU citizen. Once long-term residence has been granted, renewal is no longer needed.

    The town hall may periodically ask those exempt from renewal to confirm that they still live at the address (section 7 of the instructions), but that is a different procedure, not a renewal.

    How are the two years counted and what counts as a renewal?

    Under point 7 of section 6 of the INE instructions, the two years run from the date of registration in the padrón (alta) or from the date of the last express renewal. The main rules of the same section:

    • Renewal is not a formality. You have to state expressly that you continue to live at the same address and wish to renew the entry; a template for this statement is in Annex IV to the instructions.
    • The town hall is not obliged to warn you. Three months in advance, INE tells the town hall whose deadline is expiring, and the town hall “may” send a reminder — or it may not.
    • Online, you may be asked for proof. If the renewal is filed electronically and the town hall has doubts, it is entitled to request confirmation that you live at the address.
    • A change of address is not always a renewal. The instructions say that when you change your address or personal details you “may be asked” to renew the entry; if you file both applications together, the town hall records them as a single renewal entry.

    The last point is a common trap. The Alicante town hall states plainly that updating your padrón details counts as a renewal, but that is the practice of that particular town hall. In other towns it is safer, when you change address, to state separately in the application that you are renewing the entry, and to keep a copy. Check the date of your registration and of your last renewal with your town hall in advance, rather than relying on memory.

    What is caducidad and how does the town hall remove an entry?

    If the entry has not been renewed within two years, the town hall removes you from the register on expiry (baja por caducidad). The procedure is set out in points 7 and 8 of section 6 of the INE instructions:

    1. The town hall may declare the entry expired without a prior hearing (sin audiencia previa): you are not asked for any objections.
    2. The decision is taken by the mayor or a person delegated by the mayor, by a reasoned resolution that at the same time declares the deadline expired and removes you from the padrón.
    3. The resolution is served under the general rules of administrative procedure (Ley 39/2015). The date of removal is the date of service.

    So do not put off letters from the town hall about your registration, and make sure the letterbox at your registered address has your name on it. The Málaga town hall warns in its renewal leaflet: after removal you lose your time in the padrón from that date, which may get in the way of applying for assistance and many other procedures, and the only way back into the padrón is a new registration — gathering all the documents again, as for your first registration.

    What does a gap mean for arraigo, citizenship, school and benefits?

    Under art. 16.1 of Ley 7/1985, padrón data are proof that you live in the municipality, and certificates issued from it are official documents. That is why removal hits any procedure where it matters how long you have lived in Spain or where you live now. On the page of its “Actualiza tu padrón” campaign, the Alicante town hall states plainly that registration is needed for access to healthcare and education, as well as for arraigo social, family reunification and citizenship.

    Another person can file a renewal on behalf of an adult only if they prove their authority to represent them: under the INE instructions, with a document certified by a Spanish notary, or with a statement made by the person themselves before an official of the authority (art. 5.4 of Ley 39/2015). For minors, the renewal is made by the parents who have custody of the child, or by the legal representatives — do not forget about your children: each family member has their own entry and their own deadline.     What to do if your registration has already been removed?

    The INE instructions do not describe any procedure for retroactively restoring a removed entry. They expressly classify people removed on expiry as cases of a new registration “by omission” (alta por omisión, point 5.1.3): the person lives in the municipality but is not in the padrón, and must themselves ask to be registered. What to do:

    1. Register again as soon as possible. The new entry takes effect from the new date, and the two years until renewal start again from that date. The documents are the same as for your first registration: your passport and a document relating to your home, as explained in our article on the padrón municipal.
    2. Order a historical certificate. It shows all your periods of registration and the gap between them; if you have moved, you get one from each town hall. Which type of certificate is needed for arraigo is explained in our article on the certificado de empadronamiento.
    3. Gather proof for the gap. Rental contract, bills, records of doctor's appointments, school certificates — anything that shows that you were living in Spain in those months too.

    If a removal decision has arrived but you disagree with it (for example, you did file a renewal but the town hall did not take it into account), keep a copy of your application with its registration stamp: it is your main argument when dealing with the town hall.

    Frequently asked questions

    Do I need to renew my padrón if I already have larga duración?
    No. Under art. 16.1 of Ley 7/1985 and the 2020 INE instructions, the entry is renewed every two years only by non-EU foreigners without a long-term residence authorisation and without a card as a family member of an EU citizen. With larga duración you leave this group, but the town hall may periodically ask you to confirm that you live at the address.
    I am under temporary protection. Do I also have to renew my registration every 2 years?
    Yes, if you do not have larga duración or a card as a family member of an EU citizen. There is no separate rule for temporary protection in either Ley 7/1985 or the INE instructions: all nationals of non-EU countries without long-term residence renew. The two years are counted from the date of registration or of the last renewal.
    The town hall has not sent a letter. Does that mean I do not need to renew yet?
    No. Under the INE instructions the town hall may warn you about the deadline but is not obliged to, and an entry that has not been renewed is removed without a prior hearing. Count the deadline yourself: two years from registration in the padrón or from the last express renewal. Alicante does send reminders, but that is the practice of that particular town hall.
    The two years have already passed. Will my registration be cancelled straight away?
    The town hall removes you by a reasoned decision, and the date of removal is the date it was served on you (point 8 of section 6 of the INE instructions). The instructions do not say expressly whether the entry can still be renewed in the period before service, so contact the town hall at once. If the removal has already taken place, what remains is a new registration from a new date.
    I changed address within the same town. Does that count as a renewal?
    Not everywhere. The INE instructions say that when you change address you may be asked to renew the entry, and if both applications are filed together, a single renewal is recorded. The Alicante town hall says that updating your details counts as a renewal, but in other towns it is safer to state expressly in the application that you are renewing the entry.
    Can I renew the registration for a child or for another adult?
    For minors, the renewal is made by the parents who have custody of the child, or by the legal representatives. For another adult — only with proven authority to represent them: a document from a Spanish notary or a statement made by the person themselves before an official of the authority (art. 5.4 of Ley 39/2015). Each family member has their own deadline, so check everyone's dates.
