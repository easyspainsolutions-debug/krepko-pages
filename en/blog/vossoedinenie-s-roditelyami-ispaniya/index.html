    How to bring your parents to Spain: reunification with ascendientes

    Krepko · 24 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. Under the general reunification procedure, parents (your own or your spouse's) can be brought over only by someone who holds a long-term residence permit (larga duración), which usually means after 5 years of legal residence in Spain (Article 18.1 of the Immigration Act, LO 4/2000). The parent must be over 65, must be dependent on you, and there must be reasons why they need to live in Spain; under 65, only on humanitarian grounds (Article 17.1.d). With IPREM at €600, the income must be at least €900 a month for you and one parent and €1,200 for you and both parents. Temporary protection does not by itself give the right to bring parents over under this procedure, but its years count towards the 5 years for larga duración.

    You can bring your mother or father to live with you in Spain through family reunification (reagrupación familiar), but there are more conditions for parents than for a spouse and children: a different status required of you, age and dependency for them, and separate proof that the move is necessary.

    Below we explain which residence permit you need, which parents are admitted and on what conditions, how dependency is proven, what changes under temporary protection and why the parents of a Spanish citizen follow different rules. We checked the rules on 24 November 2026 against the BOE and the Ministry of Inclusion website.

    We handle such cases from start to finish: we check your status and income, gather proof of dependency, obtain your parents' documents with an apostille, arrange the sworn translation and submit the application; more details on the service page “Family reunification in Spain”.

    Which residence permit do you need to bring your parents over?

    A spouse and children can be brought over after a year of living in Spain, once your first permit has been renewed or the renewal application has been filed (Article 68.1 of the Regulation, RD 1155/2024). For parents the law makes a less favourable exception: they are reunited “a partir del momento en que el reagrupante adquiera la residencia de larga duración” (Article 18.1 of LO 4/2000).

    Your status Parents through reunification
    Temporary residence permit, first or renewed (employment, arraigo, non-lucrative residence) No, only a spouse, partner and children
    Application for larga duración filed You can apply, but the permit for your parent will be issued only after your larga duración is approved (Article 68.1.a)
    Larga duración or larga duración-UE issued in Spain Yes
    Temporary protection No; more in the section on temporary protection

    Separate rules apply to those living in Spain under Law 14/2013: investors, entrepreneurs, highly qualified professionals, researchers, intra-company transferees and remote workers (digital nomads). Their dependent parents can apply together with them or later (Articles 61.1 and 62.4 of Law 14/2013); this procedure is not covered here.

    Larga duración is granted after 5 years of legal and continuous residence in Spain (Articles 176.a and 183.1 of the Regulation). Continuity is not broken by absences of up to 6 months at a time, provided that in total they add up to no more than 10 months over the 5 years.

    Which parents can you bring over and on what conditions?

    Article 17.1.d of the Act and Article 66.1.e of the Regulation allow reunification with first-degree ascendants: your parents or those of your spouse or partner (registered or in a stable relationship). Three conditions must be met at once:

    • age over 65;
    • dependency (a cargo): the parent depends on you financially or physically;
    • reasons why they need to live in Spain specifically. The ministry suggests showing the parent's family, social, economic and property situation in their home country and their health.

    A parent under 65 is reunited on an exceptional basis, where there are humanitarian grounds and the other conditions are met. The Regulation lists what counts as humanitarian grounds in Article 196.6:

    • war or the social hardship it causes in the country of origin or residence;
    • a natural or man-made disaster in the same place;
    • the parent has several chronic illnesses or a serious loss of functional or mental capacity;
    • you, your spouse, registered partner, children or persons in your care living in Spain have a recognised degree of dependency (dependencia);
    • the other parent is over 65 and has already been granted a permit, and this parent is their spouse or partner;
    • the parent will care for your minor children or adult children who need support;
    • other cases, with the prior approval of the Directorate-General for Migration Management (Dirección General de Gestión Migratoria).

    Humanitarian grounds only open the way for a parent under 65: dependency, income and housing are checked as usual.

    How do you prove that a parent is dependent on you?

    Under Article 196 of the Regulation, the dependency must be real and lasting, must have arisen in the parent's home country and must exist before the application is filed. If it was created only to obtain the permit, it is not accepted.

    • Money over the last year. The ministry asks for documents showing that during your last year of living in Spain you sent your parent money or paid their expenses. Dependency is presumed if, in the year before the application, the amount for each parent was at least 51% of their country's GDP per capita according to World Bank data (Article 196.3.c).
    • Age over 80 or a serious condition. A parent over 80, as well as a parent with several chronic illnesses or a serious loss of functional or mental capacity, is considered dependent in any case (Article 196.5).
    • Physical dependency. If, for health reasons, the parent cannot look after themselves, has no family support in their home country, and the illness arose before the application (Article 196.4).

    You are checked separately: the family, including the parent, must not receive the minimum living income (ingreso mínimo vital), and your income or savings must allow you to support the parent (Article 196.3.d).

    What income do you need and what else is checked?

    The income threshold for parents is calculated using the same formula as for other relatives: 150% of IPREM for you and one relative and another 50% for each additional one (Article 67.1). In 2026 IPREM is €600 a month: it was set by Budget Act 31/2022, and this is the value given by the public employment service SEPE. For you and one parent that comes to €900 a month, for you and both parents, €1,200. Your spouse's income counts if they live in Spain with you. How to calculate for a larger family and what counts as income is covered in our article on income for family reunification in 2026.

    Besides income, you need a report on suitable housing from the autonomous community or town hall, no more than 6 months old on the date of the application, and health insurance for you and your parents (Articles 67.2 and 67.3).

    How does the procedure work and what are the timeframes?

    1. Application in Spain
    You apply: form EX-02 at the Oficina de Extranjería (the immigration office) in your province or online via Mercurio. The fee is Modelo 790, code 052, item 2.1.2, €10.94. A decision is due within 2 months; if there is no reply, the application is deemed refused.
    2. Visa for the parent
    After approval, the parent has 2 months to apply for a visa in person at the Spanish consulate where they live: with a passport valid for at least 4 months, a criminal record certificate covering the last 5 years, a medical certificate and the originals of documents proving the family relationship. The consulate replies within a month.
    3. Entry and the TIE card
    The visa is collected within a month, the parent enters Spain within a month after that and, within a month of entry, applies for a TIE card at the police (the fee for the first card is €16.08).

    Foreign documents, such as birth certificates and other certificates, are needed with an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish. The apostille is affixed in the country that issued the document, and we arrange the translation in Spain.

    The parent's permit is valid until the date of your larga duración card, but for at least a year, and their next permit will already be long-term (Article 68.9). It does not by itself give the right to work: the law allows reunited spouses and children to work without separate procedures (Article 19.1 of LO 4/2000, Article 65.2 of the Regulation). If the parent obtains a work permit, they can switch to their own residence permit (Article 69.6).

    Can you bring your parents over if you have temporary protection?

    A holder of temporary protection cannot bring parents over through family reunification: for parents the law requires larga duración, and temporary protection is a different status. The European Family Reunification Directive 2003/86 does not apply at all to those living under temporary protection (Article 3.2.b). But your parents have three routes.

    • Extend your protection to them. Under Article 21 of the Temporary Protection Regulation (RD 1325/2003), it can be extended to a parent who lived with you as one family when the events began and was wholly or partly dependent on you. The request is made by the beneficiary of protection in Spain themselves, and the case is handled by the Office of Asylum and Refuge (Oficina de Asilo y Refugio).
    • Obtain protection themselves. Ukrainian citizens who lived in Ukraine before 24 February 2022 fall under EU Council Decision 2022/382 (Article 2.1.a). Protection has been extended until 4 March 2028. From 5 August 2026, new protection is granted only to those who have fulfilled their military obligations in Ukraine, with proof where this is required (Article 2 of Decision 2026/1912, as corrected).
    • Reunification after larga duración. Instruction SEM 2/2026 allows holders of temporary protection to obtain larga duración nacional and counts the years under protection in full, together with time on another residence permit if you have switched to one (sixth instruction). If you received protection in spring 2022 and lived in Spain without long absences, you will reach five years in spring 2027. With larga duración, parents are brought over on the general conditions.

    What changes if you are a Spanish citizen?

    The parents of a Spanish citizen, or of their spouse or partner, apply not for reunification but for a permit for a relative of a Spanish citizen (Article 94.1.e of the Regulation). There is no age requirement of 65 and no five-year wait: the parent must live with you, and you need to prove that they are dependent on you and have no family support in their home country, or that there are humanitarian grounds. The permit is issued for 5 years, and it allows the holder to work (Articles 95.1 and 95.3). Details are in the article on residence for a relative of a Spanish citizen.

    Where is time most often lost?

    • Transfers to the parent started recently. The ministry looks at the last year, and the Regulation requires the dependency to have arisen before the application. It is worth starting to support your parent officially, with transfers in the parent's name, well in advance.
    • Applying before larga duración is approved. The application will be accepted, but the permit will not be issued to the parent until your larga duración is approved.
    • Missed deadlines. A housing report older than 6 months, more than 2 months before applying for the visa, more than a month before the TIE card.

    Frequently asked questions

    I am in the second year of a standard work-based residence permit. Can I already bring my mother to Spain?
    Not through family reunification. The law allows parents to be reunited only from the moment you hold a long-term residence permit (larga duración), and that is granted after 5 years of legal residence (Article 18.1 of LO 4/2000, Article 183.1 of RD 1155/2024). You can apply for your parent once you have already applied for larga duración, but the permit will be issued only after your larga duración has been approved.
    My mother is 62. Will she be refused because she is not yet 65?
    A parent under 65 is reunited only on an exceptional basis, on humanitarian grounds, if the other conditions are met. The Regulation lists such grounds in Article 196.6: among them are war in the country of residence, serious chronic illnesses of the parent, and care for your children, if you can justify it. Dependency and income are still checked.
    Can I bring my parents over if I have temporary protection?
    Not under the general reunification procedure: parents require larga duración. Temporary protection can be extended to a parent under Article 21 of RD 1325/2003 if they lived with you as one family and depended on you when the events began; Ukrainian citizens who lived there before 24 February 2022 can also request protection themselves (from 5 August 2026, new protection is granted only to those who have fulfilled their military obligations in Ukraine). Years under protection count in full towards the 5 years for larga duración nacional (Instruction SEM 2/2026), and with it parents are brought over on the general conditions.
    How much do I need to earn to bring over both parents?
    The threshold is 150% of IPREM for you and one relative and 50% of IPREM for each additional one (Article 67.1 of RD 1155/2024). With IPREM at €600 a month, that is €900 for you and one parent and €1,200 for you and both parents.The income of a spouse who lives with you in Spain counts; you also need a housing report and health insurance for everyone.
    How do I prove that my parents are dependent on me?
    The ministry asks for documents showing that during your last year of living in Spain you sent your parent money or paid their expenses. Dependency is presumed if, over the year, the amount for each parent was at least 51% of their country's GDP per capita according to World Bank data, and parents over 80 or with serious chronic illnesses are considered dependent in any case (Articles 196.3 and 196.5 of RD 1155/2024). The dependency must have arisen before the application is filed.
    I have obtained Spanish nationality. Are parents also brought over only after 65?
    No, the parents of a Spanish citizen follow a different regime: a permit for a relative of a Spanish citizen under Article 94.1.e of RD 1155/2024. There is no 65-year threshold: the parent must live with you, and you need to prove that they are dependent on you and have no family support in their home country, or that there are humanitarian grounds. The permit is issued for 5 years, and it allows the holder to work.
