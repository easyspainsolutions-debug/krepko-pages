    Criminal record certificate: two different certificates that people mix up

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    “I need a criminal record certificate”: this phrase covers two different documents. The Spanish certificado de antecedentes penales shows that you have no criminal record in Spain. The Ukrainian criminal record extract (vytiah) shows that you have no criminal record in Ukraine. One cannot replace the other: the authority that requests them looks at the country.

    Below: how to work out which one you need, how to get each of them without leaving Spain, and where people most often lose time. The guide is equally useful for those submitting documents in Spain and for those who need the certificate for Ukraine.
    How to tell them apart in a minute. Look at where you are submitting it. To a Spanish authority for a residence permit or citizenship: usually the Ukrainian one, because the authority requests information on criminal records in Spain itself. To a Ukrainian authority or for a job in Ukraine: the Ukrainian one. If they ask for one “from your country of residence”: the Spanish one, with an apostille and translation.

    The Spanish certificate: certificado de antecedentes penales

    It is issued by the Ministerio de Justicia (the Ministry of Justice). You request it online through the sede electrónica (the ministry's online portal), in person at an Oficina de Atención al Ciudadano or at the ministry's regional offices. To apply online you need a digital signature, Cl@ve or a certificate; if you do not have them, a digital certificate (firma digital) is obtained separately, and it will come in handy a dozen more times.

    The fee is tasa 006, paid using Modelo 790; the amount is small, around €4. The certificate is most often issued straight away; the administration gives itself up to 10 working days, and for requests that need data from other EU countries, up to 30 calendar days.

    The apostille is requested in the same application. If the certificate is going abroad (to Ukraine, to a consulate, to an employer in another country), you need to select the apostille option in the request form. The electronic apostille is available for download for one year from the date of issue. If you miss this tick box, the apostille will have to be arranged through a separate request.

    A foreign national needs an NIE or a passport to obtain it: identity is verified with them. Residence is not required: the certificate is also issued to those who have simply had dealings with Spanish authorities.

    The Ukrainian certificate: the criminal record extract (vytiah)

    It is issued by the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Ukraine (MVS). You order it online through Diia and the MVS website, and also through a TsNAP (administrative services centre). The electronic extract arrives quickly, but it comes with a catch that trips people up most often.

    Delivery of the paper extract only works within Ukraine. This is stated directly in the terms of the service. A person in Spain will not receive the finished document in hand: it has to be collected in Ukraine and forwarded. On top of that, the apostille on the extract is placed in Ukraine, which means someone has to collect the document there.

    The apostille on the Ukrainian criminal record certificate is placed by the MVS of Ukraine. The timeframe depends on the service option: urgent is faster, standard is slower, and in practice, for criminal record certificates, it is a matter of weeks, not days. Allow plenty of time, especially if your application has a deadline.

    What we do

    We obtain the Ukrainian certificate ourselves: ordering, the MVS apostille, translation and delivery to Spain. You do not fly to Ukraine, look for someone to collect the document or explain to relatives what an apostille is. From you: a copy of your passport and confirmation of the order.

    If a power of attorney for a representative is needed for the request, we arrange it before a Spanish notary: in your language, with a Spanish version in the same deed, followed by an apostille and a notarised translation.

    For the Spanish certificate, we take on the part where people get stuck most often: access to the sede electrónica, filling in the form, selecting the apostille in the application, and a translation that meets the requirements of the receiving authority.

    Translation and legalisation: two directions, two rules

    The direction the document is travelling determines which translation you need. People mix these up more often than anything else.

    A Ukrainian document submitted in Spain. You need an apostille from the MVS of Ukraine and a translation into Spanish. Spanish authorities accept a translation by a sworn translator (traductor jurado) or the combination of “a notarised translation in Ukraine plus an apostille on it”. The second route has the same legal force as the first, and in terms of time and cost it often comes out ahead: there are only a handful of sworn translators from Ukrainian in the whole of Spain, and the wait for them is measured in weeks.

    A Spanish document submitted in Ukraine. You need the apostille of the Spanish Ministry of Justice (the very one requested with a tick box in the application) and a notarised translation into Ukrainian. Both the certificate and the apostille are translated.

    The order of steps matters. First the apostille, then the translation. A translation done before the apostille will have to be redone: the receiving authority will require the stamp to be translated as well. More details in our guide to the apostille and sworn translation.

    How long the certificate is valid

    The certificate itself has no expiry date; what matters is the requirements of the receiving authority. In Spain, the most common requirement is a document no older than 90 days; in some cases up to 180 days is allowed. In Ukraine, a common requirement is 30 days. Always check the exact timeframe in the requirements of the authority you are submitting to: this is not a general rule but that authority's internal regulations.

    The practical takeaway: ordering the certificate in advance “to have it ready” is a bad idea. First gather the other documents, and get the certificate last.

    Common mistakes

    Getting the wrong certificate. A classic: a person who has lived in Spain for three years brings only the Ukrainian one, but the authority wanted the Spanish one because it is interested in the period of residence in the country. Or the other way round.

    Forgetting the apostille in the Spanish application. The certificate arrives without it, and everything starts again.

    Assuming that the electronic extract from Diia solves the problem. For domestic Ukrainian purposes, yes. For submission abroad you need a document that can be apostilled, and the way to it goes through Ukraine.

    Leaving the certificate until the last day. The apostille and translation take weeks, not hours. Especially if the document is coming from Ukraine.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I get the Ukrainian certificate without leaving Spain?
    Yes. The order and the apostille are handled in Ukraine through our team, and the finished document comes to Spain. We need a copy of your passport and confirmation of the order; in some cases, a power of attorney for a representative, which we arrange before a Spanish notary.
    Do I need one certificate or both?
    It depends on the authority. When you apply for a status, many Spanish authorities want a certificate from your country of citizenship, and they request information on criminal records in Spain themselves. Check the requirements of the specific procedure: they are set out there.
    How much does the Spanish certificate cost?
    The fee, tasa 006, is small: around €4, paid using Modelo 790. A translation may also be needed if the document is going abroad.
    Is the electronic certificate from Diia enough to submit in Spain?
    No. For submission abroad you need a document with an apostille, and the apostille is placed in Ukraine on a paper copy. The electronic extract covers domestic Ukrainian purposes.
    Which is faster: a sworn translation in Spain or a notarised translation with an apostille in Ukraine?
    Usually the second. There are only a handful of sworn translators from Ukrainian in Spain, and the wait is measured in weeks. A notarised translation made in Ukraine with an apostille is accepted by Spanish authorities and has equivalent legal force.
    The certificate arrived, but the authority did not accept it. What went wrong?
    The three most common reasons: the wrong type of certificate, the document is older than the permitted period, or the translation was done before the apostille and does not cover the stamp. Send us the refusal and we will see what exactly was wrong.
    Do I need an NIE to request the Spanish certificate?
    Identity is verified with an NIE or a passport. Residence is not required.
