    Certificado de empadronamiento: how to get your padrón extract

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~6 min read
    In short. Your entry in the padrón and the certificate of registration are different things: the certificate (certificado de empadronamiento) is ordered separately at the town hall or online with Cl@ve or a digital certificate. Banks and schools need a certificado bearing the town hall's stamp, while Extranjería checks your registration itself but may ask for one; a volante is a simplified information document and is not accepted everywhere. For arraigo you usually need a histórico showing your full address history, and it is ordered in advance. Checked on 24 October 2026.     Being registered and holding a piece of paper that proves it are not the same thing. Your entry in the padrón sits in the town hall's database, but Extranjería, the bank or the school where you enrol your child may ask you for a certificado de empadronamiento: a stamped extract that you have to order separately. Below we go through the points where people lose the most time: which version of the certificate you need for your purpose, how long it stays valid, and why the historical one for arraigo social should be ordered in advance.

    What a certificado de empadronamiento is

    The padrón municipal is the municipal register of a town's residents. You are entered in it when you register (empadronamiento) at the ayuntamiento, the town hall of the place where you live. But you cannot show anyone an entry in a database: it is a fact, not a document.

    A certificado de empadronamiento is an actual document bearing the ayuntamiento's official stamp, confirming that you are registered at a specific address. This is the document you present to public authorities, banks, schools and other institutions.

    The difference is simple: empadronamiento is the registration itself and your entry in the padrón; the certificado is a separate document that you order for a specific application. The certificate does not appear on its own: you have to go to the ayuntamiento or request it through the online portal. This is where people trip up most often: they arrive at the counter sure that they are registered, with nothing to show for it.

    Why you need a certificado de empadronamiento

    You will be asked for a padrón extract at almost every counter. In the cases we handle, it comes up like this:

    • Enrolling a child in school (colegio público). Spanish state schools allocate places by catchment area. Without a padrón extract linked to the school's catchment area, your child may not be admitted.
    • Getting a Tarjeta Sanitaria. To obtain a health card in the public health system (sistema público de salud), you will need proof of registration at your address.
    • Arraigo social: a residence permit after 2 years of living in Spain. Here you need the historical certificate rather than the ordinary one: the certificado de empadronamiento histórico, confirming continuous residence over two years. The two years are usually proved with this certificate, although the official requirements also allow other documents showing your presence in Spain.
    • Applying for and renewing a residence permit. Under RD 523/2006, Extranjería checks your registration itself, but the office may ask for a recent padrón extract, usually no older than 3 months. An out-of-date extract is one of the most frustrating reasons for an application being turned away right at the counter.
    • Opening a bank account. Banks, especially when the account is being opened by a non-resident, ask for proof of your address. A certificado de empadronamiento is accepted for this. We accompany you through opening an account and check the list of documents with the specific branch in advance.
    • Claiming social benefits. For the Ingreso Mínimo Vital (IMV) and regional subsidies you will need proof of registration, while for the ERTE unemployment benefit no certificate of registration is required.
    • Registering a car. When a vehicle is registered (matriculación) with the DGT, the owner's registered address must be supported by documents. Ukrainian driving licences are also exchanged for Spanish ones through the same DGT.
    • Notarial acts. For property transactions, powers of attorney and other procedures, the notary may ask for a recent padrón extract. We book notary appointments and check how old your extract is before the day of signing.
    • Other Seguridad Social payments. To confirm your place of permanent residence when applying for benefits and payments; for the unemployment subsidy (subsidio de desempleo), SEPE does not require a certificate of registration.

    Types of certificado de empadronamiento

    There is no single “padrón certificate”: each purpose has its own version. Order the wrong one and your application is pushed back by another trip to the town hall, so we check the format with the receiving authority before you go to get the document.

    Certificado de empadronamiento (standard)

    The most common version. It confirms that you are registered at a specific address as of today. Banks, schools and medical centres require it, and Extranjería may ask for it with ordinary applications. It is issued with the ayuntamiento's official stamp.

    Certificado de empadronamiento histórico (historical)

    It shows the full history of your registered addresses in that municipality, with the dates you registered at and left each address. This document is critically important when applying for arraigo social: it proves continuous residence in Spain for two years. It may also be required for certain other administrative procedures that involve proving long-term residence.

    It is ordered in the same place as the ordinary one, at the ayuntamiento or through the online portal, but your request must state clearly that you need the histórico. In large cities processing takes several days, and sometimes weeks.

    Volante de empadronamiento (volante)

    A simplified version without the official stamp: in some ayuntamientos it is just a printout or a certificate in the form of a letter. It is not accepted everywhere: some authorities expect a certificado with the official seal. Ask about the format in advance, or you will have to go a second time.

    Some ayuntamientos (especially in smaller towns) issue a volante on the spot on request, whereas a stamped certificate sometimes involves a wait. In Barcelona and Madrid the online portal lets you print a digitally signed volante yourself, and it has the same legal force as the paper version.

    Certificado colectivo (collective)

    A single document covering everyone registered at the same address. You need it when you have to show who lives in the household: family benefits, family reunification, school benefits. It is ordered like the standard one, except that your request specifies “certificado colectivo”. In family reunification cases we take this certificate on straight away, because it is asked for almost every time.

    Where and how to get a padrón extract

    In person at the ayuntamiento

    The most reliable way is to go to the town hall yourself. Most ayuntamientos have a padrón office (oficina del padrón) or a counter in the general citizen services office (oficina de atención ciudadana). The procedure is as follows:

    1. Book an appointment in advance on the ayuntamiento's website or take a ticket number (turno) on site, depending on the town.
    2. Bring an identity document: NIE, TIE, passport or temporary protection document.
    3. At the counter, say which version you need: estándar, histórico or colectivo. By default you will be given the standard one.
    4. The certificate is issued free of charge: most ayuntamientos in Spain do not charge for this service.

    In small towns and villages the certificate is often printed while you wait. In Barcelona, Madrid and other large cities it is harder: the next available appointment can be several weeks away. We look out for these slots and book the appointment, and if needed we go to the town hall with you, so you will not have to explain yourself in Spanish.

    Online through the ayuntamiento's portal

    Many large cities issue the certificate electronically, with no visit and no queue. This works if you have a digital certificate (certificado digital), Cl@ve PIN or an electronic DNI (DNI electrónico).

    • Barcelona: ajuntament.barcelona.cat → “Tràmits” → “Certificat d'empadronament”. Available with Cl@ve, certificado digital or idCAT Mòbil.
    • Madrid: sede.madrid.es → search for “certificado de empadronamiento”. You need to authenticate with Cl@ve or a certificado digital.
    • Valencia: valencia.es / sede electrónica. A similar process with identification.
    • Other towns: look for the “Sede electrónica” section on your ayuntamiento's website; most towns with a population of 20,000 or more offer this.

    After your request, the portal either gives you a digitally signed PDF straight away or emails it to you within a few hours. Such a file is legally equivalent to the stamped paper version: there is no need to print it or have it certified separately.

    Barcelona online: if you have Cl@ve PIN or idCAT Mòbil, you can request a certificado de empadronamiento on ajuntament.barcelona.cat at any time, without visiting an office. A PDF digitally signed by the ayuntamiento is accepted by all institutions on a par with the paper version. For the historical certificate, a personal visit or extra processing time may be required.

    Through the sede electrónica with a digital ID

    The Spanish DNI works as an electronic key on government portals (the TIE does not have this function). You will need a card reader or a mobile app. This is the most technical method, but it opens up the full range of online public services with no queues.

    Cl@ve is simpler. With a DNI or a digital certificate, registration takes a few minutes (with an NIE and no digital certificate it takes longer: the invitation letter arrives by post), after which you can log in to most government portals with an SMS code or PIN. If you would rather not deal with this, we take care of getting your digital certificate as a separate service, first checking which identification method is available with your document.

    How long a certificado de empadronamiento is valid

    This is where most people slip up. A certificado de empadronamiento is usually accepted for 3 months from the date of issue. After that it is considered out of date, and most institutions will simply not accept it.

    So there is no point in getting one “just in case”. A working rule: order the extract no earlier than 2–3 weeks before the day of your application. That leaves a margin, and it will not have time to expire.

    With Extranjería this is critical: appointment dates are set weeks ahead, and an extract obtained too early is already dead by that day. We count the three-month window back from your appointment date and tell you on which day to go for the certificate so that it is accepted with your application.

    For arraigo social the situation is slightly different: the historical certificate records past periods of registration, and they do not change over time. But even this one is usually accepted only within the same three months from issue.

    Histórico: the historical certificate for arraigo social

    This certificate works differently from the others. It shows not your current address but the full chronology of your registrations in that municipality: when you first registered, when you changed address within the town, when you deregistered. For arraigo social, it is this certificate that proves your years of living in Spain.

    For arraigo social you need to prove continuous residence in Spain for at least two years. If you have lived in the same town the whole time, one histórico from your ayuntamiento is enough. If you have moved, you will have to get a certificate from each municipality and fit the dates together so that there are no gaps in the two-year period. We put this together ourselves: we request extracts for all your addresses and check that the dates join up before the case goes to Extranjería.

    The catch is the timing: in Madrid and Barcelona a histórico takes anywhere from one to several weeks, because the ayuntamientos are overloaded and the issue date is set in advance. If you are aiming for a specific Extranjería appointment, you need to order the histórico at least a month before your application. In arraigo cases we start with exactly this: it is the slowest link, and everything else is planned around it.

    Some ayuntamientos do not issue the histórico online at all, only in person. Check on your municipality's website.

    Common problems and how to solve them

    You are not registered in the padrón

    Until you have completed empadronamiento, there will be no certificate: you are not in the database. So you have to start with registration: the ayuntamiento, an identity document and proof of address (a rental contract, a carta de invitación from the owner of the property or another document). You can request the certificate immediately after registering; there is no need to wait. We handle the whole registration: we put together the set of documents your town hall requires, book the appointment and accompany you to it.

    You have moved but not re-registered

    This happens a lot. You changed address and did not update the padrón, so the certificate will show the old address, yet it must match where you actually live. There is only one solution: go to the ayuntamiento of your new town (or new district, if you moved within the same town) and re-register. It takes about as long as the initial registration and is also free.

    The online system is not working or you do not have Cl@ve

    Ayuntamiento portals go down, get updated and are taken offline for maintenance. If the online request does not go through, go in person: that route always works. Without a digital certificate or Cl@ve, a personal visit is usually the only option, but in Madrid, for example, you can also order the certificate without them by calling 010. Registering for Cl@ve itself takes a few minutes with a DNI on clave.gob.es or through the app, and longer with an NIE and no digital certificate: the invitation letter arrives by post.

    The document is rejected: “it needs a stamp”

    Sometimes a volante is turned down and a certificado with the official seal is demanded. If all you have is a volante or a printout from your online account without a QR verification code, order the official certificate again, stating the format you need. A PDF digitally signed by the ayuntamiento is accepted on a par with the paper version in most places.

    You need the certificate urgently, but the next appointment is two weeks away

    In large cities this is normal. The options: 1) The online portal: there is usually no queue, and the PDF arrives straight away or the same day. 2) Walk-in queues without an appointment (turno presencial sin cita): some ayuntamientos have them, and you need to be at the entrance in the morning. 3) OAC offices in Barcelona (Oficina d'Atenció al Ciutadà): some of them deal with certain matters without an appointment. If it is urgent and none of these options work, message us on WhatsApp: we will look for a free appointment slot and tell you which route is quicker in your town.

    Quick cheat sheet: what to remember

    • Certificado: an official document with a stamp; volante: a simplified certificate without a stamp (not accepted everywhere).
    • A histórico is usually needed for arraigo social and other cases where your registration history matters.
    • A collective certificate is suitable if you need to show the members of your household at one address.
    • Usually accepted for 3 months from the date of issue. Do not get one too early.
    • Issued by the ayuntamiento where you are registered, usually free of charge.
    • Available online if you have Cl@ve, a certificado digital or an electronic DNI.
    • If you have moved, first update the padrón, then request the certificate.
    • For arraigo social, request the histórico well in advance: in Madrid and Barcelona the wait can be several weeks.

    If you would rather not spend weeks on this, we handle municipal registration in Spain: the appointment, the set of documents and support at the appointment. We will review your situation, tell you which version of the certificate your application needs, book your town hall appointment and prepare the set of documents your town requires. All we need from you is to stay in touch by message and make one visit; we discuss the cost once we understand the scope of the work.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between empadronamiento and a certificado de empadronamiento?
    Empadronamiento is the fact of being registered in the municipal register. A certificado de empadronamiento is a separate official extract bearing the ayuntamiento's stamp, which you have to request specifically for use at banks, schools and Extranjería.
    How long is a certificado de empadronamiento valid?
    Spanish public authorities accept the standard certificate as current for 3 months from the date of issue. After that, an updated extract is usually required.
    What is a certificado histórico de empadronamiento?
    It is a historical certificate confirming continuous residence at an address over several years. For arraigo social it is usually used to prove 2 years of living in Spain, although the official requirements also allow other documents showing your presence.
    How do I get a certificado de empadronamiento online?
    In most municipalities you can request the certificate on the ayuntamiento's website with a certificado digital or Cl@ve. The document arrives as an electronically signed PDF, or you can collect it at the town hall.

    Tell us what you have been asked for and where you are applying, and we will tell you which certificate you need and how quickly you can get it. We work in Barcelona, Madrid, Alicante, Valencia, Málaga and Murcia.
