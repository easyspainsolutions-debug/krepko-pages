    IRPF and taxes in Spain 2026: who pays, how much and when

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. The Modelo 100 return for 2025 is filed from 8 April to 30 June 2026 through Renta WEB with an electronic signature or Cl@ve. Checked on 24 October 2026 against the Agencia Tributaria.     When taxes in Spain become compulsory

    In Spain, taxes are paid by everyone who receives income from work, a business, renting out property or investments. The difference is on which part of that income: it all depends on your tax status, and that is determined not by what you want but by facts — the days you spend in the country and where your family lives.

    A tax resident of Spain reports to the Spanish tax office on all of their income — including salary, accounts and rent outside the country. A non-resident pays only on Spanish income. The difference between these two statuses runs to tens of thousands of euros, so we check the status before a single line of the return is filled in.

    The 183-day rule and the centre of interests

    You are automatically considered a resident if at least one condition is met:

    • You spend more than 183 days in the country in a calendar year (not necessarily in a row)
    • Your main centre of economic interests is in Spain — work, business, main income or assets
    • Your spouse and minor children live permanently in Spain (unless you prove otherwise)

    If you came to Spain under temporary protection in 2022–2024, you most likely became a resident as early as your first year — which means you also need to declare income that remained outside Spain. We review such cases document by document: we count the days, look at the double taxation agreement and tell you what to report and what not to.

    IRPF tax rates 2026: the progressive scale

    The scale is progressive, but the higher rate is applied not to all of your income — only to the part of it that falls into the next band. This is the misunderstanding people bring to us most often: they have crossed a threshold and expect their whole salary to be recalculated. Only the excess is recalculated.

    The 2026 scale — state and regional parts together, approximate:

    • €0–12,450 a year: 19%
    • €12,450–20,200: 24%
    • €20,200–35,200: 30%
    • €35,200–60,000: 37%
    • €60,000–300,000: 45%
    • Over €300,000: 47%

    The bands of the state scale have not changed since 2021, and the autonomous communities add their own part to it — the final rate differs from region to region. Current figures are published by the Agencia Tributaria, the Spanish tax office; we check them for your region before filing.

    What is subject to IRPF

    Salary and employment

    Your employer withholds IRPF itself — you receive the amount already net of tax. But the withholding almost never matches your actual liability: at the end of the year the tax office either refunds the overpayment or demands an additional payment. If you work for yourself, you calculate and pay IRPF yourself: details in the guides on registering as autónomo, setting up an SL (sociedad limitada) and the digital nomad visa.

    Rental income

    If you rent out a flat, house or room in Spain, that is taxable income. It is not the whole amount received that is taxed, but the difference after expenses: repairs, insurance, property taxes. Expenses must be backed by documents, so collect receipts throughout the year, not in June just before filing.

    Selling property

    Profit from selling a flat is taxed in the year of the sale. The base is the difference between the sale price and the purchase price, and you can include in it the costs of the purchase itself: the notary, registration, the tax on acquisition. The tax office may ask for the supporting documents in the original — if they stayed in another country, we request them and have them legalised.

    Foreign income

    For a tax resident, income from all countries counts: salary from a foreign employer, interest on accounts abroad, dividends. Spain sees these accounts through the automatic exchange of banking information, so counting on “no one will find out about them” does not work. Double taxation usually does not arise — tax paid abroad is credited under the double taxation agreement.

    Not sure whether you count as a resident?

    Both the rate and whether you have to file at all depend on it. Tell us how many days a year you spend in Spain and where your income comes from — we will look into it. We handle preparing your IRPF return.

    Modelo 100: how to file your IRPF return

    Modelo 100 is the main form of the annual IRPF return. Filing it is compulsory if at least one of the following conditions applies:

    • Annual income above €22,000 from one employer; with several payers the threshold drops to €15,876 if all of them except the one who paid the most together paid more than €1,500 over the year
    • Income comes from abroad; for a foreign salary and pension the threshold is usually €15,876
    • You are claiming tax deductions

    Filing deadlines

    The return for 2025 is filed in April–June 2026. The exact dates are announced by the Agencia Tributaria every year, but the window is always the same:

    • Filing online: April–June
    • Filing in person at the tax office: May–June

    How to file through Renta WEB

    The easiest way to file the return is through the Renta WEB portal on agenciatributaria.es. You log in with an electronic signature, Cl@ve or a reference number (número de referencia) — if you have none of these yet, you need to start with them; we take care of getting the signature:

    1. Log in with an electronic signature (DNIe) or Cl@ve
    2. The system automatically loads data from employers and banks
    3. Check the information and enter your deductions
    4. Submit the return

    If there are no discrepancies, the return is accepted within a few days. The difficulty lies elsewhere: Renta WEB only loads what the Spanish system can see. You enter foreign income, rent and deductions by hand — and that is exactly where the mistakes that are later penalised happen.

    Tax deductions: saving on IRPF

    Deduction for a home loan (mortgage)

    Mortgages on a main home bought before 2013 give the right to deduct part of the payments, if the deduction for that home was already being applied before 2013. For homes bought later there is no such deduction — check the date in the purchase contract before counting on it.

    Deduction for dependants

    Children and other dependants increase the tax-free allowance: €2,400 for the first child, more for subsequent ones. The tax office may ask you to prove the relationship with documents — a birth certificate with an apostille and a sworn translation. We arrange these documents separately if all you have is the Ukrainian original.

    Deduction for charitable donations

    Donations to charity reduce the amount of tax. You need a certificate from the foundation: without proof, the deduction is removed at an inspection and the difference is charged.

    Regional deductions

    Each autonomous community gives its own deductions on top of the state ones — for example, part of the cost of renting a home or of nursery school. The list depends on the region where you lived in the tax year, and it is not pulled into the return automatically: you need to know it and enter it by hand.

    Modelo 720: declaring assets abroad

    You will have to report if outside Spain you have:

    • Bank accounts over €50,000
    • Property worth over €50,000
    • Shares and bonds worth over €50,000 (cryptocurrency on foreign exchanges and custody services is declared separately, on Modelo 721, with the same threshold)

    Modelo 720 is filed by the end of March each year. It is a report on foreign assets, not a tax: there is nothing to pay on it, but the penalty for not filing is €20 for each piece of data or set of data, from €300 to €20,000 per block. The first time, the form is filled in completely; after that — only if a block has grown by more than €20,000, another block has exceeded €50,000 for the first time, or you have sold an asset or closed an account that you had already declared.

    Ley Beckham (the Beckham Law regime) — a regime for specialists who move to Spain: if you moved to Spain to work and were not its tax resident for the previous five years, you can pay a flat 24% on Spanish income for 6 years instead of the progressive scale. The conditions are strict, and the application is filed within a limited period after starting work — miss it and the regime is lost. If you are not sure whether your case qualifies, message us on WhatsApp: we will look at it before filing.

    Points to note for Ukrainians with temporary protection

    Temporary protection does not in itself determine your tax status — it is determined above all by how many days in the year you spent in Spain (short trips abroad count as days in Spain unless you prove tax residence in another country):

    • You spend more than 183 days in Spain → tax resident of Spain
    • Fewer than 183 days → non-resident, if the centre of your economic interests is not in Spain and your spouse and minor children do not live here (you pay tax only on Spanish income)

    There is no special tax regime for recipients of temporary protection — the rules are the same as for all residents. The difficulty usually lies elsewhere: income, accounts or property remained in Ukraine, and it is unclear which of these to report. We review such situations document by document and tell you plainly what to declare and what not to.

    What happens if you do not file a return

    Penalties for not filing IRPF:

    • 50% of the unpaid tax — if up to €3,000 was unpaid or the income was not concealed
    • 50–100% — if more than €3,000 was unpaid and the income was concealed: an unfiled return also counts as concealment; the percentage is higher for a repeat offence within 4 years
    • 100–150% — if fraudulent means were used, for example nominees (front men) or forged documents

    For filing after the deadline a recargo is charged — a surcharge that grows with each full month of delay. If the return shows an amount to pay and you filed it yourself, even if late, you pay the surcharge, not a penalty: a penalty is imposed when the return has been demanded by the tax office, and it is larger. So it is better to file an overdue return than not to file at all.

    A practical checklist for you

    • Count your days in Spain over the calendar year — they determine whether you are a resident or not
    • Gather the documents: employment contracts, statements for accounts in Spain and abroad, property papers
    • Get an NIE (foreigner identity number) — without it the return will not be accepted; we will arrange it if you do not have the number yet
    • Get your municipal registration on the padrón (empadronamiento) — regional deductions depend on your registration
    • Prepare the data a month before the deadline — at the end of the campaign there is no time left to correct mistakes
    • If you are unsure about your status or what your income consists of, message us before filing, not after a letter from the tax office

    Frequently asked questions

    When do I become a tax resident of Spain?
    A tax resident is someone who has spent more than 183 days in Spain in a calendar year or whose main centre of economic interests is here (work, business, main income). If your spouse and minor children live in Spain, you are also considered a resident unless you prove otherwise. Residents pay IRPF on their worldwide income.
    Which IRPF rates apply in 2026?
    The scale is progressive; together with the regional part, approximately: 19% on income up to €12,450; 24% up to €20,200; 30% up to €35,200; 37% up to €60,000; 45% up to €300,000; and 47% over €300,000 a year. The exact bands should be checked on the Agencia Tributaria website.
    When do I file my IRPF return in Spain?
    The Renta campaign usually runs from April to the end of June each year. The return is filed on Modelo 100 in your Agencia Tributaria online account — with a certificado digital (digital certificate), Cl@ve or through a gestor.
    Does a non-resident have to pay IRPF in Spain?
    A non-resident pays tax only on income from Spanish sources: renting out property in Spain, selling Spanish property, work performed in Spain. A separate form, Modelo 210, is used.
