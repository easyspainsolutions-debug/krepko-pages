    Ready-made SL (sociedad preconstituida): what to check before signing at the notary

    Krepko · 24 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. SL participations are transferred only by a public document, in practice before a notary (art. 106 of the Capital Companies Act, LSC), and a sale to an outside buyer, as a general rule, requires the consent of the general meeting (art. 107). Before signing, buyers ask for certificates showing no debts with the AEAT and the Seguridad Social and an extract from the Registro Mercantil, and check whether the annual accounts have been filed. The tax office may revoke the company's NIF if it has not started activity within 3 months of requesting the NIF or has not filed its annual accounts with the Registro Mercantil for 4 consecutive years, and a notary will not authorise documents on behalf of a company whose NIF has been revoked. There is no such thing as an anonymous purchase: an owner of more than 25% is the beneficial owner (titular real) under Law 10/2010. The appointment of a new director is submitted for entry in the register within 10 days of their accepting the position (art. 215 LSC).

    A ready-made SL (sociedad preconstituida) is a company that was registered in advance and has not carried on any activity, so that it can later be handed over to a new owner. The buyer gets an already existing legal entity: with a tax number (NIF), an entry in the commercial register (Registro Mercantil) and the whole history it has built up since the date of incorporation.

    Below is what to check before signing at the notary: how participations (participaciones) are transferred by law, which no-debt certificates to ask the seller for, what changes regarding the beneficial owner and the director (administrador), and why the buyer needs an NIE (foreigner identity number). The rules were checked on 24 November 2026 against the texts of the laws in the BOE (the Official State Gazette) and the websites of the tax office (AEAT) and the Seguridad Social (the Spanish social security system).

    What is a ready-made SL and what exactly are you buying?

    “Sociedad preconstituida” is not a legal term but a market name. Legally, you are not buying a “firm” but the participations (participaciones sociales) of an existing SL from its member. The company stays the same: with the same NIF, date of incorporation, articles of association and obligations. SL members are not personally liable for the company's debts (art. 1.2 LSC), but the debts it already has stay with it — and become the new owner's concern.

    After a purchase, the name, the line of business (objeto social) or the address are usually changed. Each such change is an amendment to the articles of association: it is adopted by the general meeting, formalised before a notary and entered in the Registro Mercantil (arts. 285 and 290 LSC). The director can also change the address within Spain on their own, unless the articles of association prohibit this (art. 285.2). That is why a ready-made company “for your business” almost always means one more visit to the notary.

    How are participations transferred and whose consent is needed?

    The procedure is set by the company's articles of association and the Capital Companies Act (Real Decreto Legislativo 1/2010, LSC):

    • Form. The transfer of participations must be formalised in a public document (art. 106.1). The buyer acquires the member's rights vis-à-vis the company from the moment the company learns of the transfer (art. 106.2).
    • Who can be sold to freely. Unless the articles of association say otherwise, participations are transferred without restrictions to other members, to the member's spouse, parents and children, and to companies in the member's group (art. 107.1).
    • To an outside buyer. If the articles of association are silent, the seller notifies the directors in writing of the number of participations, the buyer, the price and the terms, and the general meeting gives its consent by ordinary majority. The company can refuse only by naming, through a notary, other buyers for all the participations; the members who attended the meeting buy first (art. 107.2). If the company has not named buyers within three months, the seller is entitled to sell on the stated terms (art. 107.2.f).
    • Ban on sale in the first years. The articles of association may prohibit the transfer of participations for a period of up to 5 years from the date of incorporation (art. 108.4). For a company recently set up “for sale”, this is the first thing to look for in the articles of association.

    The company keeps a register of members (Libro registro de socios), and it regards as a member only someone who is recorded in it (art. 104). The seller is entitled to obtain an extract on their participations (art. 105.2) — it is checked against the details in the deed of incorporation.

    If you buy all the participations, the SL will have a single member (sociedad unipersonal). This, like a change of the sole member, is formalised before a notary and entered in the register (art. 13). If the company has become single-member and this has not been entered in the register within 6 months, the sole member is personally liable for the debts incurred during that time (art. 14).

    How do you check for debts and a “dormant” company?

    No-debt certificates are issued to the company itself. The AEAT issues such a certificate to a third party only if the law provides for this or with the company's prior consent, so the seller obtains them and hands them over to you before the transaction.

    Document What it shows Timeframe under the rules
    Certificado de estar al corriente de obligaciones tributarias (AEAT) The company is on the tax register, has filed the mandatory returns and has no debts in the enforced collection stage (art. 74 RD 1065/2007) Issued within 20 days; for periodic obligations it is valid for 12 months if circumstances have not changed (arts. 73 and 75)
    Certificado de estar al corriente con la Seguridad Social Whether there are debts for contributions; if there are, a separate report with the total amount and a list of unpaid documents Requested at the Seguridad Social electronic office (sede) on behalf of the company or through a registered representative (apoderado inscrito)
    Certificación or nota simple from the Registro Mercantil Incorporation details, articles of association, directors, entries on the sole member Certificación — 5 days, nota simple — 3 days (arts. 77–78 of the Commercial Register Regulation); only the certificación officially attests the content of the register

    Separately, check whether the annual accounts (cuentas anuales) have been filed. They are filed with the Registro Mercantil within a month of approval (art. 279 LSC). If the accounts have not been filed a year after the end of the financial year, the register is closed to the company: it does not enter any documents except the departure of a director and a few other exceptions (art. 282 LSC, art. 378 of the Commercial Register Regulation). A new director will not be registered until the accounts have been filed.

    Three traps of a “dormant” company. 1) The tax office may revoke the NIF if the company has not started activity and preparatory acts within 3 months of requesting the number or has not filed its annual accounts with the Registro Mercantil for 4 consecutive years (art. 147.1 c and f RD 1065/2007); the revocation is published in the BOE. Before signing, the notary checks the list of revoked NIFs and does not authorise a document on behalf of such a company (art. 23 of the Notaries Act). 2) The law treats inactivity for more than a year as a ground for dissolving the company (disolución, art. 363.1.a LSC). 3) A new director, if such a ground already exists, must call a meeting within 2 months of accepting the position, otherwise they are personally, jointly and severally liable for the company's debts incurred after accepting the position (art. 367 LSC).

    Can you buy an SL anonymously? The beneficial owner (titular real)

    Notaries and registrars are required to identify the beneficial owner under the anti-money laundering law (Ley 10/2010, arts. 2.1.n and 4). The beneficial owner is the natural person who directly or indirectly owns more than 25% of the capital or voting rights or otherwise controls the company; if there is no such person, the director is regarded as the beneficial owner (art. 4.2 b and b bis).

    • The company is required to obtain, keep and update information on its beneficial owners, and it is kept for 10 years after the person has ceased to be a beneficial owner (art. 4 bis.1).
    • The beneficial owner must immediately inform the directors of their details: full name, date of birth, identity document, country of residence, citizenship and holding (art. 4 bis.4).
    • Together with its annual accounts, an SL files a beneficial ownership declaration with the Registro Mercantil: the form was introduced by Orden JUS/319/2018 and is now part of the annual accounts forms under Orden JUS/616/2022.
    • Data from the whole country is collected by the Registro de Titularidades Reales (the Register of Beneficial Ownership) at the Ministry of Justice (additional provision 3 of Law 10/2010).

    That is why offers to buy a company “in the name of a nominee owner” are best rejected straight away: the law looks at who actually owns and manages it. From the outset, we include proof of the source of the buyer's funds in the list of documents for a participations transaction.

    How do you change the director and enter them in the register?

    The director is appointed by the general meeting (art. 214.1 LSC), and where there is a sole member, by that member's own decision (art. 15). The appointment takes effect from the moment the new director accepts the position (art. 214.3). After that, the appointment is submitted for entry in the Registro Mercantil within 10 days (art. 215.2), and entry in the register is mandatory (art. 94 of the Commercial Register Regulation). The general meeting can remove the previous director at any time (art. 223.1).

    A person who was required to register the appointment cannot rely on the fact that it has not been entered in the register (art. 4.2 of the Commercial Register Regulation), so the entry is not put off. If the accounts have not been filed, the closure of the register will block it (art. 282). How an appointment works is described on the page about appointing a company director.

    Does the buyer need an NIE, and what changes for a non-resident?

    In notarial documents that have tax consequences, the parties provide proof of their tax number, and it is entered in the document (art. 23 of the Notaries Act). For a foreigner, the tax number is the NIE (art. 20 RD 1065/2007). You need to get it before visiting the notary; we explain how an NIE differs from the TIE card in the article on the NIE and TIE.

    If the buyer is not resident in Spain and acquires 10% of the capital or more, this is a foreign investment that is declared to the Registro de Inversiones (the Investment Register) (arts. 4 and 5 RD 571/2023). When the transaction is formalised by a Spanish notary and the buyer has given the notary all the necessary details, the information is passed on by the notaries, and the buyer does not need to file a declaration separately (art. 5.3.b).

    Which to choose: a ready-made SL or a new one?

    Buying a ready-made SL Setting up a new SL
    Company history Yes: accounts, tax returns, possible debts Starts from scratch
    What to check Articles of association, AEAT and Seguridad Social certificates, register extract, whether the accounts have been filed, whether the NIF is valid Name, articles of association and capital tailored to your business
    Visits to the notary Sale and purchase of participations, then usually an amendment to the articles of association and the appointment of a director Incorporation with your articles of association and director
    Common to both routes The members' NIEs, beneficial ownership details, entry of the director in the Registro Mercantil

    The minimum capital of a new SL is €1; while it is below €3,000, at least 20% of profit is allocated to a reserve (art. 4.1 LSC). For an S.A., the minimum capital is €60,000 (art. 4.2), and shares are transferred under different rules from SL participations (arts. 120–123). A step-by-step guide to a new company is in the article how to open an SL in Spain and on the page on setting up a company.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I buy a ready-made SL in Spain without a notary, under a private contract?
    No. Under article 106 of the Capital Companies Act (LSC), the transfer of SL participations must be formalised in a public document, in practice before a notary. If the buyer is an outsider to the company, as a general rule the consent of the general meeting of members is also needed (article 107), unless the articles of association establish a different procedure.
    Can I buy a Spanish company anonymously or register it in the name of a nominee owner?
    No. Under Law 10/2010, notaries and registrars must identify the beneficial owner — the natural person who owns more than 25% of the capital or voting rights or otherwise controls the company. The company keeps this information and files a beneficial ownership declaration with the Registro Mercantil together with its annual accounts, and the data is collected by the Registro de Titularidades Reales at the Ministry of Justice.
    Am I liable for the old debts of an SL I have bought?
    An SL member is not personally liable for the company's debts (article 1.2 LSC), but the debts remain with the company and reduce the value of what you have bought. You can become personally liable in two cases: if a sole member has not had single-member status entered in the register within 6 months (article 14), and if you have become the director and have not called a meeting within 2 months when there is a ground for dissolving the company, for example inactivity for more than a year (articles 363 and 367). That is why AEAT and Seguridad Social certificates are requested before the transaction.
    Do I need an NIE to buy participations in a Spanish SL?
    Yes. In notarial documents with tax consequences, the parties provide proof of their tax number, and it is entered in the document (article 23 of the Notaries Act), and for a foreigner the tax number is the NIE (article 20 RD 1065/2007). If the buyer does not live in Spain and acquires 10% of the capital or more, the transaction is also declared as a foreign investment; when it is formalised by a Spanish notary, the information is passed on by the notaries, provided the buyer has given the notary all the details.
    Why can a company that has long been inactive be hard to transfer to a new owner?
    The tax office may revoke a company's NIF if it has not started activity within 3 months of requesting the number or has not filed its annual accounts with the Registro Mercantil for 4 consecutive years, and a notary will not authorise documents on behalf of a company whose NIF has been revoked. If the annual accounts are not filed on time, the Registro Mercantil is closed to new entries about the company, including a new director. That is why the NIF and the filed accounts are checked before signing.
    How does buying an S.A. differ from buying an SL?
    In an S.A., the capital is divided into shares, and the minimum capital is €60,000, compared with €1 for an SL (article 4 LSC). Shares are transferred under their own rules (articles 120–123): restrictions on sale apply only to registered shares and only if they are expressly written into the articles of association. For an SL, the law directly requires a notarial document and, as a general rule, the meeting's consent to a sale to an outsider.
