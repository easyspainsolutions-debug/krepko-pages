    Arraigo laboral — residence permit after 2 years of work in Spain

    Krepko · updated 23 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    If you have a record of official work in Spain — Seguridad Social (social security), an employment contract, contributions (cotizaciones) — bear in mind: arraigo laboral (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain through work) has not existed since 20 May 2025 (art. 125 RD 1155/2024), and arraigo social does not require an employment contract. After 2 years in Spain, a residence permit through work is now applied for under arraigo sociolaboral — you need a contract for at least 20 hours a week; and if your previous residence permit (not one granted for exceptional circumstances) was not renewed within the last 2 years — arraigo de segunda oportunidad. Neither of them requires a record of contributions. Below we go through which documents to gather and where the file most often falls apart. If you have no time to put it together yourself — we handle arraigo through work from start to finish, from checking the documents to the appointment at Extranjería (the immigration office).

    What arraigo laboral is

    Arraigo laboral is a type of “arraigo” (rootedness) from the previous regulation (RD 557/2011): it has not existed since 20 May 2025, and the new Reglamento recognises arraigo de segunda oportunidad, sociolaboral, social, socioformativo and familiar (art. 125 RD 1155/2024). The word arraigo itself translates as “rootedness” or “putting down roots”: the state recognises that you have already become part of Spanish society and the economy. For those who have worked in Spain officially and lived here for at least two years, two types now fit: arraigo de segunda oportunidad — if in the two years before applying you held a residence permit not granted for exceptional circumstances and it was not renewed — and arraigo sociolaboral — if you have an employment contract for at least 20 hours a week paid at no less than the SMI (minimum wage) in proportion to the hours (art. 127.a–b RD 1155/2024).

    The fundamental difference from arraigo social is this: after the reform of the Reglamento (RD 1155/2024), 2 years of residence are required for both arraigo social and arraigo sociolaboral, but arraigo social requires close relatives with a residence permit or an integration report and funds of at least 100% of IPREM for you (with a relative — 200% for the two of you), while sociolaboral requires an employment contract for at least 20 hours a week paid at no less than the SMI in proportion to the hours; the condition of “12 months of contributions in the last 18” exists neither in the new regulation nor in the previous one. If an employer is ready to sign a contract, we check it for hours and pay and tell you whether it qualifies for sociolaboral now.

    Important: arraigo laboral is not a work visa and not a renewal of a work permit. It was a separate regularisation procedure through the Oficina de Extranjería based on your past employment history in Spain; it has not existed since 20 May 2025. The types of arraigo applied for instead give a residence permit with the right to work for 1 year (art. 125.2 RD 1155/2024).

    Who arraigo laboral is for

    Arraigo laboral was a route for those who had managed to work in Spain officially but were left without a valid status; it has not existed since 20 May 2025. The situations people come to us with look like this:

    • You worked officially but did not manage to get a work visa in time. It happens that an employer started the work permit process, the person began working, and then the permit expired or there was a bureaucratic hitch. Contributions are not counted for arraigo, but if the permit expired within the last two years and was not renewed, arraigo de segunda oportunidad is the route.
    • Seasonal workers with a work history over several seasons. If you worked in agriculture, tourism or construction on seasonal contracts, for arraigo sociolaboral you can submit several consecutive seasonal contracts with different employers (art. 127.b); time worked in past seasons has not counted since 20 May 2025.
    • The student visa ended, then you worked. Many people came on a student visa, worked part-time officially (a student estancia allows you to work up to 30 hours a week), and after finishing their studies stayed in Spain and kept working. But work history is not counted for arraigo, and arraigo de segunda oportunidad does not suit former students: studies are an estancia (stay), not a residence authorisation (art. 127.a); the route is sociolaboral with a contract after 2 years in Spain.
    • Temporary protection plus official work. Ukrainians with temporary protection (protección temporal) have had the right to official employment from the very start. Contributions are not counted for arraigo, but protection holders can, without waiting for it to end, apply for arraigo — for example, for sociolaboral if they have a contract — or, after living in Spain for a year, switch to a residence permit with the right to work under art. 191 (DA 19ª RD 1155/2024 as amended by RD 316/2026). We check this against your vida laboral (employment record) before you start gathering paperwork.
    For Ukrainians: temporary protection and arraigo are two different statuses. Applying for arraigo does not cancel temporary protection while it is in force. But it is worth starting arraigo early: it gives an independent residence permit that does not depend on EU decisions on extending the directive. We handle this switch while temporary protection is still in force — so you keep time in reserve for mistakes and for sending any missing documents.

    Requirements for arraigo laboral

    1. Two years of residence in Spain

    The requirement is the same as for arraigo social: after the reform of the Reglamento (RD 1155/2024), two years are enough both for arraigo social and for the types applied for instead of arraigo laboral — sociolaboral and segunda oportunidad. Residence must be continuous — trips abroad do not break it as long as, in the two years before applying, they total no more than 90 days (Instrucciones SEM 1/2025); beyond that, continuity is broken. The main proof is the padrón histórico (the history of your municipal registration). If it has gaps, we work out what to fill them with: bank statements, rental contracts, medical certificates.

    2. Twelve months of official work in the last 18 months

    There is not and never was such a requirement in the regulation, and since 20 May 2025 arraigo laboral itself no longer exists: for arraigo sociolaboral, instead of a work record you need a signed employment contract (art. 127.b RD 1155/2024). This means work por cuenta ajena (as an employee) officially registered with the Seguridad Social. Work as an autónomo (self-employed) usually does not count for these purposes — you need employment specifically, with contributions. There can be several contracts — with different employers at the same time or consecutive seasonal ones: their hours add up, and in total you need at least 20 a week (art. 127.b). Send us the contract or a draft, and we will tell you whether it qualifies on hours and pay.

    3. No criminal record

    A standard requirement for all residence authorisations. You need a criminal record certificate from the countries where you lived for five years before entering Spain (for most Ukrainians — from Ukraine); Extranjería requests the Spanish records itself, and a certificate from another country is not needed if you have been living in Spain continuously for the last 5 years (art. 130.2 RD 1155/2024). The certificate must be current at the time of applying: Extranjería sets its validity, usually no more than 3 months from the date of issue. A foreign certificate also requires an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish. We arrange the Ukrainian certificate with an apostille and the sworn translation ourselves — all we need from you is your details and a scan of your passport.

    4. A valid passport

    Your passport must be valid on the date of application and preferably for the whole expected processing period and the validity of the residence permit. If it expires in the coming months — renew it in advance, otherwise you will have to resubmit the documents with the new passport.

    Proof of official work: what is accepted

    The central document is the informe de vida laboral. This is an official statement from the Seguridad Social listing all your periods of employment with the employer, the type of contract, the start and end dates and the number of days you were registered (días de alta). Since 20 May 2025 the months on it are not counted for arraigo, but this is where we start our review: it shows your employment history at a glance.

    You can request the informe de vida laboral in several ways:

    • Online through the Import@ss portal (importass.seg-social.es) — a digital certificate, cl@ve or an SMS code is enough (if you have given your mobile number to the Seguridad Social)
    • In person at any INSS office (Instituto Nacional de la Seguridad Social) — you need a passport or NIE
    • By phone on the Seguridad Social helpline — 901 50 20 50

    Besides the vida laboral, your employment history is confirmed by:

    • Employment contracts (contratos de trabajo) — all those covering the periods you declare. If the contract is open-ended and ongoing — you need a current copy. If the contract has ended — a finiquito or a certificate from the employer will do.
    • Nóminas (payslips) — for the months worked. Nóminas confirm that you actually received a salary and were not just listed in a contract.
    • Finiquito — if the employment relationship has already ended, this is the final settlement document. It confirms that the work took place and the date it ended.
    Working in several places: since 20 May 2025, past periods are not added up for arraigo; for sociolaboral the hours of the contracts you submit add up — at least 20 a week in total (art. 127.b). Overlapping periods (for example, working part-time for two employers at the same time) also count.

    Arraigo laboral vs arraigo social: what is the difference

    Both routes lead to the same result — a residence permit with the right to work. But their requirements and starting positions differ. Here is a brief comparison:

    Parameter Arraigo laboral Arraigo social
    Period of residence 2 years 2 years
    Employment history Not needed: this type has not existed since 20 May 2025; for sociolaboral what matters is the contract, not your work record Not required
    Employment contract For sociolaboral — signed, at least 20 hours a week paid at no less than the SMI in proportion to the hours Not needed (it is needed for sociolaboral)
    Padrón histórico For 2 years For 2 years
    Residence permit after approval 1 year with the right to work (sociolaboral, segunda oportunidad) 1 year with the right to work
    Who it suits best This type has not existed since 20 May 2025: with a contract — sociolaboral; with a residence permit that was not renewed — segunda oportunidad Those who have close relatives with a residence permit, or an integration report and funds of at least 100% of IPREM (with a relative — 200% for the two of you)

    If your work was official and shows in your contributions, you still cannot apply for arraigo laboral after 20 May 2025: you can build on your past through arraigo de segunda oportunidad — if in the last two years you held a residence permit (not one granted for exceptional circumstances) and it was not renewed. If there was no official work but you have 2 years of residence and an employer ready to sign a contract — the route is different: arraigo sociolaboral (arraigo social does not require a contract). In our review we tell you plainly which of the two routes works for you.

    Full list of documents for arraigo laboral

    1. Form EX-10 — the official application form for temporary residence authorisation. It is downloaded from the website of the Ministerio de Inclusión, Seguridad Social y Migraciones. Fill it in legibly, in Spanish. State the exact type (there is no “arraigo laboral” box on the form — there are segunda oportunidad, sociolaboral, social, socioformativo and familiar), otherwise the application may be redirected or returned.
    2. Passport — all pages including blank ones, the original plus a copy. If you have several passports (for example, a Ukrainian biometric one and an internal one) — bring both.
    3. Padrón / proof of 2 years of residence — the padrón histórico from the ayuntamiento (town hall) showing your full registration history. If there are gaps in the padrón — add bank statements, rental contracts, bills in your name, certificates from your children's school.
    4. A Spanish criminal record certificate is not needed: Extranjería requests this information itself (art. 130.2 RD 1155/2024).
    5. A criminal record certificate from the countries where you lived for five years before entering Spain, with an apostille and a sworn translation. For Ukrainians — an extract from the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Ukraine (MVS) with an apostille; the translation is done by a sworn translator (traductor jurado) in Spain; we take care of this whole chain and time the dates to your appointment. Extranjería sets its validity, usually no more than 3 months from the date of issue to the date of application.
    6. Informe de vida laboral from the Seguridad Social — for the whole period of employment. It is not on the lists of documents for arraigo (art. 130.1 RD 1155/2024), but it is useful for the review.
    7. Employment contracts (contratos de trabajo) — for arraigo sociolaboral: signed by you and the employer, totalling at least 20 hours a week paid at no less than the SMI in proportion to the hours (art. 127.b and 130.1.b). Copies + originals.
    8. Nóminas or finiquito — payslips for the periods worked. They are not required for arraigo (art. 130.1 RD 1155/2024).
    9. Fee payment receipt — Modelo 790, código 052. The fee is paid at a bank or through the AEAT website. Under the rules it can be paid within 10 working days after applying (DA 12ª RD 1155/2024), but it is better to bring the receipt straight away: you may be asked for it at the counter. Check the amount on the date of application — it changes; we fill in the form and hand it to you ready for payment.
    10. A photo is not needed with the application: it is handed in later, when the TIE is issued.
    Tip on organising the file: arrange the documents in the order of the list, each in a separate plastic sleeve. Take at least 2 copies of each document. Some Extranjería offices require the original + 2 copies, others only 1. Too much is better than too little. We give our clients the file already arranged according to an inventory — in the order in which the inspector looks through it.

    The application process

    Booking a cita previa

    Arraigo is filed electronically through Mercurio on sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es or in person at the Oficina de Extranjería — the immigration office at the delegación del gobierno of your province. To file in person you need an appointment — cita previa. You book it through the sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es portal in the section Extranjería → Cita Previa de Extranjería; since 20 May 2025 there has been no separate arraigo laboral procedure.

    In large cities — Madrid, Barcelona — there are almost no free slots. Appointments appear and disappear quickly, often early in the morning or close to midnight. You can try to catch them yourself, but it is a lottery that can go on for weeks. When we accompany you, we take care of the cita previa: we monitor slots, book you in and agree the date — all we need from you is your passport details.

    Attending in person

    Arrive strictly at the time given — being late, even by a few minutes, can cost you the appointment. Bring all originals and copies of the documents and the fee payment receipt. The inspector will look through the set, check that all the required papers are there and either accept the file or point out what is missing. The day before the appointment we run the documents through a checklist: being turned away at the door costs a new cita, and that means months.

    Once the documents are accepted, you will be given a resguardo — a receipt confirming that the application has been registered. This is an important document: it confirms that your case is being processed, and the official countdown starts from it.

    After applying

    You can track the status of your case through the Consulta de estado de expediente service on sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es — for this you need the expediente (case) number from your resguardo. The status changes slowly, but updates do appear: “en tramitación”, “resolución”, “notificación”. For our clients we check the expediente ourselves and let you know when a decision appears.

    Processing times

    Under Spanish administrative law, Extranjería must issue a decision within 3 months of the date the documents are accepted. If there is no answer within 3 months, formally this counts as silencio administrativo negativo — the silence is treated as a refusal. But in practice cases take longer: waiting 5–8 months for the outcome is normal for 2025–2026. We give you a guide figure for your province before we start, so you can plan your work and travel.

    Actual timeframes by city are roughly as follows:

    • Madrid, Barcelona — 6–9 months
    • Valencia, Alicante — 4–6 months
    • Málaga, Seville — 4–7 months
    • Smaller provinces — sometimes 3–4 months

    If more than 3 months have passed with no answer — you can file a recurso de reposición (an appeal against the silence to the same Extranjería office) or go straight to court. It is a debatable step: sometimes it speeds the case up, sometimes it adds complications, so we first look at what the expediente shows and decide together with an immigration lawyer.

    What you get after approval

    A positive decision on arraigo sociolaboral or de segunda oportunidad gives a temporary residence authorisation for 1 year with the right to work as an employee and as self-employed (art. 125.2 and 131 RD 1155/2024). Arraigo social gives the same: only arraigo familiar is issued for 5 years. For sociolaboral, the authorisation takes effect only if you are registered with the Seguridad Social within a month of notification (art. 130.5).

    After approval you have one month to apply in person for the physical card — the TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero). The appointment is again through cita previa at a comisaría de policía (police station) or extranjería; we take care of this too, so that the deadline does not run out while you wait for a slot. To get the TIE you need: the original documents, a 32×26 mm photo, and the receipt for the TIE fee (Modelo 790, código 012).

    The road ahead looks like this:

    • After a year — renewal for another 1 year (you need to be looking for work and registered with the employment service), and if you are working, you can switch to a residence and work authorisation for 4 years (art. 132 and 191 RD 1155/2024)
    • After 5 years of legal continuous residence — residencia de larga duración (long-term residence, with no expiry)
    • After 10 years — the possibility of applying for Spanish nationality (subject to a number of additional conditions)
    Right to work: a residence permit under arraigo sociolaboral or de segunda oportunidad does not tie you to a specific employer. You can work for any employer, in any region of Spain, in any sector. You can work both as an employee (cuenta ajena) and as an autónomo: the arraigo authorisation includes the right to work for yourself without a separate permit (art. 131 RD 1155/2024).

    Typical mistakes with arraigo laboral

    You have contributions, but the period does not fall within the 18 months

    The most common problem. A person worked officially — say, two years ago — and thinks it will count. But since 20 May 2025 past work history does not count for arraigo: sociolaboral requires a signed contract, and if in the last two years you held a residence permit (not one granted for exceptional circumstances) and it was not renewed, arraigo de segunda oportunidad is the route. Send us your statement: we will review it and tell you plainly whether you apply now or wait for a new contract.

    Worked unofficially, then officially

    Past periods of work have not been counted for arraigo since 20 May 2025 — neither official nor unofficial ones; sociolaboral requires only an official contract registered with the Seguridad Social. Cash-in-hand work — even if you worked honestly and a lot — does not appear in the vida laboral at all and does not exist for this procedure. Only an official contract, only cuenta ajena, only Seguridad Social.

    An expired criminal record certificate

    Extranjería sets the validity of a foreign criminal record certificate, usually 3 months. If more time has passed between its issue and the date of application, the certificate will most likely not be accepted. Plan ahead: book the cita previa first, and order the certificate from the country where you lived before entering Spain about 1.5–2 months before the appointment. The Ukrainian certificate is issued through the online services of the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Ukraine or through the consulate in Spain; the procedure changes from time to time. We order it ourselves and time the date of issue to your cita, so that its validity does not run out halfway through gathering the documents.

    Gaps in your residence history

    If the padrón histórico has no entries for some period — this is not an automatic refusal, but it raises questions. Try to fill the gaps with other evidence: bank statements with Spanish transactions, rental contracts, receipts, medical visits. Every document showing your physical presence in Spain during the period in question counts in your favour. We gather such evidence into a separate bundle and arrange it by month, so the inspector does not have to search.

    Mistakes in form EX-10

    Form EX-10 is used for several different procedures — it is important to state the type correctly: there is no “arraigo laboral” box on it; you tick segunda oportunidad or sociolaboral. Incorrect codes or ticks in the wrong box can lead to your application being processed under different rules or returned for correction. Take the current version of the form from the official website of the Ministerio de Inclusión, Seguridad Social y Migraciones and double-check all the fields before applying. When we accompany you, we fill in the form and check the codes twice — before booking and on the day of the appointment.

    Incorrect apostille or translation

    The apostille is placed on the original document — not on a copy. The translation is done only by a sworn (jurado) translator accredited by Spain's Ministerio de Asuntos Exteriores. A notarised translation, certified by a notary, is something different and is not suitable. The list of accredited translators is on the website of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, but you do not have to search: we arrange the sworn translation ourselves and hand it over together with the apostilled original.

    If you have no time to sort it out yourself — we handle arraigo through work from start to finish: we check the contract or the previous residence permit, gather the documents and have them translated, book the cita previa and accompany you to the appointment at Extranjería. Your part is to send the scans and come on the appointed day. We discuss the cost once we understand the scope.

Arraigo social requires 2 years of residence, close relatives with a residence permit or an integration report, and funds of at least 100% of IPREM for you (with a relative — 200% for the two of you); an employment contract is not needed. Neither of them requires a record of contributions.
    How many years do you need to live in Spain for arraigo laboral?
    Arraigo laboral has not existed since 20 May 2025. We prepare the empadronamiento histórico (registration history) and other proof of the two years for the application.
