    Travel consent for a minor: why the Spanish police will not issue it

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. The Spanish police authorisation is not your tool if the child is not a Spanish citizen. The option that works is a notarised consent from the other parent, drawn up before a Spanish notary, with an apostille and a translation. It is convenient to arrange it in Spain: both parents are here, not in different countries.

    Parents usually find this out at the police station: the Spanish travel authorisation for a minor is issued only to Spanish citizens. For a Ukrainian or Russian child living in Spain, this route is closed: the procedure refers directly to the law of the country of citizenship.

    Below: who really has to obtain consent and when, what the Ukrainian rules on crossing the border say, why it is better to arrange the document in Spain in advance and how it is done at the notary's office. Separately, we look at the cases where consent is not needed at all: there are more of them than people tend to think.

    What the Spanish police issue, and to whom

    The Autorización de viaje para menores is issued at Policía Nacional stations, Guardia Civil posts and by a notary. It is needed by minors who are Spanish citizens travelling outside the country alone or accompanied by an adult who is not their parent or guardian.

    The procedure states explicitly that it does not apply to entry into Spain, to Spanish minors permanently living abroad, or to foreign minors: the latter are subject to the law of their own country. Foreign children who are in Spain in transit do not need the document either.

    The practical conclusion. Going to a police station for an authorisation for a Ukrainian child is pointless: they will not issue it there. You need to look at the rules of the country whose citizenship the child holds and arrange the document in notarial form.

    What the Ukrainian rules say

    The Rules for crossing the state border by citizens of Ukraine (Resolution No. 57 of the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine, KMU) describe the situation in detail. Important: they govern leaving Ukraine. In other words, the consent requirement will apply when the child is leaving Ukraine to come back, for example after a summer visit to their grandmother; while martial law is in force in Ukraine, a child under 16 travelling with one parent leaves even without the notarised consent of the other (point 2³ of the same Rules).

    Under point 4, a child under 16 may leave accompanied by one parent on the basis of the notarised consent of the other parent, stating the country of destination and the period of stay, if that parent is not present at the border checkpoint.

    Consent is not required in the cases listed in the rule. The main ones:

    • the other parent is a foreigner or a stateless person, and this is clear from the entry about the father in the birth certificate;
    • the passport contains an entry on departure for permanent residence outside Ukraine or a mark of permanent consular registration; the latter is confirmed by a certificate from the e-Consul system;
    • the death certificate of the other parent is presented, or a court decision depriving them of parental rights, declaring them missing or legally incapable, or allowing the child to leave without their consent;
    • a birth registration extract for a single mother under Article 135 of the Family Code;
    • a certificate of child maintenance arrears whose total exceeds four months' payments.

    Separately: for a temporary trip of up to one month, consent is not needed if a decision of a court or a guardianship authority is presented establishing that the child lives with the parent who is travelling.

    About permanent consular registration. This is one of the most underrated grounds. If the child is on the permanent consular register and this is confirmed by an e-Consul certificate, the notarised consent of the other parent is not required for leaving Ukraine. Check your status before ordering the document.

    Why it is more convenient to arrange the document in Spain

    The logic is simple: as a rule, both parents are here. Arranging the consent in Spain means one half-hour appointment at the notary's office. Trying to do the same when one parent is already in Ukraine and the other is in Spain means correspondence, posting documents and lost weeks.

    The second argument is time. A consent with an apostille and a translation is prepared quickly, but it still takes several days. Before school holidays and public holidays demand rises, and getting it “done in a day” is not always possible.

    What it looks like in practice

    1. We go through the trip
    Who is travelling, with whom, to which country and for how long. This determines whether the document is needed at all and what it must say.
    2. We prepare the text
    The country of destination and the period of stay are stated explicitly: the rule requires this. The text is in your language and in Spanish in a single deed.
    3. Signing
    About 30 minutes at the notary's office. The parent who stays behind signs. A Krepko representative is present and translates every clause.
    4. Apostille and translation
    An apostille on a notarial deed in Spain is obtained quickly. Then comes a notarised translation into Ukrainian, and the document is ready to be presented.

    What else to take with you

    Besides the consent, at the border they look at the child's valid passport and birth certificate. If the parent's and the child's surnames are different, the birth certificate answers half the questions before they are even asked.

    Airlines sometimes ask for a consent even where the state does not require it: this is their internal policy. Having the document with you costs less than an argument at the check-in desk.

    Common mistakes

    Not stating the country and the period. The rule requires the consent to name the country of destination and the period of stay. Without them the document formally does not meet the requirement.

    Making it out for “any country, forever”. The opposite extreme: wording that is too broad raises questions with the border guard and is not always accepted.

    Getting the translation done before the apostille. Both the document and the stamp need to be translated. A translation made before the apostille will have to be ordered again.

    Leaving it until the week before the flight. The apostille, translation and delivery take days. During the holiday season and before public holidays, longer.

    Frequently asked questions

    My child is flying with me. Do I need the other parent's consent?
    For leaving Ukraine, while martial law is in force, no: a child under 16 travelling with one parent leaves without the notarised consent of the other (point 2³ of KMU Rules No. 57); under normal rules, yes, if the other parent is not present at the border checkpoint and none of the grounds for exemption applies. For flying out of Spain, the state does not require consent, but the airline may ask for it, and so may the local border service on entry to the country of destination.
    My child is travelling with their grandmother. What is needed?
    Under the Ukrainian rules, a child under 16 may leave accompanied by persons aged 18 or over who have been authorised by the parents through a notarised consent. So under normal rules you need a document from the parents in the name of the accompanying person; while martial law is in force in Ukraine, a child leaves with their grandmother without a notarised consent (point 2³ of the Rules), with the child's documents and documents proving the family relationship.
    We are divorced and the child lives with me. Is consent still needed?
    While martial law is in force in Ukraine, no: a child travelling with one parent leaves without the notarised consent of the other; under normal rules, divorce in itself does not exempt you from the requirement. What exempts you are specific documents: a court decision allowing the child to leave without consent, or a decision of a court or a guardianship authority establishing that the child lives with you; the latter works for trips of up to one month.
    Will the Spanish police really not issue the authorisation?
    The procedure is designed for minors who are Spanish citizens. Foreign minors living in Spain are subject to the law of their own country, so this document is not issued to them.
    Will a consent drawn up before a Spanish notary be accepted at the Ukrainian border?
    Yes, provided it has an apostille and a notarised translation into Ukrainian. The Ministry of Justice of Ukraine explicitly names arranging it before a notary in the country of residence as one of the two acceptable ways.
    For what period should the consent be made out?
    For the period of the trip, with some margin. The country of destination and the period of stay are stated: this is a requirement of the rule itself, not a notary's formality.
    How long does it take to arrange?
    Preparing the text usually takes a day or two, signing about half an hour, and the apostille in Spain is quick. Then comes the translation. In total, plan for several working days, and before the holidays allow extra time.
