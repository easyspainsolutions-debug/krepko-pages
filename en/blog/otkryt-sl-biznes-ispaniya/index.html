    How to open an SL (LLC) in Spain: step-by-step guide 2026

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    Sociedad Limitada (SL) is the Spanish equivalent of a limited liability company (LLC) and the most common choice for foreigners who start a business in Spain. Below we explain what registration involves, how long it takes and in which cases autónomo does the same job more cheaply. We handle these registrations and have gathered here what usually only comes to light along the way.

    SL vs Autónomo — which to choose?

    Before registering, it is worth answering honestly what you actually need: SL and autónomo are two different statuses with a different tax burden and different liability. We start the conversation with exactly this, and sometimes we talk people out of an SL. If you are a solo freelancer, see the guide to registering as autónomo: it is often simpler and cheaper.

    When to choose an SL

    • You have received investment or want to bring in partners
    • The risk is high and you need to protect your personal assets
    • B2B clients and framework contracts require a legal entity
    • You will have employees (more than one)
    • You plan to sell the business

    When to choose Autónomo

    • You are a solo freelancer/consultant
    • You want simplicity and a minimum of paperwork
    • Your income is modest (up to 150-200k EUR a year)
    • No employees, or one assistant
    The reality in 2026: In Spain, roughly a third of the self-employed are “autónomo sociedad” (autónomo with an SL company). They pay less tax than ordinary autónomos and get the same protection as an SL. For many this is a sensible middle ground — at the consultation we calculate both options using your figures.

    What an SL (Sociedad Limitada) is

    SL (Sociedad Limitada) is a limited liability company. Its key features:

    Main parameters

    • Share capital: a minimum of €1 (since October 2022 under the Ley Crea y Crece), but if the capital is below €3,000, at least 20% of profit is set aside in a reserve until the reserve together with the capital reaches €3,000, and on liquidation, if the assets are not enough to cover the debts, the members are jointly liable for them up to the difference between €3,000 and the capital — which is why €3,000 is more often paid in
    • Liability: limited to the amount of capital (your personal assets are not touched)
    • Upkeep: you need a gestor (accountant/adviser) and bookkeeping; an audit is mandatory if two of three thresholds are exceeded two years in a row: assets of €2.85 million, turnover of €5.7 million, 50 employees
    • Taxes: Impuesto de Sociedades (corporate tax) — 25% on profit (general rate; in 2026, with turnover up to €1 million — 19% and 21%, up to €10 million — 23%), VAT (IVA) — 21% standard
    • Quarterly reporting: yes, it must be filed

    Step-by-step instructions for opening an SL

    Step 1. Checking the company name

    It all starts with the name: it is checked for availability through the Registro Mercantil Central (Central Commercial Register). We ask for three or four options straight away — that way you rarely have to apply a second time.

    • Go to the rmc.es website
    • Use the “Denominaciones Sociales” section to check whether the name is available
    • You can check several options at once
    • Get the certificate of availability (it is valid for 3 months)

    Step 2. Opening a bank account and paying in the capital

    Before the notary appointment, the company's bank account is opened and €3,000 of share capital is paid in. This is where non-residents most often get stuck: we agree the list of documents with the bank in advance and go to the branch with you.

    • Go to a bank (BBVA, CaixaBank, Banco Sabadell — they work with SLs)
    • You need: your passport, NIE and the name certificate from the RMC; the company's tax number will appear later, and each bank has its own exact list of documents
    • Pay €3,000 into the company account
    • Get a bank certificate of the capital paid in — the notary will attach it to the escritura (notarial deed); you can do without the certificate if the money is handed to the notary directly or if the founders state in the escritura that they are jointly liable for paying in the capital

    Step 3. Notarisation (Escritura de Constitución)

    This is the key stage: the notary draws up and certifies the deed of incorporation. The notarial part is our core work: we book the notary appointment, check the set of documents before signing and accompany you at the appointment.

    • Book an appointment with a notary (notario) — you can choose any one; notary offices' contact details are on notariado.org
    • Prepare the documents: passport and NIE (without the founder's tax number the notary will not sign the escritura), the bank certificate of the capital, the name certificate from the RMC
    • The procedure takes 20-30 minutes
    • The notary will give you a signed copy of the Escritura de Constitución
    • Cost: roughly €250-400 depending on the notary

    Step 4. Getting the company's NIF (AEAT)

    The NIF is the company's tax number: it is how the tax office, the bank and counterparties identify it. Without a NIF you can neither issue an invoice nor unblock the company account at the bank.

    • Go to the aeat.es website or a tax office (AEAT) branch
    • File the Modelo 036 application (company registration)
    • Attach a copy of the Escritura de Constitución (certified by the notary)
    • NIF: since 2021 the notary enters the provisional NIF in the escritura at signing (step 3), and the permanent one is issued after entry in the Registro Mercantil (step 5)

    Step 5. Registration in the Registro Mercantil (Commercial Register)

    After the NIF, the company is entered in the provincial commercial register — until that entry is made, your SL does not legally exist for counterparties.

    • The escritura is filed with the provincial Registro Mercantil — usually the notary sends it there electronically
    • Attach: a copy of the Escritura de Constitución, a copy of the NIF, the bank certificate
    • Cost: roughly €50
    • Timeframe: 1-3 days

    Step 6. Registration with Seguridad Social (social security)

    An SL administrator who manages and controls the company (half the shares or more, or, unless proven otherwise, a third, or a quarter if they also have management functions) registers with the social security system as autónomo in RETA (autónomo societario); an administrator without control who is paid for this work registers as equivalent to an employee. A missed registration comes to light at the very first inspection, so we put it in the plan straight away, together with the NIF.

    • Go to seg-social.es
    • Submit the registration application
    • Pay contributions every month (roughly €500-600 a month for yourself)
    • Important: this is not a salary, these are pension and insurance contributions

    Step 7. Alta en IAE (registration of business activities)

    IAE (Impuesto de Actividades Económicas) is a tax on business activity; SLs with a turnover under €1 million and all new companies in their first two years do not pay it. You still need to register the type of activity, and the code you choose determines which returns you will have to file afterwards.

    • Fill in Modelo 036 and tick the type of activity
    • This is usually done together with obtaining the NIF

    Timeframes and cost of opening an SL

    Cost

    • Notary: €250-400
    • Registro Mercantil: €50
    • Gestoría (document preparation): €300-600
    • Bank (opening the account): free, but they often charge a fee for maintaining a company account
    • Total (if you file yourself): roughly €600-900
    • Gestoría handles everything: €1,000-1,500 all inclusive

    Timeframes

    • Name check: 1 day
    • Opening the account: 2-3 days
    • Notary: 1-2 days (after booking)
    • NIF: 1 day
    • Registro Mercantil: 1-3 days
    • Seguridad Social: 2-5 days
    • Total: 1-3 weeks, if the documents are ready and nobody delays the notary appointment

    Taxes for an SL in 2026

    Impuesto de Sociedades (IS) — corporate tax

    Rate: 25% on net profit — the general rate; in 2026, with turnover up to €1 million — 19% on the first €50,000 of profit and 21% on the rest, up to €10 million — 23% (there are reduced rates for start-ups — 15% in the first years).

    What this looks like in practice: the company pays tax on its profit, and you separately pay IRPF on your salary (usually 20-45% depending on income); for the company this salary is, as a rule, an expense and reduces its profit. It makes sense to work out both taxes before registering, not at the end of the year.

    IVA (VAT) — 21%

    There is no turnover threshold for VAT — an SL charges it from the first invoice (except for exempt activities): you add 21% to invoices to clients, pay 21% to suppliers, and transfer the difference to the treasury every quarter.

    Seguridad Social contributions

    SL administrator: roughly €500-600 a month in contributions (this is not a salary, it is insurance).

    Employees: you pay 30% of their salary to social security, they pay 6-7%.

    How to pay yourself a salary (Autónomo inside an SL)

    An SL administrator can take money out of the company in two ways:

    1. Salary como empleado: you pay yourself a salary like any other employee (paying social contributions)
    2. Dividends (dividendos): the company distributes profit among the founders (subject to corporate tax for the company + IRPF for you)

    In practice a combination is more common: a small salary plus dividends. The accountant works out the proportion for your turnover — there is no universal figure here.

    What a foreigner (non-resident) needs to open an SL

    If you only have a passport (Ukrainian, without an NIE)

    • Your original passport — and you need to get an NIE before signing: without the founder's tax number the escritura cannot be executed
    • An address in Spain (it can be a friend's, but it must be real)
    • A phone number, Spanish or other (but Spanish is more convenient)
    • A bank account (it can be any, but a Spanish one is more convenient)

    If you have an NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero)

    • NIE and passport
    • Then the usual order follows: name, account, notary, register

    An important point

    The bank will almost certainly ask you to prove an address in Spain. If you are a non-resident, a utility bill (agua, luz) or an internet bill in your name will do. When there is no bill in your name at all, we work through the situation before you go to the bank — there are usually two or three workable options.

    The role of gestorías (advisers/accountants)

    A gestoría is a firm that handles a company's accounting, taxes and administrative correspondence. An SL can hardly do without one: reporting is quarterly, and penalties for late filing are charged automatically.

    What a gestoría does

    • Prepares the set of documents for the notary
    • Registers the company with the AEAT and the register
    • Handles quarterly reporting
    • Prepares the annual accounts (cuentas anuales)
    • Advises on taxes and tax optimisation

    Cost

    Setup: €300-600. Monthly: €100-300 depending on the workload. For an SL with straightforward paperwork, a fixed package of €2,000-3,500 a year is often offered. These are gestoría rates; we discuss the cost of our support once we understand the scope.

    SL vs Sociedad Unipersonal — differences

    Parameter Sociedad Limitada (SL) Sociedad Unipersonal (SLU)
    Number of founders 1 or more Only 1 member (a person or a company)
    Minimum capital €1 (€3,000 is more often paid in) €1 (€3,000 is more often paid in)
    Liability Limited to the capital Limited to the capital
    Taxes 25% IS on profit (general rate; lower for small companies) 25% IS on profit (general rate; lower for small companies)
    Registration complexity Slightly more complex (one agreement, signed by all the founders) Simpler (decision of a single founder)
    Setup cost €600-1,500 €600-1,500 (roughly the same)
    When to choose There may be investors or partners Only you, no partners

    Common mistakes and difficulties

    Mistake 1: Investing your own money before the Escritura

    Do not pay the capital into a personal account: before notarisation it is paid into the account of the company being formed. The bank's certificate of the payment is valid for two months — the escritura must be signed within that period.

    Mistake 2: A random gestoría

    A weak gestoría means fines, lost documents and missed deadlines. Ask who exactly will handle your company and ask to see a sample quarterly report. If an offer raises doubts, write to us — we will go through the contract before you sign it.

    Mistake 3: Filling in Modelo 036 incompletely

    A missing type of activity or address means filing again and losing a week. We check this form line by line before it is sent.

    Difficulty: The bank requires additional documents

    For non-residents, some banks ask for proof of the source of funds — for example, a statement from a Ukrainian bank for recent months. The requirement is routine, but it adds a week or two. We put such a package together in advance so that the question does not come up at the branch.

    Conclusions

    Opening an SL in Spain is doable, but it is not a process to go into unprepared. By 2026 registration has become simpler (Ley Crea y Crece), but the notary is still mandatory (a gestoría is not required by law, but people rarely do without one), and the order of the steps cannot be changed.

    The short route: you choose the name and the type of activity, we handle preparing the documents, booking the notary and filing with the register; external costs stay within €1,500, and in 2-3 weeks you have an SL with a NIF and a bank account. Your part: your passport, your signature at the notary's and about an hour for approvals.

    Still, before registering, check whether you need a company at all: autónomo is often simpler and cheaper, especially in the first year. Message us on WhatsApp and tell us your expected turnover — we will tell you plainly which status works out better for you.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the minimum share capital for an SL in Spain?
    Since October 2022, under the Ley Crea y Crece, the minimum share capital of an SL is €1, but in practice it is recommended to pay in €3,000 — this makes dealing with banks and counterparties easier and reduces the reserve requirements.
    Which works out better: an SL or autónomo?
    Autónomo is simpler and cheaper for a solo freelancer with an income of up to €150–200k a year. An SL works out better with larger turnover, partners, investors, employees or high risk — an SL has limited liability and a corporate tax of 25% (lower for small and new companies).
    How much does it cost to open an SL in Spain?
    In total, opening an SL costs roughly €600–1,200: the notary's services, the Registro Mercantil, a gestor, plus €3,000 of share capital, which stays in the company. We handle the whole registration.
    What taxes does an SL pay?
    Impuesto de Sociedades (corporate tax) — 25% on profit; in 2026, with turnover up to €1 million — 19% on the first €50,000 of profit and 21% on the rest, up to €10 million — 23% (for new companies, 15% in the first profitable year and the following one); IVA — 21% standard; plus the director's salary is subject to IRPF and Seguridad Social contributions.
