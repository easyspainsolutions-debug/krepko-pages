    Registering a child on the padrón when the other parent is in Ukraine: what consent you need

    Krepko · 24 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. A child's place of residence can only be changed with the consent of both parents or with a court's authorisation (Article 154 of the Spanish Civil Code), and the padrón instructions as amended in 2023 require such consent whenever one parent registers a child. Alicante asks for the other parent's authorisation with a copy of their identity document, and Valencia asks for the Modelo 1 form; according to the form and the town hall's website, the authorisation is valid for one month from the date of the document. If the other parent is in Ukraine, we recommend signing the consent before a Ukrainian notary, having an apostille added by the Ministry of Justice and getting a sworn translation into Spanish: both town halls accept foreign documents only with a translation. If the parent comes to Spain, a Spanish notary is simpler: the document is in Spanish from the start, with no apostille or translation.

    A mother and child are renting a flat in Alicante or Valencia, and the father has stayed in Ukraine. At the padrón appointment the town hall asks for something they did not bring: the other parent's authorisation. This is not an official's whim: a child's place of residence in Spain is decided by both parents, and the padrón instructions expressly require their consent if only one of them comes to the town hall.

    Below we look at what the rule rests on, what exactly the Alicante and Valencia town halls ask for according to their own websites and forms, and two ways to arrange the consent if the other parent is in Ukraine. We checked the rules and the town halls' requirements on 24 November 2026.

    Why does the town hall ask for the other parent's consent?

    The rule comes from the Spanish Civil Code. Article 154 includes among the powers of parental authority (patria potestad) the right to decide where the child habitually lives, and adds: this place can only be changed with the consent of both parents or, if there is no consent, with a court's authorisation. Article 156 states that parental authority is exercised by both parents jointly or by one with the consent of the other, and in case of disagreement a court decides.

    Municipal registration (empadronamiento) on the padrón records where a person lives, so town halls apply this rule directly. The technical instructions for town halls were approved by the INE and the Directorate-General for Regional and Local Cooperation (Dirección General de Cooperación Autonómica y Local) on 17 February 2020, and published by Resolución de 29 de abril de 2020 (BOE of 2 May 2020). In 2023 the section on children was rewritten (Resolución de 3 de febrero de 2023, BOE of 8 February 2023): the explanatory note to the amendments says that the other parent's consent or a court's authorisation is needed whenever one parent registers a child, regardless of whether there is a court decision on custody. There is one exception: the other parent has been deprived of parental authority, in which case that court decision is presented.

    Ukrainian law works in a similar way: the place of residence of a child under ten is determined by agreement between the parents, and from the age of ten by joint agreement of the parents and the child (Family Code of Ukraine, Article 160). So the town hall's requirement does not contradict what the family is used to at home.

    Which situations do the padrón instructions distinguish?

    Section 2.2.1 of the instructions distinguishes several cases. Which one applies to you determines the set of papers.

    • Both parents come. The registration sheet (hoja padronal) is signed by both.
    • One comes, the other agrees. The consent can be entered on the registration sheet itself, if it has a field for this, or the other parent's written authorisation can be attached to it. This is the option you need when the father or mother is in Ukraine.
    • No consent and no court decision. As an exception, the parent submits a declaración responsable — a statement made under their own responsibility, following the template in Annex I or II. More on it below.
    • A court has established joint custody but has not said where to register the child. The town hall requires written confirmation of the parents' agreement, and without it a new court decision; a declaración responsable is not accepted in this case.

    Important: the instructions set the general framework, but the list of documents and the forms are specific to each town hall. Below is only what has been published on the websites of Alicante and Valencia. In another city, it is worth checking the requirements on its town hall's website before booking an appointment.

    How do you arrange the consent if the other parent is in Ukraine?

    Neither Alicante nor Valencia says on its website that the authorisation must be notarial. But a simple signature on a piece of paper sent from another country leaves the town hall wondering who signed it. A notarial document removes that question, so when the parent is far away, we recommend arranging the consent before a notary. There are two options.

    1. Sign in Ukraine. A Ukrainian notary certifies the authenticity of the signature on the statement of consent: the notary confirms that this particular person signed it, not the facts in the text (Law of Ukraine “On Notaries”, Article 78). The document then goes through two stages.

    • Apostille. Spain and Ukraine are parties to the 1961 Hague Convention. The Convention classes notarial acts and notarial certification of signatures as public documents (Article 1), and the only formality that may be required to certify the signature is an apostille (Article 3). For documents of Ukrainian notaries it is added by the Ministry of Justice (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 61 of 18 January 2003). According to the Ministry of Justice's guidance, if the applicant is abroad, the application for an apostille can be sent by post, and it can be submitted by any person with an identity document.
    • Sworn translation. Alicante accepts a foreign official document only with a sworn translation into Spanish, and Valencia only with a translation by a sworn translator or a public body. A sworn translation (traducción jurada) works for both town halls.

    This route takes time for post, the apostille and the translation, so it is better to book the town hall appointment once the document is ready, not the other way round. For more on how an apostille differs from a sworn translation, see the article on the apostille and translation.

    2. Sign in Spain. If the other parent comes for even a few days, the consent is drawn up by a Spanish notary. A foreign national who does not live in Spain proves their identity with a passport or another official document from their country bearing a photograph and signature (Reglamento Notarial, Article 161). The document is drawn up in Spanish; if the parent does not speak the language, the notary may prepare it in two columns, in Spanish and in the parent's language (Article 150). This is a Spanish public document for a Spanish authority: it needs no apostille or translation, and signing takes one appointment. How this works with us is described on the page “Notarial consent”.

    What should the consent say?

    The fields of Valencia's Modelo 1 form are a good guide: they show what the town hall checks. The consent should include:

    1. the first name, surname, document number and address of the parent giving consent;
    2. the first name, surname and document number of each child;
    3. the exact address in Spain at which the children are being registered;
    4. the parent who is authorised to submit the documents to the town hall;
    5. the date and signature.

    A copy of the identity document of the parent who gave the consent is attached to it: both town halls require this. If there are several children, one document for all of them is more convenient. Watch the date: Valencia states both on the form and in the general requirements on its website that the authorisation is valid for a month from the date of the document. The town hall says nothing separately about notarial consent, so it is safer to assume the same month and to coordinate the signing date with the town hall appointment.

    If the father's or mother's consent will also be needed for the child's residence permit, the requirements for it are broader than the town hall's: Extranjería (the immigration office) looks at custody, permission to live in Spain and the period of validity. We covered this case in the article on an Extranjería request for the other parent's consent. In that case it makes sense to prepare a single document from the start that will work for both authorities.

    When can a declaración responsable be used instead?

    A statement under your own responsibility is an exception, not a substitute for consent. Without a court decision on custody it is accepted only in two cases (Annex I to the instructions):

    • the other parent's consent cannot be obtained, the child's rights would suffer without registration, and the parent has already gone to court to obtain a court's authorisation; proof of the court application is attached;
    • obtaining consent is clearly impossible, and the child would suffer harm without registration.

    If a court has granted custody to one parent, one more case is added: the decision was issued in absentia or the other parent's whereabouts are unknown, and in both cases they do not exercise their right of contact with the child (Annex II). On the Alicante form, the reason it is impossible and the harm to the child must be described in your own words, and at the bottom there is a warning about civil and administrative liability for inaccurate or false information. If the other parent is in contact and ready to sign, a declaración responsable is not for you: you need their consent.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I register a child on the padrón in Spain without the father's consent?
    As a rule, no. Under Article 154 of the Spanish Civil Code, a child's place of residence can only be changed with the consent of both parents or with a court's authorisation, and the padrón instructions require this consent whenever one parent registers a child. Without consent, a court's authorisation is needed. Exceptions: the other parent has been deprived of parental authority (that court decision is presented), or a declaración responsable in narrow cases: a court claim has been filed, or obtaining consent is clearly impossible and the child faces harm.
    What kind of consent from the other parent does Alicante Town Hall accept?
    According to the Alicante Town Hall website, authorisation from both parents is needed with a copy of an identity document; if the parents live apart, the consent of both is still required. The town hall accepts a foreign official document only with a sworn translation into Spanish, and papers are brought in the original. You apply in person by appointment or through the electronic sede; registration is free of charge.
    How long is an authorisation to register a child valid in Valencia?
    The Modelo 1 form and the general requirements on the Valencia Town Hall website state that the authorisation is valid for one month from the date shown on the document. A copy of the identity document of the parent giving the authorisation is attached to the form. The town hall says nothing separately about notarial consent, so it is safer to assume the same month and time the signing date to fit the town hall appointment.
    The father is in Ukraine: how can he give consent to registering the child in Spain?
    The Alicante and Valencia town halls do not expressly require notarisation, but we recommend signing the consent before a Ukrainian notary, who certifies the authenticity of the signature. Then the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine adds an apostille to the document, and in Spain a sworn translation into Spanish is made: both town halls require a translation of foreign documents. If the father can come to Spain, it is simpler to sign the consent before a Spanish notary with his passport: such a document needs neither an apostille nor a translation.
    Does consent signed before a Spanish notary need an apostille?
    No. A document from a Spanish notary is a Spanish public document, and it is submitted to a Spanish town hall without an apostille or translation. An apostille is added to a Spanish document only when it is taken to another country. If the parent does not speak Spanish, the notary can prepare the consent in two columns, in Spanish and in the parent's language (Reglamento Notarial, Article 150).
    Will consent for the town hall also work for the child's residence permit?
    Not always. When processing a child's residence permit, Extranjería looks at custody, permission to live in Spain and the period of validity of the consent, while the town hall cares about authorisation for a specific address. If both documents are needed at the same time, it makes sense to draw up a single text from the start that meets the requirements of both authorities.
