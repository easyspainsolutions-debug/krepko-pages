    How to make a power of attorney in Spain for Russia in 2026

    Krepko · updated 8 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. Russia and Spain are parties to the Hague Apostille Convention (Russia since 31 May 1992), so a power of attorney from a Spanish notary is valid in Russia with an apostille, without consular legalisation. The apostille on notarial documents is affixed by the College of Notaries and only to the paper original, which is what is sent to the representative. If the power of attorney is in Russian from the start, usually only the Spanish part is translated — the notary's certification and the apostille: the translation is done in Russia, the translator's signature is attested by a Russian notary, and the final requirements are set by whoever accepts the power of attorney. The exception is people on the Russian Ministry of Justice list under Law No. 284-FZ: a power of attorney cannot be used to conclude transactions on their behalf or to perform other legally significant acts in their interests.     Where to arrange a power of attorney for Russia

    We arrange a power of attorney for Russia before a Spanish notary: the signing takes place in your city, with your passport or TIE, and the text can be prepared in Russian from the start. Then the College of Notaries affixes the apostille, and the original is sent to the representative. Russia has not withdrawn from the 1961 Hague Convention: in the status table of the Hague Conference on Private International Law (HCCH) it is listed as a party since 31 May 1992, so consular legalisation is not needed.

    The work is divided: the notary certifies the power of attorney, we prepare the text and accompany you at the signing, and then arrange the apostille and send the original. How this works is described on the Power of attorney in your language for another country page.

    How to arrange a power of attorney before a Spanish notary

    1. Define the task and the recipient. What exactly the representative must do and where: at a Russian notary's office, at Rosreestr or an MFC (multifunctional public service centre), at a bank. The wording depends on this.
    2. Prepare the text. The power of attorney can be drawn up in Russian from the start. It is best to show the draft in advance to whoever will accept the document in Russia.
    3. Sign before the notary. In person, with a valid passport or TIE. The law does not require an NIE for such a power of attorney. If you do not speak Spanish and the notary does not speak Russian, an interpreter is needed at the signing: since 2007 this can be any person of your choice; a sworn one is not mandatory (Reglamento Notarial, art. 150).
    4. Have the apostille affixed. On notarial documents it is affixed only by the College of Notaries (Real Decreto 1497/2011, art. 3) and only to the paper original (Orden JUS/1207/2011, art. 11). Each apostille is entered in the electronic register of the Spanish Ministry of Justice, where it can be verified (Real Decreto 1497/2011, art. 8).
    5. Send the original. For a transaction the representative needs the paper original with the apostille. It is convenient to send a scan in advance so that the Russian notary or bank can look at the text.

    You can go through these steps on your own. But if the text is missing a necessary power, this only comes to light in Russia, when the representative goes to the notary or the MFC, and the power of attorney has to be drawn up again, with a new apostille and a new dispatch. That is why, before the signing, we check the text against the requirements of the party that will accept it.

    What to include for Rosreestr and the bank

    For filing applications to register rights with Rosreestr and for transactions that require notarial form, Russian law requires a notarial power of attorney (Article 185.1 of the Civil Code of the Russian Federation). A power of attorney from a Spanish notary is a notarial document, but it works only to the extent that it is precisely worded: if the required act is not in the text, the representative will be refused.

    • details of the principal and the representative — full names as in the passport, dates of birth, passport details;
    • specific acts — not “to manage property”, but “to sell the flat at the address…, sign the contract, submit the documents for registration of the transfer of title, receive the money”;
    • details of the asset — the address and cadastral number of the property, the bank and the account number;
    • period of validity — state it directly in the text;
    • delegation — under the Civil Code, the representative may entrust the matter to another person, as a rule, only if this is permitted by the power of attorney (Article 187).

    A separate point on self-dealing. If the representative, for example, personally buys your flat or car, a court may declare such a transaction invalid on your claim if you did not consent to it and it harms your interests; harm is presumed until proven otherwise (clause 3 of Article 182 of the Civil Code). If this is what is intended, you need to give your consent to such a transaction; in what form to give it is best clarified in advance with the Russian notary who will certify it. Banks may have their own requirements for the text, so it is worth showing the draft in advance to the bank where the account is held.

    Translation: what is translated and who certifies it

    The apostille confirms the notary's signature and seal, but does not translate the text. If the power of attorney is in Russian from the start, usually only the Spanish part is translated: the notary's certification wording and the apostille. A power of attorney in Spanish is translated in full.

    In Russia the translation is done by a translator, and a notary attests the authenticity of their signature; a notary who knows the language may certify the accuracy of the translation themselves (Article 81 of the Fundamentals of the Legislation of the Russian Federation on Notaries). The scope and form of the translation are ultimately set by whoever accepts the power of attorney, so it is best to clarify them before the signing.

    Law No. 284-FZ: whom it affects

    On 4 August 2026, Federal Law No. 284-FZ “On temporary restrictive measures in respect of persons who are outside the Russian Federation and evade the execution of a sentence” was signed and officially published the same day. It has been in force since the day of publication (Article 11).

    The law does not affect everyone who lives abroad. The measures apply only if three grounds exist at the same time: a final sentence of a Russian court or a ruling imposing a penalty under one of the articles of the Code of Administrative Offences listed in the law; confirmed evasion of that penalty; confirmed presence abroad (Article 3). The decision is taken by the Russian Ministry of Justice, which enters the person on a special list. The measures apply from the day following the publication of their details on this list on the Ministry of Justice website, and until they are removed from it (Articles 2 and 4).

    For people on the list, the law, among other things, prohibits using a power of attorney to conclude transactions on their behalf or to perform other legally significant acts in their interests (clause 13, part 1, Article 2), and their applications for cadastral registration and registration of rights to real estate are returned without consideration (clause 3, part 1, Article 2). The law contains no exceptions regarding where and when the power of attorney was drawn up, so a power of attorney will not help a person on the list. The law does not affect anyone else: for them, the procedure described above remains the same.

    Spouse's consent, revocation and the money from the sale

    If the flat in Russia was bought during the marriage, selling it as a general rule requires the notarised consent of the other spouse (clause 3 of Article 35 of the Family Code of the Russian Federation), and the power of attorney does not replace it. If your spouse also lives in Spain, the spouse's consent can be arranged before a Spanish notary on the same day as the power of attorney, in Russian from the start and with an apostille.

    If your plans change, the power of attorney can be revoked before a notary. The representative and those before whom they act must be notified of the revocation (Article 189 of the Civil Code).

    If the money from the sale arrives in your account in Spain, the bank may ask you to prove its origin: what to gather is covered in the article on the source of funds. Why it is more convenient to sign a power of attorney in your own language from the start is explained in the article on a power of attorney in Russian, and if the document is needed for Ukraine, read the article on a power of attorney from Spain for Ukraine.

    Frequently asked questions

    Will Rosreestr accept a power of attorney from a Spanish notary with an apostille?
    Filing applications to register rights with Rosreestr requires a notarial power of attorney (Article 185.1 of the Civil Code of the Russian Federation). A power of attorney from a Spanish notary with an apostille is a notarial document and does not need consular legalisation: Russia and Spain are parties to the Hague Convention. It is usually accompanied by a translation of the Spanish part into Russian, with the translator's signature attested by a Russian notary. It is best to clarify the requirements for the text before the signing with the office where the representative will file.
    If the power of attorney is in Russian, does it still need translating?
    Usually only the Spanish part is translated: the notary's certification and the apostille. In Russia the translator's signature is attested by a notary (Article 81 of the Fundamentals of the Legislation of the Russian Federation on Notaries). The scope of the translation is ultimately set by whoever accepts the document.
    Can I send the power of attorney to Russia as a scan?
    A transaction requires the paper original with the apostille: in Spain the apostille on notarial documents is affixed only on paper. It is convenient to send a scan in advance so that the representative can show the text to the notary or the bank.
    Will Law 284-FZ affect me if I simply live in Spain?
    Living abroad in itself does not bring you under the law. The measures apply only if there is a final sentence of a Russian court or a ruling imposing a penalty under certain articles of the Code of Administrative Offences, evasion of the penalty and presence abroad have been confirmed, and only after the Ministry of Justice enters the person on the list and publishes their details on its website. For people on the list, a power of attorney cannot be used to conclude transactions on their behalf or to perform other legally significant acts in their interests. The law does not affect anyone else.
