    Prenuptial agreement (separación de bienes) in Spain: how to protect your assets

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~5 min read
    In Spain, the default regime is joint ownership by the spouses (sociedad de gananciales): everything acquired during the marriage is split in half on divorce. If you have a business, property or an inheritance, or you live in Catalonia, where the rules are different, the default regime works against you. Below: how separación de bienes differs from gananciales, when it makes sense to arrange it, which documents to gather and how the agreement is recognised in Ukraine and Russia. We handle the arrangement itself: we prepare the text, go through it with you in your language before signing, book the notary appointment and interpret at the signing.

    What separación de bienes is

    There are three matrimonial property regimes in Spain. Which one applies to you now determines what will have to be divided on divorce:

    Regime
    What is joint
    What is separate
    Sociedad de gananciales (default)
    everything acquired during the marriage
    what was owned before the marriage + inheritance, gifts and anything bought with those funds
    Separación de bienes
    nothing automatically
    everything (according to who acquired it)
    Régimen de participación
    capital growth during the marriage (50/50)
    all current assets

    Separación de bienes is established by a notarial deed, capitulaciones matrimoniales. It is a voluntary agreement: both spouses sign it, and the regime can be changed unilaterally only in the cases listed by law, usually through the courts.

    Important: regional differences. In Catalonia, separación de bienes is the default regime, with no special agreement needed. The same applies in the Balearic Islands (Mallorca, Menorca, Ibiza, Formentera). In most other regions, sociedad de gananciales applies by default, and the separate regime has to be arranged separately. Which regime applies to you now depends on the spouses' citizenship at the time of the wedding, on where you lived immediately after it and on the date of the marriage; we check this before we sit down to draft the text.

    When it makes sense to arrange one

    • One of the spouses is an entrepreneur: new business debts will not reach the other spouse's property, while a change of regime does not affect debts that already exist
    • A large gap in assets: one spouse has property/an inheritance from before the marriage
    • A marriage with a foreign element (RU/UA–Spain): the family codes differ, and a separate agreement removes the dispute over whose law applies to the spouses' property on divorce and inheritance
    • A second marriage: protecting assets from children of the first marriage
    • Divorce by agreement: divide the assets in advance, without going to court
    • Buying property: the spouses' shares are fixed at the time of the transaction rather than worked out years later

    How to arrange it, step by step

    1. First conversation: you describe your situation (before the marriage or already married, what assets you have, what you want to keep separate). 15–30 minutes
    2. We prepare the text of the agreement: in Spanish, as the Registro Civil requires, setting out which regime is established, what is included and what is not, how future acquisitions are divided. We send you an explanation of every clause in your language. 2–5 days
    3. Agreeing the text: we send the draft to both spouses and adjust the wording until the text matches what you have agreed between you
    4. We book the notary appointment: usually for the day after the text is agreed
    5. 30–60 minutes at the notary's office: you both come with your passports and marriage certificate (if already married) and sign; we interpret aloud so that each of you understands what you are signing
    6. Apostille: if the agreement will be needed in Ukraine/Russia, we arrange it ourselves. 1–3 days
    7. Registration in the Registro Civil (if your marriage is recorded there): the notary sends a copy of the agreement there on the day of signing, and we track it until the annotation is made, after which the new property regime appears in the marriage extract

    Documents

    • Passports of both spouses
    • Marriage certificate: if the marriage is already registered. From another country: with an apostille and a sworn translation; we take care of both
    • Empadronamiento (municipal registration) of at least one of the spouses: the notary may ask for a recent certificate, usually no more than 3 months old
    • NIE/TIE for foreign spouses
    • List of assets (not compulsory, but safer to have): property, bank accounts, business shares. We put it together with you so that no vague wording is left in the agreement
    • Previous marriage agreement: if you already arranged something in your country of citizenship, a copy with an apostille is needed: the notary must see exactly what you are changing

    Recognition in Ukraine, Russia and other countries

    Capitulaciones matrimoniales with an apostille from the notarial college are accepted in the countries of the Hague Convention: Ukraine, Russia, Kazakhstan and most EU countries. We arrange the apostille together with the agreement so that you do not have to come back to it a year later, and the translation is done according to the rules of the country of destination: for Ukraine, as a rule, in Ukraine, certified by a notary.

    What to consider before taking the agreement abroad:

    • To dispose of property in Ukraine/Russia, the agreement must be submitted to the local Register: we put together a set of documents that meets its requirements in advance
    • Some countries apply the principle of “the law of the place where the marriage was concluded”: a Spanish agreement outweighs the Ukrainian/Russian default regime
    • With dual citizenship, the rules of both countries apply: we look at your particular combination before we sit down to draft the text

    Common mistakes

    Mistake 1: arranging it without an inventory of assets

    “Separate property” without a list leaves loopholes: on divorce it is easy to argue over what exactly belongs to whom. We attach an inventory of assets as at the date of signing to the agreement, and there is nothing left to argue about.

    Mistake 2: forgetting about registration in the Registro Civil

    After signing at the notary's office, the agreement must be registered in the Registro Civil where the marriage is recorded; otherwise it does not appear in marriage extracts, and for a bank or a registrar the regime remains the same as before. The notary sends a copy there on the day of signing, and we track the registration until the annotation is made, so you will not have to go there separately.

    Mistake 3: going to the notary without an interpreter

    The agreement itself is drawn up in Spanish: that is how the Registro Civil accepts it. But if one of the spouses does not speak Spanish, the notary will not authenticate the signature without an interpreter, and the day will be wasted. We go through the text with you in advance in your language and interpret at the signing itself.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is separación de bienes in Spain?

    Separación de bienes (separate ownership) is a matrimonial property regime under which each spouse keeps what they acquire during the marriage. It is an alternative to the standard regime of sociedad de gananciales (joint ownership). It is arranged through a notarial deed, capitulaciones matrimoniales.

    When does it make sense to sign a marital agreement in Spain?

    Before the marriage: if one of the spouses has a business, property or expected assets. During the marriage: if the situation has changed (setting up a company, an inheritance, a move). On divorce: to divide assets by agreement. It is also needed in Catalonia, but for a different purpose: there separación de bienes is the default regime, and joint ownership requires arranging the opposite.

    Can a marital agreement be signed after the wedding?

    Yes. Capitulaciones matrimoniales can be arranged at any point in the marriage, before or after registration. The document takes effect from the moment it is signed before a notary, and one signed before the wedding takes effect from the day of the wedding, provided it takes place within a year. Assets accumulated before signing remain under the previous regime (unless the agreement states otherwise).

    Is a Spanish marital agreement recognised in Ukraine and Russia?

    Yes. After an apostille from the notarial college and a translation according to the rules of the country of destination, the agreement is recognised in Ukraine, Russia and other Hague Convention countries. Important: when the agreement is used to dispose of property abroad, specific features of local family law may apply, and this needs to be taken into account.

    How long does it take to arrange a marital agreement?

    Preparing the text takes 2–5 days (depending on how complex the assets are). Signing at the notary's office takes 30–60 minutes; both spouses attend in person. If you need an apostille for use abroad, add 1–3 days.
