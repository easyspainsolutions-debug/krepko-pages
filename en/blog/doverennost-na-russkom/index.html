    What a power of attorney in Spain (poder notarial) is

    A power of attorney is a document by which you transfer to another person the right to act on your behalf: to sign a contract, sell property, open an account, collect a document from a registry, or represent you in court or before a public authority. In Spain, such a power of attorney is executed before a notary (poder notarial): if it is needed for court, for managing property, for transactions executed by notarial deed, or for acts affecting third parties, the law requires a public document.

    A power of attorney can be special (for one specific action, for example, selling the flat at address X) or general (for a wide range of powers). A general one costs slightly more at the notary’s because the text is longer. We prepare both and work out which one you need from your task: for a single transaction a special one is usually enough.

    When you need a power of attorney from Spain

    Most often people come to us with these tasks:

    • Selling property in Ukraine or Russia — you are in Spain, and a relative in that country handles the sale for you
    • Collecting a pension, benefit or document at a bank/registry/civil registry office (ZAGS) — the representative collects it for you
    • Buying property in Spain remotely — you give an estate agent or a lawyer the right to sign before a notary
    • Opening a bank account — the representative opens an account for you in a country you cannot fly to
    • Divorce, inheritance, representation in court — a lawyer acts on your behalf
    • Managing a car — a friend/relative handles the car abroad. And Ukrainian driving licences can be exchanged for Spanish ones through the DGT (the Spanish traffic authority).
    • Obtaining an apostille or certificates — a representative in Ukraine collects them for you from the Ministry of Justice
    The main rule: a power of attorney from Spain works in Ukraine, Russia and other Hague Convention countries only with an apostille from the College of Notaries. Without an apostille, the document is accepted only within Spain. More on the apostille below.

    The language problem: why everyone does it only in Spanish

    Spanish notaries, as a rule, draw up powers of attorney in Spanish — it is their working language. If you do not speak Spanish and the notary does not know your language, an interpreter is needed at the signing. The law does not require a sworn one: you choose the interpreter, and they sign the document together with you, while the notary may ask for someone else if they doubt the interpreter’s competence. Such a person has to be found and their schedule matched with the notary’s — this is often a separate expense and +1–2 days.

    We prepare the text of the power of attorney directly in the language you need (RUS/UKR/ENG). The notary signs the document, and our representative acts as interpreter at the moment of signing (under Spanish law, this role can be performed by anyone you appoint who speaks Spanish and your language). You sign a text you understand, and we check in advance the requirements of the country where the power of attorney will be presented.

    This is what the service rests on: we put together the text in your language for your task, and we have someone who will go to the notary with you and interpret. You will not have to look for an interpreter or fit in with their schedule.

    A power of attorney from Spain for Ukraine/Russia — what matters about the apostille

    An apostille is a stamp that confirms the authenticity of the notary’s signature so that the document can be used in another country. Spain and Ukraine have both signed the 1961 Hague Convention, so an apostille is enough — no additional consular legalisation is needed.

    In Spain, the apostille on notarial documents is affixed by the College of Notaries (Colegio Notarial). We take care of submission and collection; the timeframe is 1–3 days. Doing it yourself usually stretches to a week or two: an appointment, travel, a second visit for the finished stamp.

    Important: the apostille is affixed to the original document. If a copy or translation is later certified by a Spanish notary, the copy or translation needs its own apostille (for the state it is a different document), whereas a translation certified in Ukraine or Russia does not need an apostille. That is why we handle the whole chain in one go: original → apostille → notarised translation (if needed) → delivery.

    Common mistakes and how to avoid them

    Mistake 1: trying to sign without an interpreter

    If you do not speak Spanish and there is neither a text in your language nor an interpreter, the notary will turn you away. Under Spanish law, the notary must make sure that the signatory understands the text. That is why we prepare the text in your language in advance, and our representative interprets at the signing.

    Mistake 2: getting the passport details wrong

    The recipient of the power of attorney (the person to whom the powers are granted) must be stated exactly as in their passport: first name, surname, series, number, issuing authority. Any typo, and the document will not be accepted in Ukraine. That is why, before the notary appointment, we check every letter against a photo of the passport, with you and with the recipient.

    Mistake 3: not specifying what the powers are for

    Under Spanish law, a power of attorney “for everything” drawn up in general terms gives the representative only the right to manage your affairs: they can sell, mortgage or donate property only if this is expressly named in the text. That is why specific actions need to be listed (sell, collect, represent). The more precise the wording, the lower the risk of refusal at the bank or the registry. We put together the list of powers for your task and add related ones that are usually asked for next.

    Mistake 4: forgetting about the period of validity

    A power of attorney can be issued for a stated period (for example, 6 months) or with no time limit. If it will be presented in Russia, it is better to state the period: under Russian law, a power of attorney with no stated period is valid for one year from the date it was issued. For a one-off task an open-ended one is excessive; for long-term representation, on the contrary, it is convenient. We discuss the period with you while preparing the text.

    Frequently asked questions

    How can I find out the cost of a power of attorney in Spain?
    Message us and we will calculate it for your case. The cost is made up of three parts: our work (depends on the tier: Spanish / your language / urgent), the notarial fee (according to the official tariff; the amount depends on the length of the document and the number of copies) and the apostille (if needed to use the document outside Spain). All amounts are shown transparently as separate items, with no surprises.
    How long does it take to get a power of attorney?
    Signing at the notary’s — 30 minutes. Preparing the text — 1–2 working days. Apostille (if needed) — an additional 1–3 working days.
    Can I sign a power of attorney in Ukrainian or Russian?
    Yes. We prepare the text directly in the language you need (Russian, Ukrainian or English), and the notary signs in the presence of a Krepko representative who acts as interpreter. Outside Spain, the power of attorney is used with an apostille, and we check the receiving party’s requirements for the text in advance.
    Does a power of attorney need an apostille?
    Yes, if the document will be used outside Spain (in Ukraine, Russia or other Hague Convention countries). The apostille on notarial documents is affixed by the College of Notaries. Within Spain, no apostille is needed.
    Can a power of attorney be arranged remotely?
    A general power of attorney is signed only in person: the law does not allow it to be executed by video call. But since 9 November 2023 the law has allowed a power of attorney for specific actions, for court or for public authorities to be executed by video call with a notary too, if you have an electronic identification recognised in Spain (in practice, a digital certificate or Cl@ve). We discuss this option individually. Everything else (preparing the text, agreeing it with the recipient, sending the finished document by courier) we do remotely. If you sign in person, one visit is needed: 30 minutes at the notary’s.
