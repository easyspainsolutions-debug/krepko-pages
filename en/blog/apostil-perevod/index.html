    Apostille and sworn translation in Spain: what they are and why you need them

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~6 min read
    “I need an apostille” and “I need a sworn translation” are the two phrases we hear from clients most often, and behind almost every one of them there is confusion: people think one replaces the other. These are different documents, and in most cases you need both. Below is how to work out what is required in your particular situation, in what order it is done, how long it takes and which part of the route we take on ourselves.

    What an apostille is

    An apostille is a special stamp or sticker on an official document that confirms its authenticity for use in another country. An apostille certifies not the content of the document but the authenticity of the signature and seal of the authority that issued it.

    The apostille is governed by the 1961 Hague Convention. Spain, Ukraine and most countries of the world are parties to the convention — this means that an apostille affixed in Ukraine is recognised in Spain, and vice versa.

    Which documents need an apostille

    • Birth certificate
    • Marriage or divorce certificate
    • Death certificate
    • Education diploma
    • Criminal record certificate
    • Documents from the civil registry (ZAGS)
    • Notarised documents
    Important: the apostille is affixed in the country that issued the document. A Ukrainian birth certificate — apostille in Ukraine. A Spanish notarial document — apostille in Spain. We cover both directions: we handle Spanish documents through the College of Notaries or the Ministry of Justice in one of 6 cities, and Ukrainian ones remotely, through the Krepko team in Ukraine. You do not need to travel anywhere in either case.

    What a sworn translation (traducción jurada) is

    A sworn translation (traducción jurada) is a translation of a document made by a certified translator accredited by the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Spain. The translation bears the translator's seal and signature, which confirm that the translation is accurate and corresponds to the original.

    This is not an ordinary translation from an agency. A simple translation without certification will not be accepted by a public office, however accurate it may be: they look at the seal, not at the quality of the text. A translation can be certified in two equally valid ways — by a sworn translator from the register of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or by a notary in the country that issued the document, with an apostille on the translation itself. Their legal force is the same; the difference is in timeframe and cost.

    When a sworn translation is needed

    • When submitting documents to Extranjería, the immigration office (residence permit, arraigo)
    • When opening a bank account (foreign income documents)
    • When taking out a mortgage or for property transactions
    • When enrolling children in school (foreign birth certificates)
    • For notarial transactions involving foreign documents
    • When registering a marriage in Spain

    Apostille vs sworn translation: what is the difference

    Apostille
    • Certifies the authenticity of the document
    • Affixed in the country of origin
    • Does not translate the text
    • Done once — valid for good
    • Without it, a foreign document is not recognised
    Sworn translation
    • Translates the text into Spanish
    • Done in Spain
    • Does not confirm the authenticity of the original
    • Sometimes needs renewing
    • Without it, Spaniards will not understand the content

    The formula is simple: apostille + sworn translation = a fully legalised document for a Spanish institution. One without the other is, as a rule, not accepted — and this is exactly where people are most often turned away. We check that the set is complete before submission so that you do not have to go to the office a second time.

    Where to get an apostille on Ukrainian documents

    In Ukraine, the apostille is affixed by the authority responsible for the type of document: for certificates from the civil registry office (DRACS), court and notarial documents and archival certificates — the Ministry of Justice of Ukraine (through its regional departments or online through the register); for diplomas — the Ministry of Education and Science; for the criminal record certificate — the Ministry of Internal Affairs (MVS); for most others — the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The service is called апостилювання (apostyliuvannia, “apostille certification”). The timeframe is from 1 to 10 working days; at the Ministry of Justice, up to 3.

    You do not need to travel to Ukraine — we handle this task remotely. The Krepko team in Ukraine obtains the document locally, has the apostille affixed, if necessary arranges a notarised translation with a double apostille and sends the package to Spain. Delivery itself takes 5–7 working days; we state the overall timeframe before we start; your part is correspondence and confirming the details. If the document is already with you in Spain but a Ukrainian apostille is needed, we prepare a power of attorney before a notary here and follow the same route.

    Apostille on Russian documents: Russia is a party to the Hague Convention. The apostille is affixed by the authority responsible for that type of document (for example, the civil registry (ZAGS), the Interior Ministry (MVD), education authorities, the Ministry of Justice) or through a multifunctional centre (MFC). Some Spanish institutions currently require additional confirmations for Russian documents — we look at each case separately and tell you before we start what exactly the specific office will ask for.

    Where to get a sworn translation in Spain

    A sworn translation is done only by a traductor-intérprete jurado — a specialist from the official register of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs. The list is publicly available on exteriores.gob.es.

    There are two ways. The first is to look for a sworn translator yourself (Barcelona, Madrid, Alicante, Valencia, Málaga, Murcia). The market price is €40–80 per page; the timeframe from Ukrainian is usually about two weeks, from Russian it is faster. The second is to hand the task to us: we find a translator from the register in the city you need, keep to the timeframe and hand you the finished translation together with the original. If the document is from Ukraine and there is time before submission, we will suggest a cheaper route through the Krepko team in Ukraine: a notarised translation plus a double apostille — for Spanish public offices it is equally valid.

    Typical situations: what you need to collect and what we take on

    Applying for arraigo social

    You will need a criminal record certificate from the countries where you lived for five years before entering Spain, with an apostille + sworn translation into Spanish. The validity period for the certificate is set by Extranjería — usually no more than 3 months at the time of submission, so the order of steps matters more than speed here. We handle the whole route: we order the certificate in Ukraine remotely, have the apostille affixed, arrange the translation and hand you the finished package by the submission date. From you we need the applicant's details and a scan of the passport.

    Enrolling a child in school

    Birth certificate: apostille + sworn translation. In some municipalities a certified copy is enough, but the full set is the safer option: there is no time to redo documents in the middle of enrolment. The same applies to family reunification. We check your municipality's requirements before we start and put the package together to meet them straight away.

    Recognition of a diploma (homologación)

    Diploma with its supplement: apostille + sworn translation. The homologación procedure itself at a Spanish ministry (for university degrees — the Ministry of Science, Innovation and Universities) takes from 6 months to 2 years, and we look at it separately for your diploma. But we prepare the basic package for submission in full: an apostille on the original and the supplement, a translation by a sworn translator, certified copies — so that the years-long queue at the ministry does not begin with the documents being returned over a formality.

    A transaction before a notary

    For most notarial transactions (powers of attorney, purchase and sale), foreign documents are needed with a sworn translation. The notary will require an apostille only if the document is itself a legal act, for example a foreign marriage certificate. When we handle the transaction, we prepare the translations and the apostille in advance: on the day of signing you arrive with the documents ready. More about the notary.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is an apostille and why is it needed?
    An apostille is a stamp or sticker on an official document confirming the authenticity of the signature and seal of the authority that issued it. Without an apostille, a foreign document in Spain (or a Spanish one abroad) has no legal force. It is governed by the 1961 Hague Convention.
    Which countries accept an apostille from Spain?
    An apostille from Spain is accepted by all 125+ countries that are parties to the 1961 Hague Convention — including Ukraine, Russia, Kazakhstan, the EU countries, the USA and the UK. No additional consular legalisation is required. For countries outside the convention (China until November 2023, the UAE), full consular legalisation is needed.
    How much does an apostille cost in Spain in 2026?
    The apostille on notarial documents is affixed by the College of Notaries (Colegio Notarial): in the ordinary procedure within 3–5 working days, urgently within 1–2 days. The apostille on civil registry documents (birth and marriage certificates) is affixed free of charge through the regional Tribunal Superior de Justicia. We submit in any of the 6 cities; the overall timeframe is 1–3 working days. We state the cost in advance as a single amount, with no extra charges along the way. Every case is unique — the final amount is given after a short conversation on WhatsApp.
    Is a translation needed after the apostille?
    Yes, if a document in a foreign language is presented in Spain, a sworn translation (traducción jurada) is needed, made once the apostille has been affixed. Both the document itself and the text of the apostille are translated as a single piece of work. A simple translation from an agency will not do — certification is required. There are two options, and they are equivalent: a sworn translator from the register of the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs, or a notarised translation with an apostille on the translation itself. For documents from Ukraine, the second route is cheaper and faster, and the package has the same legal force.
    Where is the apostille affixed to notarial documents?
    On notarial documents (powers of attorney, statements, copies), the apostille is affixed by the College of Notaries (Colegio Notarial) — its dean or a notary to whom the dean has delegated this. We handle the whole route in full: you do not need to go anywhere. We state the cost in advance as a single amount, with no extra charges along the way.
    What should I do if the document is already in Spain but the apostille has to come from the issuing country?
    A common situation: a Ukrainian diploma, certificate or official statement is already in your hands, and travelling to Ukraine is inconvenient. We arrange a notarial power of attorney in Spain, the Krepko team in Ukraine obtains a duplicate or has the apostille affixed to the original, then returns the document to Spain. Delivery itself takes 5–7 working days; we state the overall timeframe in advance. For documents from Ukraine — 100% prepayment when the order is confirmed (this is due to real non-refundable costs on the Ukrainian side). The final amount is given before we start.
