    Renewing a digital nomad residence permit: when to apply and what UGE checks

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. The international teleworker residence permit (the digital nomad visa) is renewed for two years if the conditions on which it was granted still hold (Articles 74 quinquies.3 and 76.3 of Ley 14/2013). An application filed before the authorisation expires extends its validity until a decision is made. You can also apply within 90 days after expiry, but then a fine is possible. Besides income and work for a foreign company, registration with Seguridad Social remains a condition: without it the authorisation may be terminated after a hearing, and for an autónomo (self-employed person), together with the family's authorisations. Rules checked on 1 November 2026.

    The rules on digital nomads have been in force in Spain since 23 December 2022, and an authorisation issued in Spain lasts a maximum of three years. So for those who received it for the full term in 2023, it expires in 2026, and for them this is the first renewal. It is not a formality: UGE, the unit of the Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration that handles these cases, checks again whether the conditions on which you were granted the residence permit still hold.

    Below we explain when to apply and what exactly is checked, and separately what to do if there was a gap in your registration with Seguridad Social (Social Security). We checked the rules on 1 November 2026 against the BOE and UGE documents.

    How long it is renewed for and who decides

    A nomad authorisation issued in Spain is valid for up to three years (Article 74 quinquies.2 of Ley 14/2013). It is renewed for two years, and it can be renewed again as long as the conditions still hold (Article 74 quinquies.3). Applications are handled by UGE (Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos), and the decision is made by the Directorate-General for Migration (Article 76.1).

    • Filing. By law, renewals are handled only electronically (Article 76.3).
    • Decision deadline. For the authorisations in this section the law sets 20 working days from electronic filing; if there is no answer, the application is deemed approved (Article 76.1 of Ley 14/2013 and Article 30.2 of Ley 39/2015). Article 76.3 does not name a separate deadline for renewal.
    • New card. After approval a new TIE (foreigner identity card) is issued: it is mandatory for authorisations longer than six months (Article 76.2).

    When to apply

    Article 76.3 does not set a window of “so many days before expiry”. It says something else: if an application has been filed, the authorisation remains valid until a decision on it. The same applies when the application is filed within 90 days after expiry, but in that case infringement proceedings may be opened. The immigration law classes filing for renewal up to three months late as a minor infringement (Article 52.b of Ley Orgánica 4/2000), and a minor infringement carries a fine of up to €500 (Article 55.1.a).

    Hence the practical rule: apply before the expiry date of the authorisation, and start gathering documents two to three months in advance. The “60 days before” window that is often quoted refers in the law to a different case: that is when holders of a consular nomad visa apply to UGE for an authorisation (Article 74 quater.3), and that is an initial application, not a renewal; for renewal, 60 days is named in a general UGE guide from 2018, which does not yet cover nomads.

    What UGE will check

    Renewal is granted if the conditions that gave rise to the right to the authorisation still hold (Article 76.3). The Directorate-General for Migration may request information to check this. And under DA 7ª (the seventh additional provision) of Ley 14/2013 the conditions must be maintained throughout the term of the authorisation, and the authorities may check whether they are being met. UGE does not publish a separate guide for renewing a nomad authorisation: its page on teleworkers has only guides for initial applications and FAQs, so the document package is put together based on the conditions.

    • Work for a foreign company. Under an employment contract you may work only for that company. Under a services contract Spanish clients are also allowed, but no more than 20% of all your professional activity (Article 74 bis of Ley 14/2013, question 14 of the UGE FAQ).
    • Income. The threshold is 200% of the minimum wage (SMI) for the applicant, plus 75% of the SMI for the first family member and 25% for each additional one (question 19 of the UGE FAQ). In 2026, for a single applicant this is €34,188 a year gross (RD 126/2026).
    • Seguridad Social. Registration is required throughout the term of the authorisation; more on this in the next section.
    • Taxes. Ley 14/2013 expressly states that tax obligations and Seguridad Social obligations continue to apply (Article 62.5).
    Which SMI will be used at renewal. It has not been officially clarified whether income is compared with the SMI on the filing date or with the one under which the authorisation was granted. In its guide for initial applications UGE refers to the “current” SMI, so it is safer to calculate on that basis. The SMI for 2027 had not been approved as of 1 November 2026. The threshold is linked to the SMI, so it will change when the new decree is published in the BOE. If your filing falls in early 2027, allow a margin. The amounts for families and the recalculation formula are given in the article on income for the nomad visa.

    Separately, check what has changed during the term of the authorisation. Any change in the conditions on which it was granted must be reported to UGE within 30 days (DA 7ª.2): for example, a change of company or a switch from an employment contract to a services contract. According to the UGE guide, such a change is filed as an initial application. If you did not do this, sort the matter out before applying for renewal, not after a request from UGE.

    A gap in Seguridad Social: why it is risky

    Since you work from Spain, registration with Spanish Seguridad Social is mandatory (question 6 of the UGE FAQ):

    • as an employee, the general scheme: the foreign company registers with Seguridad Social as an employer without a presence in Spain. Instead, you can submit a certificate from your own country under a social security agreement, if it expressly states that it covers remote work from Spain;
    • as an autónomo, only RETA, the scheme for the self-employed; you cannot stay in your own country's system under an agreement.

    According to the UGE guide, once registered you are obliged to pay contributions, and breaching this condition may be grounds for terminating the authorisation. Termination is not automatic: DA 7ª.4 requires a reasoned decision and a prior hearing where you can give explanations. For autónomos, UGE puts it more strictly in the FAQ: if a check shows that you did not register immediately after receiving the authorisation, it is terminated, and neither you nor your family members will be able to live and work in Spain (question 7).

    A certificate from Ukraine instead of Spanish registration. If you work as an employee with a certificate under the 1996 Spain–Ukraine agreement, check its validity. The agreement's rule on posted workers is designed for temporary work of up to two years; it is extended for another two years if the work has taken longer for unforeseen reasons and the Spanish side agrees (Articles 7.1–7.2). The authorisation, however, is valid for up to three years, and between the end of the certificate and the end of the authorisation there may be a period without registration.

    They may look not only at the filing date (DA 7ª, see above): the UGE FAQ expressly refers to checking whether an autónomo registered immediately after receiving the authorisation (question 7). So before applying, request a report of your employment history (informe de vida laboral) from Seguridad Social and check the dates: registration should begin immediately after receiving the authorisation and continue without gaps. If there is a gap, do not apply blindly: how to close and explain it is decided by a lawyer based on your documents.

    Taxes

    Anyone who spends more than 183 days in Spain in a calendar year is its tax resident (Article 9.1.a of the IRPF law) and, as a rule, files an annual tax return with the tax office (Agencia Tributaria); anyone who was registered in RETA for at least part of the year files one in any case (Article 96.2). The law does not give a separate list of tax documents for renewal. But in the guide for family members UGE expressly states that it may additionally require proof that the main applicant has no debts to the tax office or Seguridad Social.

    So before applying we advise obtaining certificates of no debts (certificado de estar al corriente) from the tax office and from Seguridad Social. That way a problem comes to light for you, not in a request from UGE. If not all tax returns were filed, resolve this before renewal: we go through such matters together with a tax adviser.

    Family members' authorisations

    Family members receive authorisations as the family of a teleworker (Article 62.4 of Ley 14/2013), and these authorisations depend on yours. This can be seen from question 7 of the UGE FAQ in the section above. When renewing, bear in mind two more things:

    • the income threshold is calculated for the whole family, and if the family has grown, the threshold has grown with it;
    • check separately the expiry dates of the cards of family members who applied later than you.

    Who can be included as family and which documents each person needs, we explain in the article on the nomad visa with family.

    If UGE sends a request for documents

    If documents are missing, UGE sends a request (requerimiento). By law you have 10 working days to respond (Articles 68.1 and 30.2 of Ley 39/2015), otherwise the application may be deemed withdrawn, and the decision deadline may be suspended for that period (Article 22.1.a). How to meet the deadline if you need documents from another country with an apostille and sworn translation, we explain in the article on UGE requests for the nomad visa.

    After five years: long-term residence

    You will not have to renew the nomad authorisation every two years forever. After five years of legal and continuous residence in Spain you can apply for long-term residence (Article 183 of RD 1155/2024). It is permanent and gives the right to live and work in Spain on the same terms as Spaniards (Article 182), without being tied to a foreign company. How the five years are counted and which trips do not break continuity, we explain in the article on long-term residence.

    How we handle the renewal

    1. Review. We check the dates of the whole family's authorisations, registration with Seguridad Social against the employment history report, income against the current SMI and changes during the term of the authorisation.
    2. Weak points before filing. If there is a gap in registration, an unreported change or an unfiled tax return, we say so plainly and suggest a course of action.
    3. Documents. We gather the package for you and your family members. According to the UGE guide, documents in a foreign language are submitted with a translation into Spanish by a sworn translator (traductor jurado); for a CV a simple translation is enough.
    4. Filing and requests. The application is filed electronically by an immigration lawyer; if UGE sends a request, we prepare the response on time.
    5. Cards. After approval we accompany you through getting the new TIEs for you and your family.

    The decision is made by the state, and we will not promise it. Our job is to make sure the application does not stumble over a formality such as a missed date or a missing certificate.

    Frequently asked questions

    When should I apply to renew the nomad visa?
    Before the expiry date of the authorisation. The law does not set a window of “so many days before”: under Article 76.3 of Ley 14/2013 a filed application extends the validity of the authorisation until a decision is made. You can also apply within 90 days after expiry, but then infringement proceedings may be opened: the immigration law classes a delay of up to three months as a minor infringement, which carries a fine of up to €500 (Articles 52.b and 55.1.a of LO 4/2000). It is best to start gathering documents two to three months in advance.
    How long is a digital nomad residence permit renewed for?
    For two years, and it can be renewed again as long as the conditions on which the authorisation was granted still hold (Articles 74 quinquies.3 and 76.3 of Ley 14/2013). After five years of legal and continuous residence you can apply for long-term residence: it is permanent, and it no longer needs to be renewed every two years.
    I had a gap in RETA. Will the authorisation be renewed?
    The risk is serious. Registration and payment of contributions are a condition of the authorisation: according to the UGE guide, breaching them may be grounds for termination, and for an autónomo who did not register, the UGE FAQ says the authorisation is terminated together with the family's authorisations. Termination is not automatic: under DA 7ª of Ley 14/2013 a reasoned decision and a hearing are required. The conditions must be maintained throughout the term of the authorisation, so they may look not only at the filing date. Request an employment history report (vida laboral) and sort out the gap before applying; the application is handled by an immigration lawyer.
    What income is needed for renewal in 2027?
    The threshold is 200% of the SMI for the applicant plus additions for the family. In 2026 this is €34,188 a year gross for a single applicant. The SMI for 2027 had not been approved as of 1 November 2026, and which SMI income is measured against at renewal has not been officially clarified. It is safer to go by the SMI in force on the filing date and allow a margin.
    Can I also work for Spanish clients with this residence permit?
    Under a services contract, yes, if Spanish clients account for no more than 20% of your professional activity. Under an employment contract you may work only for the foreign company (Article 74 bis of Ley 14/2013).
    I changed company while holding the authorisation. Will this prevent renewal?
    Not necessarily, if the new job meets the same conditions. But a change in the conditions must be reported to UGE within 30 days (DA 7ª of Ley 14/2013). If you did not do this, sort the matter out before applying for renewal: we look at such cases based on the documents, and the application is handled by an immigration lawyer.
