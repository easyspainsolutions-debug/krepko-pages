    Municipal registration in Spain (padrón municipal): why you need it and how to get it

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    In short. Municipal registration (padrón municipal) is the mandatory municipal register of residents of Spain; it is handled by the town hall (ayuntamiento), and for the appointment you need your passport and a document for your accommodation, either a rental contract or a letter of consent from the owner; no NIE is required. A padrón certificate is usually accepted for 3 months from the date of issue, and foreigners from outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland who have no long-term residence permit and no EU family member card must confirm their registration every 2 years. Checked against Ley 7/1985 (BOE) on 24 October 2026. Source: BOE — Ley 7/1985, de 2 de abril, Reguladora de las Bases del Régimen Local (texto consolidado, arts. 15–16).

    What the padrón is and why you need it

    The padrón municipal (officially, empadronamiento) is the municipal register of residents. Everyone who lives in Spain is required to register with their municipality (ayuntamiento). Together with the NIE/TIE, the padrón makes up the basic set of a resident's documents. Your status is not what matters here: you can register on a tourist visa, with temporary protection or with no status documents at all; at the ayuntamiento a passport is enough.

    You receive a certificado de empadronamiento (certificate) or a volante de empadronamiento (extract). Both documents confirm your address and the date from which you have lived here, and it is this date that Extranjería (the immigration office) later counts when you apply for a residence permit through arraigo social (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain). The earlier you register, the earlier your clock starts.

    What the padrón opens up

    • Healthcare (tarjeta sanitaria): without a padrón you will not be issued a public health insurance card or assigned to a health centre (centro de salud)
    • School: a child is admitted to a state school with priority based on the padrón of the catchment area the school belongs to; your registered address often decides where your child goes
    • Residence permit through arraigo social: you usually prove 2 years of continuous residence with your padrón history; Extranjería may read a gap in it as a gap in your residence
    • Municipal services and benefits: transport discounts, library membership, participation in social programmes
    • Opening a bank account: some banks ask for a padrón certificate when you open an account as a non-resident or when your status changes; we check the requirements of the specific branch before your visit

    How to get your padrón: a step-by-step guide

    Step 1: booking an appointment

    The padrón is handled by the ayuntamiento, the municipal administration for your area. In Barcelona, Madrid and Valencia you need to book an appointment in advance through the city council's website or app, and free slots go quickly. In small municipalities you can often be seen without an appointment during set opening hours. Booking is where people get stuck the longest: we catch the nearest slot and send you the date, the address and a list of what to bring.

    Step 2: preparing the documents

    What an adult is asked for at the appointment:

    1. A passport or other identity document (original + copy)
    2. A document confirming your address, usually a rental contract (contrato de alquiler)
    3. If there is no contract, the owner's written consent (autorización del propietario) together with a copy of their DNI/NIE and a nota simple (Land Registry extract) for the property

    For a child, add the birth certificate and both parents' documents. A foreign certificate first needs an apostille and a sworn translation. This is a separate chain, and we handle all of it: we request a duplicate and arrange the apostille and a translation that the ayuntamiento will accept without questions.

    Step 3: the appointment at the ayuntamiento

    At the appointment you fill in a form (hoja padronal), which is signed by all the adults registering at the address. The appointment itself takes 10–20 minutes. At the end you are either given a certificate (volante) on the spot or asked to come back in a few days for the full certificate. If your Spanish is not enough, we go to the appointment with you and speak for you.

    Can you get a padrón without an official rental contract?

    This is the question people bring to us most often. Yes, you can: the law does not require a rental contract registered with INCASOL or the tax office. One of two options is enough:

    • A letter from the owner (carta de acogida or autorización) in which they allow you to live at the address and register there; the signature is notarised or a copy of the owner's identity document is attached
    • Some municipalities accept informal agreements (habitación, a room); requirements vary from town to town, and we find out what your ayuntamiento requires before you put your file together
    If the landlord objects: offer them a carta de acogida, a simple letter of consent instead of a contract. Many landlords do not want to make the rental visible, but have no problem signing permission for you to register. We give you a ready-made text of such a letter in advance so that you do not have to rewrite it after being turned away at the counter.

    Updating and renewing the padrón

    The padrón does not “expire” on its own (except for the registration of foreigners from outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland without larga duración (long-term residence) and without an EU family member card, which has to be renewed every 2 years), but when you move you need to update it by registering at the new address. When you move to another town, you do not need to deregister at the old ayuntamiento: registration in the new place cancels the old one automatically.

    For arraigo social this is the decisive detail: Extranjería looks at the continuity of your registration. A gap between padrón registrations can reset the count of those 2 years, and restoring it retroactively is almost impossible. If you move, update your padrón straight away, without leaving a “gap”. If a gap has already happened, message us on WhatsApp: we will see how to cover it.

    How to get a padrón certificate

    For a residence permit, the bank or school, the fact of registration alone is sometimes not enough: you may be asked for a recent certificate, certificado o volante de empadronamiento. You can get it in three ways:

    • In person at the ayuntamiento, usually free of charge or for a small fee (€1–3)
    • Online through the municipal portal; you need a digital certificate or Cl@ve (we arrange the certificate itself separately)
    • At self-service kiosks in some municipal offices

    A certificate is usually accepted for 3 months from the date of issue. Get it right before you submit your documents, not in advance: you may be asked to replace an out-of-date certificate, which means an extra request and a delay.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can you get a padrón without a rental contract?
    Yes. You can register with a letter from the owner of the property (autorización del propietario) together with their DNI/NIE and a nota simple for the property. Municipalities also register people with no status and no NIE.
    Do you need an NIE to get a padrón?
    No, you can get a padrón with just a passport, even without an NIE, a residence permit or temporary protection. On the contrary, a padrón is often needed later to obtain an NIE or the tarjeta sanitaria and to apply for a residence permit.
    How often do you need to renew the padrón?
    Foreigners from outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland without larga duración and without an EU family member card must renew their registration every 2 years, otherwise the municipality may remove them from the register. EU citizens do not need to renew their registration, but the town hall may ask them to confirm in writing that they still live here.
    What does the padrón give you in Spain?
    The padrón gives you access to the tarjeta sanitaria, state school enrolment for your child, municipal benefits and opening a bank account. Your padrón history is used when applying for arraigo social.
