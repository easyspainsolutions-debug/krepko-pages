    Family reunification in Spain (reagrupación familiar): who can apply and how

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    Reagrupación familiar (family reunification) is the procedure by which a person with a residence permit in Spain brings their spouse, children or parents to join them. Refusals are rare, but almost always for one of four reasons: not enough income, the housing failed the check, the application lacks an apostille or a sworn translation. Below: who is entitled to apply, how much you need to earn for each family member, what documents to gather and how long to wait for a decision. We handle such cases and along the way point out the places where people usually lose months.

    What reagrupación familiar is and who can apply for it

    You can apply if you already live in Spain legally: with a residence permit, either temporary (residencia temporal) or long-term (residencia de larga duración). The minimum period of residence is 1 year (and an application to renew your residence permit already filed), but it is safer to apply once you have 2 years behind you and at least one confirmed renewal: Extranjería (the immigration office) has fewer questions about such cases.

    Temporary protection for Ukrainians is not an ordinary residence permit, and the reunification route for it is separate. If you have temporary protection, the general procedure does not apply: see the box below.

    Who you can bring over

    If your income and floor space are sufficient, you can bring over:

    • Your spouse (a legal spouse or a registered partner in a civil union)
    • Minor children (your own, adopted, or stepchildren of your legal spouse)
    • Parents (if they are over 65, dependent on you and there are reasons why they need to live in Spain, and you hold a long-term residence permit, larga duración; under 65, only on humanitarian grounds)
    • Adult children (with a disability that requires support, or if for health reasons they cannot support themselves; also to care for you if you have been recognised as having a degree of dependency, dependencia)

    Spouses and children are the most common cases, and they are almost always approved. Parents are harder: you need to prove that they depend on you financially and that there is no one in their country of origin to take care of them. We put such cases together separately and tell you in advance whether it makes sense to apply.

    Ukrainians with temporary protection: with temporary protection status, reunification follows a separate procedure through the Directorate-General for International Protection (Dirección General de Protección Internacional), not through the Oficina de Extranjería. As a rule, it goes faster than ordinary reagrupación. We handle both routes and in our first conversation determine which one applies to you.

    Requirements for the applicant

    Check yourself against two points: these are exactly where cases fall apart.

    1. Adequate housing (informe de idoneidad de vivienda)

    The report is issued by the autonomous community or the ayuntamiento (town hall), based on documents or after an inspection of the flat: it confirms that the housing is suitable for the family. What they look at:

    • Whether there are enough rooms and floor space for all occupants, according to the rules of your autonomous community (there is no single minimum of square metres per person)
    • Separate bedrooms (spouses in one, children in their own or a shared one)
    • Kitchen, bathroom, heating, ventilation
    • Normal sanitary and living conditions

    The report is free or costs a small fee (in Madrid, about €30); by law it is issued within a month. It is worth ordering it first: if the floor space is not enough, you will find out before you spend money on apostilles and translations.

    2. Sufficient income (IPREM)

    Income is calculated not in absolute amounts but in fractions of IPREM, the Indicador Público de Renta de Efectos Múltiples, the state income indicator. For 2026 IPREM is roughly €600 a month, and all the thresholds below are calculated from it.

    The threshold is set by Article 67.1 of the Reglamento de Extranjería (Immigration Regulation, RD 1155/2024) and is calculated for the family as a whole, not for each person:

    • 150% IPREM for you and one family member being reunited, together = ~€900 a month
    • +50% IPREM for each additional family member = another ~€300 each

    What this looks like in practice:

    • You + spouse: ~€900/month needed
    • You + spouse + 1 child: ~€1,200/month needed
    • You + spouse + 2 children: ~€1,500/month needed

    For children the threshold may be reduced: the same article allows the best interests of the child to be taken into account (age, condition, relationship with you). The other requirements remain in force.

    Income is proven with an employment contract, bank statements, a tax return (Declaración de la Renta) for the last year or a certificate from your employer. If your income comes from several sources (autónomo plus employment, two jobs, your spouse's earnings), we calculate the package in advance and tell you plainly whether you reach the threshold or not.

    Stages of the reagrupación familiar procedure

    1. Preparing the documents: certificates, apostilles, sworn translations; we take this stage on entirely (2–4 weeks)
    2. Filing the application with the immigration office (Oficina de Extranjería) of your province: in person or online through the Mercurio platform
    3. Checks and review: the administration checks income, housing and documents (3–6 months, sometimes longer)
    4. Approval and notification: you are sent a letter with the decision
    5. Visa at the consulate: your family member applies for a family reunification visa at the Spanish consulate in the country where they live (2–4 weeks)
    6. Arrival in Spain: your family member arrives on the visa
    7. Getting the TIE: within a month of arrival your family member gives fingerprints at the police (Policía Nacional) and receives the plastic TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) card; we take care of booking the appointment (cita previa)

    Full list of documents

    What you gather as the applicant:

    • Passport (copy and original for verification)
    • Document proving your residence status (sticker in your passport, TIE or a Policía Nacional certificate)
    • Bank account statements for the last 3 months
    • Employment contract (copy and original)
    • Income certificate from the tax authorities (Agencia Tributaria)
    • Housing suitability report (informe de idoneidad)
    • Rental contract for the flat/house or proof of ownership
    • A criminal record certificate is not required from you: Extranjería requests the Spanish records itself
    • Health insurance (seguro de enfermedad) for you and those you are bringing over

    What is needed from your family member (spouse, child or parent):

    • Passport
    • Birth certificate: original + apostille + translation
    • Marriage certificate (if you are bringing your spouse): original + apostille + translation
    • Criminal record certificate (from the age of 18; some consulates ask for it from 16) from the countries where your family member has lived for the last 5 years
    • Medical certificate
    • Custody documents (if the children are from a previous marriage, the written consent of the other parent)
    • For parents: proof that over the last year you sent them money or paid their expenses

    Every document from abroad must have an apostille (apostilla de La Haya) and a sworn translation into Spanish; without this the application will not be accepted. We take care of the apostilles and translations: you send scans, and the documents come back ready for filing. We discuss the cost once we understand the scope.

    Processing times

    The official timeframe is 2 months. In reality it is 3–6 months, and longer in busy provinces: Andalusia (Andalucía), Madrid and Barcelona move more slowly because of the volume of applications.

    What actually shortens the timeframe:

    • Filing a complete package without mistakes straight away: one missing paper means a request to send it in and several more weeks of waiting
    • Tracking the status of your case on sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es using the expediente (case file) number from the resguardo (filing receipt)
    • Calling the Oficina de Extranjería from time to time to check the status
    • Having your package checked by people who file such cases regularly: we check the set of documents before filing so that the case is not sent back for corrections

    What to do if you are refused

    Refusals are uncommon here and almost always predictable. Typical reasons:

    • Insufficient income: we make up the shortfall with additional proof (a second job, your spouse's earnings, recent statements, a certificate from your employer)
    • Poor housing conditions: a different rental contract and a new informe de idoneidad after moving are needed
    • Criminal record issues: we look into this separately (what the record is and whether it has been expunged), and if necessary we bring in a lawyer
    • Incomplete set of documents: we gather what is missing and file again; most often an apostille or a sworn translation is missing

    A refusal can be appealed in the administrative court (juzgado contencioso-administrativo). Before going to court, we go through the wording of the decision: it is often faster to resolve the reason for the refusal and file again.

    After your family member arrives

    Then comes the part people usually forget about. When your spouse or child arrives on a family reunification visa:

    • There is one month from entry to get the residence card (TIE), with an appointment at the Policía Nacional; this deadline must not be missed
    • With the TIE in hand, a bank account can be opened; they can work from entry, without a separate permit, and children are enrolled in school without waiting for the card; we also support you with the bank account and school enrolment
    • The residence permit is granted until the same date as yours, but for no less than a year. It is renewed for 4 years, and if you hold a long-term residence permit, straight to long-term residence; renewal requires your valid residence permit and the family relationship still in place, and income and housing are not checked again
    • After 5 years of continuous residence the family applies for permanent residence (residencia de larga duración); the period counts from the date of the first card

    Frequently asked questions

    Who can be brought over under reagrupación familiar?
    A spouse or registered partner, minor children (your own, adopted or stepchildren), parents over 65 who are dependent on you (younger ones on humanitarian grounds; and only if you hold a long-term residence permit, larga duración) and, in rare cases, adult children. Spouses and children are the most common scenarios.
    What is the minimum income needed for family reunification?
    Income is calculated using IPREM (in 2026, about €600 a month) and for the family as a whole, not for each person. Under Art. 67.1 RD 1155/2024 you need 150% IPREM for you and one family member together, about €900 a month, plus 50% IPREM for each additional family member. In addition, a housing suitability report is required.
    How can a Ukrainian with temporary protection apply for reunification?
    With temporary protection status, reunification follows a special procedure through the Dirección General de Protección Internacional. This differs from ordinary reagrupación familiar and usually goes faster. We handle both scenarios.
    How long does reagrupación familiar take to process?
    The standard processing time at the Oficina de Extranjería is 2 months; in practice, 3 to 6 months. After approval, your relative has 2 months from the notification to the applicant to apply for a visa at the consulate of the country where they live.
