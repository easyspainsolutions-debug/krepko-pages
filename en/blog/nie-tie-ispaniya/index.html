    NIE and TIE in Spain: what is the difference and how to get them

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    In short. The NIE is a foreigner's personal number: it is assigned once, never changes and does not, on its own, give the right to live in Spain. The TIE is a plastic resident card that already has your NIE built into it; you apply for it with a separate appointment after your residence permit has been approved, and under the regulation (RD 1155/2024, art. 209) you have one month to apply. Checked on 24 October 2026. Source: BOE — Real Decreto 1155/2024, Reglamento de la Ley Orgánica 4/2000 (art. 205 NIE, art. 209 TIE).

    What the NIE is: simply a number

    NIE stands for Número de Identificación de Extranjero, the foreigner identification number. It looks like this: a letter, seven digits, a letter (for example, X-1234567-A). In essence it is a tax and administrative identifier, similar to the taxpayer number (INN) in CIS countries, only for foreigners.

    Remember the main point: the NIE on its own is not a residence permit. It is a unique number that is assigned to you once and stays with you for good: it does not change when you get a permanent residence permit (after you acquire Spanish nationality, the DNI number is used instead). Without an NIE you can do almost nothing in Spain: buy property, open a bank account, register a car, sign an employment contract, pay taxes.

    When you need an NIE without resident status

    If you do not plan to live in Spain permanently, you can get an NIE for one specific transaction. Buying a flat as a non-resident or opening a bank account: you will need the number in either case. This kind of NIE is issued on form EX-15 and does not give the right to live in Spain: it is only an identifier in the Spanish system. We take care of it entirely: we book the appointment, fill in the application and put together the justification for your transaction.

    What the TIE is: the resident card

    The TIE, Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero, is a physical plastic card that confirms your status as a resident of Spain. This is what you show when you need to prove that you have a residence permit. The card shows your full name, photo, fingerprint, type of permit and expiry date.

    If you have obtained a residence permit through arraigo social, temporary protection, student status or any other route, you will be issued a TIE. The NIE number is already built into the card, so you do not need to obtain it separately: it comes automatically with the TIE.

    In brief: the NIE is a number. The TIE is a card with that number and confirmation of your status in Spain. A non-resident only needs the NIE without a card; a resident receives a TIE, a card with the NIE inside.

    How to get an NIE: two ways

    Option 1: through a consulate abroad

    While you are not yet in Spain, the NIE is issued at the Spanish consulate in your country. The procedure is as follows:

    1. Book an appointment at the consulate (cita previa)
    2. Fill in form EX-15
    3. Attach your passport and a copy of it
    4. Justify why you need an NIE (a property purchase agreement, an employment contract, university enrolment documents, etc.)
    5. Pay the fee: €9.84 (tasa Modelo 790, code 012)

    Processing at the consulate takes from a few days to 3 weeks, depending on how busy the particular office is. We check its requirements in advance and put together the justification for your purpose: a purchase, work or studies.

    Option 2: in Spain, at a comisaría or an extranjería office

    If you are already in Spain, the NIE is obtained at a Comisaría de Policía (a police station that handles foreigners' matters) or at an Oficina de Extranjería (the immigration office). Before booking, it is worth completing your municipal registration (padrón municipal) and getting a recent certificado de empadronamiento: some offices ask for it right at the appointment. The procedure:

    1. Book a cita previa on the website sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es
    2. Come in person with your passport, a copy, form EX-15 and the justification
    3. Pay the fee (Modelo 790, code 012) at a bank or a cash machine
    4. Receive a certificate with your assigned NIE number

    In large cities (Barcelona, Madrid) cita previa slots can be very tight: sometimes the nearest date is 3–4 weeks away. In Alicante and Valencia it is usually faster. We look for an available cita at the right office ourselves, fill in the application and the fee payment slip, prepare the justification and accompany you at the appointment as interpreters. All we need from you is a scan of your passport and fifteen minutes of messaging. If the office turns you away over a formality, we are the ones who chase the new appointment, not you.

    How to get a TIE

    You apply for a TIE only after your residence permit has been approved. The procedure takes place at the same Oficina de Extranjería or comisaría:

    1. Get your residence permit approved (resolución favorable)
    2. Book a cita previa to obtain the TIE: this is a separate appointment, do not confuse it with the appointment for submitting your documents
    3. Come with your passport, the residence permit approval, a 32×26 mm photo on a white background and the paid fee (Modelo 790, code 012, about €16)
    4. Give your biometrics (photo and fingerprints)
    5. Receive the TIE card, usually after 30–45 days
    Deadline: once your residence permit is approved, you have one month to apply for the TIE. Missing it formally breaches the conditions of the permit; in practice sanctions are rarely applied, but it is not worth delaying: available cita slots go quickly. We book the appointment in the very first days after the resolución favorable.

    Cost

    • NIE (form EX-15): fee of €9.84 (Modelo 790, code 012)
    • First TIE: about €16 (same form and code)
    • TIE renewal: €19.30 when renewing a temporary residence permit (same form and code)

    The fees themselves are small; the main costs arise where a person is needed: finding an available cita, interpreting at the appointment, a repeat visit because of a mistake in the form. We take this part on entirely, and we discuss the cost once we understand the scope: how many people are in the family, which city, and whether you need someone to accompany you at the appointment.

    Typical mistakes when getting an NIE and a TIE

    Mistake 1: mixing up the appointments

    The appointment for submitting a residence permit application, the appointment for obtaining an NIE and the appointment for obtaining a TIE are three different procedures with different categories on the cita previa website. People turn up in the wrong queue, get turned away, and everything starts again. We choose the category ourselves and send you a confirmation with the address, date and a list of what to bring.

    Mistake 2: an unpaid or incorrect fee

    Form 790 is filled in before you go to the office: you need the correct code and payment at a bank or a cash machine. If the fee was paid incorrectly or not paid at all, you will be turned away and will have to chase a new appointment. We fill in the form and check the receipt before you leave home.

    Mistake 3: expired documents

    Your passport must be valid at the time of application. The photo must be recent and meet the requirements: in colour, on a white background, with a neutral facial expression. We check the whole set against a checklist the day before the appointment.

    Mistake 4: waiting for the TIE instead of acting straight away

    Until the TIE is ready (up to 45 days), your status is confirmed by the resguardo, the proof of application that is handed to you right at the appointment. Together with your passport it works as a temporary document: with it you can open an account and sign a contract, and other authorities accept it. Do not lose it, and scan it on the very first day.

    Frequently asked questions

    What is the difference between the NIE and the TIE?
    The NIE is a number: a foreigner's tax and administrative identifier, which does not give the right to live in Spain. The TIE is a physical plastic card confirming your status as a resident of Spain, with your NIE already built into it.
    How much does it cost to get an NIE in Spain?
    The government fee for the NIE (tasa Modelo 790, code 012) is €9.84. We take care of booking the cita previa, filling in form EX-15 and accompanying you to the police.
    How quickly can you get an NIE?
    Through a consulate, processing takes from a few days to 3 weeks depending on the workload. In Spain, through the police, the NIE is usually issued on the day you submit your documents or within 1–2 weeks.
    Do I need to change my NIE when I get a residence permit?
    No, the NIE is assigned once and stays with you for good: it does not change when you get a TIE or a permanent residence permit (after you acquire Spanish nationality, the DNI number is used instead). The same number goes inside the TIE card.
