    Getting married in Spain as a foreigner: at a notary or at the civil registry

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~6 min read
    Foreigners can enter into a civil marriage (matrimonio civil) in Spain — the only question is which route to take. At the Registro Civil, in large cities you may wait 6–12 months for a cita (appointment) for the ceremony, while at a notary the same procedure usually takes 2–4 weeks. Below: who is entitled to marry, which documents to gather, in what order to get the apostille and where the timeline most often slips. We handle both procedures in your language: from the document package to the ceremony. Write to us and we will tell you which route is shorter in your case.

    Who can register a marriage in Spain

    The following can marry in Spain:

    • Two residents of Spain — the simplest case, with a minimum of documents
    • A resident + a non-resident — a common situation, with additional requirements to confirm the purpose of the marriage (protection against sham marriages)
    • Two non-residents — neither the Registro Civil nor a notary will open a marriage file: it is handled only where one of the couple lives. We look at which option remains in your case before the documents are gathered
    • Same-sex couples — recognised by Spain since 2005; the procedure is identical

    The minimum age is 18. From the age of 16 — only after emancipation, granted by the parents or a court (matrimonio de menores); this is rare nowadays. If your case is unusual — two non-residents, a recent divorce, different nationalities — write to us before you start ordering apostilles: we check the grounds in advance so that your money is not wasted.

    Two routes: Registro Civil vs a notary

    Until 2015, marriages were concluded only at the Registro Civil, at the town hall or in church. After the reform introduced by the Ley de Jurisdicción Voluntaria, notaries gained the right to register civil marriages — since then couples have had a choice, and it affects above all the timeframe.

    If time is not pressing and the free route suits you — the Registro Civil. If a visa, a residence permit or a departure date depends on it — a notary.     Documents you will need

    The list depends on the situation, but the basic package is the same for both routes. We check it with the specific notary or Registro Civil office before you start paying for apostilles and translations:

    • Passport of each spouse — original + copy of the data pages
    • Birth certificate — with an apostille from the issuing country and a sworn translation into Spanish
    • Certificado de soltería (marital status certificate) — showing that you are not married. With an apostille and translation. Usually valid for 6 months
    • Empadronamiento — an extract from the municipal register (padrón), usually no older than 3 months. At least one of the spouses must be registered in Spain
    • NIE or TIE — for a resident of Spain
    • Divorce certificate or death certificate of the spouse (where applicable) — with an apostille and translation
    The main problem is the marital status certificate. Ukraine does not issue such a certificate: status is confirmed by a statement with an authenticated signature and extracts from the civil registry office (DRACS) register, and the extracts need an apostille and a sworn translation. We get around this without a trip: you sign the statement before a Spanish notary here (we check in advance whether the Registro Civil or the notary handling your case will accept it), our representative in Ukraine obtains the extracts with the apostille, and the sworn translation is completed here in Spain. Your part: a passport and half an hour at the notary's office. We also obtain the certificate from Russia without you travelling — more on the page Marital status certificate from Russia.

    How the procedure works — step by step

    1. You write to us — we look at your situation: who is resident and who is not, nationality, which documents you already have. 15–30 minutes of messaging
    2. Gathering documents — we obtain the apostilles and sworn translations. If the documents are from Ukraine, we prepare a power of attorney for a relative so that they can collect the certificates for you. 2–6 weeks
    3. Submitting the expediente matrimonial — we submit the document file to the Registro Civil or the notary, where they check that there are no impediments to the marriage. We check the set before submission so that it is not returned because of an expired certificate
    4. Interview (audiencia reservada) — each partner is heard separately and the genuineness of the relationship is checked: questions about how you met, everyday life, plans. The day before, we go through this list with you
    5. Setting the ceremony date — after the expediente is approved. At a notary usually 1–2 weeks later, at the Registro Civil — according to the queue
    6. Ceremony — short, 15–30 minutes. Two witnesses, a reading from the Civil Code, signing of the record. Interpretation is mandatory if at least one of the spouses does not speak Spanish
    7. Certificado de matrimonio + apostille — after the marriage is entered in the Registro Civil, you receive the marriage certificate. Family books (Libro de Familia) have not been issued since 30 April 2021. If the marriage needs to be presented outside Spain, we get the apostille and arrange the translation

    What changes after the marriage

    • Residence permit for the spouse — a foreign spouse of a citizen of another EU country is entitled to residencia as a familiar de ciudadano UE (family member of an EU citizen), and the spouse of a Spanish citizen to a residence permit as a relative of a Spanish citizen. This is a separate procedure, and we handle it too: tell us about your plans in advance, as some documents are cheaper to gather for both cases at once
    • Citizenship — after 1 year of legal residence in Spain while married to a Spanish citizen, you can apply for nacionalidad por residencia (instead of the standard 10 years)
    • Taxes — a joint IRPF return is usually more advantageous if one of the spouses does not work
    • Property — by default the regime is sociedad de gananciales (joint ownership of property acquired during the marriage), but in some regions with their own civil law it is different: in Catalonia, for example, it is separate property. It can be changed to separación de bienes — there is a separate article on this

    Recognition of the marriage in Ukraine, Russia and other countries

    A Spanish marriage certificate with an apostille from the Spanish Ministry of Justice is recognised in the countries of the Hague Convention — Ukraine, Russia, Kazakhstan and many others. No further legalisation is needed, and in EU countries the apostille itself is not needed either.

    For use in your partner's country of permanent residence you need:

    • The marriage certificate (certificado de matrimonio)
    • An apostille from the Spanish Ministry of Justice (not needed for EU countries)
    • A sworn translation into the language of the destination country

    We put this package together in full: we order the certificate, get the apostille at the Ministry of Justice and arrange the sworn translation into the required language. You receive a ready file — all that remains is to submit it to the civil registry or archive of your country.

    Common mistakes

    Mistake 1: an out-of-date marital status certificate

    A certificado de soltería is usually valid for 6 months. If you obtained it in March and the wedding is in October, you will have to order the certificate again. We work out the schedule backwards from the ceremony date and order the certificate last, when everything else is ready.

    Mistake 2: missing the audiencia reservada

    For a “resident + non-resident” couple, the check of the relationship is mandatory. Not turning up or giving contradictory answers means having the expediente refused. We prepare both of you for the interview: we go through the typical questions and the order of answers, and we interpret at the interview itself.

    Mistake 3: the wrong apostille

    The apostille is placed on the original document, not on a copy or a translation; the translation is done after the apostille. A common mistake is to apostille the translation: such a document will not be accepted, and you will have to pay for the whole chain again. We set out the order ourselves and check the set before submission.

    Mistake 4: trying to save on an interpreter

    If there is no interpreter at the ceremony and one of the spouses does not speak Spanish, the ceremony will not take place: the notary or the mayor will not sign the record. We come to the ceremony and interpret ourselves, so there is no need to look for an interpreter separately.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can a foreigner get married in Spain?
    Yes. Foreigners can enter into a civil marriage (matrimonio civil) in Spain on the same terms as Spanish citizens. One of the spouses must live in Spain: the preliminary marriage file (acta or expediente) is handled by a notary or the Registro Civil where one of the couple lives, and the law sets no minimum period of municipal registration.
    Where is it faster to get married — at a notary or at the Registro Civil?
    At a notary the procedure usually takes 2–4 weeks after the documents are submitted. At the Registro Civil the waiting time for a cita can be 6–12 months, depending on the city. If time is critical, the notarial route is faster.
    Which documents does a foreigner need to get married in Spain?
    A passport (original + copy), a birth certificate with an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish, a marital status certificate (certificado de soltería) with an apostille and translation, empadronamiento in Spain, an NIE/TIE if you have one. For divorced people — a divorce certificate with an apostille.
    Is a Spanish marriage recognised in Ukraine and Russia?
    Yes. A Spanish marriage certificate with an apostille from the Spanish Ministry of Justice is recognised in the countries of the Hague Convention, including Ukraine and Russia. No further legalisation is needed — only the apostille and a sworn translation into the language of the country where it will be used.
    Can I get married in Spain without speaking Spanish?
    Yes. We handle the whole procedure in your language (RU/UK/EN): we prepare the documents, interpret at meetings with the notary or at the Registro Civil, and agree the wording. Under Spanish law, an interpreter must be present at the ceremony if at least one of the spouses does not speak Spanish.
