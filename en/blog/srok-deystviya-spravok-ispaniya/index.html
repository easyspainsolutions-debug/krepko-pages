    Certificate “no older than 3 months”: who in Spain sets this time limit

    Krepko · 11 November 2026 · ~8 min read

The “no older than three months” limit is set by whoever accepts the certificate, and it varies: for a marriage file, the civil registry of the Valencian Community accepts a historical certificate no older than one month, and the Madrid Association of Notaries no older than three months.     By law, the municipal registration certificate (padrón certificate) has no period of validity, yet it may still be handed back to you at the counter: “it must be no older than three months”. This time limit is set by whoever accepts the certificate, it differs from one authority to another, and sometimes the certificate is not requested at all.

    Below we look at what the law says about the time limit, where the limit is written into a legal rule and where only into the requirements of a specific authority, and how to time the certificate for your application. How to order the certificate and how a volante differs from a certificado is explained in the article on the certificado de empadronamiento.     What the law says about the certificate's time limit

    A padrón certificate is an extract from the padrón municipal, the town hall's register of residents. By law, padrón data prove that you live in that municipality and at which address, and the certificado de empadronamiento is a public document that is valid for any administrative purpose (Article 16.1 of Ley 7/1985). The law sets no time limit after which the certificate stops being valid.

    The INE instructions under which town halls keep the padrón say this directly: neither the certificado nor the volante needs a clause on the document's period of validity (para. 8.1.1 of the Resolución de 17 de febrero de 2020). Three months are mentioned in these instructions for a different reason: a town hall may set up a way to verify the authenticity of the certificates it issues, using a copy or electronically, and this verification must work for at least three months from the date of issue. This is the period during which the certificate can be verified, not its period of validity.

    The certificate shows where you are registered on the day of issue and says nothing about what happened afterwards. That is why the freshness limit is set by those who accept the certificate. It is usually accepted within three months of the date of issue, but this is the requirement of a specific authority, not a general rule. When we handle your municipal registration (empadronamiento), we choose the type of certificate to suit your next procedure and tell you when to get it, so that it is still recent when you submit your application.

    It is the entry, not the certificate, that has a time limit. Foreign nationals from outside the EU, the EEA and Switzerland who have neither larga duración (long-term residence) nor a residence card as a family member of an EU citizen must renew their padrón entry every two years, otherwise the town hall may cancel it (Article 16.1 of Ley 7/1985, para. 6 of the INE instructions). A certificate issued before the cancellation will not show this. So if your renewal date is approaching, renew the entry first and then get the certificate. Details are on the page about renewing your registration.

    Who sets the time limit: what official requirements say

    The freshness limit is set by the rule governing the procedure or by the list of documents of the authority you are applying to. Examples from official sources:

    Document and procedure Time limit Where it is set out
    Historical padrón certificate for a marriage file at the civil registry (Registro Civil) of the Valencian Community No older than 1 month List of documents on the Registro Civil Generalitat Valenciana website
    Padrón certificate for the Madrid Association of Notaries to appoint a notary for a marriage file No older than 3 months Requirements of the Colegio Notarial de Madrid
    Housing report for family reunification or documents replacing it No older than 6 months on the date of application Article 67.2 of RD 1155/2024
    Criminal record certificate from the country of origin for citizenship by residence The period stated on the certificate; if there is none, 6 months from the date of issue Annex to Orden JUS/1625/2016, para. 4
    Foreign criminal record certificate for arraigo (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain) Not specified Article 130.2 of RD 1155/2024, Ministry of Inclusion information sheet on arraigo social

    The time limit belongs not to the certificate but to the procedure. In Article 67.2 of RD 1155/2024 and in the annex to Orden JUS/1625/2016, it is counted on the day the application is submitted, so go by the submission date, not the day you booked your appointment.

    If there is no time limit either in the rule or in the official list of documents, check it with the authority you are applying to. This is the case with a foreign criminal record certificate for arraigo: the regulation and the Ministry of Inclusion information sheet, updated in April 2026, require a certificate from the countries where you lived for the five years before entering Spain (it is not needed if you have lived in Spain for five consecutive years before applying), but they do not state how recent it must be. The immigration office (oficina de extranjería) requests information on criminal records in Spain itself (Article 130.2 of RD 1155/2024). The same sheet lists the padrón certificate first among the examples of how two years in Spain are proved (followed by documents on hospital admission or medical appointments in the public health system), and sets no time limit for it. But the two years are counted up to the day of application, so the historical certificate is obtained shortly before it. How to get a criminal record certificate from Ukraine is explained in the article on the criminal record certificate.

    When the padrón certificate is not requested

    In procedures of Spain's central government bodies (Administración General del Estado) and the organisations attached to them, the padrón certificate is not required as proof of address: this has been the rule since 2006, under RD 523/2006. The body checks the address itself, in its own databases or through the residence data verification system (Sistema de Verificación de Datos de Residencia). The result of the check has the same force as the certificate, but the body is entitled to ask for other documents about your address. Under an additional provision of the decree, the rule also applies in procedures under the immigration law (Ley Orgánica 4/2000), unless it conflicts with that law, so check the list of documents against the information sheet for your procedure.

    You or the town hall will still be asked for the certificate if:

    • you objected to such a check: under Article 28.2 of Ley 39/2015, the body may obtain documents issued by another administration itself, unless you object;
    • your address is not in the system or does not match the one stated in the application;
    • you changed your registration in the last two months.

    The DGT application form for driving licence procedures (Mod. 03, May 2026 version) states that the DGT will electronically check your identity, registration and legal residence data itself. In a citizenship-by-residence file, you give your consent in the application, and the Ministry of Justice requests your registration data from the INE; without consent, the certificate is attached (annex to Orden JUS/1625/2016, para. 3). The spouse of a Spanish citizen also needs a certificate of joint registration or cohabitation (certificado de convivencia o empadronamiento conjunto, ibid., para. 2).

    With this kind of check, it is the padrón entry itself that is examined, so its address must match the address in the application, and the renewal must not have been missed. RD 523/2006 applies only to central government bodies. Town halls and autonomous communities, including schools and health centres, have their own document lists, although the right under Article 28.2 of Ley 39/2015 applies there too. Notaries and banks are not part of the administration, and they have their own requirements for the certificate.

    How to time the certificate for your application

    • First the time limit and the submission date, then the certificate. The time limit is found in the rule for the procedure or in the authority's list of documents. A certificate obtained “just in case” may be out of date by the day of submission.
    • The right type of certificate. A certificado is issued by the town hall secretary and is a public document; a volante is an informational extract without these formalities (Article 61 of RD 1690/1986). If the authority requires a certificado, a recent volante will not replace it. Where the address history matters, a historical certificado is ordered.
    • Online, if your town hall issues it that way. Since February 2024, the population regulation expressly allows a certificado to be issued automatically, without the direct involvement of an official (Article 61 of RD 1690/1986 as amended by RD 141/2024). If your town hall issues certificates through its online office (sede electrónica), you can get a recent certificate on the day of submission, with a digital certificate or with Cl@ve if the town hall accepts it. How to register for Cl@ve with an NIE is explained in a separate article.

You receive the type of certificate needed for your next procedure, and we work out when to get it, counting from the day of submission.

    How much the certificate costs

    The padrón certificate is usually issued free of charge, but there is no single rule. The law allows town halls to charge a fee for documents they issue on request (Article 20.4.a of the TRLRHL), and whether there is such a fee is decided by the town hall in its tax bylaw (ordenanza fiscal). For example, in San Pedro del Pinatar (Murcia), under the local ordenanza, the certificado de empadronamiento is subject to a fee, while the volante is free. If you specifically need a certificado, check your town hall's ordenanza fiscal.

    Frequently asked questions

    How long is a padrón certificate valid in Spain?
    By law, it has no time limit: the INE instructions for town halls state directly that a validity clause is not needed in the certificado or the volante. The freshness limit is set by the authority that accepts the certificate. It is usually accepted within three months of the date of issue, but it can differ: for a marriage file, the civil registry of the Valencian Community accepts a historical certificate no older than one month. So check the time limit with the authority you are applying to.
    The town hall said the certificate has no time limit, but at the counter they demand a recent one. Who is right?
    Both are. By law, the certificate has no period of validity, and the freshness limit is set by whoever accepts it: in the rule for its procedure or in its own list of documents. The certificate shows where you are registered on the day of issue, so authorities want to see a recent one.
    Do I need a padrón certificate to exchange my driving licence at the DGT?
    Usually not: the DGT form for driving licence procedures (Mod. 03) states that the DGT will electronically check your registration data itself, and RD 523/2006 sets this procedure for central government bodies. The certificate may be requested if your address is not in the system or does not match the one stated, and also if you changed your registration in the last two months.
    Does a criminal record certificate also have to be no older than three months?
    It depends on the procedure. For citizenship, a certificate from the country of origin is accepted within the period stated on it, and if there is none, six months from the date of issue (annex to Orden JUS/1625/2016). For arraigo, the regulation and the Ministry of Inclusion information sheet do not state how recent a foreign certificate must be, so check this with your immigration office. For arraigo, the office requests information on criminal records in Spain itself. More details in the article on the criminal record certificate.
    Do the volante and the certificado have different periods of validity?
    No, by law neither of them has a period of validity. The difference is in the document's legal force: a certificado is issued by the town hall secretary and is a public document for any administrative purpose, while a volante is an informational extract without these formalities. If the authority requires a certificado, a volante will not do, even a recent one.
    Can I get the certificate in advance so I do not have to go to the town hall before submitting?
    Yes, if you already know the time limit of the authority you are applying to and your submission date falls within it. If the town hall issues certificates online, you can get a recent certificate on the day of submission, with a digital certificate or with Cl@ve if the town hall accepts it.
