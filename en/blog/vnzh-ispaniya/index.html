    How to get a residence permit in Spain: a complete guide for Ukrainians and Russian speakers

    Krepko · updated 23 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    In short. A residence permit (autorización de residencia) is issued by the Oficina de Extranjería (the immigration office); the workable routes for Ukrainians and Russian speakers are temporary protection, arraigo social and arraigo sociolaboral (from 2 years of residence after the 2025 Reglamento reform) and the residence permit for a relative of a Spanish citizen for the parent of a Spanish child, where no period of residence is required. The official processing time for arraigo is 3 months, in practice 3–7 months depending on the city. Temporary protection counts in full towards the period for permanent residence (larga duración nacional) (instruction SEM 2/2026), but we plan the switch to arraigo in advance. Source: BOE — Real Decreto 1155/2024 (Reglamento de la Ley Orgánica 4/2000).

    “How do I become legal in Spain?” is a question we hear every day. The answer depends on your story: how many years you have lived in the country, whether you have a job and family, whether you came from Ukraine after February 2022. Below we go through all the workable routes, from temporary protection to arraigo social: which requirements are realistic, where applications are most often turned away and what is worth preparing in advance so as not to lose half a year.

    What a residence permit is and why you need one

    A residence permit in Spanish is an autorización de residencia, permission to reside for a period of 1 to 5 years depending on the type. Without it you are a tourist: 90 days in every 180 in the Schengen area, with no right to work and no access to the system. A residence permit opens up a health card (tarjeta sanitaria), legal work and social services, while children are enrolled in school regardless of immigration status.

    Permits are issued by the Oficina de Extranjería (extranjería). Each large city has its own: the list of documents and the timeframes differ noticeably, so we check your province's requirements before you start putting your file together.

    Type 1: Temporary protection (Protección Temporal)

    Temporary protection is a status the EU introduced in March 2022 for Ukrainians who fled the war. Spain was one of the first to apply the directive and has issued hundreds of thousands of permits. It is the fastest way into legal status and at the same time the most short-lived, so below we look separately at where to move on to from it.

    Who can get it

    • Ukrainian citizens who left after 24 February 2022
    • Stateless persons and third-country nationals with a Ukrainian residence permit, if they cannot return to their home country
    • Family members of the above

    What temporary protection gives you

    • Permission to reside and work (the TIE residence card)
    • Access to the healthcare system
    • The possibility of enrolling children in school
    • Access to some social benefits
    • The possibility of opening a bank account

    Duration and extension

    Initially protection was granted for 1 year with extension up to 3 years. As of 2025, most Ukrainians are going through the first or second extension. The EU Council has already moved the end date several times, so we check the current deadline against the latest decisions; message us on WhatsApp if you need the end date as it stands today.

    Important: temporary protection is not a classic residence permit. It counts in full towards the period for permanent residence (residencia de larga duración nacional) (instruction SEM 2/2026). If you are staying in Spain for the long term, prepare the switch to arraigo social in advance: we count your period of residence from the padrón (municipal registration) and name the month from which you can apply.

    Type 2: Arraigo social (social ties)

    Arraigo social is the most common route for those who have already lived in Spain for several years without documents or on a tourist visa. Literally it means “social rootedness”: you prove to the state that you have put down roots in local life, through housing, the padrón, work and connections.

    Main requirements

    • Continuous residence in Spain for at least 2 years (after the 2025 Reglamento reform; previously 3 years), proven by the padrón (empadronamiento), bank statements and sometimes statements from neighbours (for example, a sworn statement before a notary)
    • No criminal record in Spain and in the countries where you lived for 5 years before entering Spain (you need a criminal record certificate with an apostille and a translation)
    • Means of subsistence: at least 100% of the IPREM for you, and in the variant with a relative, 200% for the two of you; an employment contract is not needed for arraigo social, it is needed for arraigo sociolaboral
    • Social ties: close relatives with a residence permit (spouse, registered partner, parents or children) or an informe social from the autonomous community or the town hall; in some cities appointments for this report take months, so we put it first in the plan

    Duration of the residence permit

    The card is issued for 1 year with the right to work. The renewal is also for 1 year. After 5 years of continuous legal residence, permanent residence (residencia de larga duración) becomes available, so we count your period of residence across all your permits, including the years under temporary protection (instruction SEM 2/2026), and make sure no gaps appear in it.

    Typical mistakes

    • Gaps in the padrón: even a few months without registration can interrupt the count of 2 years
    • A criminal record certificate that has expired by the filing date: its validity period is set by extranjería, usually 3 months, so we order it for an appointment (cita) that has already been booked, not in advance
    • An incorrectly drawn-up employment contract for arraigo sociolaboral: we check the form and duration against the requirements of your extranjería office before the employer signs it

    Type 3: Arraigo laboral (work-based ties)

    Since 20 May 2025 arraigo laboral no longer exists (art. 125 RD 1155/2024): those who worked in Spain legally but lost their permit are now covered by arraigo de segunda oportunidad, and those who have an employment contract by arraigo sociolaboral. Both require at least 2 years of residence in Spain, and instead of 6 months of contributions, either a residence permit from the last 2 years that was not renewed and was not granted on the basis of arraigo or other exceptional circumstances (segunda oportunidad), or a contract of at least 20 hours a week with pay no lower than the SMI in proportion to the hours (sociolaboral).

    The route suits those who paid contributions but did not manage to get their card in time. For sociolaboral, the employer signs a contract with you at the time of filing; we request the necessary papers from them in advance and check that the contract meets the hours and pay requirements.

    Type 4: Arraigo familiar (family ties)

    Since 20 May 2025 arraigo familiar is granted to the parent or guardian of a child who is a national of another EU or EEA country or of Switzerland, while the parent of a child with Spanish nationality gets the residence permit for a relative of a Spanish citizen (art. 94.1.f RD 1155/2024). No period of residence is required here: it is enough to prove the family relationship and that you are genuinely involved in the child's life. You will also need a criminal record certificate; we order it, have it legalised and arrange its translation by a sworn translator.

    Tip: if you have Ukrainian citizenship and a child with a Spanish passport, the residence permit for a relative of a Spanish citizen is the shortest route: 3–6 months depending on the city. We prepare the documents proving the family relationship from Ukraine in parallel, so that they do not become a bottleneck.

    Other types of residence permit: a brief overview

    Student residence permit (visado de estudiante)

    For those studying at a Spanish university or on full-time Spanish courses of at least 20 hours a week at an official language school (EOI) or a centre accredited by the Instituto Cervantes. It gives the right to live in Spain and work up to 30 hours a week, and after university to switch to job-seeking status (búsqueda de empleo).

    Residence permit for employment (cuenta ajena)

    You need a job offer from a Spanish employer plus a labour market test (situación nacional de empleo) or grounds for an exemption. The route is hard and long, from 3 to 8 months, and most of the paperwork falls on the employer.

    Residence permit for the self-employed (cuenta propia / autónomo)

    For those who are starting a business or working for themselves. You need a business plan and proof of funds. If your clients are abroad and the work is remote, the digital nomad visa with its preferential tax regime is often more advantageous; we work out which of the two routes is yours in our very first conversation.

    Processing times

    The official processing time for arraigo is 3 months. In practice, in Madrid and Barcelona people wait 5–7 months, in Alicante and Valencia 3–4 months. Silence from extranjería after 3 months formally counts as silencio negativo (a refusal), but more often the case is simply taking longer. We keep track of the status of your case and tell you when it is time to demand an answer rather than keep waiting.

    General list of documents for arraigo social

    1. Completed form EX–10 (the official application form)
    2. Copy of your passport: all pages
    3. Criminal record certificate from the countries where you lived for 5 years before entering Spain, with an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish; we take care of this item entirely, including delivery to Spain
    4. Proof of 2 years of residence (padrón, bank statements, rental contracts)
    5. Informe social or documents proving social ties
    6. Proof of means of subsistence (at least 100% of the IPREM for you, with a relative 200% for the two of you)
    7. A photo is not needed for the application: it is submitted later, when the TIE is issued (32×26 mm, on a white background)
    8. Receipt for payment of the fee (tasas Modelo 790, code 052)

    Frequently asked questions

    What types of residence permit are available in Spain in 2026?
    The main routes: temporary protection for Ukrainians, arraigo social (2 years of residence), arraigo sociolaboral (2 years + an employment contract), the residence permit for a relative of a Spanish citizen (the parent of a Spanish child), arraigo por formación (2 years + studies), the student residence permit, the residence permit for employment, autónomo and the digital nomad visa. Since the 2025 reform of the reglamento de extranjería, arraigo por formación has been called arraigo socioformativo, and the time requirements have been simplified; the choice of route depends on your history in Spain.
    Which residence permit is the fastest for Ukrainians?
    Temporary protection: it is processed in 1–3 weeks immediately after arrival from Ukraine. If protection no longer suits you or is coming to an end, the fastest next step is the residence permit for a relative of a Spanish citizen (3–6 months) if you have a Spanish child. Almost all types of arraigo require 2 years of residence, so the switch has to be planned in advance.
    How is arraigo different from the digital nomad visa?
    Arraigo is applied for while you are already in Spain; most types require 2 years of residence and proof of ties. The digital nomad visa is applied for either from the country where you live or in Spain with tourist status: it requires remote work for a foreign company and an income of €34,188 a year or more, while the preferential Beckham tax regime is optional. A full overview of the routes through arraigo is on the page /regularizacion-2026/.
    What should I do after temporary protection ends?
    Do not wait for the last day: 3–6 months before protection ends, gather documents for the next route. If you have lived in Spain for 2+ years and worked with a contract, switch to an ordinary work-based residence permit (change of status, art. 191 RD 1155/2024). If you have 2 years on the padrón and a signed employment contract of at least 20 hours a week, arraigo sociolaboral. If you are studying or enrolled on a course, arraigo socioformativo. For these types of arraigo, absences from Spain of more than 90 days in total in the two years before filing break continuity, so record every month of residence with the padrón.
    How much does getting a residence permit through arraigo cost?
    The state fee Modelo 790 code 052 is €38.28. A criminal record certificate with an apostille and a sworn translation costs €80–150. An informe social from the municipality is free or up to €50. Third-party gestorías/abogados charge €600–1,200 for full support, depending on the city and complexity. The cost of our work on a residence permit is discussed individually and depends on the type of arraigo, the client's situation, the city and the urgency. We take payment for our work in advance, after you confirm the estimate. State fees and third-party costs (notary and translator services) are paid as incurred.
