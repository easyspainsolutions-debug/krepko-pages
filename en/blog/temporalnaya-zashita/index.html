    From 5 August 2026, temporary protection in Spain is granted under a new condition. The decision is short, but three inaccuracies have spread around it — and each one changes the answer to a single question: will the restriction apply to you or not.

    We went through the text of the Council of the EU decision line by line — not the retellings on social media. Below: what the rule says, where publications get it wrong, how to prove that obligations have been fulfilled, and what to do if you already have the status or are only about to apply.     What temporary protection is and who introduced it

    Temporary protection (Protección Temporal) is a special legal status introduced by the European Union on the basis of Directive 2001/55/EC. The Council of the EU activated it on 4 March 2022 in connection with the war in Ukraine — for the first time in the entire history of the directive.

    Under this directive Spain has taken in hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians. The status is handled by the Oficina de Asilo y Refugio (OAR) under the Ministry of the Interior. Protection is processed through the police, and what you keep is a TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) marked as temporary protection — this is the card you use to prove your status at the bank, with a landlord and at the border.

    Who is entitled to temporary protection

    • Citizens of Ukraine who left the country on or after 24 February 2022, as well as those who were already in Spain before that date and cannot return because of the war
    • Stateless persons and third-country nationals who held a valid Ukrainian residence permit and are unable to return safely to their home country
    • Family members (spouses, minor children, dependent relatives) of the persons mentioned above
    What about Russians? Russian citizenship in itself does not give a right to temporary protection. But Russians who lived lawfully in Ukraine on a valid residence permit — permanent or temporary, for example a student one — and cannot return to their country or region may, in certain cases, apply for protection in Spain. A Russian who is a family member of a Ukrainian citizen (spouse, child, dependent relative) also has the right, if the family lived in Ukraine before 24 February 2022. We look at such situations case by case: which residence permit you had in Ukraine, when and how you left, and what you still have on hand.

    How long temporary protection lasts in 2026

    The directive was written for a period of up to three years: one year, an extension for another year (two periods of 6 months) and up to one more year by decision of the Council of the EU. Since 2024 the Council of the EU has been extending protection beyond this period. By Council of the EU Decision 2026/1912 of 30 July 2026, protection has been extended until 4 March 2028. The extension applies to everyone who already has the status.

    TIE cards have their own validity period. Spain extended the validity of cards already issued by a separate order, Orden INT/96/2026 — and only until 4 March 2027; you do not need a new card for this. The EU decision on the extension until 2028 applies directly, but as of 5 November 2026 there is no Spanish order in the BOE extending the cards until 2028. We follow the changes ourselves and update this page as soon as a new text comes out — the date at the top can be read as the date of our last check.

    What changed on 5 August 2026

    Together with the extension, the Council of the EU introduced a condition that did not exist before. Temporary protection is now granted only to those who have fulfilled their military obligations in Ukraine — and, where applicable, can prove it.

    The rule is Article 2 of Council of the EU Decision 2026/1912. It was adopted on 30 July, published on 4 August and entered into force on 5 August 2026.

    The key point: the restriction applies only to new applications. If you already have protection, it does not affect you — your status is extended until 4 March 2028.

    Who the restriction does not apply to

    The text of the decision contains a proviso: the condition does not apply to those who are already benefiting from temporary protection in a specific EU country as of 4 August 2026 inclusive and keep it in that same country without interruption.

    This is where the first error circulating in publications lies. They write: “it does not apply to those who arrived before 4 August”. The decision says something else — it does not apply to those who are already benefiting from protection. The difference is decisive: a person may have arrived in July but applied in September — the proviso will not cover them.

    The second detail from the same proviso: protection must be kept in the same country. Moving to another EU country and then applying there is a new application with all the conditions.

    On age: the decision does not mention it

    “Men aged 18–60” and “men aged 23–60” have spread on social media. The text of the decision contains neither age nor sex. There is one criterion — whether military obligations have been fulfilled.

    Moreover, the preamble of the decision states directly that citizens of Ukraine may have such obligations regardless of age and sex: a person may have volunteered or have obligations related to military activity.

    Where the figures came from: “23–60” is a footnote in the European Commission's proposal describing Ukrainian legislation on the ban on leaving the country, not an EU rule. In practice the filter does indeed fall first and foremost on men who were restricted from leaving Ukraine — but the formal criterion is worded differently. The difference is not theoretical: when we examine a specific case, we start from the text of the decision, not from retellings on social media.

    How to prove it

    The decision names two ways:

    • Lawful crossing of the border when leaving Ukraine. An exit stamp in the passport is in itself considered proof that permission to leave was given. But the passports of Ukrainian citizens most often have no such stamp — how to prove your departure in that case is covered in our article on temporary protection without a stamp.
    • An official verifiable document — on paper or in electronic form, confirming exemption from or fulfilment of the obligations. The text of the decision names the Reserve+ app as an example of such a document.

    Note the word “verifiable”: a screenshot from the app and an official document that the authority can verify are not the same thing. If you are not sure which of your papers will do, send us a list of what you have on hand. We will tell you what is missing and how to obtain it.

    About the Spanish police instruction

    Screenshots of an instruction attributed to the management of the Comisaría General de Extranjería y Fronteras are circulating on social media. As of 13 August 2026, no such document had been published in the BOE or on the websites of the Ministry of the Interior, the National Police or the Ministry of Inclusion.

    This does not mean the instruction does not exist: authorities do not always publish internal documents. But it cannot yet be cited as a confirmed fact, and we do not do so.

    If you already have the status and your deadline is approaching

    For those whose protection is in force, the extension follows the same procedure as before. But a year is a short time, and switching to an ordinary residence permit depends on deadlines and paperwork. It is sensible to start in advance, not when there is a month left before your TIE expires: we work the schedule backwards from the expiry date of your card — for most people, under the order, this is 4 March 2027 — and allow several months for gathering the set of documents.

    The arraigo and family reunification routes rely on one and the same set: a criminal record certificate, birth and marriage certificates, an apostille and a sworn translation. We obtain these documents from Ukraine without you having to travel: from you, scans of your passport and the details for the request; after that, we handle the request and the apostille ourselves and send the documents to Spain. We discuss the cost once we understand the scope.

    What temporary protection gives you

    Residence The right to live legally in Spain for the entire period of temporary protection
    Work The right to work as an employee or as an autónomo without additional permits
    Healthcare Access to the public health system (tarjeta sanitaria)
    Education Enrolling children in a state school
    Bank Opening an account at a Spanish bank as a resident
    Social assistance Access to benefits on general terms — state ones, for example IMV, and regional ones; the conditions depend on the benefit and the region
    Renting a home The TIE is accepted as a document for signing a rental agreement
    Travel Entering and leaving Spain, visiting EU countries (within the Schengen rules)

    How to extend temporary protection

    Temporary protection is extended without your involvement: the EU takes a decision for everyone at once, and Spain extends the validity of the cards by a separate order. You do not need to physically renew your card — there is no police appointment and no fee for the extension. This is how it works:

    1
    EU decision
    The Council of the EU extends protection at once for everyone who has it. The decision in force is 2026/1912: the status is extended until 4 March 2028.
    2
    Spanish order
    The Ministry of the Interior extends the validity of the cards by an order published in the BOE. The order in force is Orden INT/96/2026: cards already issued are valid until 4 March 2027. As of 5 November 2026 there is no order on the cards until 2028.
    3
    The card stays the same
    You do not need to get a new card (point 2 of the order). If the plastic card shows a date earlier than 4 March 2027, the validity period from the order applies. The order does not provide for a separate certificate of extension: if the date on the card is taken to mean it has expired — at the bank, by a landlord or at the border — show the order itself. It was published in the BOE on 17 February 2026 (BOE-A-2026-3712).
    4
    When you do need to go to the police
    If the card has been lost, stolen or damaged — for a duplicate (see the question below). And if you renounce protection, for example after switching to another residence permit, the card is handed in: the order keeps this obligation.
    Do not wait until the last minute. You do not need to book an appointment for the extension, but the validity of the cards under the order in force is 4 March 2027, and there is no order yet on the cards for 2027–2028. A residence permit of your own does not depend on the extensions, and it is sensible to prepare the switch to one in advance: certificates from Ukraine take weeks. Message us — we will work out the timeframes for your documents and tell you where to start.

    Moving between EU countries with temporary protection

    Temporary protection is granted in a specific EU country and is valid only there. If you received temporary protection in Spain, you cannot automatically work or receive social assistance in Germany or Poland. Short visits (tourist trips to other Schengen countries) are possible.

    Moving to another EU country means a new application — for temporary protection or for another status, under the local rules. And keep in mind the proviso from the decision: continuity of protection is counted within one country, so changing country means the proviso no longer covers you.

    Trips to Ukraine with temporary protection

    Trips to Ukraine do not in themselves end protection. Under RD 1325/2003 (art. 24) it ends, for example, if you renounce it or if you officially declare to the authorities that you are returning to your home country, and the extension applies to everyone at once, without checking your trips. The exception is Ukrainians who were already in Spain before 24 February 2022: they were granted protection on the condition that they cannot return to Ukraine (Orden PCM/170/2022), and frequent trips there may become grounds for reviewing the status (art. 8 of Orden PCM/169/2022). For everyone else the risk lies elsewhere. For long-term residence, continuity is broken by absences of more than 6 consecutive months or more than 10 months in total over five years (art. 183.2 RD 1155/2024). And if during a long trip you miss a summons and do not appear within 45 days without a valid reason, protection may be declared terminated. If you are planning to be away for a long time, message us before you leave: we will look at your dates and tell you what you are risking and what is worth recording before the trip.

    What happens after temporary protection ends

    Sooner or later, temporary protection will come to an end. What then? There are several scenarios:

    Scenario 1: Switching to arraigo social

    If you have lived in Spain for at least 2 years continuously (with a confirmed padrón (municipal registration)), the time requirement for arraigo social is met: time on protection during those 2 years counts, and you can apply without waiting for temporary protection to end (art. 126.b RD 1155/2024, SEM instruction 2/2026). This is a full residence permit for 1 year with the right to renew it if you are looking for work and are registered with the employment service. We gather the documents for it in advance: certificates from Ukraine take the longest, and they are usually what holds up the application.

    Scenario 2: Refugee status or subsidiary protection

    Sometimes it is possible to apply for international protection (asilo o protección subsidiaria). The procedure is harder and longer, but if approved, you are given a permanent residence and work permit (art. 36.1.c Ley 12/2009). However, while the application is being considered, the rights under temporary protection and the rights of an asylum seeker are not combined (art. 22 RD 1325/2003), you cannot apply for arraigo, and this time does not count towards its 2 years (art. 126 RD 1155/2024). It does not suit everyone — we look at the circumstances of each particular family.

    Scenario 3: A residence permit through work or study

    If by that time you have an employment contract with a Spanish company or you are studying, there are separate types of residence permit for this. We check the contract and the study conditions before the application, so as not to gather the set of documents blindly.

    Practical advice: do not wait for temporary protection to expire before thinking about the next step. If you have already been living in Spain for 2–3 years with temporary protection, now is the time to get advice on switching to arraigo social. We will assess your situation and draw up an action plan.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does time on temporary protection count towards permanent residence?
    Yes, for national long-term residence (larga duración nacional): years on temporary protection count in full towards the required 5 years, and you can apply directly from your protection card (SEM instruction 2/2026, point six). You cannot apply for EU long-term residence (larga duración-UE) from a temporary protection card: the EU directive on long-term residents does not extend to holders of temporary protection. For citizenship, the question has not been officially decided. Time on arraigo social does count. For details, see the article on permanent residence after temporary protection.
    Can I buy property in Spain with temporary protection?
    Yes. Temporary protection plus a TIE is a sufficient basis for a property transaction. You also need an NIE, but you already have one: the number is assigned when you apply for protection and is shown on the TIE card, so you do not need to obtain it separately.
    Can I get a mortgage with temporary protection?
    Technically, yes — banks consider applications. In practice, getting a mortgage with temporary protection is harder than with a permanent residence permit: banks take into account the temporary nature of the status. But there are options, especially if you have a stable income and a good credit history.
    What should I do if I have lost my TIE?
    You need to go to the police with a report of the loss and request a duplicate. You will need your passport, a photo and payment of the fee.
