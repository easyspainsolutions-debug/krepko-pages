    The bank asked where the money came from: which documents to prepare

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. The bank and the notary are obliged to check where money comes from (origen de los fondos) under the anti-money laundering law, Ley 10/2010. You answer the request with documents that show the whole chain: where the money came from, how it left Ukraine and how it reached your account. Ukrainian papers are needed with an apostille and a translation into Spanish.

    You sold a flat in Ukraine, the money reached your Spanish account, and a couple of days later the bank asks you to explain where it came from. Or, a week before you sign a purchase, the notary wants to see what you are paying with and how. The transaction is on hold and the bank gives no reasons. That is what the law requires: the bank is prohibited from telling a client that their transaction is being examined.

    A request like this can only be answered with documents. Below we explain why the bank and the notary are obliged to ask, which papers cover the sale of property in Ukraine, an inheritance, a gift, a salary and a loan, and how to bring Ukrainian documents into a form that will be accepted in Spain. The rules were checked on 1 October 2026 against the BOE, the website of the National Bank of Ukraine (NBU) and the Ukrainian legislation database.

    Why the bank and the notary are obliged to ask

    Ley 10/2010 names banks and notaries as “obliged entities”. A bank must constantly check a client’s transactions against their profile, “including the source of funds” (art. 6), and re-check even a long-standing client when there is a large or complex transaction (art. 7.2). Under enhanced due diligence the bank may request documents on the source of the client’s funds and wealth: this is one of the measures it chooses according to the level of risk (art. 20.1 RD 304/2014). For a bank, a non-resident is a higher-risk factor (art. 19.3 of the same regulation).

    If the client has not provided the documents, the bank does not carry out the transaction or ends the relationship, and bears no liability for doing so (art. 7.3). It is prohibited from telling the client that the transaction is being examined (art. 24): the branch’s silence is a legal requirement, not an attitude towards you personally.

    The notary has an obligation of their own: when a home is bought, the notary states in the deed the amount, the time and the method of payment: cash, cheque or transfer (art. 24 of the Notarial Law). Where there are several risk indicators from the list drawn up by the notaries’ anti-money-laundering body, or clear signs that the transaction is simulated or intended to circumvent the law, the notary refuses to authorise it: the law expressly calls this a lawful ground for refusal (art. 19.2 Ley 10/2010). The notary asks you for the information needed to assess these indicators.

    The documents will also be useful with the tax office: money that does not match your declared income may be taxed at the general IRPF rates as an unjustified gain in wealth (art. 39 Ley 35/2006).

    When the request comes

    • A large payment into your account. The law sets no fixed threshold: the bank assesses the risk itself. For money transfers through services without an account (envío de dinero: Western Union and the like) above €3,000 and for currency exchange above €6,000, in a single operation or in total over a quarter, the regulation expressly requires enhanced due diligence (art. 19.2 RD 304/2014). This threshold does not apply to an ordinary transfer between bank accounts.
    • Buying a home. The questions come from the notary and, with a mortgage, from the bank as well.
    • A transfer of capital from another country, especially one that is unusual for your account.
    • A new product at the bank or a change in your circumstances (art. 7.2 Ley 10/2010).
    • The transaction has already been stopped. The answer is the same: documents, only now you have to gather them in a hurry.

    For Russian citizens without a residence permit of an EU country there is also a cap of €100,000 on deposits in a single EU bank (art. 5b of Regulation 833/2014); with a Spanish residence permit it does not apply.

    Which documents cover each source

    The bank looks at the chain as a whole: the basis (why the money is yours), the movement (how it travelled) and the outcome (how it ended up in your account in Spain). A gap in any link means a new question.

    Source Main document What to add
    Sale of a flat, house or land in Ukraine Notarial sale and purchase agreement (art. 657 of the Civil Code of Ukraine) A statement showing the money received from the buyer and statements along the whole route to Spain
    Inheritance Certificate of the right to inheritance from a notary (art. 1296–1297 of the Civil Code of Ukraine) The sale agreement, if the inherited property was sold, and statements
    Gift Notarial gift agreement (art. 719 of the Civil Code of Ukraine) Documents showing where the donor’s money came from
    Salary, income as a sole proprietor (FOP) Income information from the tax office, sole proprietor (FOP) tax returns Account statements for the period in which the savings built up
    Loan A written loan agreement or receipt (art. 1047 of the Civil Code of Ukraine) A statement showing the transfer of the money
    • A gift of money in Ukraine is formalised before a notary if the gift is worth more than 50 non-taxable minimum incomes, that is, practically any noticeable amount. A verbal arrangement with your parents is not a document for a Spanish bank.
    • A loan of 10 non-taxable minimum incomes or more is made in writing. In Spain a loan between private individuals is exempt from the ITP transfer tax, but a return is, as a rule, filed on the autonomous community’s form (Modelo 600): that way the loan does not look like a hidden gift. Check the procedure and the deadline in your autonomous community; it is convenient to set out the terms in a loan agreement before a Spanish notary.
    • Savings are proven with several documents: statements over the years, tax returns, income certificates. The amount must build up gradually and match your income.

    How to bring Ukrainian papers into Spanish form

    The apostille is affixed by the authority that issued the document. The Ministry of Justice of Ukraine apostilles notarial documents: the sale and purchase agreement, the certificate of the right to inheritance, the gift agreement. The State Tax Service (DPS) apostilles its own documents, for example income information. The Ministry of Foreign Affairs apostilles the rest (Cabinet of Ministers Resolution No. 61).

    A bank statement is a private document; it cannot be apostilled. Usually a notary certifies a copy, and the apostille is affixed to that certification.

    Translation. Under Spanish law, an official translation is one made by a sworn translator appointed by the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs (traductor-intérprete jurado), by the Ministry’s Translation Office (OIL), by Spanish consulates abroad (for matters they handle) and by the Consulate of Ukraine in Spain for Ukrainian public documents (DA 16ª Ley 2/2014). A Ukrainian notarised translation is not on this list: a bank or notary may accept it, but that is their decision. That is why, before ordering a translation, we check with the receiving party which one it needs. Check with the Consulate of Ukraine itself whether it does such translations.

    Since March 2025 a sworn translator can certify a translation with a qualified electronic signature (Orden AUC/213/2025), and you can receive it without a visit. Check in advance whether the bank or notary will accept the file or a printout.

    If a document is missing: a statement before a notary

    Sometimes a link in the chain is not on paper: the money was saved up in cash over years, relatives handed it over without an agreement, an old account was closed long ago. In that case a Spanish notary draws up a notarial record of your statement (acta de manifestaciones): you set out how the money came about, and the notary records what you said.

    The notary certifies that the statement was made, but not that it is true. So the record does not replace documents; it links the ones you have and explains the gaps. Whether it is enough is decided by the bank or notary that asked the question.

    The money was a gift: gift tax in Spain

    Money received as a gift is subject to gift tax. It is paid by the recipient: a Spanish resident is taxed on everything received, wherever the money is, including in Ukraine (art. 3, 5 and 6 Ley 29/1987). The tax goes to the autonomous community where the recipient lived the most days in the five years before the gift (Ley 22/2009). If you have been in Spain for less than five years, which administration is competent and whose reliefs apply should be checked before filing.

    A resident files a return with the tax office of their autonomous community, usually on form 651. The general national deadline is 30 working days from the day following the gift (art. 67 RD 1629/1991); some autonomous communities have their own deadline.

    Autonomous community Relief for close relatives Condition for money Filing deadline
    Valencian Community 99% for gifts from a spouse, parents, children, grandchildren, grandparents A public document, which can be executed within the filing deadline (for up to €4,000 over 3 years, it can be replaced by documents with a qualified electronic signature); the document states the source of the money and how it was transferred 1 month
    Madrid 99% for groups I–II, 50% for group III The source is stated in the document; a public document if more than €10,000 over 3 years 30 working days
    Andalusia 99% for groups I–II Over €5,000: a notarial deed no later than a month after the transfer, stating the source 2 months
    Murcia 99% for groups I–III A public document stating the source; it can be executed within the filing deadline 30 working days
    Catalonia Reduced scale of 5–9% for groups I–II A notarial deed; if the money was transferred without one, it must be executed before a notary within a month of the transfer 1 month

    Group I: descendants under 21; II: descendants aged 21 and over, spouses, parents and other ascendants; III: brothers and sisters, uncles and aunts, nephews and nieces, relatives by marriage.

    In the Valencian Community, Madrid, Andalusia and Murcia the relief requires a document stating where the money came from (in Andalusia, for amounts over €5,000); this is the same document the bank will ask for. In Catalonia the reduced scale requires a notarial deed, but the law does not require it to state the source of the money. The rules of the autonomous communities do not say directly whether a Ukrainian notarial gift agreement with an apostille and a translation is recognised for the relief, so it is better to choose how to formalise the gift before the money is transferred.

    How money leaves Ukraine: NBU limits

    As a general rule, NBU Resolution No. 18 prohibits cross-border transfers from Ukraine, except in cases from a closed list (para. 14). The limits are set in the national currency; we converted them into euros at the official NBU rate as of 1 October 2026:

    • paying for goods, works and services abroad: about €3,900 a month from accounts in the national currency and the same amount by transfer from foreign currency accounts (from 11 August 2026 the limit doubled, Resolution No. 90);
    • card payments from a foreign currency account for goods and services abroad: no limit on the amount, except for certain categories of merchants with their own limits;
    • renting housing and accommodation abroad from foreign currency accounts: about €9,900 a month;
    • card-to-card transfers to individuals abroad from foreign currency accounts: about €2,000 a month.

    These channels are designed for paying specific expenses. We found no separate ground on the list for a resident individual to transfer a large sum to their own foreign account, for example money from selling a flat, and there is no official NBU clarification either. It is better to plan the route of the money before the transaction. The limits change; the current text is in the reference copy of Resolution No. 18 on the NBU website.

    What not to do

    Splitting transfers. For banks’ mandatory monthly reporting, Spain’s financial intelligence unit (SEPBLAC) sets criteria for when several transactions are added up as parts of one (art. 27.2 RD 304/2014), and an unusual transaction with no clear purpose is examined regardless of the amount (art. 17 Ley 10/2010). A series of transfers in small parts raises more questions than a single transfer with documents.

    Carrying cash without a declaration. Up to €10,000 can be taken out of Ukraine without a written declaration; from €10,000, only with a full written declaration to customs (NBU Regulation No. 3), and for an amount above €10,000 a Ukrainian resident also needs bank documents showing that this cash was withdrawn from their own accounts no earlier than 90 days before departure, plus an exchange receipt if the money was exchanged (para. 18 of NBU Regulation No. 5). At the EU external border, amounts of €10,000 or more are declared on the single EU form; at Spanish customs this is form E-1. When entering Spain from another EU country, the Spanish S-1 declaration is also required (Orden ETD/1217/2022). Without a declaration, the whole amount except a subsistence minimum is temporarily seized for 30 days, extendable to 90, and if penalty proceedings are opened, the money remains held as security until they are decided (art. 35.2 and 61.2 Ley 10/2010). The fine is from €600 up to half of the amount (art. 57.3).

    Paying for a home in cash. If one of the parties is acting as a business, for example the seller is a developer, cash payments of €1,000 or more are not allowed; for a buyer who is an individual without tax domicile in Spain the threshold is €10,000, and split payments are added together (art. 7 Ley 7/2012). The notary attaches the declaration of imported cash to the deed, and if there is none, makes a note of it and reports it to the General Council of Notaries.

    Routing money through other people’s accounts. A transfer through relatives’ accounts “to make it simpler” adds a link to the chain that will also have to be explained with documents.

    Frequently asked questions

    The bank has stopped a transfer and will not explain why. Is that allowed?
    Yes. The law prohibits the bank from telling a client that their transaction is being examined (art. 24 Ley 10/2010), so there is usually no explanation. Such a request is answered with documents on the source of funds. If there are no documents, the bank is entitled not to carry out the transaction or to end the relationship with the client, and bears no liability for doing so (art. 7.3).
    From what amount does the bank ask where the money came from?
    There is no single threshold in the law: the bank assesses the risk itself and may ask about any transaction that does not look like your usual ones. For money transfers through services without an account (envío de dinero: Western Union and the like) above €3,000 and for currency exchange above €6,000, in a single operation or in total over a quarter, the regulation expressly requires enhanced due diligence (art. 19.2 RD 304/2014). This threshold does not apply to an ordinary transfer between bank accounts.
    Can I transfer the amount in parts to avoid questions?
    That does not work and usually does harm. For banks’ mandatory reporting, SEPBLAC sets criteria for when several transactions are added up as parts of one (art. 27.2 RD 304/2014), and unusual transactions with no clear purpose are examined regardless of the amount (art. 17 Ley 10/2010). A single transfer with a full set of documents is more reliable.
    Will a Spanish bank accept a Ukrainian notarised translation?
    It may, but it is not obliged to. Under the law, an official translation is one made by a sworn translator appointed by the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs (traductor-intérprete jurado), by the Ministry’s Translation Office (OIL), by Spanish consulates abroad (for matters they handle) and by the Consulate of Ukraine in Spain for Ukrainian public documents (DA 16ª Ley 2/2014). A translation by a Ukrainian notary is not on this list, so before ordering, check with the bank or notary which translation they need.
    My parents gave me money for a flat. Do I have to pay tax in Spain?
    Yes, gift tax is paid by the resident recipient, wherever the money is. Reliefs for close relatives (up to 99% in the Valencian Community, Madrid, Andalusia and Murcia) usually require a public document stating the source of the money, and the return must be filed on time: the national deadline is 30 working days (the same in Madrid and Murcia), 1 month in the Valencian Community and Catalonia, and 2 months in Andalusia.
    How much cash can I carry from Ukraine to Spain?
    Up to €10,000 can be taken out of Ukraine without a written declaration; from €10,000, only with a full written declaration, and for an amount above €10,000 a Ukrainian resident also needs bank documents showing that this cash was withdrawn from their own accounts no earlier than 90 days before departure, plus an exchange receipt if the money was exchanged. On entering the EU, amounts of €10,000 or more must be declared; in Spain this is form E-1 when arriving from a non-EU country or S-1 from another EU country. Without a declaration, the whole amount except a subsistence minimum is temporarily seized for 30 days, extendable to 90, and if penalty proceedings are opened, the money remains held as security until they are decided (art. 35.2 and 61.2 Ley 10/2010). The fine is from €600 up to half of the amount (art. 57.3).
    I have no document at all. Can a notary replace it?
    No. A notary can draw up a record of your statement about where the money came from, but the notary records what was said and does not certify that it is true. Such a record links the documents you do have and explains the gaps. Whether it is enough is decided by the bank or notary that asked the question.
