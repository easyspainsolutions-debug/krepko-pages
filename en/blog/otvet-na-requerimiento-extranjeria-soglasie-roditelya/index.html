    Extranjería request for the other parent's consent: how to reply within 10 days

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~10 min read
    In short. The other parent's consent is required by the rule itself: Article 160.1 of RD 1155/2024 together with Article 154 of the Spanish Civil Code. You have 10 working days from notification to reply; silence closes the case (desistimiento). An extension of up to 5 working days under Article 68.2 LPACAP must be requested before the deadline expires, and the office is not obliged to grant it. The most predictable form of consent: an acta before a Spanish notary, in Spanish, with no apostille needed. The acta is made valid until the children come of age, with no earlier expiry, and covers all the children at once; then it also works for the TIE card and later procedures. The reply is filed by the applicant personally via Mercurio. Checked on 23 October 2026.

    A letter from the Oficina de Extranjería (the immigration office) arrives when the child's residence permit case already seemed almost settled. It asks you to confirm custody and actual care of the child and to attach a notarial record (acta de manifestaciones) from the other parent authorising the child to live in Spain. You are given 10 working days to reply. This is a request to remedy the application (requerimiento de subsanación) under Article 160 of the Immigration Regulation (Royal Decree 1155/2024): the office is filling a gap in the file, not refusing.

    Below we look at where the requirement comes from, how to count the deadline, which forms of consent are accepted, what the acta must contain and who files the reply. We checked the rules and the offices' practice on 23 October 2026.

    What a requerimiento de subsanación is and why consent is requested

    Requerimiento de subsanación: a letter from the immigration office listing what is missing from the application you filed. For children who were not born in Spain, the residence permit is issued under Article 160 of RD 1155/2024. The standard text of a request under this article asks for one of two things: a collective municipal registration certificate (padrón colectivo), if both parents live with the child, or proof of custody and actual care together with a notarial acta from the other parent in which they authorise the child to reside in Spain.

    The requirement was not invented by the office. The second paragraph of Article 160.1 of RD 1155/2024 says it plainly: if the child is the child of only one of the spouses, there must be either sole parental authority, or exclusive custody with court authorisation, or the other parent's consent. With joint custody, the other holder of custody must consent to the child residing in the country (in the original: el otro titular del derecho de custodia haya dado su consentimiento para que resida en territorio nacional).

    Behind this is the Spanish Civil Code. Under Article 154, a child's place of residence may be changed only with the consent of both parents or by a court decision, and Article 156 establishes that parents exercise parental authority (patria potestad) jointly. Moving to Spain is precisely a change of residence.

    No court needed. On 4 July 2022 the Defensor del Pueblo (the Spanish Ombudsman) examined a case in which the office was demanding a court decision. The conclusion: the other parent's notarial consent is sufficient, and the administration agreed. Going to court remains the route for cases where consent cannot be obtained.

    How to count the 10 days and what happens if you stay silent

    The reply deadline is set in Article 160.5 of RD 1155/2024: 10 days. Under Article 30.2 of the Administrative Procedure Act (LPACAP), deadlines expressed in days are counted in working days, meaning Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays are not counted. The count starts on the day following notification.

    There is a subtlety with notification. If the application was filed electronically, the requerimiento arrives in the electronic notification inbox. Under Article 43.2 LPACAP, a notification that has not been opened within 10 calendar days of being made available is deemed served. In other words, the deadline may start running while you have not yet read the letter. Until the case is decided, check the notification inbox regularly.

    If you do not reply. The application is treated as abandoned by the applicant (desistimiento) and archived. This is not a refusal on the merits, and you can apply again, but the case starts from scratch: a new application and a new wait for a decision.

    Can the deadline be extended

    Yes, but with no guarantee. Article 68.2 LPACAP allows the office to extend the deadline for remedying the application by up to 5 working days at the applicant's request, made before the deadline expires, if the documents are hard to obtain (dificultades especiales). The general rule of Article 32 LPACAP: an extension may not exceed half of the original period, and if a party to the procedure is abroad, the maximum extension may be granted.

    Three conditions always apply. The request is filed before the main deadline expires. The decision is up to the office, and a refusal cannot be appealed. There is no guarantee of an extension, so the acta is prepared in parallel with the request, not instead of it. A typical argument: the other parent is abroad, and consular consent physically cannot be obtained within 10 working days.

    Which forms of consent are accepted

    The offices rely on the information sheets (hojas informativas) on Article 160 and on practice. There are several options, and they are not equal in reliability and timing.

    Where the other parent is Form of consent What to bear in mind
    In Spain An acta before a Spanish notary (acta de manifestaciones) The most predictable route: in Spanish, no apostille or translation, one appointment
    Abroad, with a Spanish consulate nearby Consent before a Spanish consul Accepted according to the offices' information sheets, no apostille needed. Timing depends on getting a consulate appointment
    Abroad, no consulate available Local notary + apostille + sworn translation into Spanish Accepted, but refusals happen because of the wording or the translation. The three stages rarely fit into 10 working days
    Consent cannot be obtained A court decision granting exclusive custody A long route for cases where the other parent is unreachable or refuses
    In Spain, wants to arrange it through the Ukrainian consulate A consular notarial act Whether offices accept it has not been confirmed as of 23 October 2026; we do not advise risking the deadline
    Anywhere, online Remote video notarisation Does not work: Article 17 ter of the Ley del Notariado requires electronic identification recognised by Spanish law, which the other parent usually does not have

    If the other parent is in Spain even for a day, an acta before a Spanish notary settles the matter faster than any other option. If they are abroad, look for a Spanish consulate appointment and at the same time file the request for an extension.

    What the acta must contain

    Acta de manifestaciones: the notary records the parents' statements. The weak point of such actas is not the signature but the content: the office needs not an abstract “I do not object” text but a document that shows who the parents are, who lives with whom and what the consent was given for. The structure that satisfies a request under Article 160:

    1. Parties. Both parents appear before the notary (comparecen).
    2. Parentage (filiación). Who the parents of the child or children are.
    3. Parental authority (patria potestad). That it is joint and neither parent has been deprived of it.
    4. Custody and care (guarda y custodia). Who the children actually live with, and where.
    5. The parent's status in Spain. The residence permit of the parent on whom the child's case depends.
    6. Authorisation (autorización). The other parent authorises the children to reside in Spain and empowers the first parent to handle the related procedures.
    7. The first parent's agreement (conformidad). They accept this authorisation and confirm the facts stated.
    8. Validity (vigencia) and purpose (finalidad). The acta is valid until the children come of age unless revoked before a notary or by a court decision; it is intended for the Oficina de Extranjería, with the case numbers stated, and for any other procedure (cualquier procedimiento).

    The acta is drawn up only in Spanish: that is how the office reads it. No translation or apostille is needed afterwards, as the document is made in Spain for a Spanish authority. If the other parent does not speak Spanish, the content is translated and explained to them at the signing, before they sign.

    What instruction SEM 2/2026 does not remove

    For families switching from temporary protection, instruction SEM 2/2026 of 22 June 2026 removed three barriers in Article 160: the requirement of two years of prior residence, proof of means and proof of housing. You can apply for a child even if the parent is still under temporary protection. But the instruction does not touch the other parent's consent: it remains a requirement of the rule itself and of the Civil Code. That is why the requirement applies equally to families from Ukraine and to any other foreigners applying under Article 160. More on the route out of temporary protection for children: the page on switching from temporary protection and the page on residence for minor children.

    Why the acta is made open-ended and covers all the children

    A residence permit under Article 160 is issued for 5 years straight away and cannot be renewed: after five years the child moves to long-term residence (larga duración) under Articles 183 and 184, where the other parent's consent is not required. The TIE card is issued within the same case. So the consent serves two purposes: the reply to the requerimiento now, and possible questions at the TIE stage or in another procedure later.

    Hence two rules. First: the acta is stated to be valid until the age of majority, with a revocation clause; a one-year acta will have to be redone at the first new request. Second: if there are several children and cases, one acta covers them all, listing the children and the case numbers. A separate acta for each child gives you nothing except repeat visits to the notary.

    How the reply is filed

    The reply to a requerimiento is filed in the same case through the same channel that was used to open it. If the parent filed the application electronically themselves, the reply is uploaded via the Mercurio platform with Cl@ve or a digital certificate: you select the case that is already open by its number and attach the acta and the other requested documents. If the case was handled by a lawyer, a gestor administrativo or a graduado social with access under an agreement with their professional association (colegio), they file the reply.

    Anyone else (except trade unions and non-profit organisations listed in the Registro Electrónico de Colaboradores de Extranjería, art. 197.4.c), including agencies and acquaintances, may file the reply on the applicant's behalf only with a notarial power of attorney or with apud acta representation in the Apodera registry (art. 197.4.a RD 1155/2024). A simple signed note authorising representation is not enough.

    Valencian Community. Offices in the Valencian Community do not accept additional documents via the general electronic registry (REC/GEISER) or paper registry offices: such a document is treated as not filed (tener por no presentada). Mercurio only. There are two exceptions: the applicant has neither a certificate nor Cl@ve (then in Valencia the documents are handed in by appointment at the registry of the Oficina de Extranjería itself); or a platform failure, confirmed by a screenshot and a reply from the support service (CAU), in which case REC is accepted as a fallback channel.

    We do not file the reply in our own name: the applicant sends it. Our part ends with the finished acta and clear instructions on what to upload and where, so that the document reaches the right case on time.

    Typical mistakes

    Waiting until the last day. The notary, the other parent and the Mercurio platform rarely line up in their schedules. Sign the acta in the first half of the period so that a few days are left for uploading and possible glitches.

    Attaching consent for the wrong thing. The most common substitution: the family already has a travel consent for a minor or permission for a child to live abroad drawn up for another country. The office is asking for something else: consent to residence in Spain linked to the residence permit case. A document about travel does not satisfy the requirement.

    A foreign acta with general wording. A notary abroad writes according to their own templates, and these may mention neither custody, nor purpose, nor Spain. Add a sworn translation with approximate legal terms, and the document will not be accepted even though the requirement has formally been met.

    Forgetting the case number. The acta and the cover letter must state the case number (expediente), and if there are several children, the numbers of all the cases. Otherwise the document may not be linked to the request.

    Frequently asked questions

    How many days do you get to reply to a requerimiento on a child's residence permit?
    10 days under Article 160.5 of RD 1155/2024, and these are working days (Article 30.2 LPACAP). The count runs from the day following notification. An electronic notification not opened for 10 calendar days is deemed served (Article 43.2 LPACAP), so the deadline may be running while the letter is still unread.
    Can the deadline for the reply be extended?
    You can request an extension of up to 5 working days under Article 68.2 LPACAP, citing special difficulties, for example that the other parent is abroad. The request is filed before the main deadline expires, the decision is up to the office, and a refusal cannot be appealed.
    The child's father is in Ukraine or in Poland. How do we arrange the consent?
    Two routes: consent before a Spanish consul in the country where he is, or an acta before a local notary with an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish. The second route is accepted, but refusals happen because of the wording or the translation, and it rarely fits into 10 working days. If the father can come to Spain even for a day, an acta before a Spanish notary is faster and more reliable. In parallel, file a request for an extension.
    Does an acta from a Spanish notary need an apostille?
    No. The acta is executed in Spain for a Spanish authority, so no apostille or translation is needed. An apostille together with a sworn translation into Spanish is required only for documents executed abroad, for example before a Ukrainian or Polish notary.
    Is this acta valid for the TIE card and long-term residence?
    Yes, if it is written to be valid until the age of majority with no earlier expiry and with the purpose stated as the Oficina de Extranjería and any other procedure. A residence permit under Article 160 is issued for 5 years without renewals, followed by long-term residence (larga duración), where consent is not required. The TIE card is issued within the same case.
    Can we do without a notary?
    Yes, if there is a court decision granting exclusive custody: Article 160.1 names it as an alternative to consent. But it is a long route. When the other parent agrees, a notarial acta satisfies the requirement in a single appointment, and according to the position of the Defensor del Pueblo (2022) a court is not needed for this.
    We have two children and two cases. Do we need two actas?
    No. One acta covers all the children and all the case numbers, if they are listed in the text. This saves you repeat visits to the notary and separate actas.
    Did instruction SEM 2/2026 abolish the other parent's consent for families with temporary protection?
    No. The instruction removed the requirement of two years of residence, proof of means and proof of housing. The other parent's consent remains a requirement of Article 160 of RD 1155/2024 and Article 154 of the Civil Code and applies to everyone who applies under this article.
