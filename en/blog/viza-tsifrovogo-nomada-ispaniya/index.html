    Digital nomad visa in Spain 2026: requirements, documents, pitfalls

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    Spain has opened its doors to remote workers and freelancers through the Digital Nomad Visa, introduced in 2023 under the Ley de Startups. It is an excellent way to work legally on a Spanish residence permit without being tied to a Spanish employer. But the application process requires care: many Ukrainian and Russian-speaking applicants run into problems with documents and income requirements.

    What the Digital Nomad Visa is and the new Ley de Startups

    At the end of 2022 Spain passed the Ley de Startups, a law that opened residence to entrepreneurs and remote workers. The digital nomad visa is a separate chapter of it: a residence permit for those who work for a foreign company or have their own clients outside Spain.

    The main difference from an ordinary work visa is that you do not need a Spanish employer or a work permit tied to one. You remain an employee or contractor of a foreign company, and Spain grants you residence as a remote worker. Hence the focus of the check: the consulate looks not at a job vacancy but at where the money comes from and how steadily it arrives.

    Who can apply for the Digital Nomad Visa

    Two categories of applicants can apply, and their supporting evidence is put together differently:

    • Employees of foreign companies: those who work under an employment contract for a company located outside Spain
    • Freelancers and sole entrepreneurs: those who have their own foreign clients and work as self-employed (autónomo) or have their own business

    A strict condition: at least 80% of your income must come from companies/clients located outside Spain, and an employee under an employment contract may work only for foreign companies. This is the first thing we calculate from your contracts and incoming payments. If the proportion does not add up, there is no point applying, and it is better to find this out before you pay for translations and insurance.

    Income requirements in 2026

    The income threshold is tied to the SMI (Salario Mínimo Interprofesional), the minimum wage. In 2026 the SMI is €1,221 a month in 14 payments a year (€17,094 a year), and the visa threshold shifts in line with it every year.

    The Digital Nomad Visa requires a monthly income of at least 200% of the SMI, that is €2,442 a month in 14 payments (€34,188 a year, about €2,849 a month in 12 payments). Some consulates calculate more strictly, so before filing we check the current threshold of the specific consulate you are applying to.

    Income is proven by four things, and they need to be put together so that they do not contradict each other:

    • Bank statements for the last 3–6 months
    • An employment contract with a foreign company (for employees)
    • Contracts with clients and invoices issued (for freelancers)
    • An income certificate from the employer

    Documents required for the application

    The basic package looks like this. Some items take months to prepare, so the order in which you gather them matters more than speed:

    • Passport: valid for the whole period of the visa
    • Employment contract or contracts with clients: with a sworn translation into Spanish; they do not need to be certified by a notary
    • Income certificate (for the last 3–6 months)
    • Bank statements: showing the money coming in
    • Criminal record certificate (Criminal Record): from every country where you have lived in the last 2 years, plus a declaration under your own responsibility that you have had no criminal convictions for 5 years
    • Health insurance: private, from an insurer authorised to operate in Spain, with no co-payments and no waiting period, or public through the Seguridad Social
    • A certificate from the consulate: not needed; the consulate itself checks through the police that you are not on the Schengen entry ban lists and do not pose a threat

    The application process and processing times

    There are two ways to apply. Both the timeframe and the status you end up holding depend on which you choose:

    Through a consulate abroad

    The documents are submitted to the Spanish consulate in your country of residence. Processing usually takes 2–4 weeks, after which you are issued a D visa for digital nomads for up to 1 year. Then you come to Spain: the visa itself gives you the right to live here without a TIE card, and the residence card (TIE) is obtained after switching to a UGE permit. We handle this step too: we book the appointment at the police station (comisaría), prepare the forms and accompany you when your fingerprints are taken.

    Through the UGE (if you are already in Spain)

    If you are already in Spain (for example, on a tourist visa), the application goes directly to the UGE, the Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos of the Ministry of Inclusion, Social Security and Migration. This route is faster, but some documents will have to be obtained locally: municipal registration (empadronamiento), sworn translations, sometimes a digital certificate. We take this part on while you work.

    Visa duration and renewal

    The Digital Nomad Visa is issued according to the following scheme:

    • Initial period: 1 year
    • First renewal: up to 3 years (UGE permit after the visa)
    • Second renewal: 2 years
    • Third renewal: 5 years (as a rule, permanent residence)

    If you renew on time, after 5 years of residence the chain leads to permanent residence. The application for a UGE permit on the basis of the visa is filed in the last 60 days of the visa's validity, and renewing the permit up to 90 days late risks a fine, while a longer delay breaks your period of residence and sends you back to the start, so we keep track of the renewal dates and warn you in advance.

    Tax consequences: the Beckham Law and the special regime

    This point is often the very reason people choose the visa: access to the special tax regime (Beckham Law), expressly open to employees under an employment contract (to freelancers only in special cases):

    • A flat tax rate of 24% on income up to €600,000 a year
    • Benefits for the first few years of residence in Spain
    • The possibility of getting the benefit even if you have never lived in Spain

    Instead of the progressive income tax (IRPF), with rates of up to 45–54% depending on the region, you pay a flat 24%. With an income of €3,000 a month (€36,000 a year) the regime is usually not worthwhile: under the general scale the tax on such an amount comes to less than 24%, and the gain appears only at a noticeably higher income.

    A separate step: the regime does not apply automatically. It is requested from the tax office (Agencia Tributaria, AEAT) with a separate application and within its own deadline. Consulates do not warn you about this, and people find out about the regime when it is already too late to apply. We put this application into the plan straight away, together with the visa application.

    Digital Nomad Visa vs Autónomo (self-employment)

    The Digital Nomad Visa is not the better option for everyone. If you are counting on Spanish clients, income within the country or your own business here, it makes more sense to register as an autónomo (self-employed in Spain) or set up a company: on the nomad visa you hit the ceiling of 20% of income from Spanish sources. We go through both scenarios at the outset and tell you which one is cheaper for you personally, before you start gathering documents.

    Pitfalls and typical mistakes

    This is where applications stumble most often, and what we check first:

    • Incorrectly drawn-up contracts: the consulate needs unambiguous evidence of a foreign source of income, and a simple letter from the employer is usually not enough. We tell you in advance which wording must be in the contract and ask the employer to add it before filing
    • Insufficient proof of income: a statement will be challenged if it does not show steady, regular payments. We look at the statements before filing and work out how to cover the weak months: with invoices, statements of work completed, a certificate from the employer
    • Problems with insurance: a travel policy is not suitable; the private policy must be comprehensive, with no excess and no waiting period. We check the policy terms before you pay, so that the money is not wasted
    • Refusals based on “economic criteria”: some consulates refuse simply because the required income seems low to them
    • Translation of documents: certified translations are expensive (€50–100 per document), but without them consulates do not consider the application
    • Delays with the Criminal Record: the criminal record certificate takes 1–2 months to prepare and holds up the whole process. That is why we make the request for it the first step, in parallel with gathering the rest of the package

    Alternative options for remote workers

    If the visa does not work out because of your income or client structure, other routes remain:

    • Visa for the self-employed (autónomo): requires registration in Spain, but gives more flexibility
    • Investor visa: if you have capital to invest in a Spanish business
    • Visa for an intern or student: if you work part-time and study

    If you would rather not work it all out yourself, we offer the digital nomad visa, filed through a lawyer: we'll look at your situation and tell you what will be needed in your particular case.

    Frequently asked questions

    What minimum income is required for the digital nomad visa in 2026?

    The minimum income is 200% of the SMI, that is €2,442 a month in 14 payments (€34,188 a year, about €2,849 in 12 payments) in 2026, with the SMI at €1,221. The amount changes every year in line with the SMI and may be specified further by a particular consulate.

    Who can apply for the Digital Nomad Visa in Spain?

    Employees of foreign companies with an employment contract outside Spain, and freelancers/entrepreneurs with foreign clients. At least 80% of income must come from companies or clients outside Spain, and an employee under an employment contract may work only for foreign companies.

    For how long is the digital nomad visa issued?

    Initially the visa is issued for 1 year when you apply through a consulate abroad, or you get a residence permit for 3 years straight away when you apply from within Spain. After the visa, a permit for up to 3 years is obtained in Spain, then it is renewed for 2 years at a time, and after 5 years you can move to permanent residence.

    Do I need private health insurance for the Digital Nomad Visa?

    You need insurance: comprehensive private cover in Spain, valid for the whole term of the residence permit, with no excess and no waiting period, or public cover through the Seguridad Social. If after approval you register with the Seguridad Social, the UGE does not require a private policy, but consulates may ask for one in any case.
