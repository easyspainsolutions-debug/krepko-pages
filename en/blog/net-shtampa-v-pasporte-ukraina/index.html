    Why the Ukrainian border does not stamp passports and what to do if there is no stamp

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. Ukrainian citizens are not given a border crossing stamp by default (Art. 12 of Law No. 1710-VI, Order No. 714 of the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Ukraine, MVS, in force since 1 January 2020): only at a personal request, and a stamp is not added retroactively. Since 10 April 2026 passports have also not been stamped on entry into Schengen (the EES system), and since 5 August 2026 the EU has required new applicants for temporary protection to prove that they have fulfilled their military obligations, first of all by a lawful departure (Decision 2026/1912). The crossing itself, without a stamp, is confirmed by a DPSU certificate: free if you request it yourself, up to 30 calendar days, or through a lawyer's request, by law up to 5 working days with a possible extension to 20. For temporary protection the EU also names a document from the Reserve+ app, and the certificate does not replace it. Spain has not published a list of accepted documents, so no one has a guarantee of acceptance.

    You have crossed the border, but your passport is empty: neither a Ukrainian exit stamp nor a Schengen entry stamp. The border guard has not made a mistake. For many years Ukrainian citizens have not been given the border crossing stamp («штамп про перетин кордону») by default, and since 2026 the EU has not been stamping passports either. This became a problem when people applying for temporary protection were required to prove their departure.

    We look at the legal basis on which the State Border Guard Service of Ukraine (DPSU; in Russian, GPSU) does not put a mark, who does get one, whether it can be obtained retroactively and how to prove your departure without it: with a DPSU certificate, in Ukrainian «довідка про перетин кордону» (dovidka pro peretyn kordonu). We checked the rules on 1 October 2026 against the laws of Ukraine, European Commission documents and the BOE.

    Why is there no border crossing stamp (штамп про перетин кордону)?

    Because the law says so. The Law of Ukraine “On Border Control” («Про прикордонний контроль») No. 1710-VI (Art. 12, part 3, item 1) states plainly: no border crossing mark is put in the passport of a Ukrainian citizen, except when the person has asked for it. The rule was already in the first version of the law in 2009 and has been kept in the version of 27 June 2026. The border guard who handed your passport back without a mark did nothing wrong.

    The procedure for marks is set out in Order No. 714 of the Ministry of Internal Affairs of Ukraine of 22 August 2019. It has been in force since 1 January 2020: the marks «В'їзд» (Entry) and «Виїзд» (Exit) are not put in the passports of Ukrainian citizens, except on personal request (section II, item 4). The official DPSU website says the same: after the check, a citizen's passport is returned without a mark, and a mark is put on request. If you left before 2020, check, just in case, the passport you crossed the border with at the time.

    The fact of the crossing is not lost, however: it is recorded in the border service's database, and it is from this database that the certificate is issued. In its clarifications of 18 August 2026 the European Commission also acknowledges that the passports of Ukrainian citizens are systematically not stamped.

    Are foreign nationals and stateless persons stamped?

    As a rule, yes. Foreign nationals and stateless persons receive a mark in their passport (Art. 12, part 1 of Law No. 1710-VI, section II, item 1 of Order No. 714). The law itself lists the exceptions (Art. 12, part 3): heads of state and senior officials whose visit has been announced in advance, sailors while their ship is in port, crews and passengers of cruise ships; marks are also not put in the certificates of aircraft crews.

    In some cases the mark is put not in the passport but on an immigration card: if the foreign national asked for it (part 4) or there are no pages left in the passport for marks, and under Order No. 714 also for citizens of migration-risk countries and for those who cross the border at least once a week. So an empty passport is above all a problem for Ukrainian citizens. If you have a different citizenship and there is a mark in your passport, you will most likely not need a border crossing certificate.

    Can you ask for a stamp or have it added retroactively?

    You can ask for one. The law leaves citizens the right to a mark on personal request, so at the checkpoint it is enough to tell the border guard that you need a stamp in your passport. On 20 August 2026 the Ministry of Foreign Affairs of Ukraine recommended doing exactly this when leaving for the EU, and recommended that those already abroad request a certificate from the DPSU. The European Commission gives the same advice in its clarifications: ask for a stamp when leaving.

    Retroactively, you cannot. On 21 August 2026 a DPSU spokesperson confirmed: if no stamp was put in the passport on departure, it will not be added later. A past crossing can only be confirmed with a DPSU certificate, and the service itself says so. So if there is no stamp and you need to prove your departure, the place to start is not a border checkpoint but a request to the DPSU Main Centre for Processing Special Information (GTsOSI) in Kyiv.

    Why has the lack of a stamp become a problem precisely in 2026?

    Two things have changed, and both are on the EU side. The first: there are no longer stamps on entry either. Since 10 April 2026 the EES (Entry/Exit System) has been fully operational at the external borders of Schengen: entries and exits of third-country nationals on short trips are recorded in an electronic database, and passports are not stamped by hand. Someone who left Ukraine after that date, as a rule, has no marks in their passport from either side. How to prove your date of entry now is explained in the article on entering Spain without a stamp.

    The second is more important. Since 5 August 2026 Council of the EU Decision 2026/1912 has been in force (Art. 2, with a corrigendum of the same day). New applicants for temporary protection must prove that they have fulfilled their military obligations in Ukraine. Recital 18 names as proof a lawful border crossing on departure or an exit stamp in the passport, and if this cannot be proven that way, an easily verifiable official document, for example from Reserve+. The burden of proof is on the applicant, and under recital 20 the obligations may apply regardless of sex and age.

    Spain applies the condition but has not provided a list of documents. The Ucrania Urgente portal only says that since August 2026 the fulfilment of military obligations must be proven. On 12 August 2026 the Embassy of Ukraine in Spain officially requested the list from the Spanish side; as of 1 October no response had been published. According to the European Commission's clarifications, in countries that do not border Ukraine, including Spain, a Ukrainian exit stamp is sufficient. The European Commission does not explicitly name the DPSU certificate. Other documents are accepted or rejected by the country at its own discretion, if they make it clear that the person left lawfully and in compliance with military obligations.

    Who now needs to prove their departure, and who does not?

    Under Decision 2026/1912 and the European Commission's clarifications, departure must be proven by:

    • those applying for temporary protection for the first time from 5 August 2026;
    • those moving to Spain from another EU country and filing a new application here (section III of the clarifications);
    • those who have lost their status and are applying again (section II of the clarifications).

    When status is lost in Spain is determined by Art. 24 RD 1325/2003. Protection ends automatically in the cases listed there: the protection period has expired, Spanish nationality has been acquired, the person has declared their return to their home country or renounced protection, or they have been officially transferred to another EU country. Failing to appear when summoned within 45 days without a valid reason is treated as tacit renunciation, and the Minister of the Interior declares the protection terminated (Art. 24.2). The European Commission adds that in the case of a long absence a country may, under its own rules, deregister the person and revoke the residence permit, and then the person will have to apply again, this time with proof of departure. A short trip in itself does not cancel the status.

    Not required:

    • for those who were under protection in Spain on 4 August 2026 and have kept it without interruption;
    • for those who applied before the new rules came into force;
    • for a change of residence status (modificación), arraigo, long-term residence (larga duración) and Spanish nationality: neither instruction SEM 2/2026, nor RD 1155/2024, nor the rules on nationality (Código Civil, Art. 22) require proof of departure;
    • for foreign nationals: as a rule, their crossings are marked.
    The 90-day benchmark. The European Commission's clarifications say that the date of the exit stamp should, as a rule, be no earlier than 90 days before the application for protection; exceptional circumstances are taken into account. This rule is not in the text of the decision itself, and whether Spain applies it has not been officially confirmed. If you left recently, it is better to order the document straight away. If you left Ukraine long ago and are moving from another EU country, the certificate will show an old departure date. The Commission does not explain how it will be treated in light of this benchmark, and a document from Reserve+ may be needed. This is worth finding out before you order the certificate.

    How can you prove a crossing without a stamp, and what is a DPSU certificate (довідка про перетин кордону)?

    Information on border crossings is issued by the Main Centre for Processing Special Information of the State Border Guard Service (GTsOSI DPSU) in Kyiv. Border checkpoints do not issue certificates. We have not found a channel through Diia, a TsNAP (administrative services centre) or a consulate. The DPSU online account, launched in May 2026, shows only restrictions on leaving the country; the history of crossings cannot be seen in it.

    According to descriptions by the DPSU and lawyers, the certificate states your full name, date of birth, citizenship, passport details, the date and time of each crossing, the border checkpoint and the direction: entry or exit. Since it contains passport details, it is best to give the details of the passport you used to leave. If it has since been replaced in Spain or in Ukraine, state both passports in the request.

    What the certificate does not prove. It confirms the fact, date and place of a crossing through a border checkpoint. The grounds for leaving, for example a deferment or exemption, are not mentioned in descriptions of the response. So the certificate does not replace an extract from Reserve+ or documents on a deferment or exemption: if they are asked for in your case, they are submitted separately.

    How to submit a request from Spain step by step is explained in the article on the Ukrainian border crossing certificate. For other ways of proving departure when applying for protection, see the article on temporary protection without a stamp.

    Should you request the certificate yourself or through a lawyer?

    There are two lawful routes, and the first is free. You submit a written application to the GTsOSI DPSU yourself, with a scan of the first page of your passport: in person, by post (26 Volodymyrska Street, Kyiv, 01601) or by e-mail to gcosi-dpsu@dpsu.gov.ua, attaching a scan of the application signed by hand or a file with a qualified electronic signature (KEP). E-mail is expressly permitted on the DPSU page of 18 December 2025, although a newer text from the service of 24 March 2026 names only submission in person and by post. The timeframe under the Personal Data Protection Act (No. 2297-VI, Art. 16) is up to 30 calendar days from the day the request is received, so if you send it by post from Spain, add time for delivery. Free access is set out in Art. 19 of the same law: a person's access to data about themselves is free of charge.

    The second route is a lawyer's request. Under Art. 24 of the Law on the Bar (No. 5076-VI), a response is given within 5 working days at the latest. If there is a lot of information, the DPSU may extend the period to 20 working days by notifying the lawyer in writing within the first 5 days. But the same law excludes restricted-access information from a lawyer's request, and information on border crossings is confidential personal data. That is why your written consent is attached to the request, and the 5-working-day period is a benchmark set by law, not a guarantee. A lawyer's request is usually faster, but it gives no additional right of access: you are entitled to obtain the document itself without it.

    In the experience of Ukrainian lawyers, timeframes are currently unstable: some people get a response in 5 working days, others in 15 or 20, and there is no pattern. The response comes as a certificate in your name, not as a letter to the lawyer. There is no official clarification on the form of such a response, so this is an observation by practitioners, not a promise. Whether a Spanish authority will accept the certificate cannot be said in advance: Spain has not published a list of accepted documents.

    What should you do if there is no stamp in your passport?

    1. Check the passport you used to leave. If it has an exit stamp, you most likely do not need a certificate.
    2. Check the list above: do you need to prove your departure at all? For a change of residence status, arraigo and long-term residence the certificate is not required.
    3. Ask the authority you are applying to what it needs: a paper original or a translation. For a document from Reserve+ the European Commission recommends a sworn translation into English or the language of the country, while its clarifications say nothing about the DPSU certificate; an apostille is not placed on it.
    4. If you need the certificate, order it straight away. By law the DPSU response takes up to 30 calendar days for a request made on your own or up to 5 working days (extendable to 20) for a lawyer's request, in practice 5 to 20 working days, and translation and delivery add time.
    5. If you cross the Ukrainian border again, ask the border guard to put a mark in your passport: this is your right under Art. 12 of Law No. 1710-VI.

    Frequently asked questions

    Why was my passport not stamped when I left Ukraine?
    Because by default Ukrainian citizens are not given a border crossing mark. This is set out in Art. 12 of the Law “On Border Control” No. 1710-VI and in MVS Order No. 714, in force since 1 January 2020. A mark is put only at a personal request, so the border guard has not made a mistake.
    Can a stamp be added retroactively?
    No. On 21 August 2026 the DPSU confirmed that stamps are not added retroactively. A past crossing is confirmed with a DPSU border crossing certificate (dovidka pro peretyn kordonu): it states the date, time, border checkpoint and direction.
    Are foreign nationals stamped when leaving Ukraine?
    As a rule, yes: foreign nationals and stateless persons receive a mark in their passport (Art. 12, part 1 of Law No. 1710-VI). The exceptions are listed in part 3 of the same article: heads of state, sailors while their ship is in port, crews and passengers of cruise ships. At the foreign national's request, and in some cases under MVS Order No. 714, the mark is put not in the passport but on an immigration card.
    Why is there no Schengen stamp in my passport either?
    Since 10 April 2026 the EES system has been fully operational at the external borders of Schengen. Entries and exits of third-country nationals on short trips are recorded in an electronic database, and passports are no longer stamped by hand.
    Do I need the certificate if I have been under temporary protection in Spain since 2022?
    As a rule, no. The new condition does not apply to those who were under protection in Spain on 4 August 2026 and have kept it without interruption. A short trip to Ukraine does not in itself cancel the status, but a long absence or failing to appear when summoned within 45 days may lead to its loss (RD 1325/2003, Art. 24; European Commission clarifications, section II), and then you will have to prove your departure when applying again. For a change of residence status, arraigo and long-term residence the certificate is not required either.
    Will a DPSU certificate be accepted in Spain?
    There is no guarantee. Spain has not published a list of accepted documents: the Embassy of Ukraine requested it on 12 August 2026, and as of 1 October there was no response. The European Commission does not explicitly name the DPSU certificate: each country accepts other documents at its own discretion, if they make it clear that the person left lawfully and in compliance with military obligations.
    How long does it take to get a DPSU certificate?
    For a request made on your own: up to 30 calendar days from the day the request is received, free of charge (Law No. 2297-VI, Art. 16 and 19). For a lawyer's request: by law up to 5 working days, extendable to 20 (Law No. 5076-VI, Art. 24), but in practice timeframes are currently unstable: some people get the certificate in 5 working days, others in 15 or 20. Translation and delivery to Spain add time.
