    Arraigo social — residence permit after 2 years of living in Spain

    Krepko · updated 23 November 2026 · ~7 min read
    In short. Arraigo social (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain) is a residence permit with the right to work for those who have been living in Spain continuously for at least two years (art. 126 Reglamento de Extranjería, RD 1155/2024; previously three were required). You need proof of the two years (usually the padrón histórico), a criminal record certificate from the countries where you lived for five years before entering Spain (Extranjería requests the Spanish records itself), close relatives with a residence permit or an integration report, and funds of at least 100% of IPREM for you (with a relative — 200% for the two of you); an employment contract is not needed. The first authorisation is issued for 1 year, and renewals are also for 1 year. Source: BOE — Real Decreto 1155/2024, Reglamento de Extranjería, art. 126.

    Arraigo social is one of the most workable ways to regularise your status in Spain if you have already been living here for several years. Two years — and you have a chance of a full residence permit with the right to work. It sounds simple, but the devil is in the details: the padrón, certificates, the integration report, the queue at Extranjería (the immigration office). We go through it all step by step.

    What arraigo social is and who it is for

    Arraigo social literally translates as “social rootedness”. The idea is that you prove to the Spanish state that you have been here a long time, have become part of society, have ties, a home, work — and deporting you would be strange. Spain recognises this and issues you a legal residence permit.

    This route suits those who:

    • have been living in Spain for 2 years or longer, but without papers or only with a tourist visa
    • came as a tourist, stayed, and now want to get out of the grey zone
    • lost their previous status (a student residence permit expired, a seasonal contract ended) and did not manage to renew in time
    • are under temporary protection but want to switch to a more stable status
    Important for Ukrainians: if you arrived under temporary protection in 2022, by 2025–2026 you have already built up the 2 years of residence required for arraigo social. This opens the way to regularisation, and it makes sense to start the switch while temporary protection is in force. We count your time from your entry dates and the padrón and tell you whether you already have what is required.

    Requirements: what you need to meet

    1. Two years of continuous residence in Spain

    This is the main requirement. After the reform of the Reglamento de Extranjería (RD 1155/2024), two years are enough — previously three were required. The two years are counted by your actual presence in Spain immediately before applying, not from the moment of registration. The key word is continuous. Short trips abroad (a holiday, visiting family) still count as residence, but absences totalling more than 90 days in the two years before applying break continuity.

    You need to prove the two years with documents. The main tool is the padrón histórico: an extract from the municipal register showing the full history of your registration at your addresses. Beyond that, bank statements, rental contracts, utility bill receipts and certificates of doctor's visits go into the file — the more parallel traces, the stronger your position.

    2. Empadronamiento (municipal registration)

    Registration on the padrón is not just a formality; it is your main documentary trail in Spain. The earlier you registered and the more steadily your addresses changed (without big gaps), the stronger your position. If there are holes in the padrón — for example, you were not registered for half a year — it is a problem, but not a dead end: the gap is filled with other traces of your presence, from a rental contract to records of doctor's visits.

    3. No criminal record

    You must provide a criminal record certificate from the countries where you lived for five years before entering Spain (for most Ukrainians — from Ukraine); Extranjería requests the Spanish records itself. The Ukrainian certificate must have an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish. Extranjería sets its validity — usually no more than 3 months on the date of application, and you need to count from the day of your appointment at Extranjería, not from the day you ordered it. The Krepko team in Ukraine obtains the Ukrainian certificate with an apostille and translation — we time its issue to your cita previa (appointment) date, so you do not have to order it twice.

    4. An employment contract or financial means

    With the reform of the Reglamento de Extranjería (RD 1155/2024, from 20 May 2025) the requirements changed: an employment contract is not needed for arraigo social — it is needed for arraigo sociolaboral. You need close relatives with a residence permit (a spouse, registered partner, parents or children) or an informe de integración social — an integration report from the autonomous community or the town hall. In both cases you prove means of subsistence of at least 100% of IPREM for you, and in the variant with a relative — 200% for the two of you: bank statements, income from renting out property, remote work with proven income.

    If you have a contract, look at arraigo sociolaboral: it requires a contract for at least 20 hours a week with a salary of no less than the SMI (minimum wage) in proportion to the hours, with official employment registered with the Seguridad Social. A civil-law service contract or cash-in-hand work will not do. We read the contract before it is signed and tell you whether your Extranjería office will accept it.

    Arraigo familiar as an alternative: if you are the parent or guardian of a child who is a national of another EU country, the EEA or Switzerland, look at arraigo familiar; parents of children with Spanish nationality are entitled to a separate authorisation for relatives of Spanish citizens (art. 94 RD 1155/2024). There is no requirement for a period of residence, only proof of the relationship and of involvement in the child's upbringing. It is often faster. And if you have worked officially in Spain under a contract, bear in mind: since 20 May 2025 it has not been possible to apply for arraigo laboral — it does not exist in the new regulation; with a signed employment contract, arraigo sociolaboral applies, and if in the last two years you held a residence permit (not one granted for exceptional circumstances) and it was not renewed — arraigo de segunda oportunidad (the residence period is the same — 2 years).

    Which documents are needed

    The list is standard, but every item is checked meticulously: any shortcoming turns into a refusal or a request for clarification (requerimiento) and adds months to the wait. We put together the whole set — we check the validity of the certificates, order missing documents from Ukraine and give you a folder ready for the appointment.

    1. Form EX–10 — the official application form. It is downloaded from the website of the Ministerio de Inclusión, Seguridad Social y Migraciones and filled in in Spanish. Mistakes on the form are one of the most common reasons for documents being returned.
    2. Passport — all pages, including blank ones. A copy + the original. If your passport expires in the coming months — renew it in advance.
    3. Padrón histórico — an extract from the municipal register covering 2 years. It is requested from your ayuntamiento (town hall). Some offices issue it on the spot, others after a few days.
    4. A criminal record certificate from Ukraine with an apostille + a sworn translation. Extranjería sets its validity, usually 3 months from the date of issue.
    5. A Spanish criminal record certificate is not needed: Extranjería requests the information from the Registro Central de Penados (Central Register of Convicted Persons) itself.
    6. Documents on close relatives with a residence permit, or an integration report (informe de integración social), plus proof of funds of at least 100% of IPREM for you (with a relative — 200% for the two of you); an employment contract is not needed for arraigo social, it is needed for arraigo sociolaboral.
    7. Additional proof of residence — rental contracts, bank statements, receipts, medical certificates.
    8. A photo is not needed with the application: it is handed in later, when the TIE is issued.
    9. Fee payment receipt — tasa Modelo 790, código 052. Paid at a bank or online.
    Tip: make several copies of each document. Some Extranjería offices require the original + 2 copies, others only 1. Better to take extra than to have to come back.

    The application process: cita previa and the OPE

    Applying for arraigo social is not done by post or through an online form (in most cases). It is an in-person visit to the Oficina de Extranjería (OPE) — the office for foreigners' affairs at the relevant delegación del gobierno. For this you need an appointment — cita previa.

    How to book

    You book through the sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es website in the section extranjería → autorización de residencia → arraigo social. In large cities (Madrid, Barcelona), appointments are booked up several months ahead — this is normal. Slots appear in bursts — most often early in the morning or late in the evening, when someone cancels. We watch the booking window in your province and get you the nearest available appointment.

    At the appointment

    Arrive strictly on time — being even 5 minutes late costs you the appointment. Bring all originals and copies. The inspector will check the set, accept the documents and give you a resguardo — confirmation of submission. If Spanish is not yet your language, we go to the appointment with you and speak to the inspector. From this moment the official processing period begins.

    If you do not yet have an NIE (Número de Identidad de Extranjero), it is assigned to you automatically when your application begins to be processed (art. 205 RD 1155/2024) — you do not need to request it separately. The NIE is needed for any legal and financial transactions in Spain.

    Processing times

    By law, Extranjería must decide on the application within 3 months. In practice, in 2025–2026 the average time is 6–9 months. Madrid and Barcelona — on average 7–9 months, Alicante and Valencia — 4–6, small provincial offices — sometimes faster.

    If there is no answer 3 months after the date of application, officially this counts as silencio administrativo negativo (silence = refusal). But in practice most cases simply take longer. An appeal over the silence does not always work: sometimes it pushes the case forward, sometimes it only complicates things. We bring in an immigration lawyer — they look at your case and tell you whether it will produce a result in your province.

    How to track the status: the Consulta de estado de expediente portal on the sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es website shows what stage your case is at. You need the expediente (case) number from your resguardo. If we are handling the case, we check the status ourselves and let you know when it changes.

    Typical mistakes and reasons for refusal

    Gaps in the padrón

    This is the number one reason for refusal. If your registration history has a period without registration — for example, you moved to a new flat and did not re-register for several months — Extranjería may consider that continuity has been broken. The solution: fill the gap with other proof of presence — bank statements, doctor's visits, a rental contract. We look at the padrón first and, if there is a gap, gather evidence to cover it before applying, not after a request from Extranjería.

    An expired criminal record certificate

    Extranjería sets the validity period: usually the document must have been issued no more than 3 months before the date of application. If more time has passed between getting the certificate and applying, you need a new one. This is especially relevant if you wait a long time for a cita previa.

    An incorrect employment contract

    A contract is not needed for arraigo social, but for arraigo sociolaboral it must be concluded under Spanish employment law, contain all the mandatory details, provide for at least 20 hours a week with a salary of no less than the SMI in proportion to the hours, and be signed by both parties. Templates from the internet often do not meet the requirements of a particular Extranjería office. If the employer is hiring a foreign national for the first time, we go through the contract before it is signed and bring in a lawyer when the wording needs to be corrected.

    Mistakes in form EX–10

    An incorrectly stated procedure type, the wrong code — and the application will be returned. We fill in the form together with you and check it against the current template on the Ministerio's website: we need your passport details and address, and the employer's details only for arraigo sociolaboral.

    Incorrectly apostilled documents

    The apostille is placed on the original document, not on a copy. The translation is done only by a sworn (jurado) translator — an ordinary notarised translation will not do. We take care of the apostille and the sworn translation: you will not have to look for a translator on the MAEC list and check whether their signature will be accepted.

    What to do after approval

    Getting the TIE

    After a positive decision you will have one month to book an appointment to get the TIE (Tarjeta de Identidad de Extranjero) — the physical residence card. The appointment is again through cita previa at the oficina de extranjería or a comisaría de policía (police station). At the appointment you need to bring the original documents, a photo and the receipt for the TIE fee (Modelo 790, código 012).

    The TIE is issued about 30–45 days after the appointment. Until you receive it, you have the resguardo of approval — this is enough for most legal purposes. We also take care of the TIE appointment and the fee payment: all we need from you is a photo and your signature at the appointment.

    The right to work legally

    Arraigo social is initially granted for 1 year with the full right to work — as an employee (cuenta ajena) or as self-employed (autónomo). You can work for any employer, in any sector. That is exactly why arraigo social is so valued: it does not tie you to a specific employer, as many other work visas do.

    Renewal and the path to permanent residence

    After a year, arraigo social is renewed — also for 1 year (you need to be looking for work and registered with the employment service), and if you are working, you can switch to a residence and work authorisation for 4 years (art. 191 RD 1155/2024). After 5 years of legal continuous residence in Spain you can apply for residencia de larga duración (long-term residence) — it is issued with no expiry and gives almost the same rights as citizenship. After 10 years you can apply for Spanish nationality (if the other conditions are met).

    Don't forget: your passport must be valid for the whole period of the residence permit. If your passport expires before the residence permit — renew your passport in advance, otherwise problems may arise when you renew the residence permit.

    Frequently asked questions

    How many years do you need to live in Spain for arraigo social?
    At least 2 years of continuous residence (after the reform of the Reglamento de Extranjería — RD 1155/2024; previously 3 were required). The count is based on your actual presence in Spain in the two years before applying, not from the moment of registration on the padrón. Short trips abroad (holidays, visits to family) still count as residence, but if in the two years before applying you spent more than 90 days in total outside Spain, continuity is broken and you will have to postpone the application.
    What counts as proof of residence in Spain?
    The main document is the padrón histórico, an extract from the municipal register showing the full history of your registration at your addresses. The following are also suitable: rental contracts, bank statements, utility bill receipts, medical certificates and records of doctor's visits, your children's school documents. The more parallel traces, the stronger your position.
    Do you need an employment contract for arraigo social?
    No. Since the reform of the Reglamento de Extranjería (RD 1155/2024, from 20 May 2025), an employment contract is not needed for arraigo social: it is needed for arraigo sociolaboral — a contract for at least 20 hours a week with a salary of no less than the SMI in proportion to the hours. Arraigo social requires close relatives with a residence permit or an integration report, plus means of subsistence of at least 100% of IPREM for you, and in the variant with a relative — 200% for the two of you: bank statements, income from renting out property, remote work with proven income.
    How long does getting a residence permit through arraigo social take?
    By law, Extranjería must decide on the application within 3 months. In reality, in 2025–2026 the average time is 6–9 months: Madrid and Barcelona — 7–9 months, Alicante and Valencia — 4–6, small provincial offices sometimes faster. On top of this, add the wait for a cita previa (from a couple of weeks to 3–4 months in large cities) and 30–45 days to get the TIE after approval.
    What if arraigo social is refused?
    You have 1 month to file a recurso de reposición (a pre-court appeal) with the same Extranjería office or 2 months for a recurso contencioso-administrativo (through the courts). In parallel you can submit a new application if the reasons for the refusal have been removed (for example, you have updated your padrón or obtained an integration report). If the refusal is due to gaps in residence — we gather additional evidence and rebuild the case. We bring in an immigration lawyer, who gives you a strategy within one day.
