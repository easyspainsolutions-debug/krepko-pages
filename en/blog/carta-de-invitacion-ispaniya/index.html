    What a carta de invitación is and why you need it

    A carta de invitación is a formal invitation from a resident of Spain, in which they confirm that they are prepared to host a foreign guest at their home. The document is needed:

    • To apply for a Schengen visa at the Spanish consulate in Ukraine, Russia and other countries — without an invitation, the chances of a tourist visa being approved are low
    • To confirm the purpose of the visit at the border — if your relative is travelling visa-free (for example, a citizen with a Ukrainian biometric passport), the border officer may ask for the document
    • To confirm accommodation in the visa application form

    The invitation states: the details of the person inviting (including their resident status and address), the details of the person invited, the purpose of the visit, the dates, and who pays the costs and accommodation. This is a legally significant statement: as a resident of Spain, the person inviting is responsible for the guest's stay. That is why we proofread every line before signing — a typo in a name or date costs a second trip to the notary.

    Where it is usually arranged — and why a cita at migración is a real problem

    The standard route is a police station (Comisaría Nacional de Policía / Oficina de Extranjería). The cita previa appointment is booked through sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es, category “Carta de Invitación”.

    The reality of 2024-2026: getting a cita is almost impossible. Appointments are snapped up within minutes of being released in the system. In Barcelona, Madrid and Alicante the nearest free slots are 1-3 months away. In peak seasons (April, June, December) — up to 4-5 months. Bots and paid services charge for an appointment in advance and hand over a slot as luck would have it.

    If your relative needs the visa now — with the embassy appointment just 2 weeks away — the standard route does not work. This is the most common reason people come to us.

    This is not a problem specific to Alicante — it is the whole of Spain. Because of the general overload of immigration services since 2022, cita previa for any procedure (NIE, TIE, residence permits, invitations) has become a bottleneck. The alternatives are a notary or paid cita services with unpredictable results.

    The alternative — a carta de invitación before a notary on the same day

    Fewer people know about the second route: the invitation is drawn up as a notarial deed — a declaración jurada de invitación or carta de invitación notarial. It is a full alternative, legally valid, and accepted by consulates and border officers.

    How to arrange it — step by step

    1. Message us on WhatsApp. Tell us: who you are inviting (name, passport, relationship), for which dates, and which visa they are applying for. Your part at this step is 5 minutes of messaging
    2. We prepare the text of the invitation in two languages (Spanish + your language), filling in your TIE/NIE details, address and income. 1 working day
    3. We book the notary appointment in Alicante — usually for the next day, or the same day if urgent
    4. 30 minutes at the notary's office — you come with your passport, TIE, empadronamiento and income certificate. You sign, and we translate every paragraph aloud
    5. Apostille from the College of Notaries — we get it ourselves if the document is going to the authorities of another country (Hague Convention); a Spanish notarial document is submitted to the Spanish consulate without an apostille. 1-3 days as standard, the same day if urgent
    6. Delivery — we send the original to your relative by courier and track it until it is delivered

    Which documents the person inviting needs

    • TIE or NIE + passport — bring the originals to the notary
    • Empadronamiento — a certificate no older than 3 months. If you do not have one or it has expired, we request a new one while the text is being prepared
    • Proof of accommodation — a rental contract, escritura (title deed) or an extract from the Land Registry (nota simple)
    • Income certificate covering 3-6 months — a bank account statement, a certificate from your employer or a tax return. The minimum is 10%of the monthly minimum wage (SMI) for each day of the visit per guest, but not less than 90%of the SMI per person (Orden PRE/1282/2007); we work out the current threshold for your dates when you message us
    • Details of the person invited — a copy of their passport, their full name exactly as in the passport, date of birth, address, and the purpose and dates of the visit
    Income is the most common stumbling block. If you have no official income, you can use a statement of savings (extracto bancario showing the balance). The minimum savings are calculated as 10%of the SMI × number of days of the visit × number of people invited, but not less than 90%of the SMI per guest. Send us the statement before booking the notary — we will check the amount against your dates and number of guests and tell you whether it is enough.

    Common mistakes

    Mistake 1: the details of the person invited do not match the passport

    First name, surname and patronymic must be given strictly as in the passport, in Latin letters (the transliteration letter for letter as in the passport). Any typo and the consulate will refuse, and the document will have to be redone. We check every letter against a photo of the passport before the text goes to the notary.

    Mistake 2: an empadronamiento older than 3 months

    A recent empadronamiento is compulsory. If your last certificate is from January and it is now April, the notary will not accept it. The ayuntamiento (town hall) issues a new certificate in 1-2 days — we request it for you while the text is being prepared.

    Mistake 3: insufficient income

    If the income certificate is below 10%of the SMI × days of the visit × guests, the notary will sign, but the consulate may refuse. It is better to show savings or combine your income with a balance statement. We look at your documents in advance and tell you whether you meet the threshold or not.

    Mistake 4: forgetting the apostille

    Without an apostille the document works in Spain, including at Spanish consulates abroad. For the authorities of another country, for example Ukraine or Russia, an apostille is usually needed. We get the apostille on notarial documents in Alicante ourselves — at the College of Notaries it takes 1-3 days.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can a carta de invitación be done before a notary rather than at the police?
    Yes. A notarial invitation (carta de invitación notarial / declaración jurada de invitación) is an alternative to the form from the Comisaría. Spanish consulates, as a rule, accept it for Schengen visa applications. The main advantage is that it is done on the same day, with no need to wait 1-2 months for a cita previa at migración.
    How do I find out the cost of a carta de invitación before a notary?
    Message us and we will work it out for your situation. The cost is made up of three parts: our work (depends on the service — standard or urgent same-day), the notary fee (state-regulated) and the apostille (if the document is submitted to the authorities of another country; the Spanish consulate does not need it). We state all amounts transparently, as separate items.
    How long does it take to arrange a carta de invitación before a notary?
    Preparing the text takes 1 day. Signing before the notary takes 30 minutes (the appointment is usually for the next day). The apostille takes 1-3 days. In total, 2-5 working days for everything. The urgent service is same-day.
    Which documents are needed to arrange an invitation before a notary?
    From the person inviting (a resident of Spain): TIE or NIE + passport, empadronamiento (recent, no older than 3 months), a rental contract or proof of ownership, an income certificate (the last 3-6 months). From the person invited: a copy of their passport, passport details, and the purpose and dates of the visit.
    Does the Spanish consulate accept a carta de invitación notarial?
    Yes. In current practice, Spanish consulates in Ukraine, Russia and other countries accept both the form from the Comisaría and a notarial invitation. If you have doubts about a particular consulate, check before applying. In our practice (Alicante 2024-2026) there have been no refusals.
