    How much money you need for family reunification in Spain in 2026

    Krepko · 24 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. In 2026 IPREM is €600 a month (€7,200 a year): this value was set by the Budget Act for 2023, and that budget has been extended into 2026 as well. For a family of two people (you and one relative) you need a regular income of at least 150% of IPREM, that is, €900 a month, plus 50% of IPREM, €300, for each additional family member: for three people €1,200, for four €1,500 a month (Article 67.1 of RD 1155/2024). A salary is counted in full, together with the share of extra payments, and an autónomo's income by the net result. Employees prove their income with their contract and payslips (nóminas) for the last 6 months.

    The main question before family reunification (reagrupación familiar) is whether your income is enough. The Regulation sets the threshold not in euros but as a percentage of the state indicator IPREM, and it is calculated for the whole family, not for each person separately. Where it is easy to go wrong is elsewhere: which money counts, whether it is gross or net, and how to prove the income.

    Below: the value of IPREM for 2026, a ready-made table of thresholds for 2–5 people per month and per year, the calculation rules from Article 67 of the Regulation and the list of documents the ministry asks for. How the whole procedure works step by step is covered in our article on family reunification in Spain. We checked the figures and rules on 24 November 2026 against the BOE, the SEPE website and the Ministry of Inclusion information sheet.

    The money threshold is one of several conditions: besides it, they check your housing, health insurance and how long you have been living in Spain. We check all of this before the application and gather the complete set of documents; more details on the service page “Family reunification in Spain”.

    What IPREM is in 2026 and where the €600 figure comes from

    IPREM (indicador público de renta de efectos múltiples) is the indicator used in Spain to calculate the thresholds for benefits and for many types of residence permit. Its value is set by the State Budget Act. The last budget adopted is Ley 31/2022 for 2023: its additional provision 90 sets IPREM at €20 a day, €600 a month and €7,200 a year.

    No new budget has been adopted since then. On 23 December 2025 the government approved the arrangements for applying the extended budget in 2026, and SEPE (the public employment service), in its table of current values, still gives IPREM as €600 a month and €7,200 a year, citing Ley 31/2022. These are the figures we take as the basis. If, before your application, a new Budget Act with a different IPREM is published in the BOE, the thresholds below are recalculated using the same formula.

    How much you need for 2, 3, 4 and 5 people

    Article 67.1 of the Immigration Regulation (RD 1155/2024) requires “stable and regular” means: 150% of IPREM for a family made up of the applicant and one relative being brought over, and 50% of IPREM for each additional one. The ministry information sheet (Hoja 8) specifies that the amount is monthly. The annual figures below are the monthly threshold multiplied by 12.

    Family members, including you % of IPREM Per month Per year
    2 people (you and your spouse or child) 150% €900 €10,800
    3 people 200% €1,200 €14,400
    4 people 250% €1,500 €18,000
    5 people 300% €1,800 €21,600

    The threshold can be lowered when the people being brought over are children or persons whose legal representative you are. The decision is made by the authority, taking into account the interests of the child: their age, their ties with you, the number of family members. For a family of two people where the second is a minor, the Regulation in that case requires 110% of the annual guaranteed amount of the minimum income (Ingreso Mínimo Vital), plus 10% for each additional child. This is a possibility, not an automatic reduction: we do not advise counting on it in advance.

    Which income counts and which does not?

    The calculation rules are listed in the same Article 67.1. The main ones:

    • salary from employment: in full, before tax and contributions are deducted (valor íntegro), together with the share of extra payments (pagas extraordinarias). If you receive 14 payments of €800, that comes to €11,200 a year, i.e. about €933 a month, which is above the threshold for two;
    • autónomo income: not by turnover, but by the net result of the activity (rendimiento neto), that is, after expenses;
    • pensions and benefits: public and private, contributory and non-contributory, in full with the share of extra payments;
    • savings and assets: the Regulation takes into account “stable” assets at their average amount over the last 6 months. A large sum paid into an account a week before the application will hardly show in the six-month average;
    • income in another currency: the Regulation expressly allows an amount “in euros or its equivalent in foreign currency”; it is assessed by its nature and regularity.

    The following do not count: grants and study aid, aid for renting or buying a home, maintenance payments received and compensatory pensions (pensión compensatoria), except those awarded in favour of the person you are bringing over, and also payments from the social assistance system.

    Family income is added together. Besides your own money, they take into account the income of your spouse or partner and of first-degree relatives in the direct line (parents and children) if they are already residents of Spain and live in the same household as you (last paragraph of Article 67.1 of the Regulation; for a spouse, also Article 18.2 of the Immigration Act). The earnings of a spouse who still lives abroad do not fall under this rule.

    How do you prove your income?

    The ministry gives the list of documents in information sheet Hoja 8. It depends on how you earn your living:

    How you earn your living What you attach
    Employment (cuenta ajena) A copy of the employment contract and payslips (nóminas) for the last 6 months; if you have one, your latest annual IRPF tax return
    Your own business (cuenta propia, autónomo) Proof of the activity; if you have one, your latest annual IRPF tax return or the last two quarterly returns
    No work in Spain A bank certificate of funds; or certified cheques, traveller's cheques, letters of payment or credit cards together with a bank certificate of the limit available on the card

    Documents from other countries, for example a contract with a foreign employer or a statement from a foreign bank, are submitted with a sworn translation into Spanish. Official documents from another country must, in addition, be apostilled or legalised, unless a treaty exempts them from this. We arrange the sworn translation, and the apostille is covered in detail on the page “Apostille for documents for Spain”.

    In practice, income is checked not with a single certificate but as a set: the contract shows that the job is ongoing, the nóminas that the money arrives regularly, the tax return the annual amount. If the figures in these papers differ, for example the nóminas show one amount and the tax return a noticeably smaller one, additional documents may be requested (requerimiento), so it is better to explain the discrepancy before you apply.

    What you need besides income: housing and insurance

    Besides the money, you need suitable housing and health insurance for yourself and for everyone you are bringing over (Article 67.2 and 67.3). Housing is proven with a report from the services of your autonomous community or town hall: it must be issued within a month of the request, and on the date of the application it must be no more than 6 months old. These points are covered in detail in the article on family reunification.

    What changes if you are bringing over your parents?

    The income formula for parents is the same: a parent counts as a family member you are bringing over, so 150% of IPREM for you and the first relative and 50% for each additional one. But there are more conditions.

    The first is your status: parents can be brought over only when you hold a long-term residence permit (larga duración or larga duración-UE). You can apply as soon as you have applied for it, but the permit for your parent will be issued only after you have been granted it (Article 18.1 of the Immigration Act, Article 68.1 of the Regulation).

    The second is the parent themselves. Under Article 66.1.e you can bring over a parent (your own or your spouse's) who is dependent on you, over 65 and for whose move there are justified reasons; a parent under 65, only in exceptional cases on humanitarian grounds. The ministry asks you to prove that during your last year of living in Spain you sent your parent money or paid their expenses. All the conditions for parents are in the article on how to bring your parents to Spain. We prepare such cases separately and, before the application, tell you whether it makes sense to apply now.

    What we do

    Before the application we calculate the threshold for your family using the current IPREM, sort your income according to the rules of Article 67.1 (what will count and what will not) and select documents that are consistent with one another. At the same time we obtain Ukrainian marriage and birth certificates, have them apostilled, arrange the sworn translation and handle the case until the whole family has their cards. You can also apply on your own, in person at the Oficina de Extranjería (the immigration office) or online, but it is better to check the threshold and the documents in advance than to respond to a request after you have applied.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much do you need to earn to bring your wife to Spain in 2026?
    For a family of two people you need a regular income of at least 150% of IPREM. In 2026 IPREM is €600 a month, so the threshold is €900 a month, or €10,800 a year (Article 67.1 of RD 1155/2024). A salary is counted in full, before tax and contributions are deducted, together with the share of extra payments.
    How much money do you need for family reunification for a family of four?
    For the applicant and one relative, 150% of IPREM; for each additional one, another 50%. For four people that is 250% of IPREM: €1,500 a month, or €18,000 a year with IPREM at €600 in 2026. For children the authority may lower the threshold, but that is its decision, not a rule.
    Can my salary and my husband's salary be added together?
    Yes, if your husband is already a resident of Spain and lives with you as one family: the Regulation takes into account the income of the applicant, the spouse or partner and first-degree relatives in the direct line who live in Spain with you (Article 67.1). The income of a spouse you are only now bringing over from abroad does not fall under this rule.
    I am an autónomo. Which income counts for reunification: turnover or profit?
    Profit. Under Article 67.1, income from business activity is calculated by the net result (rendimiento neto), that is, after expenses. You prove the activity and attach your latest annual IRPF tax return or the last two quarterly returns, as stated in the ministry information sheet Hoja 8.
    Can savings in an account be used instead of a salary?
    The Regulation takes into account stable assets at their average amount over the last 6 months, so a large sum paid into an account shortly before the application will hardly show in the six-month average. If you do not work in Spain, the ministry asks for a bank certificate of funds or credit cards with a bank certificate of the limit. Whether your savings will be enough instead of a regular income is best checked before you apply.
    What if IPREM changes before I apply?
    The threshold is set as a percentage, so only the base changes: 150% of IPREM for two and 50% for each additional person. For now, in 2026, the extended 2023 budget is in force and IPREM is €600 a month. If a new Budget Act is published in the BOE, recalculate the threshold using the new value on the date of your application.
