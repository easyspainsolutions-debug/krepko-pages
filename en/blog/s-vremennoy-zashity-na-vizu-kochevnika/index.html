    From temporary protection to a digital nomad residence permit: applying to the UGE from Spain

    Krepko · 1 November 2026 · ~9 min read
    In short. Since 16 April 2026 temporary protection holders can apply from Spain for the residence permit for international teleworking, which in everyday speech is called the digital nomad visa: this is expressly allowed by DA 19ª of the Immigration Regulation as amended by RD 316/2026, and SEM Instruction 2/2026 confirms it. The application goes not to a consulate but to the UGE, the department of the immigration authority that handles such permits: the residence permit is granted for up to three years and renewed for two years at a time, and by law the decision is due within 20 days of electronic filing (art. 74 quinquies and 76 Ley 14/2013). The route is for those who work remotely for a company or clients outside Spain: the company has been operating for at least a year, and your relationship with it has lasted at least three months. While the application is being examined, protection remains in force, and it is renounced only after approval (SEM 2/2026, seventh point). Rules checked on 1 November 2026.

    If you are on temporary protection and work remotely for a Ukrainian or European company, under an employment contract or as a sole proprietor (FOP), there is a separate residence permit for you. Until April 2026 the regulation only promised a ministerial order on the conditions for protection holders, and there was no direct right to apply for this residence permit. Since 16 April 2026 they have been expressly allowed to apply for the digital nomad residence permit: from Spain, without a trip to a consulate. According to the instruction of the immigration authority, protection is renounced only after approval.

    Below we look at who this route suits and who should choose a change of status (modificación), what the UGE will check about your work, what to do about social security if you are a FOP, and what happens with taxes for past years. We checked the rules on 1 November 2026 against the BOE and the documents of the immigration authority.

    What changed in 2026

    The nomad residence permit is issued under Ley 14/2013, and anyone who is here legally can apply for it from Spain (art. 74 quinquies.1).

    RD 316/2026, published in the BOE on 15 April 2026, rewrote the additional provision DA 19ª of the Immigration Regulation (RD 1155/2024). Since 16 April protection holders have been expressly entitled to apply for permits whose procedure starts in Spain, «incluidas las contempladas … en la Ley 14/2013». On 22 June the State Secretariat for Migration (SEM) signed Instruction 2/2026, and its fifth point repeats this. Neither the decree nor the instruction introduces special conditions for protection holders.

    Officially it is the residencia para teletrabajo de carácter internacional, and there is no need to leave Spain for a visa. How we handle such applications is described on the service page “Digital nomad visa”.

    The rule was introduced by royal decree and is in force now; whether it will change after the elections of 29 November 2026 is unknown.

    Nomad permit or change of status: which route is yours

    The choice of route depends on where your employer or clients are.

    • Employer in Spain or your own business here. Most likely your route is the change of status under art. 191 of the Regulation (SEM 2/2026, first point); it is covered in the article on the change of status from temporary protection.
    • Remote work for a company or clients outside Spain, under an employment contract or as a FOP. Here the nomad residence permit is the closer fit, and this article is about it.

    For long-term residence, the time on protection is not lost on either route: years on temporary protection count towards the 5 years for larga duración nacional and are added to the time on the new residence permit (SEM 2/2026, sixth point). You cannot apply for larga duración-UE from a protection card, and for citizenship the question has not been officially settled. If you are close to five years, see the article on permanent residence after temporary protection.

    Requirements: what the UGE will check about your work

    Requirement What to know
    Work is remote only (art. 74 bis.1, 74 ter.b) You need a letter from the company: it allows you to work from Spain, describes the position and duties, expressly confirms the remote format and states the pay in euros.
    The company has been operating for at least a year (art. 74 ter.a) This is confirmed with an official extract from the companies register of its country.
    Relationship with the company of at least three months (art. 74 ter.c–d) The last three months before the application are counted. Under a service contract there may be several companies. An employment contract does not confirm a relationship under a service contract, and vice versa.
    Spanish clients (art. 74 bis.1) Under an employment contract, not allowed at all. Under a service contract (contrato mercantil), no more than 20% of your total professional activity.
    Qualifications (art. 74 bis.2) A university degree, a vocational training (FP) qualification or a business school qualification, or at least three years of experience.
    Income (art. 62.3.f; UGE FAQ, question 19) At least 200% of the minimum wage (SMI): in 2026, €34,188 gross a year per applicant. This is confirmed with payslips or invoices for three months and a bank statement showing the same payments.

    How income is calculated for a family is covered in the article on income for the nomad visa. For regulated professions the UGE asks for homologation of the degree or a statement before a notary that you will not work in that profession in Spain: the notary certifies it, and we prepare the text and accompany you at the signing.

    If you are a FOP. For the UGE this is self-employment under a service contract, not being on a company's staff. It qualifies if the contract with the foreign company has been in force for at least three months (UGE FAQ, question 5).

    According to the UGE information sheet, the general requirements (art. 62.3 Ley 14/2013) are confirmed with these documents:

    • a copy of all pages of a passport that is valid on the date of filing;
    • criminal record certificates from the countries where you have lived for the last two years, and a declaration on your own responsibility that you have had no convictions in the past five years;
    • health insurance: a private policy is not needed if after approval you register with Spanish social security;
    • the receipt for payment of the fee (tasa): €73.26 per applicant and the same for each family member (Modelo 790, code 038; UGE FAQ, question 20, checked on 1 November 2026).

    Social security: RETA for FOPs, separate rules for employees

    Since you work from Spain, registration with Spanish social security (Seguridad Social) is mandatory (UGE FAQ, question 6). When applying, you undertake to register, and you register after approval.

    • Self-employment, including as a FOP: only the autónomo (self-employed) scheme (RETA). The UGE does not allow autónomos to remain in the Ukrainian system: a certificate from Ukrainian social security under the agreement is accepted there only from employees (UGE FAQ, question 6). More details in the article on being autónomo in Spain.
    • Employment: the foreign company registers with Spanish social security as an employer without a representative office in Spain, and you are registered under the general scheme. Instead, you can bring the certificate itself from Ukrainian social security (the UGE does not accept an application for it) under the 1996 agreement between Spain and Ukraine, if it expressly states that it covers remote work from Spain. But the agreement allows you to remain in the Ukrainian system in the case of a temporary posting: up to two years and another two with the consent of the Spanish side (art. 7). For those who have lived in Spain since 2022, the certificate is not always suitable, and we check this before filing.

    The conditions of the permit must be maintained while it is valid, and any change in them must be reported to the UGE within 30 days (DA 7ª Ley 14/2013). If the conditions are not met, the permit may be terminated after first hearing you; for autónomos not registered with RETA, the FAQ adds that family members also lose the right to live and work in Spain (question 7). On renewal the conditions are checked again; see the article on renewing the nomad residence permit.

    Taxes and contributions for past years

    You had the right to work while on protection (art. 5.1 Orden PCM/169/2022), but Ley 14/2013 stipulates that tax obligations and social security obligations remain (art. 62.5).

    If since 2022 you have spent more than 183 days a year in Spain, under Spanish law you are a tax resident (art. 9.1.a LIRPF). The consequences for past years depend on where you paid taxes and contributions. This is no reason to postpone the switch, but it is better to sort it out before filing: we work through this question with a tax adviser.

    The Beckham Law regime is not available to those who moved to Spain in 2022. It is designed for those who become tax residents as a result of moving, and the move must take place in the first year of the regime or the year before for a reason named by the law, for example for work, including remote work (art. 93.1 LIRPF). But you moved in 2022, and for a different reason.

    The switching procedure: from application to card

    1. Passport
    Before filing you need a valid passport: the UGE asks for a copy of all its pages. If it has expired or will soon expire, start with replacing it; what to arrange at a notary's office before the replacement is covered in the article on an expired passport.
    2. Documents on work and income
    Foreign official documents are needed with an apostille and a sworn translation into Spanish (UGE information sheet).
    3. Filing with the UGE
    Electronically, yourself or through a representative, with the receipt for payment of the fee. Filing extends your current status until the decision (art. 76.1 Ley 14/2013), and protection remains in force.
    4. Decision
    By law, within 20 days of electronic filing; if there is no answer, the permit is deemed granted (silencio positivo, art. 76.1 Ley 14/2013). If the UGE requests documents, you are given 10 working days to respond (art. 68.1 and 30.2 Ley 39/2015). How to respond is in the article on a UGE request.
    5. Renouncing protection and the card
    After approval you file an express renunciation of temporary protection, and you show the receipt for it when you request the TIE (foreigner identity card) (SEM 2/2026, seventh point). The card is needed if the permit is granted for more than six months (art. 76.2 Ley 14/2013).
    Do not renounce protection in advance. It ends at the moment of renunciation (art. 24.1.d RD 1325/2003), and until the new permit is approved you will be left without status. The renunciation is irreversible: you can get protection again only through a new application, on the conditions that art. 2 of EU Decision 2026/1912 introduced for new applicants. How to prepare for renunciation is in the article on renouncing temporary protection.

    If you are moving with your family

    A spouse or partner, children (adult children if they are your dependants) and dependent parents can apply together with you or later (art. 62.4 Ley 14/2013), and they may work in Spain without restrictions (DA 4ª Ley 14/2013; UGE FAQ, question 17). If your relatives are also on temporary protection, the seventh point of SEM 2/2026 applies to each of them: they renounce protection after their own permit is approved. More details in the article on the digital nomad's family.

    Frequently asked questions

    Can I apply for a digital nomad visa from temporary protection?
    Yes. Since 16 April 2026 this is expressly allowed by DA 19ª of the Immigration Regulation as amended by RD 316/2026, and SEM Instruction 2/2026 confirms it. The application goes not to a consulate but to the UGE, from Spain: it is the residence permit for international teleworking for up to three years, renewed for two years at a time.
    Will I lose protection while my application is being examined?
    No. Filing extends your current status until the decision (art. 76.1 Ley 14/2013), and protection is renounced only after the new permit is approved (SEM 2/2026, seventh point). A renunciation filed in advance ends protection immediately, and it is irreversible.
    I am a FOP and work for a Ukrainian company. Do I qualify?
    You may qualify. For the UGE a FOP is self-employment under a service contract: the company must have been operating for at least a year, the contract with it must have been in force for at least the last three months, and Spanish clients must account for no more than 20% of your activity. After approval you need to register with RETA: the UGE does not allow autónomos to remain in the Ukrainian social security system.
    I work remotely for a Spanish company. Is this residence permit right for me?
    No. Under an employment contract, the nomad residence permit allows you to work only for companies outside Spain, and under a service contract no more than 20% of the work may be for Spanish companies (art. 74 bis.1 Ley 14/2013). If your employer is in Spain, the closer fit is the change of status under art. 191 of the Regulation: protection holders can also apply for it under SEM 2/2026.
    How long does the UGE take to examine an application?
    By law, 20 days from electronic filing; if there is no answer, the permit is deemed granted (art. 76.1 Ley 14/2013). If the UGE requests documents, you are given 10 working days to respond, and the time limit for the decision may be suspended for that period (art. 22.1.a and 68.1 Ley 39/2015).
    Can I get the Beckham Law regime with this residence permit?
    Not if you moved to Spain in 2022: the regime is designed for those who become tax residents as a result of moving, and the move must take place in the first year of the regime or the year before for a reason named by the law, for example for work, including remote work (art. 93.1 LIRPF). Taxes and contributions for past years are a separate question, which we work through with a tax adviser before filing.
