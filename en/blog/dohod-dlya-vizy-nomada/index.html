    Income for the digital nomad visa in 2026: how much you need and how to prove it

    Krepko · updated 12 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. The applicant needs an income of at least 200% of the Spanish minimum wage (SMI): in 2026 that is €34,188 gross a year. Consulates state €2,442 a month over 14 payments; converted to 12 months, it comes to €2,849. For the first family member you add 75% of the SMI, and 25% for each additional one. The threshold changes with the SMI, and it has not yet been approved for 2027.

    For the digital nomad visa in 2026, the applicant needs €34,188 gross a year. Converted to a monthly figure, that is €2,442 or €2,849: it all depends on whether the annual amount is divided into 14 payments or into 12. That is why different figures circulate on websites, and sometimes ones that do not appear in any official document at all.

    It is worth checking your income before you pay for translations, apostilles and insurance: if it does not meet the threshold, you will not need the other documents. Below we explain where the threshold comes from, how much is needed for a family, how income is proven at the consulate and at the UGE (Unidad de Grandes Empresas, the unit of the immigration authority that accepts applications from within Spain) and what will change when the decree on the minimum wage for 2027 comes out. We checked the figures on 1 October 2026 against the BOE, UGE documents and the pages of Spanish consulates.

    Where the threshold comes from

    The threshold is tied to the minimum wage, the salario mínimo interprofesional (SMI). For 2026 it was set by Real Decreto 126/2026 of 18 February, published in the BOE on 19 February: €1,221 a month over 14 payments, €17,094 a year. The decree came out in February, but it applies retroactively from 1 January to 31 December 2026.

    The law itself contains no percentages: Ley 14/2013 only requires sufficient means for yourself and your family. The percentages were set by a joint instruction of the consular and immigration directorates of March 2023, and the UGE FAQ and guidance notes repeat them. For comparison: in 2025 the SMI was €1,184, and the applicant's threshold was €33,152 a year.

    €2,442, €2,849 or €2,700: which figure is correct

    The first two are correct, and the same amount lies behind both. In Spain the SMI is calculated over 14 payments a year, so 200% of the SMI is €2,442 × 14 = €34,188 a year. The same annual amount divided by 12 months gives €2,849.

    The figure consulates publish is €2,442:Buenos Aires writes “durante 14 pagas”, and Houston and Hanoi give the same amount. The figures of €2,690 and €2,700 found online do not match any official one. With 12 payments a year, going by the annual amount, they leave you short of the threshold by €1,788–1,908.

    How to compare your income. Multiply your gross monthly pay by the number of payments in the year and compare the result with €34,188. If you are paid 12 times a year, the benchmark is €2,849 a month; if 14 times, €2,442.

    One caveat. The UGE, which reviews applications from within Spain, does not publish amounts in euros, only the formula “200% of the SMI per month”, and which figure it compares incoming payments with has not been officially confirmed. So with 12 payments there is only one safe benchmark: at least €2,849 gross a month.

    The threshold for a family

    For the first family member, 75% of the SMI is added to the 200%, and for each additional one 25%. The 75% supplement applies to whoever comes first, not necessarily a spouse: if you are travelling only with a child, the 75% is for the child.

    Who is applying % of SMI Per month, 14 payments Per year Per month, 12 payments
    Applicant 200% €2,442.00 €34,188.00 €2,849.00
    Applicant + 1 275% €3,357.75 €47,008.50 €3,917.38
    Applicant + 2 300% €3,663.00 €51,282.00 €4,273.50
    Applicant + 3 325% €3,968.25 €55,555.50 €4,629.63

    Each family member after the first adds €4,273.50 a year, or €356.13 a month over 12 payments. The table is built the way consulates calculate it: the 75% is a supplement on top of the 200%, so a family of two comes to 275%. The text of the instruction also allows another reading, but we found no UGE clarification on this point, so plan according to the table.

    Your family can apply later. If your relatives apply together with you, it is enough for you to prove income for everyone. If they apply later, once you already hold the permit, income is proven again: your contract, nóminas or facturas for the 3 months before their application, and a bank certificate.

    How income is proven

    All amounts are calculated gross, before taxes and contributions are deducted. The UGE guidance note says so directly: the threshold is compared with the amount before deductions, not with what you take home.

    When applying to the UGE, that is, from within Spain, you need:

    • nóminas (payslips) or facturas (invoices to clients) for the 3 months before the application;
    • a bank certificate in your name, with the bank's stamp or signature, for the same 3 months. The incoming payments in it must match the nóminas or facturas and be marked separately;
    • if your income is below the threshold, a bank certificate of savings or other liquid funds that cover the difference for the entire term of the permit.

    At the consulate. Under the joint instruction, income can be proven by any evidence, and it is assessed individually. Consulates write exactly that, while the UGE guidance note lists a specific set of documents (above). In any case, an employment contract, a firm job offer (oferta firme de empleo) or a commercial contract is accepted. What you need to show is that the money belongs to you, was obtained lawfully and is available.

    What is checked alongside income

    Even with the required income, the application will not succeed without meeting the conditions of articles 74 bis and 74 ter of Ley 14/2013:

    • the company you work for has actually been operating for at least 1 year;
    • your contract with it has been in force for at least 3 months before the application;
    • there is a document showing that the work can be done remotely;
    • under an employment contract you may work only for companies outside Spain. Under a commercial contract, Spanish clients are allowed if they account for no more than 20% of all your professional activity;
    • a university degree, a vocational training (FP) qualification or a business school diploma, or at least 3 years of experience. For a regulated profession (doctor, lawyer, architect, etc.), the UGE requires homologation of the diploma or a statement before a notary that you will not work in that profession in Spain.

    Health insurance is required: public cover through the Seguridad Social or private cover from a company authorised to operate in Spain, without co-payments (copagos) and without a waiting period (carencia). Travel insurance is not acceptable. The UGE does not require a private policy if, after approval, you register with the Seguridad Social. Consulates have different requirements: Houston, for example, asks for a policy in any case.

    Criminal record certificates are needed from all the countries where you have lived in the last 2 years, plus a declaration under your own responsibility that you have had no criminal convictions in the last 5 years. The consulate in Houston requires certificates covering all 5 years, so check your consulate's page. The UGE does not ask for the certificate again if you already hold a Spanish residence or study permit valid for more than 6 months and you submitted one for it. We obtain the Ukrainian certificate with an apostille and translation without you travelling to Ukraine.

    Consulate or UGE: what is the difference

    The threshold is the same in both cases, but the term of the document and the procedure differ. Under the law, the picture is as follows:

    Consulate: visa UGE: residence
    Where you apply At the consulate of your country of residence From Spain, if you are here lawfully
    Term Up to 1 year Up to 3 years, renewable for 2 years at a time
    Decision 10 working days, unless there is a consultation under Article 22 of the EU Visa Code 20 days; silence means approval
    TIE card Not needed Needed if the permit is longer than 6 months
    Fee Check with your consulate €73.26 (Modelo 790, code 038) per applicant

    Within 60 days before the visa expires, you can apply to the UGE for residence and then renew for 2 years at a time. You cannot apply for residence from abroad; from there the only route is through the consulate.

    Applying to the UGE extends your current status until the decision, but you cannot switch from just any status. You cannot switch from a residence permit without the right to work (no lucrativa). You can switch from a student permit if you have been registered with the Seguridad Social since you started working for the foreign company. The consulate in Houston writes that those who have arrived in Spain may apply for residence here, without a visa. How the UGE applies this in practice has not been officially clarified, so it is worth checking the practice before applying.

    After approval you must register with the Seguridad Social: an employee in the general scheme (their foreign company registers there in advance), a self-employed worker (autónomo) in RETA, without exception. An employee may instead submit a certificate of applicable legislation from their country, if that country has an agreement with Spain and the certificate states explicitly that it covers work from Spain. There is an agreement with Ukraine, but it is designed for postings of up to 2 years and does not include medical care, so with such a certificate you will need private insurance.

    If you do not register, the UGE may terminate the permit. For the self-employed, the UGE FAQ states directly that it will be terminated together with the permits of family members. The consulates in Houston and Buenos Aires ask for proof of registration with the Seguridad Social already when you apply for the visa.

    Applying in early 2027: the threshold will shift

    As of 1 October 2026, the SMI for 2027 has not been approved. According to press reports, negotiations on it will begin in the fourth quarter. The last two SMI decrees came out in February, on 12 February 2025 and 19 February 2026, and applied retroactively from 1 January.

    The consulate in Hanoi, for example, switched to the 2026 amounts from 19 February 2026, the day the decree was published in the BOE (“a partir de 19/02/26”). We found no single rule for all consulates. The UGE guidance note refers to the SMI “vigente”, that is, the one in force on the date of application. How this applies to applications submitted between 1 January and the publication of the new decree has not been officially clarified.

    The shift is easy to estimate: each 1% rise in the SMI adds €24.42 a month to the applicant's threshold over 14 payments, or €341.88 a year. This is not a forecast, just arithmetic. The SMI in the examples is rounded to the nearest euro, as in the decrees of recent years, so the amounts differ slightly from the formula:

    • if the SMI rises by 3%, to €1,258, the threshold will be €2,516 over 14 payments, €35,224 a year and €2,935.33 over 12;
    • if by 5%, to €1,282, the threshold will be €2,564, €35,896 a year and €2,991.33 over 12.
    If you are applying in January–March 2027, build in a margin: an income that only just meets the 2026 threshold may not meet the new one. The same applies to renewal: the law requires the conditions to be maintained, but it does not say which SMI they should be calculated against. The word “vigente” appears in the guidance note for initial applications; for renewal there is no separate clarification.

    Common mistakes

    Confusing 12 and 14 payments. €2,500 gross 12 times a year gives €30,000 a year: going by the annual amount, you are €4,188 short of the threshold. Counting on the UGE to compare month by month with €2,442 is risky; this has not been officially confirmed. The same €2,500, paid 14 times a year, gives €35,000, and the threshold is met.

    Forgetting about your family. With a spouse and a child you need €51,282 a year, not €34,188.

    The bank statement does not match the documents. The money arrives in an account that is not in your name, or the amounts in the statement differ from the invoices to clients. The UGE requires the incoming payments to match the nóminas or facturas.

    Applying too early. If your contract is less than 3 months old or the company has been operating for less than a year, the application will not succeed even with a good income.

    Having the contract certified by a notary. This is not required. According to the UGE guidance note, documents in a foreign language need a sworn translation (a simple one is enough for a CV), and foreign official documents also need an apostille or legalisation. Translation formats are covered in more detail on the page about sworn translation of Ukrainian documents.

    If your income falls short

    • Make up the difference with savings. The UGE guidance note allows you to cover the difference with savings for the entire term of the permit: with a shortfall of €1,788 a year and a 3-year permit, that is €5,364 in the account. How willingly the UGE accepts this is not officially clear: its FAQ talks about income from work.
    • Apply alone first. Your family will join when your income grows, and then you will prove it again for everyone.
    • Wait three months. The UGE looks at the 3 months before the application. If your income is growing, apply once you have three full months at the new level.
    • Choose a different ground. If you are far from the threshold, the nomad visa is not your route. Other grounds for a residence permit are collected in the section on immigration.

    Sources (checked on 1 October 2026): Real Decreto 126/2026 on the SMI, Ley 14/2013, articles 62 and 74 bis–76, UGE FAQ, UGE guidance note for applicants, social security agreement between Spain and Ukraine, page of the consulate in Houston.

    Frequently asked questions

    How much do you need to earn for the digital nomad visa in 2026?
    The applicant needs at least €34,188 gross a year. That is €2,442 a month over 14 payments or €2,849 over 12. With one family member you need €47,008.50 a year, with two €51,282, with three €55,555.50.
    Why do some websites say €2,442 and others €2,849?
    It is the same annual amount, €34,188, divided into 14 payments or into 12 months. Consulates publish €2,442 over 14 payments. If you are paid 12 times a year, aim for €2,849 gross a month.
    I am paid €2,700 gross 12 times a year. Do I qualify?
    Going by the annual amount, no: 2,700 × 12 = €32,400, which is €1,788 short of the 2026 threshold. The UGE guidance note allows you to cover the difference with savings for the entire term of the permit, but how willingly this is accepted in practice is not officially clear. It is safer to reach €2,849 gross a month.
    Is income calculated before or after tax?
    Before. The UGE guidance note states directly that the amounts are gross, before deductions for taxes and social security contributions. The document of the consulate in Hanoi also gives the SMI gross.
    I am going with my spouse and child. What income do I need?
    300% of the SMI: €3,663 over 14 payments, €51,282 a year, €4,273.50 a month over 12 payments. For the spouse as the first family member, 75% of the SMI is added, and 25% for the child.
    What will the threshold be in 2027?
    It is not yet known: as of 1 October 2026, the SMI for 2027 has not been approved. Each 1% rise in the SMI raises the applicant's threshold by €24.42 a month over 14 payments. If you are applying in early 2027, build in a margin.
    I am in Spain under temporary protection. Can I apply for the nomad visa from here?
    The law allows those who are in Spain lawfully to apply to the UGE. Since 16 April 2026 this has also been explicitly allowed for holders of temporary protection: the Immigration Regulation permits their applications from Spain for permits under Ley 14/2013 (DA 19ª of RD 1155/2024 as amended by RD 316/2026), which SEM instruction 2/2026 confirms. The income threshold for you is the same. Other routes are collected in the section on switching from temporary protection.
