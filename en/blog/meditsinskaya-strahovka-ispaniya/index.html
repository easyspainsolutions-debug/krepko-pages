    Health insurance in Spain: public and private

    Krepko · updated 13 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    Public healthcare in Spain is free and covers almost everything, but access to it is not open to everyone and not on the day you move. Below we look at who enters the system automatically, who will not have a residence permit approved without a private policy, and which contract clauses make Extranjería (the immigration office) return the documents. If you are putting together a file for a visa, start with the section on private insurance: it also contains the list of what we check in a policy before you pay.

    Public healthcare: who is entitled

    The Spanish public health system is called the Sistema Nacional de Salud (SNS). Access to it is confirmed by a card — the tarjeta sanitaria individual (TSI). It is issued at your local health centre (centro de salud) — the district clinic you are assigned to according to the address of your municipal registration.

    Who enters the system automatically

    • Employees with an employment contract for whom contributions are paid to the Seguridad Social — they are enrolled automatically; nothing needs to be arranged separately
    • Autónomos (self-employed) who are registered and pay contributions — the same access as employees
    • Holders of a residence permit with the right to work — if you are officially employed or fall into a preferential category
    • Children under 18 — registered regardless of the parents' status; the padrón is enough
    • Pregnant women — accepted regardless of status; only the padrón is needed
    • Ukrainians with temporary protection — the same access to public healthcare as residents; there is no need to buy a separate policy

    How to get a tarjeta sanitaria

    1. Get your municipal registration (empadronamiento) at the address where you live — without it the centro de salud will not accept you
    2. Go to the nearest centro de salud with your passport, NIE/TIE and padrón certificate
    3. Fill in the application and register with a general practitioner (médico de cabecera)
    4. Collect your TSI — it is usually issued straight away or sent by post within 2–3 weeks
    What the card covers: doctor's visits, tests, emergency care and subsidised prescriptions (co-pago — a small co-payment for medicines). Planned operations go through a waiting list (lista de espera): depending on the region and the specialty, the wait ranges from several weeks to several months. It is precisely because of the waiting list for planned treatment that people also buy a private policy.

    Do you need insurance for a specific application?

    Not every policy is suitable for a residence permit: consulates and immigration offices have their own requirements. Tell us where you are applying and we will tell you what will be accepted. We handle preparing documents for your application.

    Private health insurance

    Private insurance (seguro médico privado) gives you private clinics without waiting lists, a choice of doctor and appointments at a convenient time. But more often it is bought not for comfort: for several types of residence permit, a policy is on the mandatory list of documents, and without it the application will not be accepted.

    When private insurance is mandatory for a residence permit

    • “Golden investor” visa (Golden Visa) — new applications have not been accepted since 3 April 2025 (Ley Orgánica 1/2025)
    • Residence visa without the right to work (visado de no lucrativa) — mandatory: full coverage and no excess; a cheap basic package does not qualify here
    • Digital nomad visa (Digital Nomad Visa) — not always: the UGE (Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos) does not require a private policy if you register with the Seguridad Social after approval, while consulates have differing requirements; the policy must have no excess, no co-payments (copagos) and no waiting period (carencia), with cover in Spain, and it is attached to the initial application
    • Student visa (visado de estudiante) — mandatory when first applying from abroad; the policy goes into the consular set
    • Arraigo social, arraigo sociolaboral (residence on the grounds of ties to Spain) — a policy is not required, but with one you do not depend on waiting lists while your status is still being processed

    The main insurers: whom we compare

    Sanitas — a subsidiary of the British Bupa and one of the largest players on the market, with clinics all over Spain. For an adult aged 30–40, a policy costs from €60 to €130 a month depending on the package. Its strengths are service, the app and telemedicine; its weakness is the price for full coverage.

    Adeslas (SegurCaixa Adeslas) — the largest private insurer in Spain by number of clients, with a strong network in Catalonia, Madrid and Andalusia. Prices from €50 to €110 a month. It does have cheap packages “for a visa”, but it is precisely these that we check most carefully for excesses.

    Asisa — well represented in large cities, with prices comparable to Adeslas. Convenient when you need a policy urgently for an application: they issue it quickly, and it is accepted for all types of residence permits and visas.

    DKV (Deutsche Krankenversicherung) — a German insurer operating in Spain. People choose it for the quality of cover, especially in Alicante and Valencia. Prices from €55 to €100 a month.

    What a basic private insurance package includes

    • Visits to a GP, a paediatrician and specialists without an appointment or with a short wait
    • Basic tests and diagnostics
    • Dentistry — as a rule, only in a separate extended package
    • Emergency hospitalisation — planned operations are treated separately; check the package terms
    Before you apply: if the policy goes into the set of documents for a residence permit or visa, check that it has no exclusiones por condición preexistente (exclusions for chronic illnesses) and no franquicia (excess). Consulates and Extranjería usually require exactly this kind of contract, and the UGE expressly does not accept policies with co-payments (copagos) or a waiting period (carencia). We read the terms before you pay a year in advance: reissuing a policy after a refusal costs more and takes longer than choosing the right one straight away.

    Public vs. private: which to choose

    If you have a residence permit and official employment, public healthcare covers your basic needs fully and free of charge. A private policy is needed in four cases:

    • You are applying for a visa or residence permit for which a policy is on the mandatory list
    • You need a planned operation without months of waiting
    • You want to choose the doctor and clinic yourself
    • You need an appointment on the day you ask, without a queue

    One does not exclude the other: many people in Spain hold both a public tarjeta sanitaria and a private policy — the first covers emergencies and prescriptions, the second removes the wait for planned treatment.

    If you would rather not work it out yourself, see the “Immigration (extranjería)” section: insurance is part of the document package, and we will look at your situation and tell you what you will need in your particular case.

    Frequently asked questions

    Who in Spain is entitled to free public healthcare?
    Employees with an employment contract and autónomos who have paid their contributions, holders of a residence permit with the right to work, children under 18, pregnant women and Ukrainians with temporary protection status are entitled to the SNS.
    When is private health insurance mandatory for a residence permit?
    Private insurance with full coverage and no excess is mandatory for the non-lucrative visa (no lucrativa): without it the application is not accepted. For the digital nomad visa, the UGE does not require a private policy if you register with the Seguridad Social after approval, while consulates have differing requirements. New Golden Visa applications have not been accepted since 3 April 2025.
    How much does private health insurance cost in Spain?
    The price depends on age and coverage: for a young person, from €30–50 a month; for people aged 40–50, €60–100; for pensioners, €150+. We will compare Sanitas, Adeslas, DKV and Asisa for your situation.
