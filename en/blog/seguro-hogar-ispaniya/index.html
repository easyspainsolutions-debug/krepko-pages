    Home insurance in Spain: how to choose a seguro de hogar

    Krepko · updated 11 November 2026 · ~6 min read
    In short. Home insurance in Spain is not compulsory by law, but for a mortgage the bank almost always requires at least seguro de continente (the structure of the building), and a landlord may write third-party liability insurance (responsabilidad civil) into the contract. For a tenant, a contenido policy for their own belongings is usually enough, and responsabilidad civil is most often already included in it. Before signing, read the exclusiones section and state the value of your belongings honestly: the payout will not exceed the declared amount. Checked on 24 October 2026.     Seguro de hogar, home insurance in Spain, usually comes up in two situations: the bank requires a policy for a mortgage or the landlord has written it into the contract. We read such contracts for clients all the time and see where insurance covers a bill of several thousand euros and where you pay for a clause that will never apply. Below we look at how continente differs from contenido, what the basic package includes, what it costs on the market and what to check in the policy before signing, separately for tenants and owners.

    Do you need home insurance in Spain

    Under Spanish law, seguro de hogar is not compulsory for all residents. Exceptions, however, are more common than you might think. If you take out a mortgage (hipoteca) when buying property in Spain, the bank will almost certainly require at least seguro de continente, insurance for the structure of the building itself. There is nothing to argue about here: without a policy the loan will not be approved.

    When renting a home in Spain the story is different: the landlord (propietario) is entitled to include in the contract a requirement to take out third-party liability insurance (responsabilidad civil). We regularly see such a clause in contracts in Madrid and Barcelona. And it almost never obliges you to go to a particular insurer: any policy with a responsabilidad civil section will do.

    Even when no one requires anything, a policy almost always pays for itself. Daños por agua (water damage) is the most common insurance claim in Spain. In old buildings pipes leak regularly, and repairs after a flood cost €3,000–10,000. With a policy the insurer pays this money; without one, you do.

    What seguro de hogar is: two types

    Policies fall into two types, and confusing them is what costs the most: owners underinsure the structure, and tenants pay for walls that do not belong to them. The difference is simple:

    Seguro de continente: insurance for the structure

    Continente is the building itself: walls, floor slabs, floors, roof, windows, built-in plumbing and electrics. This type is needed above all by homeowners. If there is a fire in the flat and a load-bearing wall is damaged, it is continente that pays for restoring the structure.

    Banks require exactly this type for a mortgage: their collateral must be insured. The point where people most often lose money: the sum insured is calculated from the construction cost, not from the market price of the flat. If the bank officer has put the purchase price into the policy, you overpay for inflated cover for years; we check this figure when we look at a client's mortgage package.

    Seguro de contenido: insurance for belongings

    Contenido is everything inside: furniture, household appliances, electronics, clothing, personal belongings. This type is especially relevant for tenants: the walls and floor do not belong to you, but what you brought with you is worth protecting.

    In the event of theft, fire or flooding, contenido will reimburse the value of the damaged or lost belongings. Everything comes down to the amount you declare when taking out the policy; read about this trap in the section on typical mistakes.

    Combined package: continente + contenido

    Most insurers offer a combined policy covering both the structure and the belongings. For an owner this is the most convenient: one annual premium, one contract, one emergency assistance service. For a tenant, such a package is useful when the flat is furnished and you are responsible for someone else's appliances as well as your own.

    What standard home insurance covers

    A basic seguro de hogar usually covers the following set of risks; use it to check the offer you have been sent:

    • Daños por agua (water damage): burst pipes, leaks, flooding due to faulty plumbing. The most common insurance claim in the country. It covers both damage to your flat and damage to the neighbours below.
    • Incendio (fire): damage to belongings and structures as a result of a fire, including damage from smoke and from water used to put it out.
    • Robo (theft): theft of belongings in a break-in. There are often limits on the amount and conditions (for example, a lock of a certain class).
    • Responsabilidad civil (third-party liability): if you accidentally flood your neighbours or cause them other damage, the insurance will cover their claims. This is one of the most valuable parts of the policy.
    • Rotura de cristales (broken glass): glass panels, mirrors, glass worktops and windows. Often included in the basic package, sometimes as an option.
    • Asistencia en el hogar (emergency assistance): calling out a plumber, electrician or locksmith in an emergency. It usually covers a certain number of call-outs a year and a limited list of work.
    • Daños eléctricos (electrical damage): power surges, lightning strikes, appliances failing for electrical reasons. Important for those who have expensive electronics.
    From practice: if your upstairs neighbours flood you, their insurance (responsabilidad civil) should cover your damage. But when the neighbours have no policy or the payout drags on for months, the money comes faster under your own “daños por agua” section, and the insurer then deals with the person at fault itself. That is why a policy benefits both sides.

    What is usually not in the basic package

    A standard policy does not cover everything. Read the exclusiones (exclusions) section before signing: that is where the reason for a later refusal to pay out is hidden:

    • Terremotos e inundaciones (earthquakes and floods): natural disasters in Spain are covered through the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros, a state fund. A small contribution to it is automatically included in a home insurance policy, and in the event of a flood or earthquake the Consorcio pays for the same things and up to the same amounts as stated in your policy.
    • Pollution and intentional damage: no insurer covers damage caused on purpose. If intent is proven, the payout will be refused.
    • Equipment older than a certain age: many policies limit payouts for appliances older than 5–10 years or apply depreciation.
    • Damage due to negligence: if a pipe burst because you had known about a leak for a long time and did not call a repairer, the insurer may refuse to pay out.
    • Commercial use of the home: if you let the flat on a short-term basis through Airbnb, a standard seguro de hogar, as a rule, does not apply. This requires a special policy.

    How much home insurance costs in Spain

    The price is made up of the floor area of the home, the neighbourhood, the declared value of the belongings and the set of risks. Market benchmarks are as follows:

    • Tenant, contenido only: from €80 to €150 a year. This is the minimum option, which protects your belongings and includes responsabilidad civil.
    • Owner, continente + contenido: from €200 to €400 a year for a standard flat of 70–90 m². For a large house or a luxury home, more.
    • Extended package with additional risks: from €350 a year. It includes, for example, insurance against appliance breakdown, protection for bicycles or extended theft cover.

    The region noticeably affects the price. On the coast (Costa Blanca, Costa del Sol) rates are higher: humidity and salty air wear out wiring and appliances faster. In Madrid and Barcelona prices are average for the market, but depend on the neighbourhood and the age of the building.

    Where to save: many insurers give a 10–20% discount to existing customers with another policy, for example car insurance (seguro de coche). If your car is insured with Mapfre or Mutua Madrileña, ask about a combined rate before taking out home insurance with a competitor.

    Has the insurer refused to pay out?

    A refusal is almost always based on a clause in the contract. Tell us what happened and what you were told, and we will see what you can rely on. If necessary, we bring in a legal review of the situation.

    Leading insurers in Spain

    The market is crowded, and the difference between companies is not so much in price as in how quickly they settle a siniestro. These are the ones worth comparing:

    Mapfre

    Mapfre is the largest insurer in Spain, with offices throughout the country. An option for those who want a real agent rather than a chat. In Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia there are Russian-speaking agents. The policies are reliable but rarely the cheapest.

    Allianz

    Allianz is an international insurer with a strong online service and a mobile app. It suits those who are used to managing everything from their phone. The procedure for filing an insurance claim (siniestro) is clear, and support responds quickly. The policies are competitively priced.

    Línea Directa

    Línea Directa is an online insurer without physical offices, which makes its prices noticeably lower. It suits you if you are ready to arrange everything through the website or app and do not expect to meet an agent. In terms of value for money (price versus cover), it is one of the best options.

    Mutua Madrileña

    Mutua Madrileña works on a mutual insurance model. It is advantageous above all for those who already have its car insurance: the discount for adding a seguro de hogar can be substantial. Good cover and a reliable home assistance service.

    AXA

    AXA is an international brand that is particularly popular in tourist areas: Barcelona, the Costa Blanca, Mallorca. If you live in a resort area or are considering short-term letting, AXA offers specialised products.

    It is convenient to compare through the aggregators Rastreator.com and Acierto.com: you enter the details of your home and get several options with a breakdown by cover and price. It takes 5–10 minutes and usually gives a price 20–30% lower than applying directly to a company.

    How to take out a seguro de hogar

    You can take out a policy in three ways, and they differ noticeably in price:

    Online through the insurer's website or a comparison site

    The fastest and usually the cheapest route. You go to the website of the company or aggregator (Rastreator, Acierto), fill in the form about your home, choose the cover and pay. The policy arrives by email; the whole process takes 15–30 minutes.

    Through the insurer's office

    An option for non-standard cases: an old building, an unusual floor area, expensive appliances, additional risks. An agent will select cover for your situation and explain the terms in person, but the rate will almost always be higher than online.

    Through a bank

    CaixaBank, Sabadell and BBVA readily sell home insurance in a package with an account or a mortgage. It is arranged in one go, but it is usually more expensive than the market, by 15–30%. Before agreeing, check prices on Rastreator: a bank rarely objects to a client's policy from another insurer if it meets the mortgage conditions.

    What you will need to take out a policy

    • NIE: foreigner identity number (mandatory)
    • Address of the home: exact, including the floor and flat number
    • Floor area of the home: in square metres (usually stated in the rental contract)
    • Value of belongings: an estimated amount for the things you want to insure (for contenido)
    • Year the building was built: affects the rate, especially for old buildings

    Renting: when the landlord asks for insurance

    Landlords increasingly write into the contract a requirement to take out seguro de responsabilidad civil, third-party liability insurance. This is not a whim: if you forget to turn off a tap or cause a short circuit, someone has to compensate for the damage to the neighbours and the building, and without a policy that someone will be you.

    Such basic insurance costs €50–100 a year, and most often it is already built into a standard contenido package. In other words, by taking out an ordinary seguro de contenido for your belongings, you get responsabilidad civil included, and this policy is enough to meet the landlord's requirement.

    If the landlord insists on a particular company, you are entitled to offer an equivalent policy from another insurer. There is one thing to look at: the document must have a “responsabilidad civil” section with cover of at least €150,000; this is the market standard, and that is usually where the dispute ends.

    A case from practice: a tenant in Valencia left a tap running and went out for several hours. The water flooded the flat below, and the neighbours' repairs came to €4,200. He had a seguro de contenido with responsabilidad civil of €300,000, and the insurer covered the full amount. He paid nothing out of his own pocket.

    Typical mistakes when choosing home insurance

    Most often people are disappointed with their insurance not because of the company but because of four mistakes when taking out the policy. Each of them cuts the payout or cancels it entirely:

    Not reading the exclusiones section

    Exclusiones is the list of what the policy does not cover. Run through it before signing. This applies in particular to those who live in a flood-prone area, near a river or in a low-lying spot: if “inundaciones” are among the insurer's exclusions, flood damage under a home policy is reimbursed by the Consorcio.

    Underestimating the value of your belongings

    When taking out contenido, you will be asked to state the approximate value of your things. Many people understate it to bring down the premium. But the payout will not exceed the declared amount: you declared €5,000, and €12,000 worth of belongings burned, so you cover the difference yourself. Spend 20 minutes and count honestly: appliances, furniture, clothing, jewellery, a bicycle.

    Choosing the cheapest insurance with a high excess

    Franquicia (excess) is the amount you pay yourself for each insurance claim. A policy with a €600 excess is cheaper, but for minor damage (a leaking pipe, a €400 repair) it will give you nothing. Think about how often you have small breakdowns and choose the excess accordingly.

    Not reporting changes to your home

    Have you done an expensive renovation, bought appliances or moved to another flat? Tell the insurer straight away. Otherwise, when you make a claim, it will turn out that the policy describes a home that no longer exists, and the payout will be cut.

    Frequently asked questions

    Is home insurance compulsory in Spain?
    By law, seguro de hogar is not compulsory for all residents. But when granting a mortgage the bank requires at least seguro de continente, and a landlord may include a requirement for third-party liability insurance in the rental contract.
    What is the difference between seguro de continente and contenido?
    Continente is insurance for the structure itself: walls, floor slabs, floors, roof, built-in plumbing. Owners need it. Contenido is insurance for the belongings inside: furniture, appliances, personal belongings. It is relevant for tenants.
    How much does home insurance cost in Spain?
    A basic seguro de hogar package for a flat of 70–90 m² costs approximately €150–300 a year. Extended cover with theft protection, legal assistance and higher limits costs €350–600 a year.
    What does home insurance in Spain most often cover?
    Daños por agua (water damage) is the most common claim: repairs after a flood from the neighbours. Fire, theft, damage to electrical appliances, third-party liability towards neighbours and legal protection are also covered.
