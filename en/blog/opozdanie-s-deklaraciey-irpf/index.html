    Late IRPF tax return: how much extra you pay and when there is a penalty

    Krepko · 11 November 2026 · ~8 min read
    In short. If your IRPF return shows an amount to pay and you filed it late on your own, before any request from the tax office, there is no penalty: a surcharge (recargo) is added to the tax — 1% plus another 1% for each full month of delay, and after 12 months — 15% plus late-payment interest (art. 27 Ley General Tributaria); the surcharge is reduced by 25% if the tax is paid when filing and the surcharge itself within the deadline in the notice. For a late return with a refund or a zero result, the law provides for a penalty of €100 if filed before a request and €200 after one (art. 198), and the right to the refund is kept. If the tax office sent a request first, instead of the surcharge there is interest and a possible penalty starting at 50% of the unpaid amount (art. 191). The deadline for the 2025 return ended on 30 June 2026; the rules were checked on 11 November 2026.

    The key point: who got there first — you or the tax office

    Spain's General Tax Act (Ley General Tributaria, LGT) distinguishes two cases. If you filed a return with an amount to pay late on your own, without a request from the tax office, you pay a late filing surcharge (recargo, art. 27 LGT) instead of a penalty. If the tax office acted first, there is no longer a surcharge: the tax is assessed with interest and a penalty may be imposed (art. 191 LGT).

    The law treats as a request any action by the tax office that you have been formally notified of and that leads to an inspection, clarification or additional assessment of the tax (art. 27.1 LGT). So, if a letter from the AEAT has already arrived, the first step is to understand what it is: a request concerning a specific year or an informational message.

    Messages during the campaign. In the guidelines of the tax control plan for 2026 (BOE of 12 March 2026), the AEAT states that during the IRPF filing period it will keep sending messages to people who, according to its data, are obliged to file a return but in all likelihood have unintentionally not done so. The AEAT calls these mailings preventive: they go out while the return can still be filed on time.

    If there is a letter from the tax office, we review it together with your situation — more on the page for the annual IRPF tax return. Whether you were obliged to file for that year is covered in the article on the €22,000 threshold.

    A return with an amount to pay: the surcharge by month

    The surcharge is calculated on the amount to pay shown in the return: 1% plus another 1% for each full month that has passed since the end of the filing and payment period (art. 27.2 LGT). For the 2025 return, that period ended on 30 June 2026 (art. 7 Orden HAC/277/2026). Once 12 months have passed since the deadline, the surcharge becomes a fixed 15%, and for the time from the end of those 12 months to the day of filing, late-payment interest (interés de demora) is added.

    When the return is filed after the deadline Surcharge
    Within the first month 1%
    Within the second month 2%
    Each following month another 1%
    Within the twelfth month 12%
    More than 12 months later 15% plus late-payment interest

    Late-payment interest is the statutory interest rate (interés legal del dinero) increased by 25%, unless the budget act sets a different one (art. 26.6 LGT); according to the AEAT Renta 2025 manual, in 2026 it is 4.0625% a year — until the 2026 budget act comes into force.

    You do not calculate the surcharge yourself. The tax office opens a separate procedure on its own initiative: it sends a proposed assessment (propuesta de liquidación), gives you a period to object and then issues the assessment of the surcharge (liquidación). It is reduced by 25% if both conditions are met (art. 27.5 LGT):

    • all the tax on the return is paid at the time of filing — or within the deferral or instalment periods that you requested when filing and were granted against a bank guarantee (aval) or a surety insurance policy (certificado de seguro de caución);
    • the remaining surcharge is paid within the deadline in the notice about it: if the notice is received between the 1st and the 15th of the month, by the 20th of the following month; if between the 16th and the end of the month, by the 5th of the second month after that (art. 62.2 LGT).

    Filing without paying costs more

    If you did not pay the tax when filing and did not request a deferral or instalments, the surcharges of the enforced collection period are added to the late filing surcharge (art. 27.3 LGT). For a late return, this period starts on the day after filing (art. 161.1.b LGT), and the size of the surcharge depends on when you pay (art. 28 LGT):

    • 5% — if the whole debt is paid before the notice of enforced collection (providencia de apremio);
    • 10% — if the debt and this surcharge are paid within the deadline stated in the notice;
    • 20% plus late-payment interest — in all other cases.
    Splitting the payment into 60% and 40% without interest is not possible for a late return: this right only exists for a return filed on time (art. 62.2 of the IRPF Regulation, art. 11.5 Orden HAC/277/2026). If you cannot pay everything at once, a deferral or instalments (aplazamiento, fraccionamiento) are requested when filing: the law allows this when your financial situation temporarily prevents you from paying on time (art. 65 LGT).

    A return with a refund: a possible €100 penalty

    If the return entitles you to a refund or the result is zero, there is nothing to calculate a surcharge on. But the law treats being late with such a return as an infringement that causes no loss to the Treasury: the penalty is €200, and if filed before a request from the tax office — half that, €100 (art. 198.1–2 LGT). If you pay it within the deadline in the notice and do not appeal, the penalty is reduced by a further 40% (art. 188.3 LGT).

    This also applies to those who were not obliged to file and file a return to get a refund. In ruling V2852-23 of 24 October 2023, the DGT clarified: such a return can be filed after the deadline too, and it triggers the refund procedure, but the delay itself is an infringement under art. 198 LGT. The penalty is imposed only if there is fault (art. 183.1 LGT), and the tax office is the one that assesses it.

    Being late does not cancel the right to a refund. The tax office has six months to check the return and pay the refund, and for a late return they are counted from the filing date (art. 103.1 of the IRPF Act); if the refund is delayed through no fault of yours, interest is paid on the amount (art. 103.4). The 2025 return can be filed online until 1 July 2030 inclusive (art. 8.10 Orden HAC/277/2026).

    If the tax office wrote first

    After a request, there is no longer a late filing surcharge. The tax office assesses the tax with late-payment interest (art. 26.2 LGT) and may impose a penalty for non-payment: 50% of the unpaid amount, and if it exceeds €3,000 and income was concealed — from 50 to 100% (art. 191 LGT). The law also treats a return that was not filed as concealment (art. 184.2 LGT). The full scale of penalties is in the article on IRPF and taxes in Spain.

    The penalty under art. 191 is reduced by 30% if you agree with the assessment, and then by a further 40% if you pay the rest on time and do not appeal either the assessment or the penalty (art. 188 LGT). If the letter has already arrived, send it to us straight away, together with your income details: we will work out what it asks for and by what date.

    How to file a late return

    Each year is filed as a separate return: the surcharge instead of a penalty applies only if the return states the year and contains only data for that year (art. 27.4 LGT). Income forgotten from the previous year and entered in the return for the following year does not fall under this rule: such non-payment is treated as a minor infringement with a 50% penalty (art. 191.2 and 191.6 LGT).

    You can log in to Renta WEB with an electronic signature (a digital certificate or DNIe), Cl@ve Móvil or a reference number (número de referencia, art. 8.1 Orden HAC/277/2026); how to get the number with your NIE is covered in the article on the reference number. You can also file the return yourself. Bear in mind that a corrected return (autoliquidación rectificativa) filed after the deadline also counts as filed late (AEAT Renta 2025 manual), so it is better to file a late return without errors from the start.

    We check which years you were obliged to file for and gather certificates of income and withholdings, including from abroad, and the calculation and filing are done by our tax specialist. If a refund is due, we pass on the confirmation and keep track of the refund. It pays to settle the tax on a late return straight away when filing: that keeps the option of reducing the surcharge by 25% (art. 27.5 LGT). The return itself is best filed as early as possible: in the first 12 months, each full month adds 1% to the surcharge, after that late-payment interest grows, and if the tax office writes first, there will be interest and a possible penalty instead of the surcharge (art. 27 and 191 LGT).

    Frequently asked questions

    I filed my 2025 return myself in October and had no letters from the tax office. Will there be a penalty?
    If the return showed an amount to pay and the tax office had not sent any requests about IRPF for that year before you filed, there is no penalty: you pay a surcharge — 1% plus another 1% for each full month after 30 June 2026 (art. 27 LGT). The surcharge is calculated by the tax office, which sends a separate notice. If you paid the tax when filing and pay the surcharge within the deadline in the notice, it is reduced by 25% (art. 27.5 LGT). If, however, the return was a refund return, a €100 penalty is possible (art. 198 LGT).
    My return was a refund return, but I filed it in September. Will I still get the refund?
    Being late does not cancel the right to a refund: the return triggers the refund procedure, and the tax office has six months from the filing date to check it and pay out (art. 103 of the IRPF Act). At the same time, for the delay the law provides for a €100 penalty if you filed before a request from the tax office (art. 198.2 LGT), and it is reduced by 40% if paid on time without an appeal. According to the DGT clarification (V2852-23), this also applies to those who were not obliged to file, and whether a penalty is imposed depends on whether the tax office establishes fault.
    A letter came from Hacienda, and I still have not filed the return. If I file now, will there only be a surcharge?
    If the letter is a request or the start of an inspection concerning IRPF for that year, then no, not any more: the surcharge applies only when filing without a request (art. 27.1 LGT). In that case the tax is assessed with interest and a penalty may be imposed under art. 191 LGT — starting at 50% of the unpaid amount; it is reduced by 30% if you agree with the assessment and by a further 40% if paid on time without an appeal (art. 188). Under its plan, the AEAT sends preventive messages during the filing period, while the return can still be filed on time. Send us the letter: we will work out exactly what it asks for.
    I split the payment into 60% and 40% and did not pay the second part by 5 November. Does that count as a late return?
    No, the return was filed on time. The second part for 2025 had to be paid by 5 November 2026 (art. 11.5 Orden HAC/277/2026), and from 6 November the enforced collection period started on the unpaid part (art. 161.1.b LGT). If you pay it before the enforced collection notice (providencia de apremio), the surcharge is 5%; within the deadline in that notice — 10%; later — 20% plus interest (art. 28 LGT).
    I have not filed returns for several years. Can I file everything in one return?
    No: each year is filed as a separate return with only that year's data, otherwise instead of a late filing surcharge it is an infringement with a penalty of 50% of the unpaid amount (art. 27.4 and 191.6 LGT). The surcharge for each year is counted from its own deadline: if more than 12 months have passed since it, it is 15% plus late-payment interest. We check which years you were obliged to file for, and the calculation and filing are done by our tax specialist.
    Along with IRPF, I also missed Modelo 720. Is there a surcharge there too?
    No: Modelo 720 is an informative return with no tax due on it, so for a delay the general penalty under art. 198 LGT applies, which has been used since the special regime was abolished by Ley 5/2022: €20 for each item or set of data, from €300 to €20,000 for each block. If you file late on your own, before a request from the tax office, it is half that: from €150 to €10,000. More on the page Modelo 720 — assets abroad.
